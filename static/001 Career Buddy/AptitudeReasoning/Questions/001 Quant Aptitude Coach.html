    HCF & LCM — Finding Both Easy
    ❓ Find the HCF and LCM of 36 and 48. Also verify: HCF × LCM = Product of numbers.
    Step-by-step Solution
    ①Prime factorize: 36 = 2² × 3² and 48 = 2⁴ × 3
    ②HCF = product of lowest powers of common factors = 2² × 3 = 12
    ③LCM = product of highest powers of all factors = 2⁴ × 3² = 144
    ④Verify: HCF × LCM = 12 × 144 = 1728 = 36 × 48 ✓
    ✅ HCF = 12  |  LCM = 144
    2
    BODMAS — Complex Simplification Medium
    ❓ Simplify: 18 ÷ 6 × (4 + 2²) − 3 × 5 + 12 ÷ 4
    Step-by-step Solution
    ①Brackets: (4 + 2²) = (4 + 4) = 8   →   18 ÷ 6 × 8 − 3 × 5 + 12 ÷ 4
    ②Division & Multiplication (L→R): 18÷6 = 3; 3×8 = 24; 3×5 = 15; 12÷4 = 3
    ③Addition & Subtraction: 24 − 15 + 3 = 12
    ✅ Answer = 12
    3
    Surds & Indices — Simplification Medium
    ❓ Simplify: (√75 − √48 + √108) and express in simplest surd form.
    Step-by-step Solution
    ①√75 = √(25×3) = 5√3
    ②√48 = √(16×3) = 4√3
    ③√108 = √(36×3) = 6√3
    ④5√3 − 4√3 + 6√3 = (5 − 4 + 6)√3 = 7√3
    ✅ Answer = 7√3

    🚀 Advanced Level — Remainders, Cyclicity & Factorial Zeros

    Once the basics are solid, most exams shift to modular arithmetic: finding remainders and unit digits without doing the full multiplication.

    // ADVANCED NUMBER SYSTEM TOOLKIT Unit digit cyclicity = digits 2,3,7,8 repeat every 4 powers; 4,9 repeat every 2; 0,1,5,6 never change Remainder shortcut = (a × b) mod n = [(a mod n) × (b mod n)] mod n Trailing zeros in n! = ⌊n/5⌋ + ⌊n/25⌋ + ⌊n/125⌋ + … (count of factor 5's) No. of factors of N = if N = p1^a × p2^b × p3^c → factors = (a+1)(b+1)(c+1) Sum of factors of N = [(p1^(a+1)−1)/(p1−1)] × [(p2^(b+1)−1)/(p2−1)] × …
    4
    Trailing Zeros in a Factorial Hard
    ❓ How many trailing zeros are there in 125!?
    ①Zeros come from pairs of 2×5 — factor 5 is scarcer, so count powers of 5
    ②⌊125/5⌋ = 25, ⌊125/25⌋ = 5, ⌊125/125⌋ = 1
    ③Total = 25 + 5 + 1 = 31 trailing zeros
    ✅ 31 trailing zeros
    5
    Cyclicity — Finding Unit Digit / Remainder Hard
    ❓ Find the remainder when 7123 is divided by 5.
    ①Powers of 7 mod 5 cycle: 7¹≡2, 7²≡4, 7³≡3, 7⁴≡1, then repeats (cycle length 4)
    ②123 mod 4 = 3 → same remainder as 7³
    ③7³ mod 5 = 343 mod 5 = 3
    ✅ Remainder = 3
    ⚠️
    Interview/Exam Tip Whenever you see "remainder when a huge power is divided by a small number," look for a cycle — never try to compute the full power directly.

    Percentages, Profit & Loss

    One of the most frequently tested topics across all competitive exams — especially in banking and MBA entrances.

    Successive %Compound InterestBreak-even
    // KEY PERCENTAGE FORMULAS Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100 % Increase = (Increase / Original) × 100 % Decrease = (Decrease / Original) × 100 Successive % = a + b + (a×b)/100 [net effect of two changes a% and b%] // PROFIT & LOSS Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100 → Profit = SP − CP Loss % = (Loss / CP) × 100 → Loss = CP − SP SP = CP × (1 + Profit%/100) Marked Price = CP × (1 + Markup%/100) SP after discount= MP × (1 − Discount%/100) // INTEREST Simple Interest = (P × R × T) / 100 Compound Interest= P × (1 + R/100)^T − P [annual] Half-yearly = P × (1 + R/200)^(2T) − P Quarterly = P × (1 + R/400)^(4T) − P
    💡 Profit & Loss — Visual Flow
    💡
    Exam Trick — Successive Percentage If a price increases by a% then decreases by b%, net effect = a − b − ab/100. When a = b, net = −a²/100 (always a net LOSS).
    1
    Profit % Calculation Easy
    ❓ A shopkeeper buys an item for ₹640 and sells it for ₹800. Find profit percentage.
    ①Profit = SP − CP = 800 − 640 = ₹160
    ②Profit % = (160/640) × 100 = 25%
    ✅ Profit = 25%
    2
    Successive Percentage Change Medium
    ❓ A population increases by 20% in Year 1 and decreases by 10% in Year 2. What is the net % change?
    ①Formula: Net % = a + b + ab/100 where a = +20, b = −10
    ②Net % = 20 + (−10) + (20 × −10)/100 = 10 − 2 = +8%
    ③Verify: 100 → 120 → 120 × 0.9 = 108 → net = +8% ✓
    ✅ Net Increase = 8%
    3
    Compound Interest — Half-Yearly Hard
    ❓ Find CI on ₹10,000 at 10% per annum compounded half-yearly for 1.5 years.
    ①Half-yearly rate = 10/2 = 5%; Number of periods = 1.5 × 2 = 3
    ②Amount = 10000 × (1 + 5/100)³ = 10000 × (1.05)³
    ③= 10000 × 1.157625 = ₹11,576.25
    ④CI = 11,576.25 − 10,000 = ₹1,576.25
    ✅ CI = ₹1,576.25
    4
    Discount & Break-Even Medium
    ❓ A trader marks goods 25% above CP and offers a 10% discount. Find profit/loss %.
    ①Let CP = 100. MP = 125 (25% markup)
    ②SP = 125 × (1 − 10/100) = 125 × 0.9 = 112.5
    ③Profit = 112.5 − 100 = 12.5 → Profit % = 12.5%
    ✅ Profit = 12.5%

    🚀 Advanced Level — CI-SI Gaps, Depreciation & Reverse Problems

    Advanced papers rarely ask for a plain profit % — they hide the CP/SP or ask you to work backwards from tax, discount, or growth data.

    // ADVANCED PERCENTAGE / INTEREST TOOLKIT CI − SI (2 yrs) = P × (R/100)² CI − SI (3 yrs) = P × (R/100)² × [(R/100) + 3] Depreciation = P × (1 − R/100)^T [value after T years] Population growth = P × (1 + R/100)^T Reverse % (find CP) = CP = SP / (1 + Profit%/100) or SP / (1 − Loss%/100)
    4
    CI − SI Difference (2 years) Hard
    ❓ The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 2 years at 12% p.a. is ₹720. Find the sum.
    ①CI − SI (2 yrs) = P × (R/100)²
    ②720 = P × (12/100)² = P × 0.0144
    ③P = 720 / 0.0144 = ₹50,000
    ✅ Sum = ₹50,000
    5
    Reverse Percentage — Find the Original Price Hard
    ❓ After a 20% discount and then 10% tax on the discounted price, a shirt costs ₹1,584. Find the original marked price.
    ①Let MP = x. After 20% discount: SP = 0.8x
    ②After 10% tax: Final = 0.8x × 1.10 = 0.88x
    ③0.88x = 1584 → x = 1584 / 0.88 = ₹1,800
    ✅ Marked Price = ₹1,800

    Ratio, Proportion & Variation

    Used in partnership problems, mixtures, and real-world comparisons across almost every exam.

    MixturesAlligationPartnershipDirect/Inverse
    🧪 Alligation Method — Visual
    1
    Ratio — Partnership Profit Split Medium
    ❓ A, B, C invest ₹5000, ₹8000, ₹10000 respectively for a year. Total profit = ₹46,000. Find each person's share.
    ①Ratio = 5000 : 8000 : 10000 = 5 : 8 : 10 (total parts = 23)
    ②A's share = (5/23) × 46000 = ₹10,000
    ③B's share = (8/23) × 46000 = ₹16,000
    ④C's share = (10/23) × 46000 = ₹20,000
    ✅ ₹10,000 : ₹16,000 : ₹20,000
    2
    Alligation — Mixing Two Varieties Medium
    ❓ In what ratio should rice at ₹24/kg and ₹36/kg be mixed to get a mixture at ₹30/kg?
    ①Cheaper = 24, Dearer = 36, Mean = 30
    ②Parts of cheaper = D − M = 36 − 30 = 6
    ③Parts of dearer = M − C = 30 − 24 = 6
    ④Ratio = 6 : 6 = 1 : 1
    ✅ Mix in ratio 1 : 1

    🚀 Advanced Level — Repeated Replacement & Time-Weighted Partnership

    Two patterns that trip up even experienced candidates: repeated dilution of a mixture, and partnerships where capital or time differs between partners.

    // ADVANCED RATIO TOOLKIT Repeated replacement = Final qty of A = Initial × (1 − replaced/total)^n Partnership (unequal time) = Profit ratio = (Capital₁×Time₁) : (Capital₂×Time₂) : … Combined ratio (a:b, b:c) = a:b:c = (a×b') : (b×b') : (c×a') [LCM the common term]
    3
    Repeated Replacement — Milk & Water Hard
    ❓ A container has 81 litres of pure milk. 27 litres is withdrawn and replaced with water, and this is repeated 2 more times (3 total operations). How much milk remains?
    ①Fraction removed each time = 27/81 = 1/3, so fraction remaining each time = 2/3
    ②Final milk = 81 × (2/3)³ = 81 × 8/27
    ③= 24 litres
    ✅ Milk remaining = 24 litres
    4
    Partnership — Different Time Periods Hard
    ❓ A invests ₹8,000 for the full year. B invests ₹12,000 but only for 8 months. If the total profit is ₹9,400, find each one's share.
    ①A's capital-time = 8000 × 12 = 96,000
    ②B's capital-time = 12000 × 8 = 96,000
    ③Ratio = 96,000 : 96,000 = 1:1 → each gets 9400/2 = ₹4,700
    ✅ A = ₹4,700 | B = ₹4,700

    Averages & Statistics

    High-weight topic covering mean, median, mode, standard deviation, and probability fundamentals.

    ⭐ HIGH WEIGHTProbabilityPermutationStandard Deviation
    📊 Measures of Central Tendency on a Distribution
    // MEASURES OF CENTRAL TENDENCY Mean (AM) = Sum of all values / Count Weighted Mean = Σ(wᵢ × xᵢ) / Σwᵢ Median = Middle value (when sorted). If even n: avg of n/2 and n/2+1 Mode = Most frequently occurring value // DISPERSION Range = Max − Min Variance (σ²) = Σ(xᵢ − mean)² / n Std Dev (σ) = √Variance // PROBABILITY P(A) = Favourable outcomes / Total outcomes P(A') (complement) = 1 − P(A) P(A|B) = P(A∩B) / P(B) [Conditional] nPr = n! / (n−r)! [Permutation] nCr = n! / [r!(n−r)!] [Combination]
    1
    Mean, Median, Mode & SD Medium
    ❓ For the data set: 4, 7, 7, 10, 12, 13, 13, 13, 15 — find Mean, Median, Mode, and Range.
    ①Sum = 94; Count = 9 → Mean = 94/9 = 10.44
    ②Sorted: 4,7,7,10,12,13,13,13,15 → Middle (5th) = 12 = Median
    ③13 appears 3 times → Mode = 13
    ④Range = 15 − 4 = 11
    ✅ Mean≈10.44 | Median=12 | Mode=13 | Range=11
    2
    Probability — Card Drawing Medium
    ❓ A card is drawn from a standard deck of 52. What is P(King or Heart)?
    ①P(King) = 4/52; P(Heart) = 13/52; P(King of Hearts) = 1/52
    ②P(King ∪ Heart) = P(K) + P(H) − P(K∩H) = 4/52 + 13/52 − 1/52 = 16/52 = 4/13
    ✅ P = 4/13 ≈ 0.308
    3
    Permutation vs Combination Hard
    ❓ In how many ways can a committee of 3 be chosen from 8 people? And a password of 3 digits from 0–9 (no repeat)?
    ①Committee (order doesn't matter) = ⁸C₃ = 8!/(3!×5!) = 56 ways
    ②Password (order matters) = ¹⁰P₃ = 10×9×8 = 720 ways
    ✅ Committee: 56 | Password: 720
    4
    Conditional Probability (Bayes' basics) Hard
    ❓ A bag has 5 red and 4 blue balls. Two balls drawn without replacement. P(2nd is red | 1st is red)?
    ①After 1st red: remaining = 4 red, 4 blue = 8 balls total
    ②P(2nd red | 1st red) = 4/8 = 1/2
    ✅ P = 1/2 = 0.5

    🚀 Advanced Level — Variance Shortcut & Circular Permutations

    For experienced candidates, exams push into shortcut-based variance calculation and permutation problems with restrictions.

    // ADVANCED STATISTICS TOOLKIT Variance (shortcut) = (Mean of squares) − (Square of mean) = Σxᵢ²/n − (Σxᵢ/n)² Combined mean = (n1×m1 + n2×m2) / (n1 + n2) Circular permutation = (n−1)! [n distinct objects around a circle] Permutation w/ repetition = n! / (p! × q! × …) [p, q = counts of repeated items] Bayes' theorem = P(A|B) = [P(B|A) × P(A)] / P(B)
    4
    Variance — Shortcut Method Hard
    ❓ Find the variance of the numbers: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10.
    ①Mean = (2+4+6+8+10)/5 = 30/5 = 6
    ②Sum of squares = 4+16+36+64+100 = 220 → Mean of squares = 220/5 = 44
    ③Variance = 44 − 6² = 44 − 36 = 8
    ✅ Variance = 8 (SD = √8 ≈ 2.83)
    5
    Circular Arrangement Hard
    ❓ In how many ways can 6 people be seated around a circular table?
    ①Circular permutations of n distinct objects = (n−1)!
    ②= (6−1)! = 5! = 5×4×3×2×1 = 120
    ✅ 120 ways

    1
    AP — Sum of First n Terms Easy
    ❓ Find the sum of first 20 terms of AP: 3, 7, 11, 15, …
    ①a = 3, d = 4, n = 20
    ②S₂₀ = 20/2 × [2(3) + 19(4)] = 10 × [6 + 76] = 10 × 82 = 820
    ✅ S₂₀ = 820
    2
    Quadratic — Roots using Formula Medium
    ❓ Solve: 2x² − 5x + 3 = 0
    ①a=2, b=−5, c=3 → D = 25 − 24 = 1
    ②x = (5 ± 1)/4 → x = 6/4 = 3/2 or x = 4/4 = 1
    ✅ x = 3/2 or x = 1

    🚀 Advanced Level — Progressions & Nature of Roots

    Beyond solving simple equations, advanced papers test arithmetic/geometric progressions and reasoning about a quadratic's roots without solving it.

    // ADVANCED ALGEBRA TOOLKIT AP nth term = a + (n−1)d AP sum = n/2 × [2a + (n−1)d] GP nth term = a × r^(n−1) GP sum (finite) = a(r^n − 1)/(r−1) [r≠1] GP sum (infinite) = a/(1−r) [only valid when |r| < 1] Discriminant D = b² − 4ac // D > 0 → real, distinct roots | D = 0 → real, equal roots | D < 0 → complex roots Sum/Product of roots = Sum = −b/a, Product = c/a
    4
    Infinite GP Sum Hard
    ❓ Find the sum of the infinite series: 8 + 4 + 2 + 1 + 0.5 + …
    ①a = 8, r = 4/8 = 1/2 (|r| < 1, so sum converges)
    ②Sum = a/(1−r) = 8/(1 − 0.5) = 8/0.5 = 16
    ✅ Sum = 16
    5
    Nature of Roots Without Solving Hard
    ❓ Without solving, determine the nature of the roots of 3x² + 2x + 5 = 0, and find the sum and product of its roots.
    ①D = b² − 4ac = 2² − 4(3)(5) = 4 − 60 = −56 → D < 0 → complex, unequal roots
    ②Sum of roots = −b/a = −2/3
    ③Product of roots = c/a = 5/3
    ✅ Complex roots | Sum = −2/3 | Product = 5/3

    Volume of Cone Easy
    ❓ A cone has radius 7 cm and height 24 cm. Find its volume. (π = 22/7)
    ①V = ⅓ × π × r² × h = ⅓ × 22/7 × 49 × 24
    ②= ⅓ × 22 × 7 × 24 = ⅓ × 3696 = 1232 cm³

    Coordinate Geometry — Distance & Slope Medium
    ❓ Find distance between A(3, 4) and B(7, 1). Also find slope of AB.
    ①Distance = √[(7−3)² + (1−4)²] = √[16 + 9] = √25 = 5 units
    ②Slope = (1−4)/(7−3) = −3/4 = −0.75

    Frustum of a Cone Hard
    ❓ A bucket (frustum) has top radius 20 cm, bottom radius 12 cm, and height 21 cm. Find its volume. (π ≈ 22/7)
    ①Volume = (πh/3)(R² + r² + Rr) = (22/7 × 21/3)(400 + 144 + 240)
    ②= (22 × 1)(784) = 17,248 cm³
    ✅ Volume ≈ 17,248 cm³

    Area of a Triangle from Coordinates Hard
    ❓ Find the area of the triangle with vertices A(1,2), B(4,6), C(6,2).
    ①Area = ½|1(6−2) + 4(2−2) + 6(2−6)|
    ②= ½|4 + 0 − 24| = ½ × 20 = 10 sq. units
    ✅ Area = 10 sq. units

    Data Sufficiency Hard
    ❓ What is the average of 5 consecutive even numbers?
    Statement I: Smallest number is 18.
    Statement II: Largest number is 26.
    ①Using Stmt I alone: Numbers = 18,20,22,24,26 → Avg = 22. Sufficient
    ②Using Stmt II alone: Largest = 26 → 18,20,22,24,26 → Avg = 22. Sufficient
    ③Either statement alone is sufficient.
    ✅ Answer: Either statement alone is sufficient
    4
    Approximation in DI Medium
    ❓ In 2022, combined sales of A+B = ₹700 Cr. If total industry sales = ₹2810 Cr, what is approximate market share of A+B?
    ①Exact: 700/2810 × 100 ≈ 24.9%
    ②Approximate: 700/2800 × 100 = 25% (fast method — round 2810 → 2800)
    ✅ ≈ 25% market share

    🚀 Advanced Level — CAGR & Caselet-Style Data Sufficiency

    Beyond single-year percentage change, advanced DI sets ask about growth rate compounded over multiple years, and "can this question be answered at all" logic.

    // ADVANCED DI TOOLKIT CAGR = [(Ending Value / Beginning Value)^(1/n) − 1] × 100 Data Sufficiency = Ask: does Statement I alone answer it? Does Statement II alone? Do BOTH together? Approximation rule = Round to nearest convenient number only when options are far apart (>3% gap)
    4
    CAGR Over Multiple Years Hard
    ❓ A company's revenue grew from ₹200 Cr in 2020 to ₹392 Cr in 2024 (4 years). Find the CAGR.
    ①Ratio = 392/200 = 1.96
    ②CAGR = (1.96)^(1/4) − 1 → 1.96 ≈ 1.4² so (1.96)^(1/4) ≈ 1.4^(1/2) ≈ 1.183
    ③CAGR ≈ 0.183 × 100 = ≈18.3% per year
    ✅ CAGR ≈ 18.3%
    5
    Data Sufficiency Hard
    ❓ What is the profit percentage of a shop?
    Statement I: Cost price is ₹500.
    Statement II: Selling price is 20% more than cost price.
    Which statement(s) are sufficient?
    ①Statement I alone: only CP known, no SP → cannot find profit % → NOT sufficient
    ②Statement II alone: gives the relationship SP = 1.2×CP directly → profit % = 20% regardless of actual CP value → sufficient alone
    ✅ Statement II alone is sufficient

    Speed, Distance & Work

    Trains, boats, streams, pipes — a consistently tested set with predictable patterns once you learn the tricks.

    Relative SpeedBoats & StreamsPipes & CisternsWork Efficiency
    ⚡ SDT Triangle & Relationship
    // TRAINS CROSSING Train crossing a pole → Time = Train Length / Speed Train crossing a bridge → Time = (Train + Bridge Length) / Speed Two trains (same dir) → Time = (L₁+L₂) / (S₁−S₂) Two trains (opp dir) → Time = (L₁+L₂) / (S₁+S₂) // SPEED CONVERSIONS km/hr → m/s = multiply by 5/18 m/s → km/hr = multiply by 18/5
    1
    Train Crossing a Platform Easy
    ❓ A 200m train travelling at 72 km/hr crosses a platform. It crosses a pole in 10s. Find platform length.
    ①Speed = 72 km/hr = 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s
    ②Crossing pole in 10s → Length = 20 × 10 = 200m (train length confirmed)
    ③If it takes 20s to cross platform: 20 × 20 = 400m = train + platform → platform = 200m
    ✅ Platform Length = 200 m
    2
    Boats & Streams Medium
    ❓ A boat goes 30 km downstream in 2 hours and 18 km upstream in 3 hours. Find boat speed and stream speed.
    ①Downstream speed = 30/2 = 15 km/hr → B + W = 15
    ②Upstream speed = 18/3 = 6 km/hr → B − W = 6
    ③Adding: 2B = 21 → B = 10.5 km/hr; W = 15 − 10.5 = 4.5 km/hr
    ✅ Boat = 10.5 km/hr | Stream = 4.5 km/hr
    3
    Time & Work — Combined Efficiency Medium
    ❓ A can do a job in 12 days, B in 15 days. They work together for 4 days then A leaves. In how many more days will B finish?
    ①Combined rate = 1/12 + 1/15 = 5/60 + 4/60 = 9/60 = 3/20 per day
    ②Work done in 4 days = 4 × 3/20 = 12/20 = 3/5. Remaining = 2/5
    ③B alone: 2/5 ÷ 1/15 = 2/5 × 15 = 6 more days
    ✅ B needs 6 more days
    4
    Pipes & Cisterns Medium
    ❓ Pipe A fills a tank in 6 hours, B fills in 8 hours, C empties in 12 hours. All open together — how long to fill?
    ①Net rate = 1/6 + 1/8 − 1/12
    ②LCM(6,8,12) = 24 → 4/24 + 3/24 − 2/24 = 5/24 per hour
    ③Time = 24/5 = 4.8 hours = 4 hrs 48 min
    ✅ Tank fills in 4 hrs 48 min
    5
    Average Speed (Classic Trap!) Hard
    ❓ Ravi travels from A to B at 60 km/hr and returns at 40 km/hr. What is his average speed for the whole journey?
    ①⚠️ Trap: Average speed ≠ (60+40)/2 = 50 (WRONG)
    ②Formula: Avg speed = 2ab/(a+b) = 2×60×40/(60+40) = 4800/100 = 48 km/hr
    ③Always use harmonic mean for equal-distance, different-speed problems
    ✅ Average Speed = 48 km/hr
    🎯
    Coach's Golden Rule for Speed Problems When equal distances are covered at different speeds, always use the formula: Avg Speed = 2ab/(a+b). This is the harmonic mean — it will always be less than the arithmetic mean. This is one of the most common traps in exams!

    Two Trains Crossing Each Other Hard
    ❓ Two trains of length 120 m and 100 m run in opposite directions at 54 km/hr and 36 km/hr. Find the time they take to cross each other.
    ①Relative speed = 54 + 36 = 90 km/hr = 90 × 5/18 = 25 m/s
    ②Total length = 120 + 100 = 220 m
    ③Time = 220 / 25 = 8.8 seconds
    ✅ Time = 8.8 seconds
    7
    Race with Head Start Hard
    ❓ In a 400 m race, A beats B by 40 m. If A's speed is 10 m/s, find A's time and B's speed.
    ①A's time to finish 400 m = 400/10 = 40 seconds
    ②In that same 40 s, B covers only 400 − 40 = 360 m
    ③B's speed = 360/40 = 9 m/s
    ✅ A's time = 40s | B's speed = 9 m/s

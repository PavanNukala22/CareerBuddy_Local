    1.1 Figure Series Completion

    In figure series questions, a sequence of figures follows a rule involving rotation, reflection, shading shifts, or element addition/removal. You must identify the rule and choose the next figure.

    Common transformations: 90°/180° clockwise/anti-clockwise rotation; mirror image; shading alternation; element count increasing/decreasing by 1 each step.

    Fig 2 — Figure Series with Rotation Rule (90° Clockwise)
    1
    Figure Series — Shading Shift Pattern
    A 2×2 grid has 4 squares. Step 1: top-left shaded. Step 2: top-right shaded. Step 3: bottom-right shaded. Which square is shaded in Step 4?
    A
    Top-left square
    B
    Top-right square
    C
    Bottom-left square ✓
    D
    All four squares
    ✓ C — Bottom-left
    Rule: Shading moves clockwise around the 4 squares → TL → TR → BR → BL → back to start. Step 4 = Bottom-left.
    💡 Strategy
    Always trace the shading path clockwise and anti-clockwise. Clockwise in 2×2: TL→TR→BR→BL. Count which position comes next.

    1.2 Odd One Out in Figure Sets

    Check: Number of sides, Type of angle, Symmetry axis, Shading direction, Curved vs straight lines.

    1.3 Cube & Dice Visualization

    Key Rules:

    1. Standard die: opposite faces sum to 7 — pairs: (1,6), (2,5), (3,4).
    2. In a cube net, trace which faces become opposite when folded.
    3. If two die positions share two common faces in same orientation → determine the third by elimination.
    4. Adjacent faces are NEVER opposite faces.

    Fig 3 — Cube Net with Opposite Face Identification
    3
    Dice — Opposite Face Problem
    Two positions of a standard dice: Position 1: Top=1, Front=2, Right=3. Position 2: Top=2, Front=4, Right=5. What face is opposite to face 3?

    Using standard rule (opposite faces sum to 7):

    1↔6, 2↔5, 3↔4 → Face 3 is opposite to Face 4

    Verify with positions: In Pos1, faces visible are 1,2,3 — none can be opposite each other. Face 4 is not visible → could be opposite to 3 ✓

    In Pos2, faces 2,4,5 are visible → 2 and 5 are NOT opposite (they're adjacent) confirming 2↔6, 4↔... let's check 4: not 2, not 5 → 4 opposite 3 ✓ (or 1)

    A
    1
    B
    4 ✓
    C
    6
    D
    5
    ✓ B — Face 4 is opposite to Face 3
    Standard rule: opposite faces of a die sum to 7. (3+4=7). Verified: In both positions, faces 3 and 4 never appear together — confirming they are opposite.
    🎯 Quick Rule
    For any two dice positions: faces visible together are ADJACENT (never opposite). Always eliminate those from opposite candidates first.

    1.4 Paper Folding & Water Images

    Paper Folding: Holes punched on folded paper appear symmetrically when unfolded. Fold once → 2 layers → 1 punch = 2 holes (mirror symmetric about fold line).

    Water Image: Reflection in still water = vertical (top-to-bottom) flip.
    Mirror Image: Reflection in mirror = horizontal (left-to-right) flip.

    4
    Water Image vs Mirror Image
    The letter "R" stands normally. Identify its water image and its mirror image.
    Water image: R flipped upside-down (vertical flip) — the top becomes the bottom, like a reflection in still water below the object.
    Mirror image: R reversed left-right (horizontal flip) — the right leg of R moves to the left side. Unlike A or H, R is asymmetric so both images look distinctly different.
    💡 Memory Trick
    Water = Went down (vertical ↕ flip). Mirror = Moved sideways (horizontal ↔ flip).
    5
    Paper Folding — Hole Position
    A square paper is folded in half (left half onto right half). A hole is punched in the upper-right corner of the folded paper. When unfolded, where are the holes?
    ✓ Two holes appear — one in the upper-right and one in the upper-left, perfectly symmetric about the centre fold line. Rule: n folds → 2ⁿ holes from 1 punch.

    Deductive Reasoning
    ⚖️ Medium–High

    Deductive reasoning moves from general principles to specific conclusions. If the premises are true and the logic is valid, the conclusion MUST be true. Key types: Syllogisms, Logical connectives, Assumptions & Conclusions, Cause & Effect.

    Fig 4 — Syllogism Venn Diagram Framework with Rules
    6
    Syllogism — Venn Diagram Method
    Statements: (1) All cats are animals. (2) All animals are living beings.
    Conclusions: I. All cats are living beings.   II. Some living beings are cats.
    A
    Only Conclusion I follows
    B
    Only Conclusion II follows
    C
    Both I and II follow ✓
    D
    Neither follows
    ✓ C — Both Conclusions follow
    All A→B + All B→C → All A→C. Cats ⊂ Animals ⊂ Living Beings → All cats are living beings (I ✓). If all cats are living beings, some living beings are cats (valid conversion, II ✓).

    2.2 Logical Connectives

    Fig 5 — Logical Connectives: Truth Tables & Plain-English Equivalents
    7
    Logical Connective — Unless & Contrapositive
    Statement: "Unless a student practices daily, they will not clear the exam." Which conclusion is valid?
    A
    If a student practices daily, they will clear the exam.
    B
    If a student clears the exam, they must have practiced daily. ✓
    C
    A student who does not practice will certainly fail.
    D
    Practice is not necessary to clear the exam.
    ✓ B — Contrapositive is the valid conclusion
    "Unless P (practice), NOT Q (fail)" = "If ¬practice → fail." Contrapositive: "If NOT fail (i.e. pass) → practiced." Option B states this correctly. Option A is the inverse (not guaranteed). Option C is the original statement restated but not validly deduced without more.

    Analytical Reasoning
    🔥 High Weight

    Analytical reasoning tests ability to extract and organise information from conditions and constraints to answer questions about positions, relationships, or sequences.

    Key types: Linear seating · Circular arrangements · Scheduling & sequencing · Blood relations · Direction & distance sense

    Fig 6 — Linear vs Circular Seating Arrangement Framework
    8
    Linear Seating Arrangement
    Five friends A, B, C, D, E sit in a row. Conditions: (1) A is at one end. (2) B sits immediately right of A. (3) D sits between C and E. (4) C is not adjacent to B. Who sits in the middle (Position 3)?

    Step 1: A at one end → Pos 1 or 5.

    Step 2: B immediately right of A → A=Pos1, B=Pos2.

    Step 3: Remaining positions 3,4,5 for C, D, E.

    Step 4: D between C and E → C-D-E or E-D-C in positions 3,4,5.

    Step 5: C not adjacent to B(Pos2) → C cannot be Pos3. So E-D-C is the only valid order: E=3, D=4, C=5.

    Final: A(1) — B(2) — E(3) — D(4) — C(5)

    A
    A
    B
    D
    C
    E ✓
    D
    C
    ✓ C — E sits in Position 3 (middle)
    Final arrangement: A–B–E–D–C. Clue 4 (C not adjacent to B) eliminated the C-D-E order and confirmed E-D-C for positions 3-4-5.

    Inductive Reasoning
    ⚖️ Medium

    Inductive reasoning builds general rules from specific observations. In exams: number series, letter series, analogy completion, and classification (odd-one-out) questions.

    Fig 9 — Six Major Number Series Pattern Types with Examples
    11
    Number Series — Find the Wrong Term
    Find the wrong term: 2, 3, 6, 15, 54, 162, 486

    Check the multipliers:

    3/2 = 1.5 → ×1.5

    6/3 = 2 → ×2

    15/6 = 2.5 → ×2.5

    54/15 = 3.6 → ×3.6 ← breaks the pattern! (should be ×3 → 15×3=45)

    Pattern: multipliers increase by 0.5 each time: ×1.5, ×2, ×2.5, ×3, ×3.5...

    Expected: 2, 3, 6, 15, 45, 157.5... This doesn't stay integer-friendly.

    Better check: 2×1+1=3, 3×2=6, 6×2+3=15, 15×3+9=54, 54×3+0=162, 162×3=486 ✓

    All check out → the series as given has no error if the rule is complex. But if pattern is ×1.5,×2,×2.5,×3,×3... then 54 should be 37.5 (not integer). Re-examine: the question means find which term breaks simple pattern. Answer: 54 should be 45 (15×3) if the rule is ×1.5,×2,×2.5,×3...

    A
    6
    B
    15
    C
    54 (should be 45) ✓
    D
    162
    ✓ C — 54 is wrong; should be 45
    Pattern: multipliers increase by 0.5 each step — ×1.5, ×2, ×2.5, ×3, ×3.5... → 2→3→6→15→45→157.5. The term 54 breaks this. Corrected: 15×3=45.
    🎯 Wrong Term Strategy
    Check differences first, then ratios, then difference of differences. The term that breaks ALL candidate patterns is the wrong term.

    Critical Reasoning
    ⚖️ Medium–High
    Fig 11 — Critical Reasoning: Argument Anatomy with Strengthen/Weaken Framework
    13
    Assumption Identification
    Argument: "Students who spend more than 2 hours on social media daily score lower on exams. Therefore, reducing social media use will improve exam scores." Which is the underlying ASSUMPTION?
    A
    All students use social media.
    B
    Social media use CAUSES lower scores (not just correlated). ✓
    C
    Exams are the only measure of academic success.
    D
    Students have exactly 2 hours of free time daily.
    ✓ B — Causation is assumed, not just correlation
    The conclusion "reducing social media WILL improve scores" requires that social media use CAUSES low scores. If it were merely correlated (both caused by a third factor like poor time management), reducing social media alone wouldn't help. This causal link is the unstated assumption.
    14
    Weakening an Argument
    Argument: "City X installed speed cameras, and road accidents fell by 40% in the next year. Therefore, speed cameras reduce accidents." Which option MOST WEAKENS this?
    A
    Speed cameras are expensive to maintain.
    B
    Other cities that installed cameras also saw accident reductions.
    C
    City X also repaired all major roads and increased police patrols that same year. ✓
    D
    Speed cameras operate 24 hours a day.
    ✓ C — Alternative explanation weakens the argument
    If road repairs AND police patrols also happened simultaneously, the 40% reduction cannot be credited solely to speed cameras. This confounding variable undermines the causal claim. A is irrelevant (cost ≠ effectiveness). B actually strengthens the argument.
    15
    Statement–Conclusion Evaluation
    Statement: "In Country Y, 80% of professionals with advanced degrees are unemployed."
    Conclusions: I. Advanced degrees do not guarantee employment in Country Y.   II. The education system in Country Y is a failure.
    A
    Only Conclusion I follows. ✓
    B
    Only Conclusion II follows.
    C
    Both follow.
    D
    Neither follows.
    ✓ A — Only Conclusion I follows
    I: Directly follows — if 80% are unemployed despite advanced degrees, degrees clearly don't guarantee employment. ✓
    II: Over-generalisation — unemployment doesn't automatically mean the education system "is a failure." Economic, structural, or political factors may be responsible. Too broad to conclude from the statement alone. ✗

    6.1 Input–Output (Machine Coding)

    A machine processes words/numbers through multiple steps following a fixed rule. You must identify the rule applied at each step and apply it to find a specific step's output.

    Common rules: Sorting alphabetically, arranging numbers ascending/descending, shifting one element at a time, alternating between ends of the sequence.

    16
    Input–Output Machine
    A machine processes as follows:
    Input: eagle mango cherry banana date
    Step 1: banana mango cherry eagle date
    Step 2: banana cherry mango eagle date
    Step 3: banana cherry date eagle mango
    What is the pattern? What would Step 4 look like?

    Identify the rule:

    Input → Step 1: "banana" moved to front (alphabetically smallest word is placed at position 1)

    Step 1 → Step 2: "cherry" moved to position 2 (next alphabetically smallest among remaining)

    Step 2 → Step 3: "date" moved to position 3

    Rule: Bubble sort — each step moves the next alphabetically smallest word to its correct position from left.

    Step 4: "eagle" moves to position 4 → banana cherry date eagle mango

    A
    mango banana cherry date eagle
    B
    banana cherry date eagle mango ✓
    C
    banana date cherry eagle mango
    D
    cherry banana date mango eagle
    ✓ B — banana cherry date eagle mango
    The machine performs alphabetical bubble sort from left: each step locks one more word into its correct alphabetical position. Step 4 completes with "eagle" in position 4, leaving only "mango" at the end. Final = fully sorted.

    Coding-Decoding — Letter Shift Pattern
    In a certain code: FRIEND is written as HUMGPF. How is CANDLE written in that code?

    Apply to CANDLE:

    C(3)+2=E  |  A(1)+3=D  |  N(14)+4=R  |  D(4)+2=F  |  L(12)+2=N  |  E(5)+2=G

    → EDRFNG

    A
    EDRFMG
    B
    EDRFNG ✓
    C
    DCPFNG
    D
    ECRFNG
    ✓ B — EDRFNG
    Rule: Positions 1,2,3 shift by +2,+3,+4 (increasing). Positions 4,5,6 all shift by +2 (constant). Applying to CANDLE: C+2=E, A+3=D, N+4=R, D+2=F, L+2=N, E+2=G → EDRFNG.
    🎯 Coding Strategy
    Always decode at least 4-5 letters of the given example before concluding the rule. Verify with the last letter too. Some codes apply different rules to odd/even positions.

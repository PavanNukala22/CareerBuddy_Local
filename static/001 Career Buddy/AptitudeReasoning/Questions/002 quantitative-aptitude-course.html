    Natural numbers, integers, rational & irrational numbers. Divisibility, HCF, LCM, BODMAS, surds, indices, roots.

    Types of Numbers

    Every number you encounter belongs to a hierarchy. Understanding this hierarchy is the foundation for all quantitative reasoning.

    Fig 1.1 — The Real Number System hierarchy

    Classify each: −7, 0, ¾, √5, 22/7, π

    NumberNaturalWholeIntegerRationalIrrational

    Divisibility Rules

    Divisibility rules let you instantly check if a large number is divisible without performing full division.

    Fig 1.2 — Divisibility rules cheat sheet with worked example
    📗 Example 2 — Divisibility by 11

    Is 9,185,768 divisible by 11?

    Sum of digits at odd positions (from right): 8 + 7 + 8 + 9 = 32

    Sum of digits at even positions: 6 + 5 + 1 = 12

    Difference: 32 − 12 = 20. Since 20 is NOT divisible by 11, the number is not divisible by 11.

    HCF and LCM

    HCF (Highest Common Factor) is the largest number that divides two or more numbers exactly. LCM (Least Common Multiple) is the smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.

    📐 Key Formula
    HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b

    This relationship is extremely useful — if you know any three of these four values, you can find the fourth.

    📗 Example 3 — Finding HCF by Prime Factorization

    Find HCF of 60 and 84.

    60 = 2² × 3 × 5  |  84 = 2² × 3 × 7

    Common prime factors with lowest powers: 2² × 3 = 12

    📗 Example 4 — Finding LCM

    Find LCM of 12, 18, 24.

    12 = 2² × 3  |  18 = 2 × 3²  |  24 = 2³ × 3

    Take highest power of each prime: 2³ × 3² = 8 × 9 = 72

    BODMAS Simplification

    BODMAS defines the order of operations: Brackets → Orders (powers/roots) → Division → Multiplication → Addition → Subtraction.

    Fig 1.3 — BODMAS order of operations
    📗 Example 5 — BODMAS

    Simplify: 48 ÷ 8 × 3 + 6² − (4 + 2)

    1
    Brackets: (4 + 2) = 6 → Expression becomes 48 ÷ 8 × 3 + 6² − 6
    2
    Orders: 6² = 36 → 48 ÷ 8 × 3 + 36 − 6
    3
    Division: 48 ÷ 8 = 6 → 6 × 3 + 36 − 6
    4
    Multiplication: 6 × 3 = 18 → 18 + 36 − 6
    5
    Add/Sub (left to right): 18 + 36 − 6 = 48

    Surds & Indices

    📐 Laws of Indices
    aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ  |  aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ  |  (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ  |  a⁰ = 1  |  a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ
    📗 Example 6 — Rationalizing Surds

    Rationalize: 5 / (√3 − 1)

    Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate (√3 + 1):

    = 5(√3 + 1) / [(√3 − 1)(√3 + 1)] = 5(√3 + 1) / (3 − 1) = 5(√3 + 1) / 2

    📗 Example 7 — Simplifying Indices

    Simplify: (2³ × 4²) / 2⁵

    Convert 4² = (2²)² = 2⁴

    = (2³ × 2⁴) / 2⁵ = 2⁷ / 2⁵ = 2² = 4

    📗 Example 8 — Cube Roots

    Find ∛1728.

    1728 = 12 × 144 = 12 × 12 × 12 = 12³, so ∛1728 = 12

    📗 Example 9 — HCF & LCM Application

    Two alarm bells ring at intervals of 12 and 18 minutes. If they ring together at 8:00 AM, when will they ring together next?

    LCM(12, 18) = 36 minutes → They ring together at 8:36 AM

    📗 Example 10 — Mixed Application

    The HCF of two numbers is 14 and their LCM is 420. If one number is 84, find the other.

    HCF × LCM = Product of numbers → 14 × 420 = 84 × x → x = 5880/84 = 70

    36 = 2² × 3²  |  48 = 2⁴ × 3  |  72 = 2³ × 3²

    HCF = lowest powers of common primes = 2² × 3 = 12

    LCM = highest powers of all primes = 2⁴ × 3² = 16 × 9 = 144

    📘 Exercise 2

    Simplify: 18 + 6 ÷ 3 × 2 − (5 − 3)²

    Brackets: (5 − 3)² → first inner: 5−3 = 2, then order: 2² = 4

    Division: 6 ÷ 3 = 2 → Multiplication: 2 × 2 = 4

    Result: 18 + 4 − 4 = 18

    📘 Exercise 3

    Is 3,412,578 divisible by 6?

    Divisible by 2? Last digit = 8 (even) → ✓

    Divisible by 3? Digit sum = 3+4+1+2+5+7+8 = 30 → 30 ÷ 3 = 10 → ✓

    Since both pass → Yes, divisible by 6

    📘 Exercise 4

    Simplify: (27)^(2/3) × (8)^(−1/3)

    27 = 3³ → (3³)^(2/3) = 3² = 9

    8 = 2³ → (2³)^(−1/3) = 2⁻¹ = 1/2

    Result: 9 × 1/2 = 4.5

    📘 Exercise 5

    Rationalize: 7 / (√5 + √2)

    Multiply by conjugate (√5 − √2) / (√5 − √2):

    = 7(√5 − √2) / (5 − 2) = 7(√5 − √2) / 3

    📘 Exercise 6

    Three runners complete a lap in 8, 12, and 15 minutes. If they start together, after how many minutes will they be at the starting point together again?

    LCM(8, 12, 15): 8 = 2³, 12 = 2² × 3, 15 = 3 × 5

    LCM = 2³ × 3 × 5 = 120 minutes (2 hours)

    📘 Exercise 7

    Find the largest 4-digit number divisible by 12, 15, and 18.

    LCM(12,15,18) = 2² × 3² × 5 = 180

    Largest 4-digit = 9999. Divide: 9999 ÷ 180 = 55.55

    So 55 × 180 = 9900

    If √(x + 5) = 7, find x.

    Square both sides: x + 5 = 49 → x = 44

    📘 Exercise 9

    Simplify: √50 + √18 − √8

    √50 = 5√2, √18 = 3√2, √8 = 2√2

    = 5√2 + 3√2 − 2√2 = 6√2

    📘 Exercise 10

    The product of two numbers is 1680 and their HCF is 6. Find their LCM.

    HCF × LCM = Product → 6 × LCM = 1680 → LCM = 280

    Percentages, Profit & Loss

    Percentage computations, successive changes, cost/selling price, markup, discount, simple & compound interest.

    Percentage Fundamentals

    "Percent" means "per hundred." It's simply a way to express a number as a fraction of 100. Mastering percentage ↔ fraction ↔ decimal conversion is crucial.

    Fig 2.1 — Quick reference: percentages and their fraction equivalents
    📗 Example 1 — Basic Percentage

    A class has 45 students. 27 are girls. What percentage are girls?

    (27/45) × 100 = 60%. So 60% of the class are girls.

    📗 Example 2 — Percentage Increase

    A salary increases from ₹25,000 to ₹28,000. Find the percentage increase.

    Increase = 3,000. Percentage = (3000/25000) × 100 = 12%

    Successive Percentages

    📐 Successive Change Formula
    Net effect of a% then b% = (a + b + ab/100)%

    If a price increases by 20% then decreases by 10%: net = 20 + (−10) + (20×(−10))/100 = 20 − 10 − 2 = +8%

    📗 Example 3 — Successive Changes

    A population increases by 10% in year 1 and 20% in year 2. If initial population is 5000, find final population.

    After Year 1: 5000 × 1.10 = 5500

    After Year 2: 5500 × 1.20 = 6600

    Net change: (10 + 20 + 200/100)% = 32%. Verify: 5000 × 1.32 = 6600 ✓

    Profit & Loss

    Fig 2.2 — Profit & Loss visual relationships and formulas
    📗 Example 4 — Profit Percentage

    A shopkeeper buys an item for ₹400 and sells it for ₹500. Find profit %.

    Profit = 500 − 400 = 100. Profit% = (100/400) × 100 = 25%

    📗 Example 5 — Discount & Markup

    A shirt has MRP ₹1200. A discount of 15% is offered. Find the selling price.

    Discount = 15% of 1200 = 180. SP = 1200 − 180 = ₹1,020

    📗 Example 6 — Cost Price from SP and Profit%

    An article is sold for ₹660 at a profit of 10%. Find the cost price.

    SP = CP × (1 + 10/100) → 660 = CP × 1.1 → CP = 660/1.1 = ₹600

    Simple & Compound Interest

    📐 Interest Formulas
    SI = (P × R × T) / 100
    CI: A = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ  →  CI = A − P

    For half-yearly: rate becomes R/2, time becomes 2n periods.

    📗 Example 7 — Simple Interest

    ₹8,000 at 5% p.a. for 3 years. Find SI.

    SI = (8000 × 5 × 3)/100 = ₹1,200

    📗 Example 8 — Compound Interest

    ₹10,000 at 10% p.a. compounded annually for 2 years.

    A = 10000 × (1.1)² = 10000 × 1.21 = 12,100. CI = 12100 − 10000 = ₹2,100

    📗 Example 9 — SI vs CI Difference

    The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 2 years at 8% is ₹32. Find the sum.

    For 2 years: CI − SI = P × (R/100)². So 32 = P × (8/100)² = P × 0.0064

    P = 32/0.0064 = ₹5,000

    📗 Example 10 — Break-even

    A vendor buys 20 pens at ₹10 each and sells 16 at ₹12 each. The remaining 4 are unsold. What is the profit or loss?

    Total CP = 20 × 10 = 200. Total SP = 16 × 12 = 192. Loss = 200 − 192 = 8. Loss% = (8/200)×100 = 4% loss

    Solved Exercises

    📘 Exercise 1

    A number is increased by 25% and then decreased by 20%. Find the net change.

    Net = 25 + (−20) + (25 × −20)/100 = 25 − 20 − 5 = 0% (no change)

    📘 Exercise 2

    CP = ₹450, SP = ₹405. Find loss%.

    Loss = 450 − 405 = 45. Loss% = (45/450) × 100 = 10%

    📘 Exercise 3

    Find CI on ₹15,000 at 12% p.a. for 2 years, compounded half-yearly.

    Rate per half-year = 6%, periods = 4. A = 15000 × (1.06)⁴ = 15000 × 1.2625 = 18937.50

    CI = 18937.50 − 15000 = ₹3,937.50

    📘 Exercise 4

    A shopkeeper marks goods 40% above CP and allows a 10% discount. Find profit%.

    Let CP = 100. Marked Price = 140. SP = 140 × 0.9 = 126. Profit% = 26%

    📘 Exercise 5

    In what time will ₹4,000 become ₹4,630.50 at 5% p.a. CI?

    4630.50 = 4000 × (1.05)ⁿ → (1.05)ⁿ = 1.157625

    (1.05)³ = 1.157625. So n = 3 years

    📘 Exercise 6

    The price of sugar rises by 25%. By what percentage must consumption be reduced to maintain the same expenditure?

    Reduction = (increase / (100 + increase)) × 100 = (25/125) × 100 = 20%

    📘 Exercise 7

    A man sells two articles for ₹99 each. On one he gains 10%, on the other he loses 10%. Find overall gain/loss.

    CP₁ = 99/1.1 = 90. CP₂ = 99/0.9 = 110. Total CP = 200, Total SP = 198.

    Loss = 2. Loss% = (2/200) × 100 = 1% loss

    📘 Exercise 8

    What sum of money becomes ₹6,050 in 2 years at 10% p.a. SI?

    📘 Exercise 9

    A's salary is 20% more than B's. By what percent is B's salary less than A's?

    Let B = 100, A = 120. Difference = 20. B less than A by (20/120) × 100 = 16⅔%

    📘 Exercise 10

    A TV is sold at two successive discounts of 20% and 10% on MRP ₹5,000. Find selling price.

    After 20%: 5000 × 0.8 = 4000. After 10%: 4000 × 0.9 = ₹3,600

    Ratio, Proportion & Variation

    Ratios, direct & inverse proportion, partnership, mixtures and alligations.

    Ratio Basics

    A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind. If A:B = 3:5, for every 3 parts A gets, B gets 5 parts. Total parts = 8.

    Fig 3.1 — Partitioning a total using ratios
    📗 Example 1 — Dividing in a Ratio

    Divide ₹2,400 between A and B in the ratio 3:5.

    A's share = (3/8) × 2400 = ₹900, B's share = (5/8) × 2400 = ₹1,500

    📗 Example 2 — Combining Ratios

    If A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5, find A:B:C.

    Make B common: A:B = 8:12, B:C = 12:15. So A:B:C = 8:12:15

    Proportion

    If a:b = c:d, we say a, b, c, d are in proportion. The product of extremes equals the product of means: a × d = b × c.

    📗 Example 3 — Finding Missing Term

    If 3:x = 12:20, find x.

    3 × 20 = x × 12 → 60 = 12x → x = 5

    Direct & Inverse Variation

    📗 Example 4 — Direct Proportion

    If 5 kg of rice costs ₹200, what does 12 kg cost?

    Cost ∝ Quantity → 200/5 = x/12 → x = 200 × 12/5 = ₹480

    📗 Example 5 — Inverse Proportion

    12 workers finish a job in 15 days. How many days for 20 workers?

    Workers × Days = constant → 12 × 15 = 20 × d → d = 180/20 = 9 days

    Partnership

    📗 Example 6 — Simple Partnership

    A invests ₹50,000 and B invests ₹30,000 in a business. Profit is ₹16,000. Divide the profit.

    Ratio = 50000:30000 = 5:3. A's share = 5/8 × 16000 = ₹10,000. B = ₹6,000

    📗 Example 7 — Time-based Partnership

    A invests ₹40,000 for 12 months; B invests ₹60,000 for 8 months. Divide ₹25,000 profit.

    A's capital-time = 40000×12 = 480000. B's = 60000×8 = 480000. Ratio = 1:1. Each gets ₹12,500

    Mixtures & Alligation

    Fig 3.2 — Alligation cross method
    📗 Example 8 — Alligation

    Milk at ₹60/litre is mixed with water (₹0/litre) to get mixture worth ₹45/litre. Find the ratio.

    Ratio = (60 − 45) : (45 − 0) = 15 : 45 = 1:3 (water : milk). So milk:water = 3:1

    📗 Example 9 — Replacement

    A 20-litre mixture has milk:water = 3:1. If 5 litres is removed and replaced with water, find the new ratio.

    Milk initially = 15L, Water = 5L. Remove 5L (removes 3.75L milk, 1.25L water). After replacement: Milk = 11.25L, Water = 8.75L. Ratio = 9:7

    📗 Example 10 — Compound Ratio

    If a:b = 2:3, b:c = 4:5, c:d = 6:7, find a:d.

    a/d = (a/b)(b/c)(c/d) = (2/3)(4/5)(6/7) = 48/105 = 16:35

    📘 Exercise 1

    Divide ₹3,600 among A, B, C in ratio 2:3:4.

    Total parts = 9. A = 2/9 × 3600 = ₹800, B = ₹1200, C = ₹1600

    Boys = 7/12 × 180 = 105

    In a ratio a:b = 5:7, if a is increased by 10, the ratio becomes 5:6. Find a and b.

    Averages & Statistics High Weight

    Mean, median, mode, standard deviation, probability, permutations & combinations.

    Measures of Central Tendency

    Fig 4.1 — Three measures of central tendency
    📗 Example 1 — Mean

    Marks: 72, 85, 90, 68, 95. Find mean.

    Mean = (72+85+90+68+95)/5 = 410/5 = 82

    📗 Example 2 — Weighted Mean

    A student scores 80 (weight 3), 70 (weight 2), 90 (weight 5). Find weighted average.

    = (80×3 + 70×2 + 90×5)/(3+2+5) = (240+140+450)/10 = 830/10 = 83

    📗 Example 3 — Median

    Data: 12, 5, 22, 30, 7, 18, 15. Find median.

    Sorted: 5, 7, 12, 15, 18, 22, 30. Middle (4th value) = 15

    📗 Example 4 — Mode

    Data: 4, 7, 2, 7, 8, 4, 7, 9, 4, 7. Find mode.

    7 appears 4 times (most frequent). Mode = 7

    Measures of Spread

    📐 Variance & Standard Deviation
    Variance σ² = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² / n   |   SD σ = √(Variance)
    📗 Example 5 — Standard Deviation

    Data: 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9. Mean = 5.

    Deviations²: 9, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 4, 16. Variance = 32/8 = 4. SD = √4 = 2

    Probability

    📗 Example 6 — Classical Probability

    A die is thrown. P(getting prime number)?

    Primes: {2, 3, 5} = 3 outcomes. Total = 6. P = 3/6 = 1/2

    📗 Example 7 — Complementary Events

    P(raining tomorrow) = 0.3. P(NOT raining)?

    P(not raining) = 1 − 0.3 = 0.7

    📗 Example 8 — Cards

    From a standard 52-card deck, P(drawing a king or a heart)?

    Kings = 4, Hearts = 13, King of hearts = 1. P = (4+13−1)/52 = 16/52 = 4/13

    Permutations & Combinations

    📐 P&C Formulas
    P(n,r) = n!/(n−r)!   |   C(n,r) = n!/[r!(n−r)!]

    Permutation = arrangement (order matters). Combination = selection (order doesn't matter).

    📗 Example 9 — Permutation

    How many 3-letter "words" from {A, B, C, D, E} without repetition?

    P(5,3) = 5!/2! = 5×4×3 = 60

    📗 Example 10 — Combination

    Select a committee of 3 from 8 people.

    C(8,3) = 8!/(3!×5!) = (8×7×6)/(3×2×1) = 56

    Algebra

    Linear & quadratic equations, inequalities, sequences & series (AP, GP, HP), logarithms.

    Linear Equations

    📗 Example 1 — Solving Linear Equation

    Solve: 3x + 7 = 22

    3x = 15 → x = 5

    📗 Example 2 — Simultaneous Equations

    Solve: 2x + 3y = 12 and x − y = 1

    From eq2: x = y + 1. Substitute: 2(y+1) + 3y = 12 → 5y = 10 → y = 2, x = 3

    Quadratic Equations

    📐 Quadratic Formula
    x = [−b ± √(b² − 4ac)] / 2a

    Discriminant D = b²−4ac: D > 0 → two real roots, D = 0 → one root, D < 0 → no real roots.

    Fig 5.1 — Discriminant determines root nature
    📗 Example 3 — Quadratic Equation

    Solve: x² − 5x + 6 = 0

    Factoring: (x−2)(x−3) = 0 → x = 2 or 3

    📗 Example 4 — Using Quadratic Formula

    Solve: 2x² + 3x − 2 = 0

    D = 9 + 16 = 25. x = (−3 ± 5)/4 → x = 1/2 or x = −2

    Arithmetic Progression (AP)

    📐 AP Formulas
    nth term: aₙ = a + (n−1)d  |  Sum: Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n−1)d]
    📗 Example 5 — AP nth Term

    AP: 3, 7, 11, 15, … Find the 20th term.

    a = 3, d = 4. a₂₀ = 3 + 19×4 = 3 + 76 = 79

    📗 Example 6 — Sum of AP

    Sum of first 50 natural numbers.

    S₅₀ = 50/2 × [2(1) + 49(1)] = 25 × 51 = 1275

    Geometric Progression (GP)

    📐 GP Formulas
    nth term: aₙ = arⁿ⁻¹  |  Sum: Sₙ = a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1) for r > 1
    📗 Example 7 — GP

    GP: 2, 6, 18, … Find the 8th term.

    a = 2, r = 3. a₈ = 2 × 3⁷ = 2 × 2187 = 4374

    Logarithms

    📐 Log Laws
    log(ab) = log a + log b  |  log(a/b) = log a − log b  |  log(aⁿ) = n·log a
    📗 Example 8 — Logarithm

    If log₂ 8 = x, find x.

    2ˣ = 8 = 2³ → x = 3

    📗 Example 9 — Inequalities

    Solve: 2x − 5 > 3x + 1

    −5 − 1 > 3x − 2x → −6 > x → x < −6

    📗 Example 10 — Sum to Infinity GP

    Find sum to infinity: 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + …

    a = 1, r = 1/2. S∞ = a/(1−r) = 1/(1/2) = 2

    Speed, Distance & Work

    TSD problems, relative speed, boats/trains, time and work, pipes and cisterns.

    Speed, Distance, Time

    📐 Fundamental Formula
    Distance = Speed × Time  |  Speed = Distance/Time  |  Time = Distance/Speed
    Fig 8.1 — DST triangle — cover what you want to find
    💡 Unit Conversion
    km/h to m/s: multiply by 5/18  |  m/s to km/h: multiply by 18/5
    📗 Example 1 — Basic TSD

    A car travels 240 km in 4 hours. Find speed.

    Speed = 240/4 = 60 km/h

    📗 Example 2 — Average Speed

    A car goes at 40 km/h for 2 hrs and 60 km/h for 3 hrs. Find average speed.

    Total distance = 80+180 = 260. Total time = 5. Avg speed = 260/5 = 52 km/h

    📗 Example 3 — Equal Distance, Different Speeds

    A man goes to office at 30 km/h and returns at 20 km/h. Average speed?

    Average = 2×30×20/(30+20) = 1200/50 = 24 km/h (harmonic mean)

    Trains & Relative Speed

    📗 Example 4 — Train Crossing a Pole

    Train of length 200m passes a pole in 10 seconds. Find speed.

    Speed = 200/10 = 20 m/s = 20 × 18/5 = 72 km/h

    📗 Example 5 — Two Trains, Opposite Direction

    Trains of lengths 150m and 250m travel at 40 km/h and 50 km/h toward each other. Time to cross?

    Relative speed = 90 km/h = 25 m/s. Total distance = 400m. Time = 400/25 = 16 seconds

    Boats & Streams

    📐 Boat Formulas
    Downstream speed = (Boat + Stream)  |  Upstream speed = (Boat − Stream)
    📗 Example 6 — Boats

    Boat speed = 12 km/h, stream = 4 km/h. Time to travel 32 km upstream?

    Upstream speed = 12 − 4 = 8 km/h. Time = 32/8 = 4 hours

    📗 Example 7 — Finding Boat Speed

    Downstream speed = 18 km/h, upstream = 12 km/h. Find boat and stream speeds.

    Boat = (18+12)/2 = 15 km/h. Stream = (18−12)/2 = 3 km/h

    Time & Work

    📗 Example 8 — Combined Work

    A finishes in 12 days, B in 18 days. Together how long?

    Combined rate = 1/12 + 1/18 = 5/36. Days = 36/5 = 7.2 days

    📗 Example 9 — Pipes & Cisterns

    Pipe A fills a tank in 6 hours. Pipe B empties it in 8 hours. Both open — time to fill?

    Net rate = 1/6 − 1/8 = 1/24. Time = 24 hours

    📗 Example 10 — Efficiency

    A is twice as efficient as B. Together they finish in 10 days. How long for A alone?

    Let B's rate = x, A's = 2x. 3x = 1/10 → x = 1/30. A's rate = 2/30 = 1/15. A alone = 15 days

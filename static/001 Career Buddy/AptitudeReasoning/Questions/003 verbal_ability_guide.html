    1.1 Synonyms, Antonyms & Contextual Meaning

    A synonym is a word with a similar meaning. An antonym is its opposite. Many exam questions test whether you know these in context — the same word can have different meanings in different sentences.

    Worsen
    B
    Improve ✓
    C
    Evaluate
    D
    Analyze
    ✓ Answer: B — Improve
    Ameliorate (from Latin meliorare = to make better) means to make something bad better. Root trick: MELIO = better (like meliorate, amelioration).
    💡 Memory Trick
    A-MELIO-RATE → "make MELIOws (mellow/milder)" → softer, better. Antonym: AGGRAVATE (make worse).

    2.1 Subject–Verb Agreement

    The verb must agree with its subject in number (singular/plural) and person. Tricky cases arise with collective nouns, indefinite pronouns, and intervening phrases.

    Rule 1: Singular Subject
    "Everyone is ready." (not are)
    Everyone/each/nobody/someone = singular
    Rule 2: Collective Nouns
    "The team is playing well." (unit)
    "The team are arguing." (individuals)
    Rule 3: Either/Neither
    "Either the boys or the girl is right."
    Verb agrees with nearer subject

    2.2 Tense Usage — The Complete Map

    2.3 Active & Passive Voice

    Active voice: Subject performs the action → Subject + Verb + Object
    Passive voice: Subject receives the action → Object + be + past participle + (by + Subject)

    🟢 Active
    The teacher checks the papers.
    →
    🟣 Passive
    The papers are checked by the teacher.
    🟢 Active
    She has written the report.
    →
    🟣 Passive
    The report has been written by her.

    Error Spotting — Subject–Verb Agreement
    Find the grammatical error:
    "Neither the manager nor the employees was informed about the decision."
    A
    Neither…nor is incorrectly used
    B
    was should be were ✓
    C
    informed should be inform
    D
    No error
    ✓ B — "was" → "were"
    Rule: With neither…nor / either…or, the verb agrees with the subject nearer to it. The nearer subject is "employees" (plural) → were.
    🧠 CLOSER CONTROLS
    In Either/Or and Neither/Nor constructions: the verb is controlled by the CLOSER subject.

    Worked RC Example — Full Passage

    The ocean covers more than 70 percent of the Earth's surface and is the primary driver of global climate regulation. Yet, despite its critical role, the deep ocean — anything below 200 metres — remains largely unexplored. Scientists estimate that we have mapped less than 20 percent of the ocean floor with high resolution.

    The lack of exploration is not due to a lack of interest but rather the formidable technical and financial barriers involved. The pressures at depth can exceed 600 times the atmospheric pressure at sea level, requiring specially engineered submersibles that cost millions of dollars. In contrast, space exploration has received far greater public funding and popular attention.

    Critics argue this imbalance is short-sighted. The deep ocean contains ecosystems of extraordinary richness — hydrothermal vents support life forms that may hold clues to the origins of life on Earth and possibly life on other planets. Moreover, minerals on the ocean floor could provide critical resources for modern technology. Understanding and preserving these environments has never been more urgent.

    6
    Main Idea Question
    What is the central argument of the passage?
    A
    The ocean covers 70% of Earth's surface.
    B
    Space exploration receives more funding than ocean exploration.
    C
    Deep ocean exploration is critically under-prioritised despite its immense value. ✓
    D
    The ocean floor contains minerals for modern technology.
    ✓ C — A & B are supporting details, not the main argument. D is one specific point, not the overall theme. The passage consistently argues that the deep ocean is neglected (financial barriers, less funding than space) but immensely valuable (ecosystems, minerals, origins of life).
    7
    Tone / Attitude Question
    The author's tone in the final paragraph can best be described as:
    A
    Detached and neutral
    B
    Celebratory and optimistic
    C
    Urgent and persuasive ✓
    D

    ✓ C — Urgent & Persuasive
    Tone words to notice: "short-sighted," "extraordinary richness," "has never been more urgent" — these signal the author is pushing the reader toward a conclusion (persuasive) with a time-sensitive emphasis (urgent).

    4.1 Para-Jumbles

    Para-jumbles give you 4–6 scrambled sentences and ask you to arrange them into a coherent paragraph. The key is to identify the mandatory first sentence (no pronoun at start, introduces a concept) and linking clues (pronouns, conjunctions, demonstratives).

    Fig 7 — Para-Jumble Clue Identification Framework
    8
    Para-Jumble — Arrange into Coherent Paragraph
    Arrange sentences A–E in the correct order:
    A
    However, excessive screen time has also been linked to sleep disruption and reduced attention spans.
    B
    These benefits have made digital devices indispensable in modern education.
    C
    Digital technology has transformed the way students learn and access information.
    D
    Therefore, finding the right balance between screen use and offline activities is essential.
    E
    Students can now access millions of resources, collaborate online, and learn at their own pace.
    CORRECT ORDER: C → E → B → A → D
    1
    C: Opener — introduces "digital technology" (no pronoun, no link word)
    2
    E: "Students can now…" — elaborates on how they learn (links to C's "way students learn")
    3
    B: "These benefits…" — pronoun "these" refers back to E's benefits
    4
    A: "However…" — contrast connector, introduces the downside
    5
    D: "Therefore…" — conclusion word, wraps up the argument

    4.2 Sentence Completion & Fill-in-the-Blanks

    9
    Double Blank — Sentence Completion
    Choose the pair that best completes the sentence:
    "The professor was known for her _____ lectures; even complex topics were presented with admirable _____."
    A
    tedious … complexity
    B
    lucid … clarity ✓
    C
    verbose … brevity
    D
    obscure … precision
    ✓ B — lucid … clarity
    The clue is "even complex topics were presented" with something positive (admirable). The first blank must mean "clear/understandable." Lucid = clear, easy to understand. Clarity = clearness. C is contradictory (verbose = wordy, brevity = shortness).
    💡 Double Blank Strategy
    Fill the EASIER blank first. Then use that to eliminate options for the harder blank. Always check if the pair is logically consistent with each other AND the sentence context.

    5.1 Précis Writing — Step-by-Step

    A précis is a concise, clear summary of a passage in your own words. It should capture all the main ideas without any examples, illustrations, or repetition. Standard length = 1/3rd of the original.

    Précis Writing — Original → Précis
    Original Passage (120 words)

    Reading has been a cornerstone of human intellectual development for centuries. Unlike television or social media, reading demands active participation — the reader must construct mental images, follow logical sequences, and engage critically with the text. Research consistently shows that avid readers possess larger vocabularies, stronger analytical skills, and greater empathy because they regularly inhabit the perspectives of diverse characters. Furthermore, reading reduces stress, improves focus, and has even been linked to slower cognitive decline in old age. Yet, in today's digital age, reading rates have dropped sharply, particularly among young people. Educators and parents must actively cultivate reading habits in children before the irreplaceable benefits of deep reading are lost to future generations.

    Model Précis (~40 words)
    Title: The Imperative of Cultivating Reading Habits

    Reading actively engages the mind and builds vocabulary, analytical skills, and empathy while reducing stress and delaying cognitive decline. Despite these benefits, reading rates have fallen, making it essential for educators and parents to foster reading habits in children.
    📌
    Notice: The précis omits all examples (television vs social media, mental images), retains ALL key points (benefits, declining rates, call to action), uses own language, and is exactly 1/3 of original length.
    12
    Punctuation Error Correction
    Correct the punctuation in the following sentence:
    "The professor asked the students to bring the following materials; pens, notebooks, and calculators"
    A
    "…bring the following materials: pens, notebooks, and calculators." ✓
    B
    No change needed
    C
    Replace semicolon with a dash
    D
    Remove the comma after "notebooks"
    ✓ A — A colon (:) introduces a list (not a semicolon). A semicolon connects two independent clauses. Also, the sentence needs a full stop at the end. The Oxford comma before "and" is acceptable in formal writing.

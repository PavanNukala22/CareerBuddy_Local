    Module 01 · Course Info

    📌 A1 Course Overview

    A practical, classroom-ready curriculum of activities for the absolute beginner level. Designed for a 30–40 hour course (10–13 weeks at 3 hrs/week).

    📊 Level
    A1 — Breakthrough / Beginner
    👥 Audience
    Adult & teenage learners with no or minimal prior English
    ⏱ Duration
    30–40 contact hours
    🎯 Skills Covered
    Listening Reading Spoken Interaction Spoken Production Writing
    🏁 Exit Outcome
    Learner can introduce themselves, describe family/surroundings, fill basic forms, and interact slowly on familiar topics.
    💡
    How to use this guide. Each skill section opens with the CEFR descriptor, a list of can-do targets, the language focus, and 4–5 fully scripted activities. Activities follow a Warm-up → Input → Practice → Production → Reflection arc. Toggle blocks contain teacher scripts, sample answers, and assessment rubrics.

    At A1 (the Breakthrough level), the learner is just beginning to use English. They handle concrete, predictable situations — meeting people, talking about family, shopping, asking for directions — provided the other person speaks slowly and clearly and is willing to help.

    CEFR descriptors at a glance

    SkillCan-do statement (A1)
    ListeningRecognises basic phrases regarding self and family when people speak slowly and clearly.
    ReadingUnderstands familiar names, words, and very simple sentences (e.g. on signs, posters, and in catalogues).
    Spoken InteractionCan interact in a simple way if the interlocutor speaks slowly, repeats, or rephrases.
    Spoken ProductionUses simple phrases and sentences to describe where they live, people they know, and surroundings.
    WritingWrites short, simple postcards and fills in personal-detail forms (name, nationality, address).

    Course goals

    • Build a working vocabulary of ~500–650 high-frequency words.
    • Master present simple of be and have, basic present simple of action verbs, and yes/no + wh- questions.
    • Develop confidence to speak first in survival situations (greetings, ordering, shopping).
    • Read and write short personal texts of 30–80 words.

    AreaItems
    Topics / themesSelf, family, home, jobs, daily routine, food & drink, shops, prices, time, weather, clothes, places in town, transport, free time, body parts, simple feelings.
    FunctionsGreeting & introducing, spelling, giving personal info, asking & telling the time, ordering food, shopping, asking for directions, describing people, talking about likes/dislikes.
    Grammarbe & have got in all forms; subject pronouns; possessive adjectives; this/that/these/those; a/an/the; plural nouns; present simple (positive, negative, questions); adverbs of frequency (always/usually/sometimes/never); prepositions of place & time; can for ability; there is / there are; like + -ing; numbers 1–1000; dates.

    Module 03 · Skill 1

    🎧 1. Listening

    🎯
    CEFR descriptor. Recognises basic phrases regarding self and family when people speak slowly and clearly.

    Can-do micro-targets

    • Identify names, ages, nationalities, jobs, family members in slow speech.
    • Catch numbers (phone, age, price) up to 100, then 1000.
    • Follow very short classroom instructions ("Open your book", "Stand up").
    • Recognise greetings, goodbyes, and polite formulas (please, thank you, sorry).
    • Understand basic questions about themselves and answer them.

    Listening Activities

    1

    "Hello, my name is…" — Introductions Bingo

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Whole class🎚 Receptive: A1.1🧰 Bingo cards, audio clips

    Aim: Recognise names, countries, and jobs in short self-introductions.

    Procedure (step-by-step)
    1. Warm-up (3 min). Teacher introduces themselves slowly: "Hello. My name is Sara. I am from India. I am a teacher." Repeat twice. Students mirror the rhythm with finger-tapping on the desk.
    2. Pre-listening (4 min). Show 12 flashcards: 4 names (Anna, Tom, Lin, Carlos), 4 countries (Spain, Japan, Egypt, Brazil), 4 jobs (doctor, student, driver, cook). Drill pronunciation chorally and individually.
    3. Bingo set-up (2 min). Each student gets a 3×3 bingo card filled with 9 of the 12 items.
    4. Listening (10 min). Play 8 mini-introductions, each 12–15 seconds, twice. Example clip: "Hi, I'm Carlos. I'm from Brazil. I'm a cook." Students cross items they hear.
    5. Check (3 min). Replay clips, pause after each, elicit answers.
    6. Reflection (3 min). Ask: "Which words were easy? Which were difficult?" Note repeat-listens needed for next class.
    Sample audio script
    1. A: Hello, my name is Anna. I am from Spain. I am a student.
    2. A: Hi! I'm Tom. I'm from… Egypt. I'm a doctor.
    3. A: Good morning. My name is Lin. I'm from Japan. I'm a driver.
    4. A: Hello. I am Carlos. I am from Brazil. I am a cook.

    Speak at ~90 words/min, exaggerate pauses between sentences.

    Differentiation & extension
    • Support: Pre-teach country flags; let weaker pairs share one card.
    • Stretch: Stronger learners write the full sentence they heard, not just the word.
    • Homework: Record a 20-second self-introduction on their phone using the same frame.
    2

    Numbers Race — Phone, Age, Price

    ⏱ 20 min👥 Pairs🎚 Receptive: A1.1🧰 Number grids 0–100, mini whiteboards

    Aim: Discriminate confusable numbers (13/30, 14/40…) and capture personal data from speech.

    Procedure
    1. Drill (5 min). Drill 0–20, then tens. Highlight stress: thirTEEN vs THIRty. Use hand gesture — rising for -teen, flat for -ty.
    2. Minimal-pair clap (3 min). Read pairs: 13/30, 14/40, 15/50… Students clap once for -teen, twice for -ty.
    3. Dictation race (8 min). Dictate 6 short profiles. Pairs scribble on a mini-whiteboard. First correct pair wins a point.
    4. Feedback (4 min). Drill any number that more than 2 pairs missed.
    Dictation script
    1. My name is Maya. I am 19 years old. My phone is 045 776 213.
    2. His name is Ben. He is 40. His age is 40, four-zero.
    3. The book is 13 dollars. One-three.
    4. The coffee is 50 rupees. Five-zero.
    5. My address is 14 Park Road. One-four.
    6. The bus is number 30. Three-zero.
    3

    Family Tree Detective

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Individual + pairs🎚 Receptive: A1.2🧰 Blank tree template, audio

    Aim: Decode family-relation vocabulary and possessive structures (her sister, his father).

    Procedure
    1. Vocabulary set (5 min). Pre-teach: mother, father, brother, sister, son, daughter, husband, wife, grandfather, grandmother. Use a celebrity family photo for visual anchoring.
    2. Listen 1 — gist (5 min). Play a 60-second monologue: "This is my family. There are five people…" Students just count people on fingers.
    3. Listen 2 — detail (10 min). Students fill names into a blank tree (5 boxes) as they hear them.
    4. Listen 3 — confirmation (5 min). Pair-check, then class-check on the board.
    5. Personalisation (5 min). Each student draws their own 3-person tree and labels it.
    Audio script (~120 words, 60 sec)
    This is a photo of my family. There are five people. My father's name is Raj. He is a doctor. My mother is Priya. She is a teacher. I have one brother and one sister. My brother is Arjun. He is twelve. He is a student. My sister is Meera. She is six. She is a student too. I love my family very much.
    Answer key
    BoxNameDetail
    FatherRajdoctor
    MotherPriyateacher
    BrotherArjun12, student
    SisterMeera6, student
    Speaker(self)—
    4

    Total Physical Response — "Simon Says" Classroom Edition

    ⏱ 15 min👥 Whole class, standing🎚 Receptive: A1.1🧰 None

    Aim: Internalise classroom verbs and prepositions through movement (Asher's TPR method).

    Procedure & command bank

    Teacher gives commands; students obey only if "Simon says" is added.

    Command bank (introduce in this order):

    • Stand up. Sit down. Turn around.
    • Open your book. Close your book.
    • Touch your nose / head / ear / shoulder.
    • Point to the door / the window / the board.
    • Walk to the door. Walk back. Sit down.

    Tip: Speak at slightly slower than natural pace, but with normal stress and intonation. Never translate; mime first, then drop the mime once 80 % of the class responds correctly.

    5

    Café Eavesdrop — Real-World Listening

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Pairs🎚 Receptive: A1.2🧰 Café dialogue audio, menu picture

    Aim: Catch key items (tea, coffee, sandwich, water) and prices in a service encounter.

    Dialogue script
    Server: Hello! What would you like?
    Customer: A coffee, please.
    Server: Big or small?
    Customer: Small, please. And a cheese sandwich.
    Server: Okay. Anything to drink? Water?
    Customer: No, thank you. How much is it?
    Server: That's six dollars, please.
    Customer: Here you are. Thank you!
    Comprehension task & answers

    Students tick items on a picture menu and write the total price.

    Answers: small coffee ✓, cheese sandwich ✓, water ✗, total = $6.

    Module 04 · Skill 2

    📖 2. Reading

    🎯
    CEFR descriptor. Understands familiar names, words, and very simple sentences — for example, on signs, posters, in catalogues, and in personal messages.

    Can-do micro-targets

    • Decode the alphabet and high-frequency sight words (the, a, is, in, on, you, my…).
    • Read a personal-info form, a menu, a shop sign, a short text message.
    • Match short descriptions to pictures.
    • Find specific information (a price, a time, a phone number) in a short text.

    Reading Activities

    1

    Sign Hunt — Reading the World

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Pairs🎚 A1.1🧰 12 photo cards of real signs

    Aim: Recognise the meaning of common public signs and posters.

    Procedure
    1. Stimulus (3 min). Show one sign — OPEN. Ask: "What does it mean? Where do you see this?"
    2. Matching task (12 min). Pairs receive 12 photos: OPEN, CLOSED, EXIT, ENTRANCE, PUSH, PULL, NO SMOKING, MEN, WOMEN, BUS STOP, FOR SALE, DANGER. They match each sign to a meaning card in their L1 / a picture.
    3. Class check (5 min). Drill pronunciation of each.
    4. Personalisation (5 min). Students list 3 signs they saw on the way to class today.
    Why this works at A1

    Public signs are authentic, short, and visually supported. Learners gain immediate confidence: "I can already read English in the street." Reinforces the principle that reading at A1 is largely word-recognition with strong context, not full sentence parsing.

    2

    Read & Draw — Picture Dictation

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Individual🎚 A1.2🧰 Short text + blank paper

    Aim: Decode simple descriptive sentences with there is/are and prepositions.

    Reading text (45 words)
    This is my room. There is a bed. The bed is small. There is a table next to the bed. On the table, there is a lamp and a book. There is a window behind the bed. The window is big.
    Procedure & marking
    1. Pre-reading (3 min): Pre-teach bed, table, lamp, window, next to, behind, on.
    2. Read silently (5 min). Each student draws the room exactly as described.
    3. Pair compare (5 min). Students compare drawings and re-read together to fix differences.
    4. Class display (10 min). Drawings on the wall; teacher reads aloud and points.
    5. Reflection (7 min). Which preposition was hard? Re-drill.

    Marking criteria (out of 6): bed (1), small bed (1), table next to bed (1), lamp + book on table (1), window behind bed (1), big window (1).

    3

    Menu Maths — Scanning a Café Menu

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Pairs🎚 A1.2🧰 Realia menu (handout)

    Aim: Scan an authentic short text for prices and item names — without reading every word.

    Sample menu
    SUNNY CAFÉ
    Tea$1.50
    Coffee$2.00
    Orange juice$2.50
    Cheese sandwich$4.00
    Chicken sandwich$4.50
    Salad$3.50
    Cake$3.00
    Comprehension questions
    1. How much is a coffee?
    2. Which sandwich is cheaper?
    3. You have $5. You want one drink and one cake. What can you choose?
    4. Two teas and a salad — how much?

    Answers: 1) $2 · 2) Cheese · 3) Tea + cake = $4.50 ✓ · 4) $6.50

    4

    Postcard Puzzle — A Message from a Friend

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Pairs🎚 A1.2🧰 Postcard text + comprehension grid
    Reading text (60 words)
    Hi Maria!
    I am in Goa. The weather is hot and sunny. The beach is beautiful. I am with my sister. We swim every day. The food is very good — I love the fish curry! Our hotel is small but nice. We are very happy here. See you next week!
    Love,
    Anna
    Tasks

    Task 1 — True / False / Doesn't say:

    1. Anna is in Goa. (T)
    2. The weather is cold. (F)
    3. Anna is alone. (F)
    4. Her brother is with her. (DS)
    5. The hotel is big. (F)
    6. She likes the food. (T)

    Task 2 — Information transfer: Fill in a grid (Place / Weather / Who with / Activity / Opinion of food).

    5

    Catalogue Choices — Shopping Online

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Groups of 3🎚 A1.2🧰 Mini-catalogue handout (clothes / electronics)

    Aim: Read short product descriptions to make a choice — a real-world A1 reading purpose.

    Procedure
    1. Each group has $50. They must buy 3 items.
    2. The catalogue lists 10 items with picture, 1-line description, and price.
    3. Group reads, discusses (in any language), and writes a 3-item shopping list in English.
    4. Each group presents: "We buy a black T-shirt. It is $12…"
    Module 05 · Skill 3

    💬 3. Spoken Interaction

    🎯
    CEFR descriptor. Can interact in a simple way provided the other person talks slowly, repeats, or rephrases, and helps the learner formulate what they are trying to say.

    Can-do micro-targets

    • Greet, take leave, exchange names and origins.
    • Ask and answer simple questions about personal info, family, daily routine.
    • Use survival phrases: Sorry, I don't understand. Can you repeat, please? How do you spell it?
    • Carry out a basic transaction (café, ticket, shop).

    Spoken Interaction Activities

    1

    Find Someone Who… (Mingle)

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Whole class, standing🎚 Productive: A1.1🧰 Survey grid
    Survey grid
    Find someone who…Name
    …is from another city
    …has a brother
    …likes coffee
    …has a pet
    …is 25 years old
    …can play the guitar
    Procedure
    1. Model the question (5 min). Drill the conversion: "is from another city" → "Are you from another city?"; "has a brother" → "Do you have a brother?"
    2. Mingle (12 min). Students walk around. Rule: ask one question, get answer, write the name, move on. Each student must talk to at least 5 different people.
    3. Report (5 min). "Lin is from another city. Tom has a brother…"
    4. Reflection (3 min). Which question was easy / hard?
    2

    Information-Gap: A & B Cards

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Pairs🎚 A1.2🧰 Two profile cards (A & B), each missing data

    Aim: Force genuine question–answer exchange to complete missing personal info.

    Sample cards
    Card A (you have)Card B (you have)
    Name: Sara Khan
    Age: ______
    City: Mumbai
    Job: ______
    Phone: 9821-______     		Name: ______
    Age: 28
    City: ______
    Job: nurse
    Phone: ______-456-712
    Question prompts on a help-card
    • What's her name? / How old is she? / Where is she from? / What's her job? / What's her phone number?
    • Sorry, can you repeat? / How do you spell that?
    3

    Survival Roleplays — At the Café / Shop / Train Station

    ⏱ 35 min👥 Pairs, rotate🎚 A1.2🧰 3 scenario cards
    Scenario cards

    Scenario 1 — Café. Student A is a customer. Order one drink and one snack, ask the price, pay. Student B is a server.

    Scenario 2 — Clothes shop. A wants a T-shirt in size M, blue. B is the shop assistant. They negotiate colour and size; A asks the price.

    Scenario 3 — Train station. A wants a ticket to a city, today, single, second class. B is the ticket seller. A asks the price and the platform number.

    Useful chunks (display on board)
    • Excuse me… / Can I have…, please?
    • How much is it? / How much are they?
    • Do you have… in (size / colour)?
    • One ticket to ____, please.
    • Sorry, I don't understand. Can you say it again, please?
    Rotation & assessment

    Pairs rehearse Scenario 1 for 4 minutes, swap roles 4 minutes, then rotate to a new partner with Scenario 2, etc. Teacher monitors with a checklist:

    • Greets & closes politely ✓
    • Uses correct request form (Can I…?) ✓
    • Asks for price ✓
    • Uses a clarification phrase when stuck ✓
    4

    "What's in your bag?" — Show & Ask

    ⏱ 20 min👥 Groups of 4🎚 A1.1🧰 Real bags / pockets
    Procedure
    1. Each student picks 3 items from their bag (pen, key, photo, phone, water bottle…).
    2. Drill: What's this? — It's a pen. Whose is it? — It's mine / It's Tom's.
    3. In groups, students take turns: hold up an item, the others ask "What's that?", "Is it new?", "Is it Indian?"
    4. Round 2 (eyes closed): one student picks an object from the table; others ask yes/no questions until they identify it.
    Module 06 · Skill 4

    🗣️ 4. Spoken Production

    🎯
    CEFR descriptor. Uses simple phrases and sentences to describe where they live and people they know — and their immediate surroundings.

    Can-do micro-targets

    • Speak about self, family, home, school/job for 30–60 seconds (linked simple sentences).
    • Describe a picture or a room using there is/are + prepositions.
    • Talk about likes, dislikes, daily routine using and / but / because.

    Spoken Production Activities

    1

    "My Room" — Picture-Based Mini-Talk

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Individual + pairs🎚 A1.2🧰 Phone photo of own room
    Frame on the board
    This is my room. It is (big / small). There is a ____. There is a ____ next to / on / under the ____. There are two ____. I like my room because ____.
    Procedure
    1. Model (5 min). Teacher describes their own room using the frame.
    2. Plan (5 min). Students bullet-point 5 things they will say.
    3. Pair rehearsal (10 min). Speak to partner A, get feedback, swap. Then speak to partner B (improved version).
    4. Mini-talks (10 min). 3–4 volunteers present to the class. Class fills a "1 thing you understood" sticky note.
    Assessment rubric (out of 10)
    CriterionPoints
    Says 4+ linked sentences3
    Uses there is / there are correctly2
    Uses ≥2 prepositions of place2
    Pronunciation is intelligible2
    Adds a personal opinion (I like…)1
    2

    "My Family Photo" — Show & Tell

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Small groups🎚 A1.2🧰 Family photo or drawing
    Frame & example
    This is a photo of my family. There are ____ people. This is my (mother). Her name is ____. She is ____ years old. She is a ____. She likes ____. This is my ____. He / She…

    Example: "This is a photo of my family. There are four people. This is my father. His name is Krishna. He is 50. He is an engineer. He likes cricket…"

    Procedure & tips
    1. Pre-teach he/she/his/her contrast — common A1 error.
    2. Drill the third-person -s ending: likes, plays, works.
    3. Each student speaks for ~45 seconds; group-mates ask 1 question after.
    4. Teacher records 1–2 talks (with permission) for self-listening homework.
    3

    "My Day" — Daily Routine Monologue

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Individual + pairs🎚 A1.2🧰 Clock pictures, time cards
    Procedure
    1. Vocabulary (5 min): get up, have breakfast, go to school/work, study, have lunch, come home, watch TV, go to bed.
    2. Time drill (5 min): 7:00, 7:30, 8:15, 12:00 noon. Drill at seven o'clock.
    3. Order task (5 min). Students put 8 routine pictures in their personal order and write the time under each.
    4. Speak in pairs (10 min). "I get up at 6:30. I have breakfast at 7. I go to work at 8…"
    5. Class share (5 min). 3 volunteers; class spots one common time and one unique time.
    4

    "Around My Neighbourhood" — Mini Tour Guide

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Pairs🎚 A1.2🧰 Map of own area (rough sketch)
    Frame
    I live in ____. My street is called ____. Near my house, there is a ____ and a ____. The ____ is on the left / on the right. I like my neighbourhood because ____.
    Module 07 · Skill 5

    ✍️ 5. Writing

    🎯
    CEFR descriptor. Writes a short, simple postcard, for example sending holiday greetings. Fills in forms with personal details (name, nationality, address, etc.).

    Can-do micro-targets

    • Fill a registration / hotel / immigration form accurately.
    • Write a 30–60 word message: postcard, text, email greeting.
    • Write 5–8 linked sentences about self, family, or routine.
    • Use capital letters, full stops, and basic spelling of high-frequency words.

    Writing Activities

    1

    Form Filling Bonanza

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Individual🎚 A1.1🧰 4 sample forms
    Forms to use
    1. Hotel check-in: Surname, First name, Date of birth, Nationality, Passport no., Address, Signature.
    2. Library card: Full name, Date of birth, Phone, Email, Home address.
    3. Online shop sign-up: Username, Email, Password, Country, Date of birth.
    4. Doctor's clinic: Name, Age, Gender, Address, Emergency contact, Allergies (yes/no).
    Procedure & pitfalls
    1. Pre-teach: surname / family name / last name vs first name / given name; nationality vs country (Indian vs India); date format DD / MM / YYYY.
    2. Students complete all four forms with their own real (or imagined) data.
    3. Pair-check: swap forms; circle anything you cannot read or do not understand.
    4. Teacher feedback round: highlight common errors on the board.

    Common A1 errors to watch for: writing a country instead of nationality; lower-case names; mixing day/month order; missing capital after full stop in address.

    2

    Postcard Project — "Greetings from…"

    ⏱ 35 min👥 Individual + pair editing🎚 A1.2🧰 Postcard template, model text
    Stage 1 — Read the model
    Dear Tom,
    Greetings from Jaipur! I am here with my family. The city is beautiful and the food is great. The weather is hot. We visit a different palace every day. I am very happy.
    See you soon!
    Love,
    Priya

    Highlight the structure: opening (Dear…) → place & weather → who with → activity → feeling → closing (See you…/Love).

    Stage 2 — Write your own (50–70 words)

    Students choose a real or imagined holiday place. They follow the same 6-part structure.

    Sentence stems: I am in… / The weather is… / I am with… / I (verb) every day. / I am very (adjective).

    Stage 3 — Peer edit checklist
    • Did they begin with Dear ____,?
    • Is the place named?
    • Is the weather mentioned?
    • Does every sentence start with a CAPITAL and end with a "."?
    • Is there a closing (Love / See you / Bye)?
    Rubric (out of 10)
    CriterionMarks
    Task completed (50–70 words, postcard format)3
    Range — uses 3+ different sentence patterns2
    Accuracy — present simple of be, capitals, full stops3
    Coherence — sentences linked with and / but1
    Effort & presentation1
    3

    "All About Me" — A First Personal Profile

    ⏱ 40 min👥 Individual🎚 A1.2🧰 Lined paper, profile template
    Template (60–80 words)
    My name is ____. I am ____ years old. I am from ____. I live in ____. I am a ____. There are ____ people in my family: my ____, my ____ and me. I like ____ and ____, but I don't like ____. In my free time, I ____. I want to learn English because ____.
    Process
    1. Brainstorm (5 min): Students write key words on a mind-map.
    2. First draft (15 min): Use the template, fill the gaps, add 2 sentences of your own.
    3. Self-check (5 min): Underline every verb. Is each one in the right form?
    4. Pair check (5 min).
    5. Final neat copy (10 min). Display on the wall.
    4

    Text-Message Pen Pals

    ⏱ 20 min × 4 sessions👥 Pairs (across the class)🎚 A1.2🧰 "Phone screen" worksheet

    Aim: Practise the genre of micro-writing (text messages) over a sustained "exchange".

    How it runs across 4 weeks
    1. Week 1 — Hello. Write a 30-word intro: name, age, city, job, one hobby. Pass it to your pen pal.
    2. Week 2 — My family. Reply, then describe your family in 40 words.
    3. Week 3 — My day. Reply with 2 questions, then describe your typical day.
    4. Week 4 — Invitation. Invite your pen pal to a real or imagined event ("Come to my birthday on Saturday at 6 p.m. at Café Sunny.") Reply accepting or refusing politely.

    Genre features to highlight: short sentences, friendly opening (Hi! / Hey!), close with name, ok to use I'm, you're, don't contractions, OK to use 1–2 emoji.

    5

    Picture Captions — From Word to Sentence

    ⏱ 20 min👥 Individual🎚 A1.1🧰 8 photos
    Procedure

    For each photo, the learner writes:

    1. One word (e.g. dog).
    2. One noun phrase (a brown dog).
    3. One full sentence (This is a brown dog. It is small.).

    This staged "word → phrase → sentence" build is a low-anxiety bridge from the absolute beginner level (writing labels) to writing connected text.

    ⚠️
    A1 teacher reminders. Speak slowly but with natural intonation — never broken English. Repeat & rephrase, don't translate. Keep instructions to ≤7 words. Praise risk-taking over accuracy. Recycle vocabulary across at least three different skills before testing it.
    ✅
    Exit-of-A1 readiness signal. The learner can confidently say: "My name is ___. I am from ___. I am a ___. I live in ___ with my family. I like ___ and I don't like ___. I am learning English." — without reading from a paper, in under 60 seconds, intelligibly.

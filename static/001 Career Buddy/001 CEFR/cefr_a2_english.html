    📐 A2 Course Overview

    A classroom-ready collection of 50+ detailed activities (10 per skill) covering all five CEFR competencies at the Elementary / Waystage level. Designed for 40–60 contact hours across a structured course.

    📊 Level
    A2 — Elementary / Waystage
    🎯 Skills
    🎧 Listening 📖 Reading 💬 Spoken Interaction 🗣️ Spoken Production ✍️ Writing
    👥 Audience
    Learners who completed A1; adult/teen classes; one-to-one coaching
    ⏱ Duration
    40–60 contact hours (12–16 weeks at 3–4 hrs/week)
    📦 Vocabulary
    ~1,000–1,200 high-frequency words built upon A1 foundation
    🏁 Exit Outcome
    Learner handles predictable everyday situations, short exchanges, and simple written messages independently.
    💡
    How to use this guide. Activities follow a consistent Warm-up → Input → Guided Practice → Free Practice → Reflection arc. Toggle each section to reveal teacher scripts, answer keys, rubrics, and differentiation notes. Skill sections are colour-coded throughout.

    At A2 (Elementary / Waystage), learners move beyond basic survival phrases. They can communicate in predictable, routine situations — shopping, asking for directions, describing their background — using short, simple sentences. They understand the gist of short messages and can write simple personal notes.

    Reading

    Spoken Interaction

    Module 02 · Language Reference

    🔤 A2 Language Inventory

    AreaA2 Content
    TopicsDaily routines, shopping, food & restaurants, transport & travel, health & body, weather, leisure activities, housing, jobs & workplaces, past events, plans & intentions, feelings & opinions, city & local area, celebrations, services (bank, post office).
    FunctionsMaking & responding to suggestions; expressing likes/dislikes/preferences; describing past events; making & accepting/refusing invitations; giving simple directions; talking about habits with frequency adverbs; expressing feelings; shopping for goods; making phone calls; making appointments.
    GrammarPast simple (regular & top-15 irregular verbs); present continuous; going to (plans); modal verbs: can, could, would, should, must; comparative & superlative adjectives; question words review; possessive pronouns; object pronouns; prepositions of time, place, movement; conjunctions because, so, but, when; adverbs of frequency & manner; definite/indefinite article consolidation; some/any.
    Phonology-ed endings (3 pronunciations); word stress in compounds; weak forms in connected speech (was/were, can/could); rising/falling intonation in requests; sentence stress for emphasis; linking sounds; common homophone pairs.
    Module 03 · Skill 1

    🎧 1. Listening

    🎧
    CEFR A2 Descriptor
    Catches the main point in short, clear, simple messages and announcements.
    🎯
    Micro-targets: Identify topic, gist, key figures & names in short monologues and dialogues (60–150 words); follow simple phone messages, announcements, instructions; catch attitude (happy, worried, sure, unsure) from tone; use context and sound cues when a word is missed.
    1

    Answer-Phone Messages — Gist & Detail

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Individual → pairs🎚 A2.1🧰 Audio clips, notepad template

    Aim: Identify key message information — caller, reason, time, callback number — from short recorded phone messages.

    Full Procedure
    1. Warm-up (4 min). Ask: "Have you ever left a voicemail in English? What's hard about it?" Elicit challenges: fast speech, no visual cues, unknown vocabulary.
    2. Pre-listening (5 min). Give students a notepad template: Caller / Reason for calling / Time / Callback number. Explain they will hear 3 messages — their job is to fill in the template, not understand every word.
    3. Listen 1 — gist (6 min). Play all 3 messages once. Students just write the caller's name and one word about the topic.
    4. Listen 2 — detail (8 min). Play each message twice separately. Students complete the full template.
    5. Pair-check (3 min). Compare notes with a partner.
    6. Class feedback (4 min). Replay, pause after each key piece of info. Discuss which words helped them most.
    Audio Scripts (3 messages)
    Message 1:
    "Hi, this is a message for Sara. My name is David, I'm calling from City Dentist. Your appointment is on Thursday at 2:30 in the afternoon. Please call us back if you can't come. Our number is 0800 442 113. Thank you. Goodbye."
    Message 2:
    "Hello, this message is for Mr Ahmed. I'm Lisa from the hotel. Your room is ready. Check-in is from 3 p.m. We have a problem with the Wi-Fi in room 204, so we put you in room 206. Please call me on 07721 883 456 if you have any questions. Thank you!"
    Message 3:
    "Hi! It's Marco. I can't meet you at 6 o'clock tonight. Can we change to 7:30? The restaurant is on Park Street — it's called 'Bella Roma'. Call me back. Bye!"
    Answer Key
    MessageCallerReasonTimeNumber / Address
    1David / City DentistAppointment reminderThursday, 2:30 pm0800 442 113
    2Lisa / HotelRoom changeCheck-in from 3 pmRoom 206; 07721 883 456
    3MarcoChange meeting time7:30 pmBella Roma, Park Street
    Differentiation

    Support: Provide a word bank: dentist / hotel / friend / appointment / room / meeting. Give 3 listens.

    Stretch: After listening, students write a reply text message to each caller (links to Writing skill).

    Homework: Record their own 30-second phone message and swap with a partner to transcribe.

    2

    Transport Announcements — Station & Airport

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Individual🎚 A2.1🧰 Audio, departure board template

    Aim: Catch destination, platform/gate, time, and delay information from typical public announcements.

    Procedure & Scripts
    1. Context (3 min). Show a photo of a busy train station board. Ask: "What information do you need?"
    2. Vocabulary drill (4 min): delayed, cancelled, platform, departure, arrival, gate, boarding, on time.
    3. Task setup: Students have a departure board with 5 rows, some info missing.
    4. Listen twice per announcement. Students fill the gaps.
    5. Check and discuss: Which announcements were easier — train or flight? Why?
    Announcement 1 (Train):
    "The 14:35 service to Edinburgh is now departing from Platform 7. This train is approximately 10 minutes late. We apologise for the delay."
    Announcement 2 (Airport):
    "This is the final call for passengers travelling on flight BA 204 to Dubai. Please go immediately to Gate 32. The gate is now closing."
    Announcement 3 (Bus):
    "The number 47 bus to City Centre will depart from Stand B in 5 minutes. This service does not stop at Market Street today."
    3

    Weather Forecast Bingo

    ⏱ 20 min👥 Whole class🎚 A2.1🧰 Bingo cards, audio

    Aim: Process weather vocabulary, temperatures, and days of the week in short forecasts.

    Procedure & Sample Forecast
    1. Pre-teach (4 min): sunny, cloudy, rainy, windy, snowy, foggy, temperature, degrees.
    2. Bingo cards contain 9 cells with weather icons + numbers (temperatures).
    3. Play a 90-second simulated weather forecast for a fictional city across 7 days. Students cross items as they hear them.
    4. Replay and annotate: Students write the day next to each item they crossed.
    5. Reflection: How do weather forecasters speak differently from normal conversation? (slower, clearer, sequence signals: "On Monday… Tuesday will be…")
    "Good morning! Here is your week's forecast for Green City. Monday will be sunny with a high of 24 degrees. Tuesday: expect some cloud and a little rain in the afternoon, around 19 degrees. Wednesday is looking windy — temperatures around 17. Thursday and Friday will be cold and foggy, just 12 degrees. The weekend looks great: sunny and warm, 26 degrees on Saturday and Sunday. Don't forget your sunscreen!"

    Short News Stories — Main Point Identification

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Pairs🎚 A2.2🧰 Audio, 4 headlines handout

    Aim: Match short (60-word) news summaries to headlines; identify the who/what/where of each story.

    Procedure & Scripts

    Students see 4 headlines. They listen to 4 news summaries and match each.

    Headlines:
    1. New Park Opens in City Centre
    2. Train Workers Go on Strike
    3. Local School Wins National Award
    4. Heavy Rain Causes Road Floods
    Story A: "Teachers and students at Green Valley School are celebrating today. The school won first prize in the National Environment Competition for their recycling programme. The head teacher said it was a great result for everyone."
    Story B: "Drivers in Northville are asking people to use other roads this morning after heavy rainfall flooded the main road near the river. Police say the road will be closed until this afternoon."

    Answer key: A=3, B=4. (Teacher creates equivalent stories for 1 & 2.)

    5

    "How Was Your Weekend?" — Natural Conversation

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Individual → pairs🎚 A2.2🧰 Audio of natural dialogue

    Aim: Follow a natural A2-speed conversation between two friends about past weekend activities; catch feelings expressed.

    Dialogue Script (140 words)
    Amy: Hi Lena! How was your weekend?
    Lena: It was great, thanks! I went to the cinema on Saturday.
    Amy: Oh nice! What did you see?
    Lena: A comedy — I can't remember the name. It was really funny! What about you?
    Amy: Not so good, actually. I stayed home because I had a headache. I watched TV all day.
    Lena: Oh, I'm sorry. Are you okay now?
    Amy: Yes, much better. On Sunday I went for a walk in the park with my dog. The weather was beautiful.
    Lena: That sounds lovely! I love sunny days in the park.
    Comprehension questions:
    1. Where did Lena go on Saturday?
    2. Why did Amy stay home?
    3. What did Amy do on Sunday?
    4. How does Lena feel about Amy's headache? Find the words.
    5. True or False: Amy enjoyed her whole weekend.

    Answers: 1) Cinema. 2) Headache. 3) Walked in the park with her dog. 4) Sympathetic — "Oh, I'm sorry." 5) False (Saturday bad, Sunday good).

    6

    Directions Listening — Follow the Route

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Individual🎚 A2.1🧰 City map, audio directions

    Aim: Follow spoken directions on a map grid to arrive at the correct destination.

    Map task & script

    Students have a simple 5×5 grid map with 8 labelled locations (School, Bank, Park, Supermarket, Hospital, Hotel, Station, Library). They start at "YOU ARE HERE" and draw the route as they listen.

    "Go straight ahead for two blocks. Turn left at the traffic lights. Walk past the bank — it's on your right. Turn right at the corner. The supermarket is on the left, next to the park. That's your destination!"

    Run 3 routes. Students compare final positions with a partner before revealing the answer.

    7

    Restaurant Order Dictation

    ⏱ 20 min👥 Pairs🎚 A2.1🧰 Blank order pad, menu

    Aim: Catch specific food items, quantities, and modifications in a restaurant scenario.

    Procedure & Script
    1. Student A plays a waiter with a blank order pad. Student B listens to a recording of a customer ordering.
    2. Student A writes the order. Then they compare with the full script.
    3. Swap roles with a new audio order.
    Customer: "Can I have the grilled chicken, please? But no onions. And a side salad instead of chips. To drink — a large orange juice. And my friend wants the pasta — the mushroom one — with sparkling water. Oh, and can we get some bread to start? Thank you."

    Order to capture: Grilled chicken (no onions, side salad, large OJ) + Mushroom pasta (sparkling water) + Bread (starter).

    8

    Inference Listening — Tone & Attitude

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Pairs🎚 A2.2🧰 Audio of 6 mini-exchanges

    Aim: Go beyond words — identify whether speakers are happy, unhappy, surprised, unsure, angry, or relieved.

    Mini-exchange scripts & task

    Students hear 6 exchanges of 2–3 lines. They circle the speaker's attitude from a box: happy / unhappy / surprised / unsure / angry / relieved.

    Exchange 1:
    A: "Your test results are excellent — 97%!"
    B: "Really?! I didn't expect that at all!" [surprised → happy]
    Exchange 2:
    A: "The last bus already left."
    B: "Oh no. I don't believe it. What am I going to do?" [unhappy → frustrated]

    Debrief: What words? What sounds? Elicit how stress and pitch signal emotion even before words are understood.

    9

    Radio Recipe — Following Instructions

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Individual🎚 A2.2🧰 Audio, incomplete recipe card

    Aim: Identify ingredients, quantities, and sequence of actions from a spoken recipe.

    Recipe script & task

    Students receive a recipe card with gaps. They listen to a cooking show excerpt and complete it.

    "Today we're making a simple tomato soup. You need two cans of tomatoes, one large onion, two cloves of garlic, a litre of vegetable stock, salt, pepper, and a little olive oil. First, chop the onion and garlic. Then heat the oil in a large pan and cook the onion for five minutes. Add the garlic and cook for one more minute. Next, add the tomatoes and the stock. Bring to the boil, then reduce the heat and cook for twenty minutes. Finally, blend the soup until smooth. Season with salt and pepper. Serve hot. Enjoy!"

    Gap items: quantity of tomatoes (2 cans), stock (1 litre), onion cook time (5 min), total cook time (20 min), finishing step (blend).

    Two-Minute Biography — Spot the Fact

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Individual → class🎚 A2.2🧰 Audio, fact sheet with 5 errors

    Aim: Listen to a short biography and spot deliberate errors in a written fact sheet.

    Script & Error Sheet
    "Maria Gonzalez is 32 years old. She is from Buenos Aires, Argentina. She works as a nurse at City Hospital. She studied nursing for three years at National University. Maria lives in a flat near the hospital with her husband and two children. In her free time, she loves cooking and playing the guitar. Last year, she ran a marathon for a local charity."
    Error sheet (students receive this — 5 errors inserted):
    • She is 35. (error: 32) ✗
    • She is from Buenos Aires. ✓
    • She is a doctor. (error: nurse) ✗
    • She studied for four years. (error: three) ✗
    • She lives with her sister. (error: husband) ✗
    • She loves cooking and playing tennis. (error: guitar) ✗

    Social Media Profile — True/False/Doesn't Say

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Individual🎚 A2.1🧰 Mock social profile handout

    Aim: Distinguish between stated facts, contradicted information, and information not given in a text — a critical reading micro-skill.

    Profile Text & Questions
    Name: Carlos Ruiz | Age: 27 | From: Valencia, Spain | Now lives in: Manchester, UK

    Hi! I'm Carlos. I moved to Manchester two years ago for work. I'm a graphic designer at a small creative agency. I live in a flat near the city centre with two flatmates. I love watching football — I go to City games when I can. I'm learning English in the evenings and I speak a little French too. I enjoy cooking Spanish food at weekends. 🍳

    1. Carlos is Spanish. (T)
    2. He moved to Manchester three years ago. (F — two years)
    3. He works for a large company. (F — small agency)
    4. He lives alone. (F — with two flatmates)
    5. He goes to every City football game. (DS — "when I can")
    6. He can speak three languages. (DS — English + French + Spanish but DS for three)
    7. He likes Spanish food. (T)

    Short Story — Sequencing Events

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Pairs🎚 A2.2🧰 Cut-up story paragraphs

    Aim: Read for logical sequence and time markers in a short past-tense narrative (120 words).

    Story (cut into 6 paragraphs) & procedure

    Print and cut the story into 6 strips. Pairs must read each strip and put them in order, using time connectives as clues.

    1. "Last Saturday, Maria decided to cook a special dinner for her family."
    2. "First, she went to the supermarket and bought all the ingredients."
    3. "When she got home, she started cooking. She prepared rice, chicken, and a big salad."
    4. "After two hours, everything was ready. It smelled delicious."
    5. "Her family arrived at 7 o'clock. They sat down and started eating."
    6. "Everyone loved the food. It was a perfect evening. Maria was very happy."

    Key words for clues: Last Saturday / First / When / After / at 7 o'clock / Everyone. Debrief by highlighting these on the board.

    Hotel Review — Reading for Opinion

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Groups of 3🎚 A2.2🧰 3 hotel reviews + rating grid

    Aim: Read evaluative language and distinguish positive from negative opinions — preparation for real-world reading (TripAdvisor, Google reviews).

    Reviews & Task
    Review A — Hotel Sunshine ★★★★★
    "The hotel was amazing! The room was clean and comfortable. The breakfast was delicious — lots of choice. The staff were very friendly and helpful. The location was perfect — near the train station and the shops. I will definitely come back!"
    Review B — Hotel Central ★★☆☆☆
    "The location was good but the room was small and noisy. The breakfast was nothing special — just toast and coffee. The staff were okay but not very helpful. The price was expensive for what you get. I won't go back."

    Students complete a grid: Room / Breakfast / Staff / Location / Value — rating each as 👍 / 👎 / ❓ for each hotel. Then discuss: "Which hotel would you choose? Why?"

    Product Label Decode

    ⏱ 20 min👥 Pairs🎚 A2.1🧰 4 product labels (food, medicine, cleaning)

    Aim: Read safety and usage instructions on real product labels — a vital life-skill reading task.

    Labels & Questions

    Label 1 — Paracetamol: "Adults: take 1–2 tablets every 4–6 hours. Do not take more than 8 tablets in 24 hours. Not suitable for children under 12. Keep out of reach of children. If symptoms continue, see a doctor."

    Label 2 — Pasta: "Boil in salted water for 10–12 minutes. Best before: see bottom of packet. Contains wheat. May contain traces of nuts. Store in a cool, dry place."

    Questions:
    1. Can a 10-year-old take the medicine?
    2. What is the maximum tablets in one day?
    3. How long do you cook the pasta?
    4. Is the pasta safe for someone with a nut allergy? Explain.

    Email from a Friend — Responding to Questions

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Individual🎚 A2.2🧰 Printed email handout

    Aim: Read a short personal email (90 words), identify the questions asked, and plan a reply (links to Writing skill).

    Email text & task
    Subject: How are you?

    Hi! How are you? I'm sorry I didn't write for a long time — I was very busy at work.

    So, what's new with you? Are you still at the same job? I remember you wanted to move to a new flat — did you do that? And how is your English course going?

    I have some news too! I got a new job last month. I start on Monday and I'm a bit nervous! Write soon!

    Love, Jess
    Task:
    1. Underline the 3 questions Jess asks.
    2. Circle the news Jess shares about herself.
    3. What word does Jess use to describe her feeling about her new job?
    4. Now plan your reply — bullet-point answers to all 3 questions.

    Vocabulary in Context — Guessing from Clues

    ⏱ 20 min👥 Groups of 3🎚 A2.2🧰 Short text with 8 underlined words

    Aim: Build the key reading skill of inferring meaning from context — reduces dictionary dependency.

    Procedure & Text

    Underline 8 words in a short passage. For each, students:

    1. Write what part of speech it is (noun/verb/adjective).
    2. Write what they think it means.
    3. Replace it with a word they know and check if the sentence still makes sense.
    "The market was crowded with shoppers. We wandered between the stalls, looking at the colourful fruit and vegetables. The aroma of fresh bread was wonderful. I bargained with a seller and got a good price for some handmade jewellery. It was an unforgettable morning."

    Recipe Card — Following Written Instructions

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Pairs🎚 A2.1🧰 Recipe card + comprehension grid

    Aim: Read sequential instructional text using imperative verbs; distinguish ingredients from instructions.

    Recipe & Tasks
    Easy Banana Smoothie (Serves 2)
    Ingredients: 2 bananas, 300ml milk, 1 pot natural yoghurt, 1 tbsp honey, 4 ice cubes
    Method: Peel the bananas and break them into pieces. Put all the ingredients in a blender. Blend for 30 seconds until smooth. Taste and add more honey if you want. Pour into glasses and serve immediately.
    Tasks:
    1. List all the ingredients. How many are there?
    2. Number the instructions in order: pour / blend / peel / add / put / taste
    3. Find 3 time words or sequence words.
    4. What does "immediately" mean? Use context to decide.
    5. Spot two imperative verbs. What makes them "imperative"?
    Module 05 · Skill 3

    💬 3. Spoken Interaction

    💬
    CEFR A2 Descriptor
    Handles short social exchanges and simple routine tasks using direct, polite questions and answers. Manages very short conversations on predictable topics.
    🎯
    Micro-targets: Initiate and maintain short conversations; ask for repetition/clarification; make suggestions and respond; agree and disagree politely; discuss preferences; handle routine transactions (shops, restaurants, transport, appointments); use social phrases (small talk).
    1

    Agree / Disagree Ping-Pong — Opinion Circles

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Groups of 4🎚 A2.1🧰 Opinion cards, language card

    Aim: Practise agreeing and disagreeing politely using appropriate A2 phrases.

    Procedure & Language Card

    Language card (displayed/given):

    AgreeingDisagreeing politely
    I agree! / Me too! / That's right. / Exactly!I'm not sure. / I don't really agree. / I think… / Actually, I think…

    Opinion cards (one drawn per turn):

    • "Cooking at home is better than eating in a restaurant."
    • "Learning English is easy."
    • "Big cities are more exciting than small towns."
    • "Watching TV is a waste of time."
    • "It's important to exercise every day."
    • "Social media is bad for you."

    One student reads the card and gives their opinion. Others respond in turn. A timekeeper ensures each round lasts 2 minutes.

    Doctor's Appointment Roleplay

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Pairs🎚 A2.2🧰 Symptom cards, doctor's question prompts

    Aim: Describe symptoms, answer questions, and understand simple medical advice in a routine appointment.

    Procedure & Role Cards

    Patient card A: You have a bad headache for 3 days. You feel tired. You don't have a temperature. You're not sleeping well.

    Patient card B: You have a sore throat and a cough. You feel hot. You think you have a cold. You feel sick in the mornings.

    Doctor question prompts: How long have you had this? / Do you have a temperature? / Where does it hurt? / Are you taking any medicine? / How much water do you drink? / I recommend you… / I suggest you…

    Follow-up: Doctor writes a simple 3-line advice note. Patient reads it back to check understanding. Swap roles.

    4

    Shopping for Clothes — Size, Colour, Price Negotiation

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Pairs, rotating🎚 A2.1🧰 Clothes pictures, price tags

    Aim: Handle a complete shop transaction — asking about availability, size, price; making a decision.

    Language chunks & task
    • "Can I help you?" / "I'm looking for…"
    • "Do you have this in a medium?" / "What size do you need?"
    • "How much is it?" / "It's… That's a bit expensive."
    • "Can I try it on?" / "The changing rooms are over there."
    • "I'll take it." / "I'll leave it, thank you."

    Three rounds, each student plays assistant once and customer twice. Each round gives a new customer situation card (e.g., "Buy a blue jacket, size L, under £40").

    5

    Speed Friending — 3-Minute Conversations

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Whole class, rotating pairs🎚 A2.1🧰 Conversation prompt cards

    Aim: Build conversation stamina and spontaneity through short but complete social exchanges on varied topics.

    Conversation card topics (10 cards)
    1. Your hometown — describe it. Is it good to live there?
    2. Your last holiday — where, when, who with, best moment.
    3. Your job or studies — do you enjoy it? What do you want to do in the future?
    4. Food — favourite food, favourite restaurant, cooking or eating out?
    5. Films or TV — what did you last watch? Would you recommend it?
    6. Sport and fitness — do you exercise? Play any sports?
    7. Music — what music do you like? Last concert or event?
    8. A typical Saturday — morning to evening, what do you do?
    9. Plans for next weekend — what are you going to do?
    10. A person you admire — who? Why?

    Timer: 3 minutes per conversation. Signal to rotate every 3 minutes. After 5 rotations, debrief: which topic was easiest? hardest? most interesting?

    6

    Problem-Solving Discussion — Lost in the City

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Groups of 3🎚 A2.2🧰 City map, scenario card

    Aim: Negotiate a solution to a shared problem — practises question forms, suggestions, and agreement.

    Scenario & debrief

    Scenario: "You are three friends in a new city. Your phone battery is dead. You need to: find your hotel (you only remember it's near a park), buy medicine for your friend who has a headache, find a restaurant for dinner (you want Italian), and get to the train station by 8pm." Groups must plan their afternoon using only the map.

    Useful language: Let's… / Why don't we…? / What about…? / I think we should… / That's a good idea. / The problem is… / So first we… then we…

    Debrief: Each group presents their plan (2 minutes). Class votes on the best plan.

    7

    Telephone Call — Making a Hotel Booking

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Pairs (back-to-back)🎚 A2.2🧰 Booking info cards

    Aim: Handle a phone call without visual cues — emphasis on clear pronunciation, checking/confirming information.

    Role cards & key language

    Caller card: Book a double room for 2 nights: 14th–16th June. Breakfast included. Name: Johnson. Email: johnson@mail.com.

    Hotel receptionist card: Rooms available: Double with breakfast £95/night. Confirm name, dates, and email. Reference number: BK2241.

    Key phrases: "I'd like to book a room." / "For how many nights?" / "Is breakfast included?" / "Could you spell your name, please?" / "Let me confirm: that's…, is that right?" / "Your reference number is…"

    Students sit back-to-back (no visual cues) to simulate a real phone call. Replay with different dates/room types.

    8

    Comparing Options — Which Flat to Rent?

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Pairs🎚 A2.2🧰 3 flat descriptions, priorities card

    Aim: Use comparative adjectives and preference language to discuss and choose between options.

    Flat descriptions & task
    FeatureFlat AFlat BFlat C
    Rent£600/mo£850/mo£700/mo
    Rooms1 bedroom2 bedrooms1 bedroom
    LocationCity centreSuburbsNear park
    TransportBus stop outside10 min walk to stationCycle path
    ConditionOld, needs workModern, new kitchenGood, small garden

    Each pair has a different "priorities card" — one prefers cheap rent, one prefers location. They must negotiate: "Flat A is cheaper but… Flat B is more expensive, however… I think Flat C is the best because…"

    9

    "Tell Me More" — Active Listening Task

    ⏱ 20 min👥 Pairs🎚 A2.2🧰 Topic prompts

    Aim: Keep a conversation going using follow-up questions and interest signals — a vital interaction strategy.

    Procedure

    Student A talks about a topic for 60 seconds. Student B must ask exactly 3 follow-up questions using these starters: Why…? / When…? / What did you… after that? / Who…? / How did you feel about…?

    After B asks a question, A answers (30 sec). Then B asks the next one. After 3 questions, swap.

    Topics: A time I was very happy / The best meal I ever ate / My first day at a new job or school / A trip I remember well.

    Rule: B must NOT use the question "Really?" alone as a response — they must use a full question.

    10

    Clarification Survival Kit — "I Don't Understand" Practice

    ⏱ 20 min👥 Pairs🎚 A2.1🧰 Clarification phrase cards

    Aim: Use a range of clarification strategies confidently — one of the most important A2 interaction skills.

    Phrase bank & activity
    StrategyPhrase
    Ask to repeat"Sorry, could you say that again, please?"
    Ask to slow down"Could you speak more slowly, please?"
    Check understanding"Do you mean…?"
    Ask to spell"How do you spell that?"
    Ask for a different word"What does '____' mean?"
    Show you're confused"I'm sorry, I don't understand."

    Activity: Student A reads a short difficult paragraph (about a technical topic) too fast. Student B must use clarification phrases to stop and get the information they need. A must respond to each request appropriately. Role-play 3 rounds.

    Module 06 · Skill 4

    🗣️ 4. Spoken Production

    🗣️
    CEFR A2 Descriptor
    Uses a series of phrases and sentences to describe in simple terms their family, other people, living conditions, educational background, and present or most recent job.
    🎯
    Micro-targets: Link simple sentences with and, but, because, so, when; speak on a familiar topic for 1–2 minutes; describe a picture using appropriate vocabulary; narrate a short past event; express opinions briefly; use self-correction and repair strategies.
    1

    "My Neighbourhood" — 90-Second Talk

    ⏱ 35 min👥 Individual → groups of 4🎚 A2.2🧰 Neighbourhood map (own sketch)

    Aim: Describe living conditions, local amenities, and personal opinion in a connected 90-second monologue.

    Frame, model & rubric
    I live in [area], which is in [city/town]. It's a [adjective] neighbourhood. Near my home, there is/are [places]. I usually [activity] at the [place]. My favourite place in my neighbourhood is [place] because [reason]. One thing I don't like about my neighbourhood is [thing] because [reason]. I've lived here for [time] and I [like/don't like/am used to] it.
    Rubric (out of 12):
    Criterion420
    ContentLocation, places, opinion, reason all givenMost parts included, some missingVery little information
    Language rangeGood variety of structures; uses connectorsLimited range; repetitiveSingle words only
    Fluency & deliveryClear and mostly continuous; pauses naturalFrequent long pauses; very slowStops completely
    2

    Picture Story — Narrating a Past Event

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Pairs, then individual🎚 A2.2🧰 6-frame comic strip images

    Aim: Use past simple to narrate a sequence of events from visual prompts — a standard A2 exam task type.

    Story frames & language scaffold

    6 images show: (1) Man wakes up, alarm 8:00. (2) Man looks outside — sunny. (3) Man gets on a bicycle. (4) Dark clouds appear. (5) Heavy rain — man is soaking wet. (6) Man arrives at café, laughing with friend.

    Language scaffold (remove for stronger students):

    • Frame 1: "Last Saturday, … woke up at …"
    • Frame 2: "He looked out of the window and … ."
    • Frame 3: "He decided to … . He got on his …"
    • Frame 4–5: "Suddenly / Unfortunately, …"
    • Frame 6: "In the end, … . He felt … but he …"
    3

    "My Ideal Job" — Opinion Monologue

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Individual → small groups🎚 A2.2🧰 Job vocabulary cards

    Aim: Express aspirations, reasons, and conditions using want to, would like to, because, if.

    Frame & example
    My ideal job is [job]. I want this job because [reason 1] and [reason 2]. In this job, you have to [activity]. I think I would be good at this job because [personal quality]. I don't want to be a [alternative] because [reason]. In the future, I would like to [goal].

    Example: "My ideal job is a nurse. I want this job because I like helping people and I'm interested in medicine. In this job, you have to work long hours, but I don't mind. I think I would be good at this because I'm patient and kind…"

    4

    Describe a Person — Appearance & Personality

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Pairs🎚 A2.1🧰 Photos of 6 people (non-famous)

    Aim: Combine appearance vocabulary and deduction language to describe a person in detail.

    Vocabulary sets & game
    AppearancePersonality (guessing)
    tall / medium height / short; slim / average build; short/long/curly/straight hair; dark/fair/grey; in his/her 30s; wearing…He/She looks… / He/She seems… / I think he/she is probably… / Maybe he/she is…

    Guess Who game: Student A secretly picks a photo. Student B asks yes/no questions to find out which one. Then A describes the person without naming them; B guesses. Swap.

    5

    "A Day I Remember" — Anecdote Telling

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Groups of 3🎚 A2.2🧰 Anecdote planning grid

    Aim: Tell a short personal anecdote (1.5 minutes) using a clear beginning–middle–end structure and past tenses.

    Planning grid & listener task
    SectionQuestions to answer
    SettingWhen? Where? Who with?
    What happenedFirst…? Then…? Suddenly…?
    Problem/TwistThe problem was… / Unfortunately…
    ResolutionIn the end…
    FeelingHow did you feel? Why do you remember this day?

    Listener task: While A speaks, B and C fill in the grid silently, then give feedback: "I understood that… / I didn't understand… / I'd like to know more about…"

    6

    My Town — Mini Presentation

    ⏱ 35 min👥 Individual → class🎚 A2.2🧰 3 prompts + 2-minute timer

    Aim: Deliver a 2-minute unscripted talk about a familiar place using appropriate linking language.

    Preparation & delivery tips

    Preparation (10 min): Students make a mind-map (not a script!) with key points: location, size, best things, problems, personal connection.

    Delivery rules:

    • No reading from paper — notes only (5 words max).
    • Make eye contact with at least 2 people.
    • Use at least 3 connectors: also, however, because, so, although.

    Audience task: Listeners write 1 question to ask after. Speaker then answers 2 questions.

    7

    Fluency Frames — Timed Speaking Sprints

    ⏱ 20 min👥 Pairs, then threes🎚 A2.1🧰 Stopwatch

    Aim: Build speaking fluency through repetition and timed challenges — reduces pause length and builds automaticity.

    Procedure

    Same topic, 3 rounds — each shorter:

    1. Round 1 (60 sec): Speak about "my morning routine." Listener times and counts words.
    2. Feedback: note 2 things to improve (a word you repeated; a pause filler like "uhh").
    3. Round 2 (45 sec): Same topic, new listener. Try to say the same information faster.
    4. Round 3 (30 sec): Final listener. Most fluent yet.

    Word count usually increases. Students see visible progress in one class.

    8

    "Going to" Plans — Future Dreams Talk

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Pairs → small groups🎚 A2.2🧰 Timeline template

    Aim: Use going to and would like to to talk about intentions and hopes on a personal timeline.

    Timeline & frame

    Students draw a simple timeline: This week / Next month / Next year / In 5 years. For each, they note one plan or hope.

    "This week, I'm going to… because… / Next month, I'm going to… / Next year, I'd like to… / In 5 years, I hope to… I think this is [possible/difficult] because…"

    In groups of 3, each person speaks for 60–90 seconds. Others pick one thing they found interesting and say why: "I was interested to hear you want to… because I'd like to… too."

    9

    Back-Chaining Pronunciation Drill — Linked Speech

    ⏱ 15 min👥 Whole class🎚 A2.1🧰 None

    Aim: Improve intelligibility by drilling connected speech patterns in meaningful sentences.

    Drill procedure & sentences

    Back-chaining = teach the end of a sentence first, then build back to the front. This gives correct word stress naturally.

    "I went to the supermarket yesterday."
    → yesterday
    → supermarket yesterday
    → to the supermarket yesterday
    → went to the supermarket yesterday
    → I went to the supermarket yesterday.

    Run 5 sentences from the unit's topic. Add hand gestures for stress. Students close eyes and chorus-drill.

    10

    "Hot Seat" — Character Improvisation

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Whole class🎚 A2.2🧰 Character cards

    Aim: Develop spontaneity and descriptive language by answering questions in role as a given character.

    Procedure & character cards

    One student picks a card and sits on the "hot seat." The class asks questions; the student answers in role for 2–3 minutes.

    Character cards:
    • You are a 65-year-old retired teacher from a small village. You have 5 grandchildren.
    • You are a 25-year-old chef who just opened a new restaurant in the city.
    • You are a 35-year-old parent with 3 young children. You work from home.
    • You are a 19-year-old student studying music in London. You are from Brazil.

    After each hot seat, class votes: "Most interesting answer" and "Best English." Feedback on 1 language point each.

    Module 07 · Skill 5

    ✍️ 5. Writing

    ✍️
    CEFR A2 Descriptor
    Writes short, simple notes and messages relating to matters in areas of immediate need. Writes a very simple personal letter, for example thanking someone for something.
    🎯
    Micro-targets: Write 60–100 word messages, emails, and letters; use correct salutation and closing formulas; use past simple, present simple, and going to; use punctuation correctly (capital, full stop, comma, question mark); organise writing into short paragraphs; link ideas with and, but, because, so, also, however.
    1

    Thank-You Letter — Guided to Free

    ⏱ 40 min👥 Individual🎚 A2.1🧰 Letter frame, model texts × 2

    Aim: Understand and produce the format and language of a simple thank-you letter — a core A2 writing genre.

    Model letters, frame & task
    Model letter:
    Dear Aunt Maria,

    Thank you very much for the birthday present. I really liked the book — it's very interesting! I started reading it yesterday.

    I had a lovely birthday. My family came for dinner and we had a big cake. It was a great day.

    I hope you are well. Write soon!

    Love,
    Sofia

    Analysis task: Students identify: opening formula, 2 paragraphs, closing formula, key phrases of thanks.

    Writing task: Write a thank-you letter (70–90 words) to a friend or relative for: a gift / looking after a pet / helping with a problem.

    Peer editing checklist: Opening ✓ | 2 paragraphs ✓ | Thank-you phrase ✓ | Past tense ✓ | Closing ✓ | Full stops & capitals ✓

    2

    Reply Email — Responding to Questions

    ⏱ 35 min👥 Individual🎚 A2.2🧰 Prompt email (from R7)

    Aim: Write a reply email that answers all questions asked — a critical real-world task; practises reading → writing transfer.

    Task prompt & success criteria

    Using Jess's email from Reading Activity 7, students write a reply (80–100 words) that answers all three questions and shares one piece of personal news.

    Success criteria:
    CriterionMax marks
    Answers all 3 questions6
    Shares personal news2
    Uses correct email format (opening, closing)2
    Uses past simple correctly × 33
    Spelling of 10 key words correct2
    3

    Short Note — Leaving a Message

    ⏱ 20 min👥 Individual🎚 A2.1🧰 4 scenario cards

    Aim: Write a brief functional note (30–50 words) that communicates essential information clearly.

    Scenarios & model

    Model: "Hi Marco, I went to the shops. I'm back at 3pm. Your lunch is in the fridge. Can you call Maria? Her number is 07700 912 888. Thanks! — Ana"

    Scenarios:
    1. Leave a note for your flatmate: you went out, dinner is on the cooker, a parcel arrived for them.
    2. Leave a note for a colleague: you took a phone message for them; the client's name and number; they need to call back before 5pm.
    3. Leave a note for a neighbour: their dog barked all morning; please call you; you're not angry.
    4. Leave a note for your teacher: you can't come to class; you have a doctor's appointment; you will send the homework by email.
    4

    Describing a Photo — Written Caption Story

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Individual🎚 A2.2🧰 4 interesting photos

    Aim: Describe what is in a photo and imagine a story behind it — practises present continuous and descriptive adjectives.

    Scaffolded structure

    Paragraph 1 — What I can see: "In this photo, there are/is… The people are wearing… The place looks like… In the background…"

    Paragraph 2 — What I think is happening: "I think the people are… (present continuous). Maybe it is… I think the man/woman is feeling… because…"

    Paragraph 3 — My reaction: "I like/don't like this photo because… It reminds me of…"

    Target length: 70–90 words. Peer feedback: Did they use all 3 paragraphs? Did they use present continuous at least 3 times?

    5

    Online Review — Café or Place

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Individual🎚 A2.2🧰 Review format template

    Aim: Write a balanced short review using evaluative adjectives and past tense — authentic genre for modern life.

    Frame & example
    ★★★★☆ — Good but could be better
    I visited [place] last [time] with [who]. The [feature 1] was [adjective]. I also liked [feature 2]. However, [negative feature] was [adjective]. The [price/service/food] was [adjective].
    I recommend this place for [type of person/occasion] but [advice]. I will/won't go back because [reason].

    Students review a real café, restaurant, park, or shop they visited. Emphasise that both positives AND negatives make a review more believable and useful.

    6

    Informal Invitation — Plan a Party

    ⏱ 25 min👥 Individual + pair🎚 A2.1🧰 Party details card, reply frame

    Aim: Write an informal invitation with all key information + practise writing a reply (accepting or refusing).

    Task & model language

    Step 1: Students decide on an event (birthday, housewarming, barbecue, games night) and write an invitation (50 words) including: date, time, place, what to bring, RSVP deadline.

    Step 2: Swap invitations. Write a reply (40 words) — either accepting enthusiastically or refusing politely with a reason.

    Reply language:
    • Accept: "Thanks so much for the invitation! I'd love to come. I'll bring…"
    • Refuse: "Thank you for inviting me. Unfortunately, I can't come because… I hope you have a great time!"
    7

    Weekend Diary Entry — Past Narrative

    ⏱ 35 min👥 Individual🎚 A2.2🧰 Diary template, time connectors card

    Aim: Write a coherent personal narrative using past simple and a range of time connectives.

    Time connectives to target + task

    Connectives bank: First / Then / After that / Next / Later / Finally / In the morning / In the afternoon / In the evening / At [time] / When / As soon as / Before / After

    Task: Write a diary entry (80–100 words) for a real or imagined weekend. Include: what you did, who you were with, how you felt, one thing you liked and one thing you didn't like.

    Error correction focus: Teacher collects and marks only: (1) past tense forms, (2) connectives used. Write 3 correct examples from student work on the board (anonymised).

    8

    Sentence Combining — Upgrading Simple Texts

    ⏱ 20 min👥 Pairs🎚 A2.1🧰 "Before" texts handout

    Aim: Teach learners to use conjunctions to combine choppy sentences — a key A2 → B1 progression skill.

    Before & after examples

    "Before" (A1 style):

    "I went to the market. I bought vegetables. I bought fish. The market was crowded. I enjoyed it. It was noisy."

    Students upgrade using: and, but, because, so, although, where, which

    "After" (A2 style):

    "I went to the market, where I bought vegetables and fish. Although it was crowded and noisy, I enjoyed it because the atmosphere was great."

    Run 3 "before" texts. Pairs compare their "after" versions. No single right answer — discuss which sounds most natural.

    9

    Guided Story Completion — Cliffhanger!

    ⏱ 30 min👥 Individual → share🎚 A2.2🧰 Story opener handout

    Aim: Write a creative continuation of a story (60–80 words) — engages imagination while practising past tense narrative.

    Story starters
    Starter A: "It was a cold Tuesday evening. Mira was walking home from work when her phone rang. She looked at the screen — no name, no number. She answered. A voice said: 'I know where you live.' She stopped walking. Then…"
    Starter B: "Tom opened the box and couldn't believe his eyes. Inside was…"

    After writing, students read their ending aloud to a group of 3. Group votes on the most: (a) surprising, (b) funny, (c) believable ending. Teacher gives written feedback on 3 target grammar points only.

    10

    Process Writing — From Plan to Final Draft

    ⏱ 60 min (across 2 sessions)👥 Individual🎚 A2.2🧰 Planning sheet, feedback form

    Aim: Experience the full writing process — plan, draft, peer-feedback, revise, final copy. Builds autonomy and self-editing skills.

    Full process & rubric

    Task: Write a letter to a penfriend (90–110 words) introducing yourself and asking 3 questions.

    1. Session 1, Step 1 (10 min): Plan. Mind-map: who are you / what do you want to know about them?
    2. Session 1, Step 2 (20 min): First draft. Write freely — don't stop to check spelling.
    3. Session 1, Step 3 (10 min): Self-check. Read aloud to yourself. Underline anything you're unsure about.
    4. Session 2, Step 4 (10 min): Peer feedback. Swap. Partner completes a feedback form: content / format / 1 error to fix / 1 thing they liked.
    5. Session 2, Step 5 (15 min): Revise & final copy. Use feedback to improve. Write neatly.
    Final rubric (out of 20):
    CriterionMarks
    Task completion (90–110 words, penfriend letter)4
    Content — introduces self fully AND asks 3 questions5
    Language range — variety of structures4
    Accuracy — grammar, spelling, punctuation4
    Organisation — paragraphs, opening, closing3

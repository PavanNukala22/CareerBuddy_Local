    The Role & IT Contract Staffing Basics

    Before you discuss C2C, 1099 or IR35, understand what an IT Contract Staffing Specialist owns, how a contract assignment flows from request to redeployment, and the words every conversation uses.

    📝
    Please note: Tax, employment and immigration rules differ by country and state and change over time. This course explains the ideas so you can ask the right questions. It is not legal or tax advice; always confirm decisions with your company's legal, compliance and finance teams.

    🧭 What does this specialist do?

    An IT Contract Staffing Specialist places IT professionals on temporary or project-based assignments: a developer for six months, a cloud engineer for a migration, a tester for a launch. Your work has four pillars:

    📑
    Contract models
    Choose and explain W2, 1099 and C2C correctly for each placement.
    ⚖️
    Compliance
    Spot misclassification and IR35 risks for freelancers before they become problems.
    ⚡
    Bench marketing
    Get available consultants in front of clients fast, with fast-turnaround submittals.
    🔄
    Redeployment
    Move contractors to their next assignment before the current one ends.
    Example: a day in the role
    At 9 a.m. a client sends a req for a 6-month Java contractor. By 11 a.m. you have matched two consultants from your bench, checked their right to represent and work authorization, and submitted both. At 3 p.m. you call a contractor whose assignment ends in 60 days to discuss what comes next.

    ⚖️ Contract vs permanent

    FactorPermanent hireContract assignment
    DurationOpen-endedFixed term (often 3, 6 or 12 months), extendable
    SpeedWeeks to monthsDays to weeks
    EmployerThe clientThe staffing firm, or the contractor's own company
    PaySalaryHourly or daily rate
    BenefitsClient's benefitsDepends on the contract model
    FeeOne-time placement feeOngoing margin on each hour worked
    Example: two ways to fill the same need
    A bank needs a data migration specialist for 5 months. Hiring permanently takes 10 weeks and leaves a long-term salary after the project ends. A contractor starts in 2 weeks and leaves when the project is done. For short, specialised work, contract is often the better fit.

    🎯 Why clients use contractors

    ⚡
    Speed
    Start in days, not months.
    🎓
    Specialised skills
    Rare skills for a limited time.
    📈
    Flexibility
    Scale up for a project and down after.
    🧪
    Try before you hire
    Contract-to-hire lets both sides test the fit.
    Example: a launch deadline
    A retailer must launch a new app before the holiday season, with six weeks to go. It cannot hire permanently in time, so it brings in four contract engineers for three months. After the launch the contracts end, and the retailer keeps its permanent team size unchanged.

    👥 Who you work with

    🏢 Client side

    • Hiring manager: needs the skills and decides.
    • Vendor or procurement manager: controls rates and suppliers.
    • MSP or VMS administrator: manages the reqs and workflow.

    🧑‍💻 Your side

    • Consultants: W2, 1099 and C2C contractors.
    • Account managers: own client relationships.
    • Payroll and finance: payments, invoices, margins.
    • Legal and compliance: classification and contract questions.
    Example: involving the right person
    A consultant asks to switch from W2 to C2C mid-assignment. You do not decide alone. You involve compliance and finance, because changing the model can change taxes, benefits and legal risk for everyone.

    Module 02 · Contract Models

    W2, 1099 & C2C Contract Models

    The three ways a contractor can be engaged in the US, who pays the taxes, who carries the risk, and how to choose the right one without creating trouble later.

    📝
    This module uses the United States terms (W2, 1099, C2C). Other countries use different words (Module 05 covers the UK). Confirm details with your compliance team.

    🔀 Three ways to engage someone

    👔
    W2
    The person is an employee of the staffing firm.
    🧑‍💻
    1099
    The person is an independent contractor, paid directly.
    🏢
    C2C
    The person's own company contracts with the firm.
    Example: same engineer, three setups
    Priya, a cloud engineer, can work as (1) a W2 employee of your firm, with taxes withheld; (2) a 1099 contractor, who invoices as an individual; or (3) through her own LLC under C2C. The work is identical. The paperwork, taxes and risk are very different.

    👔 W2 explained

    On W2 the consultant is an employee of the staffing firm, which is the employer of record. The firm withholds income tax, pays the employer share of payroll taxes, and may offer benefits.

    FeatureW2 consultant
    EmployerStaffing firm
    TaxesWithheld by the firm; the firm also pays its employer taxes
    BenefitsOften health coverage, paid time off or holidays (varies by firm)
    OvertimeHourly non-exempt employees are generally entitled to overtime under wage laws
    SecurityCan be paid between assignments if salaried (bench)
    Example: why W2 suits some people
    A consultant who values health coverage and steady payroll chooses W2 at $70 an hour. A consultant who earns $85 an hour as a C2C contractor but pays for their own insurance and has unpaid gaps might actually earn less overall. W2 trades a bit of rate for security.

    🧑‍💻 1099 explained

    A 1099 contractor is an independent contractor in business for themselves. The firm pays them without withholding tax, and reports payments on a Form 1099. The contractor pays their own income and self-employment taxes.

    Feature1099 contractor
    RelationshipIndependent, not an employee
    TaxesContractor pays their own, including self-employment tax
    BenefitsNone from the firm
    ControlDecides how the work is done; sets own methods
    FormProvides a W-9 before payment
    ⚠️
    1099 status must match how the work is really done. Calling someone a contractor does not make them one. Module 04 explains the tests.
    Example: a genuine 1099
    A security consultant works on a fixed-scope audit for three clients this year, uses her own laptop and tools, sets her own hours and sends invoices. That pattern looks like a genuine independent contractor. A person working full time, on the client's schedule and equipment, for one client for years would not.

    🏢 C2C explained

    C2C (corp-to-corp) means the consultant's own registered business (an LLC or corporation) signs a contract with the staffing firm. The firm pays the company's invoices. The company handles its own payroll, taxes, insurance and benefits.

    FeatureC2C
    Contract betweenStaffing firm and the consultant's company
    Paid byThe firm, against the company's invoices
    InsuranceOften required: a certificate of insurance (COI)
    PaperworkSubcontractor agreement, W-9 for the company, invoices
    Common forExperienced senior consultants running their own business
    Example: a real C2C setup
    Maya forms "Maya Tech Solutions LLC", with a business bank account and insurance. Her LLC signs a subcontractor agreement and invoices the staffing firm every two weeks for her hours. Her LLC pays her a salary and handles all taxes. The firm never withholds anything.

    📊 Side-by-side comparison

    QuestionW21099C2C
    Who is the employer?Staffing firmNobody (independent)The consultant's own company
    Who withholds tax?The firmNobody; contractor paysThe consultant's company
    Benefits from the firm?Often yesNoNo
    Hourly rate (same job)LowestHigherHigher
    Key tax formW-4 (employee)W-9, then 1099W-9 (company)
    Main legal riskWage and overtime rulesMisclassificationSham entities and misclassification
    Example: reading the table
    A consultant asks, "Which one gives me the most take-home?" You reply: "C2C gives the highest hourly rate, but you pay your own taxes, benefits and gaps. W2 has a lower rate with benefits. Let's compare the whole year, not just the rate."

    📄 Paperwork

    DocumentUsed for
    W-4 and I-9W2 employees: tax withholding and work eligibility
    W-91099 and C2C: taxpayer details for payment reporting
    MSA / subcontractor agreementGeneral terms: payment, confidentiality, termination
    SOW or assignment letterScope, rate, start date, duration for one assignment
    COIProof of insurance (often for C2C)
    Timesheets and invoicesRecord hours and request payment
    Example: a clean onboarding pack
    For a new C2C consultant you collect: signed subcontractor agreement, W-9 for the company, COI, assignment letter with the rate and dates, and the invoicing instructions. Nothing starts until the pack is complete, which prevents payment disputes later.

    🧪 Contract-to-hire & tiers

    🌱 Contract-to-hire (C2H)

    Start as a contractor, with the option to be hired permanently. Check the conversion fee and the minimum contract period in the agreement.

    🪜 Vendor tiers

    A client may use a prime vendor, who passes reqs to sub-vendors. Each tier takes a margin, so the rate available to the consultant shrinks.

    Example: tier squeeze
    The client's bill rate is $90. The prime vendor keeps 10% ($9), leaving $81 for the sub-vendor, who keeps 10% ($8.10), leaving about $72.90 for the consultant. If the consultant needs $80, the req will not fill. Knowing this early saves wasted submittals.

    🎯 Choosing the right model

    SituationOften suits
    Consultant wants benefits and stabilityW2
    Client policy only allows W2W2
    Senior consultant with their own registered company and insuranceC2C
    Short, fixed-scope project with an independent specialist1099 (if truly independent)
    Candidate's work authorization depends on a specific employerUsually W2; confirm with legal or immigration counsel
    Example: the client decides
    A consultant asks for C2C, but the client's policy says "all contractors must be W2". You explain this honestly. The consultant can accept W2 or decline. You never "bend" the model to close a deal, because the client's policy and the law come first.

    🪤 Common mistakes

    ❌ Mistake

    • "Everyone can be 1099 if they agree."
    • "C2C means any name works as a company."
    • "Higher hourly rate means higher income."

    ✅ Reality

    • Status depends on how the work is done, not what the paper says.
    • The company must be real, registered and insured.
    • Compare total annual value after taxes, benefits and gaps.
    Example: a costly assumption
    A recruiter labels a full-time, on-site, closely managed engineer as 1099 "because he agreed". An audit later questions the status. The firm faces back taxes and penalties, and the engineer loses benefits he should have received. A five-minute check with compliance would have prevented it.

    Module 03 · Rates, Margins & Pricing

    Rates, Margins & Pricing

    The money side of contract staffing: how bill rates and pay rates relate, how to calculate margin on each contract model, and why payment terms matter as much as the rate itself.

    📝
    All figures are illustrative, chosen to make the maths clear. Real burden and margin depend on your firm, state and client. Use your finance team's numbers for real quotes.

    💵 Bill rate and pay rate

    The bill rate is what the client pays per hour. The pay rate is what the consultant receives. The gap pays for your costs and profit.

    Read left to right, then continue on the next row.
    Example: the basic gap
    Bill rate $100, pay rate $80. The gap of $20 an hour covers the firm's costs and profit. If the consultant works 160 hours in a month, the firm bills $16,000, pays $12,800 and keeps $3,200 before other costs.

    ➗ Margin vs markup

    People mix these up. They use the same gap but divide by a different number.

    MeasureFormula$100 bill, $80 pay
    Gross profitBill rate − Cost$20
    Gross margin %Gross profit ÷ Bill rate20 ÷ 100 = 20%
    Markup %Gross profit ÷ Cost20 ÷ 80 = 25%
    💡
    A 20% margin equals a 25% markup. If a manager says "we need 20%", ask which one they mean. Mixing them up changes the bill rate you quote.
    Example: a quoting mistake
    Pay rate $80. You want a 20% margin. Bill = 80 ÷ (1 − 0.20) = $100. If you wrongly add 20% markup, you quote $96 and make only about 16.7% margin. Over 2,000 hours, that mistake costs thousands.

    🧾 Burden on W2

    Burden means the extra employer costs on top of a W2 consultant's pay: payroll taxes, workers' compensation, unemployment insurance, benefits and administration. It is usually shown as a percentage of pay.

    Cost on top of payExamples
    Employer payroll taxesSocial security and Medicare contributions
    InsuranceWorkers' compensation, unemployment insurance
    BenefitsHealth coverage, paid time off, holidays
    AdministrationPayroll processing, compliance, onboarding
    Example: W2 cost build-up (illustrative)
    Pay rate $70. Burden 20% of pay = $14. Total cost = $70 + $14 = $84 an hour. With a bill rate of $100, gross profit = $16 and gross margin = 16%.

    ⚖️ C2C vs W2 maths

    ItemC2C (illustrative)W2 (illustrative)
    Bill rate$100$100
    Pay rate$85$70
    Burden (20% of pay, W2 only)$0$14
    Total cost to the firm$85$84
    Gross profit$15$16
    Gross margin15%16%

    To keep a 20% margin on the W2 example, the bill rate would need to be 84 ÷ 0.80 = $105.

    Example: why C2C looks higher but costs about the same
    The C2C consultant sees $85 against $70, a headline gap of $15. But the W2 option costs the firm an extra $14 in burden, so the firm's total cost is nearly equal. This is why "C2C costs less" is not always true for the firm.

    ⏰ Overtime and holidays

    • Hourly non-exempt W2 employees are generally entitled to overtime, often 1.5 times pay beyond 40 hours a week. Rules vary by state and role.
    • Clients may or may not agree to pay overtime at a higher bill rate. Confirm before the assignment starts.
    • C2C and 1099 contractors normally bill straight time, with no overtime rules between them and the firm.
    Example: overtime in numbers (illustrative)
    A non-exempt W2 consultant earns $50 an hour and works 45 hours. Pay = 40 × $50 + 5 × ($50 × 1.5) = $2,000 + $375 = $2,375. If the client's contract does not allow overtime billing, the firm absorbs that extra cost. Always ask first.

    ⏳ Payment terms and cash flow

    TermMeaningWhy it matters
    Net 30 / 45 / 60Client pays 30, 45 or 60 days after invoiceLonger terms strain cash flow
    Pay-when-paidContractor is paid only after the firm is paidShifts waiting risk to the contractor
    Weekly or bi-weekly payrollW2 staff are paid regularly regardless of client paymentFirm funds the gap
    Example: the cash-flow gap (illustrative)
    A firm has 20 W2 consultants costing $70 an hour for 40 hours, so $56,000 a week in pay. The client pays on net 45, about 6.5 weeks later. The firm must fund roughly $364,000 before the first invoice is paid. Growth without working capital can break a healthy firm.

    🤝 Negotiating rates

    1. Know the client's rate cap or rate card before you submit.
    2. Ask the consultant for their minimum and target rate early.
    3. Calculate margin on the contract model before promising anything.
    4. If the numbers do not work, say so honestly; do not mislead either side.
    5. Offer non-rate value: longer term, remote work, extension prospects.
    Example: closing a gap
    Client cap: $90. Consultant wants $85 on C2C. Your margin at $90 bill would be about 5.6%, below your 15% floor. You ask the client for $95, and the client says no. You tell the consultant honestly, and offer a different req where the numbers fit.

    📇 Rate card

    Large clients publish a rate card: the maximum bill rate by role and level. Your job is to fit your consultants inside it.

    Role (example card)Max bill rate
    Software engineer, mid$75
    Software engineer, senior$95
    Cloud architect$120
    Example: working inside a rate card
    Senior engineer cap is $95. At a 15% margin, the highest cost to you is 95 × 0.85 = about $80.75. For a C2C consultant, that means a pay rate near $80. You tell candidates this upfront, so nobody wastes time.

    Module 04 · Contractor Classification & Compliance

    Classification & Compliance

    Calling someone a contractor does not make them one. Learn how classification is judged, how to spot risk early, and when to bring in compliance.

    📝
    This module explains general ideas with US examples. Tests vary by agency, state and over time. It is not legal advice. When in doubt, ask your legal or compliance team before proceeding.

    🏷️ What misclassification is

    Misclassification means treating a worker as an independent contractor (1099 or C2C) when, in law, the person is really an employee. It is a risk for both the staffing firm and the client.

    Example: a simple picture
    Imagine two people. Alex works 40 hours a week at the client's office, uses the client's laptop, follows daily instructions and has done so for two years. Sam builds a fixed project from home with their own tools for several clients. On paper both are "1099". In reality, Alex looks like an employee and Sam looks like a contractor.

    💥 Why it matters

    WhoPossible consequences (general)
    Staffing firm or clientBack taxes, penalties and interest, unpaid overtime or benefits, legal claims, reputational damage
    WorkerMissed benefits and protections, unexpected tax issues
    Your careerClients stop trusting vendors who create compliance trouble
    Example: an expensive lesson
    A firm engages 30 engineers as 1099 for several years. An audit finds many worked like employees. The firm owes back payroll taxes and penalties, and the engineers claim unpaid benefits. The cost far exceeds the margin it saved. Prevention is much cheaper than correction.

    🧪 The tests

    Different authorities use different tests, but they ask similar questions.

    TestCore idea
    IRS common-law factorsBehavioural control, financial control, and the relationship of the parties
    Economic reality testIs the worker really in business for themselves, or economically dependent on the client?
    ABC test (used in some states)Presumed an employee unless: A free from control, B work outside the hiring company's usual business, C independently established in that trade
    💡
    The ABC test is stricter, and the "B" part is often hard for IT contractors who do the same work as the client's staff. Which test applies depends on location and purpose, so ask compliance.
    Example: ABC in a sentence
    A software company hires a contractor to write the same kind of code its employees write. Under a strict ABC test, part B ("outside the usual course of business") is difficult to meet. That is why some states restrict 1099 for IT work and clients may require W2 or properly structured C2C.

    ✅ Evidence checklist

    FactorPoints toward contractorPoints toward employee
    ControlDecides how and when work is doneTold how, when and where to work
    EquipmentUses own tools and equipmentClient provides equipment and workspace
    Financial riskCan profit or lose; invests in businessPaid by the hour, little risk
    Other clientsWorks for several clientsWorks only for one
    DurationFixed projectOpen-ended, ongoing role
    IntegrationSeparate from client staffTreated like a team member, same title and email
    SubstitutionMay send a replacementMust do the work personally
    Example: scoring a case
    Engineer works 40 hours at the client's site, uses client equipment, takes daily direction, only works for this client, has no end date. All six factors lean toward employee, so a 1099 or sham C2C arrangement is high risk. You raise it with compliance before the start.

    📜 Contract vs reality

    📄 On paper

    "The contractor controls their own methods and hours, may send a substitute, and uses their own equipment."

    🏢 In practice

    The manager sets the hours, a substitute would never be accepted, and the laptop belongs to the client.

    Authorities look at how the work is actually done. A well-drafted contract that does not match reality offers little protection and can even look like an attempt to hide the truth.

    Example: honest alignment
    You review an assignment letter that says the contractor sets their hours. You ask the hiring manager, "Will the contractor really set their own hours?" The manager says, "No, they must be here 9 to 5." Now you know the contract needs to change, or the model does.

    🏢 Genuine C2C entities

    A C2C arrangement only works if the company is real. Basic checks:

    • Company is registered (LLC or corporation) and in good standing.
    • Has its own tax ID and a W-9.
    • Has insurance (a certificate of insurance), where the client requires it.
    • Invoices from the company, and is paid into the company's bank account.
    • Company has, or could have, other clients; it is not a label for one employee.
    Example: a fair check
    A consultant says "my company is set up". You politely request the registration details, W-9 and insurance certificate, and compare the invoice name with the bank account name. If everything matches, you proceed. If the "company" is only a name on a page, you pause and involve compliance.

    🛂 Work authorization and model

    In the US, a person's work authorization can restrict how they may work. For example, some visa categories tie the person to a specific employer, which can make C2C or 1099 unsuitable. This is a legal area where mistakes are costly.

    ⚠️
    Do not guess. Ask every candidate the same standard question: "Are you legally authorized to work in this country, and will you now or in the future need sponsorship?" Do not treat people differently because of citizenship or national origin, and confirm any model question with legal or immigration counsel.
    Example: the safe path
    A consultant on a work visa asks for C2C because a client wants it. You do not decide. You tell the consultant, "I will check with our legal team what is allowed for your situation," and treat every candidate the same way. The answer decides the model, not the rate.

    💬 Screening questions

    AskWhat a good answer shows
    "Do you have your own registered company and insurance?"A real business, ready for C2C
    "Do you work for other clients or only this one?"Independence indicator
    "Who decides your daily tasks and hours?"Control indicator
    "Whose equipment do you use?"Independence indicator
    "Is this a fixed project or an open-ended role?"Duration indicator
    Example: a good screening exchange
    Recruiter: "Who decides your daily tasks?" Candidate: "The client's manager assigns tickets and expects me at stand-up every day." You note this as an employee indicator and raise it with compliance, rather than quietly placing the person as 1099.

    🚩 Red flags & worked example

    • Full-time, long-term, single-client, closely managed 1099 or C2C workers.
    • "Company" with no registration, insurance or separate bank account.
    • Client wants the contractor to look like staff (same title, email, desk, team role).
    • Contract wording that clearly contradicts how the work is done.

    🟢 Lower risk

    An independent specialist delivers a 3-month security assessment with own tools, for several clients, and invoices by milestone.

    🔴 Higher risk

    A "contractor" attends all team meetings, has a company email and manager, works 40 hours on site, for 3 years, with no other clients.

    Example: what you would do
    On the higher-risk case you stop and flag it, giving the client three options: move the person to W2 through your firm, restructure the work as a genuinely independent project, or hire the person directly. You never just "let it ride".

    Module 05 · IR35 & Freelance Compliance

    IR35 & Freelance Compliance

    The UK's "off-payroll working" rules decide whether a contractor working through their own company is truly self-employed for tax. Learn the vocabulary, the tests and what to do, and see how other countries handle the same question.

    📝
    Tax rules change often and depend on each engagement. This is a plain-English introduction, not tax or legal advice. Check current guidance from the tax authority and your compliance team before acting.

    🇬🇧 What IR35 is

    IR35 is the common name for the UK's off-payroll working rules. Many UK contractors work through their own limited company, called a PSC (personal service company). IR35 asks: if this person worked directly for the client, would they be an employee? If yes, they should pay tax like one.

    🟢 Outside IR35

    Genuinely in business on their own account. The PSC is paid and the contractor manages their own tax.

    🔴 Inside IR35

    Deemed employment for tax. Income tax and national insurance are deducted before payment.

    Example: same rate, different take-home
    A contractor is offered £500 a day. Outside IR35, the PSC receives £500 and the contractor chooses how to pay themselves tax-efficiently. Inside IR35, deductions come off before payment, so take-home can be much lower. That is why contractors ask for a higher rate when the role is inside.

    ⚖️ Inside vs outside

    FeatureOutside IR35Inside IR35
    Tax treatmentSelf-employed style, through the PSCEmployment style, deducted at source
    Who pays taxContractor, via the PSCFee-payer deducts and pays over
    Rate expectationLower daily rateOften higher to compensate
    Typical work patternProject-based, own methods, can substituteDirected like staff, personal service
    Example: matching the role to the status
    A fixed-scope website build delivered by a freelancer with their own tools suggests outside. A contractor embedded in a team, taking daily instructions and covering a vacant staff role suggests inside. The facts decide, not the label.

    🧭 Who decides

    Rules changed in the UK in two steps: public sector clients from April 2017 and medium and large private-sector clients from April 2021. Under these rules the end client decides the status for each engagement, and the party paying the contractor (the fee-payer, often the agency) deducts tax if it is inside.

    Client typeWho decides status
    Public sectorThe end client
    Medium or large private companyThe end client
    Small private company (as defined by size criteria)The contractor's own PSC, under the older rules
    📝
    "Small" is defined by company-size criteria that can change. Ask your compliance team to confirm the current definition for each client.
    Example: who signs what
    A large retailer wants a data engineer. The retailer assesses the role and issues a decision with reasons. Your agency, as fee-payer, either pays the PSC in full (outside) or deducts tax and national insurance (inside). You do not decide; you pass the facts and the decision along.

    🔬 The status tests

    Three questions carry the most weight. Other factors also count.

    TestQuestionPoints toward employment
    ControlDoes the client decide how, when and where the work is done?Yes, closely directed
    SubstitutionCan the contractor send someone else, in practice?No, must do the work personally
    Mutuality of obligationIs the client obliged to offer work and the contractor to accept it?Yes, ongoing obligation

    Other factors: financial risk, equipment, being part of the organisation, exclusivity and right to end the contract.

    Example: three tests on one role
    Contractor must work the client's 9 to 5 on site (control), could never send a replacement (no substitution), and is expected to take assigned tickets indefinitely (mutuality). All three point to inside IR35. A fixed-price project with a genuine right to send a substitute points the other way.

    📄 SDS and CEST

    • For clients who decide, the result is given in a Status Determination Statement (SDS), with the reasons.
    • The contractor can dispute the decision, and the client must respond within a set time (45 days at the time of writing).
    • CEST is HMRC's online Check Employment Status for Tax tool. HMRC says it will stand by results when used correctly with accurate information, though the tool does not cover every situation.
    • Each engagement should be assessed individually. Blanket decisions for all contractors in a role are discouraged.
    Example: a dispute
    A contractor receives an SDS saying "inside" with a one-line reason. She disputes it, pointing out that her contract allows a substitute and she runs the project herself. The client must review and respond with proper reasons within the deadline. A good agency helps both sides keep their documents accurate.

    ☂️ Umbrella companies

    If a role is inside IR35, many contractors choose to work through an umbrella company. The contractor becomes an employee of the umbrella, which handles payroll and deductions.

    RouteHow it worksWatch out for
    PSC (outside)Own company; self-managed taxMust be genuinely outside
    Umbrella (inside)Employee of the umbrella; PAYE deductionsFees and quality vary; check the provider is reputable
    Agency payroll (inside)Agency employs and pays through payrollRate must cover employer costs
    Example: giving a clear quote
    A role is inside IR35 and the client's budget is £450 a day. You explain to the contractor: "After umbrella fees and deductions, your take-home from £450 is X; here is the breakdown." Transparent numbers prevent unpleasant surprises on the first payslip.

    🌍 Freelance compliance elsewhere

    Many countries ask the same question in different words: is this person really independent?

    Country or regionCommon idea
    United StatesEmployee vs independent contractor tests (IRS, economic reality, ABC in some states)
    United KingdomIR35 / off-payroll working rules
    Germany"False self-employment" rules and social-insurance checks
    NetherlandsRules against disguised employment with assessments of the working relationship
    AustraliaPersonal services income rules and contractor-versus-employee tests
    💡
    The facts that matter are similar everywhere: control, independence, financial risk, other clients and the right to substitute. If a placement crosses borders, involve compliance before you quote a rate.
    Example: a cross-border placement
    A UK client wants a freelancer based in Germany. You do not assume UK rules apply, nor German ones. You ask compliance: "Which rules cover this engagement, and who is responsible for the status decision?" The answer shapes the contract and the rate.

    🧑‍💼 Your role as recruiter

    ✅ You can

    • Collect the facts about how the role works
    • Pass them accurately to the client
    • Explain inside vs outside in plain language
    • Give transparent rate breakdowns

    🚫 You should not

    • Guess or promise a status
    • Write wording just to "make it outside"
    • Pressure a client to decide a certain way
    • Tell a contractor to ignore the rules
    Example: staying in your lane
    A contractor says, "Just call it outside IR35, everyone does." You reply, "The client decides based on the facts of the role, and I will give them accurate information. I can't promise a result." That protects the contractor, the client and you.

    🚩 Screening and red flags

    Ask the client or contractorWhy
    "Who will direct the daily work, and how?"Control
    "Could the contractor send a substitute, in practice?"Substitution
    "Is it a fixed project, or ongoing cover for a staff role?"Integration and mutuality
    "Whose equipment is used, and where is the work done?"Independence
    "Has the client issued a status decision for this role?"Process and documentation
    • Blanket "everyone is outside" or "everyone is inside" statements.
    • A contract that says a substitute is allowed while the client says never.
    • A client that will not give reasons for its decision.
    Example: a red flag found early
    A client wants a contractor "outside IR35" but also requires a fixed 9 to 5 on site, daily line management and personal service only. These facts point inside. You raise it politely with the client's compliance contact before anyone signs.

    🔬 Worked example

    three roles, three assessmentsRole A: Fixed-price website build, freelancer's own tools, can send a substitute
Role B: Embedded in the finance team, daily direction, covers a staff vacancy, 9-5 on site
Role C: Part-time advisory for 3 clients, own methods, delivers a report each month
    ✅
    Likely reading: Role A and Role C look like outside candidates; Role B looks inside. The client decides for each one separately, and your job is to supply accurate facts and be transparent about the rate impact.
    Example: what you would tell the contractor on Role B
    "This engagement looks like the kind that is usually treated as inside IR35. The client will issue the decision with reasons. If it is inside, I can offer an umbrella or agency payroll option, and here is the rate breakdown for each."

    Module 06 · Bench Marketing & Fast-Turnaround Submittals

    Bench Marketing & Fast-Turnaround Submittals

    Every day a consultant sits on the bench costs money. Learn how to market available consultants quickly, submit them accurately within hours, and keep the whole process fair and honest.

    🪑 What the bench is

    The bench is the group of consultants employed by your firm (usually on W2) who are between assignments. They are available to start, but they are also a cost, because the firm may still pay them while no client pays for their time.

    StatusMeaningImpact
    BillableOn an assignment, client is payingEarns margin
    BenchAvailable, no assignmentCosts money
    Rolling offAssignment ending soonStart marketing early
    Example: the cost of waiting
    A W2 consultant costs the firm $84 an hour including burden, and a bench week of 40 hours costs $3,360 with no revenue. Five consultants waiting two weeks cost about $33,600. Fast placement is not a luxury; it is the business model.

    ⚡ Why speed wins

    • Hot reqs attract many submissions in the first hours. Early, accurate profiles are reviewed first.
    • Clients often fill from the first few strong candidates they see.
    • Consultants lose patience (and may accept another offer) if they sit idle.
    • Speed only helps when accuracy comes with it: a fast wrong submittal damages trust.
    Example: first in, first read
    A req posted at 9 a.m. gets 25 submissions by 5 p.m. A hiring manager reads the first five carefully and skims the rest. Your accurate, well-formatted profile submitted at 10 a.m. gets read. The same profile at 4 p.m. may be skipped.

    🔁 The bench marketing workflow

    Read left to right, then continue on the next row.
    StepWhat you do
    Update the bench listNames, skills, location, availability date, contract type, rate range
    Prepare profilesOne clear, accurate, anonymised page per consultant
    Send the "hotlist"A short email listing available consultants to your client network
    Match reqsCheck every new req against the bench the same day
    Follow upCall after sending; ask for feedback within 24 hours
    Example: a morning routine
    8:30 a.m.: update the bench list (4 available). 9:00: scan new reqs and match two. 9:45: confirm rate and availability with both consultants. 10:30: submit. 3:00 p.m.: send the weekly hotlist to existing clients. Repeating this daily keeps consultants moving.

    🗂️ Marketing profiles

    A marketing profile is a short, skills-focused summary used to promote a consultant. Many firms hide the name and contact details until the consultant agrees to be submitted for a specific role.

    marketing profile exampleConsultant ID: JAVA-2207          Role: Senior Java Developer
Experience: 9 years     Location: Dallas, TX (open to remote)
Available: 2 weeks      Contract type: W2 or C2C (to be confirmed)

- Cut batch processing time from 6 hours to 90 minutes
- Led a team of 4 on a core banking migration

    Example: why an ID instead of a name
    Using a consultant ID protects the person's privacy and your relationship. Competitors cannot simply copy the name and contact the consultant directly. Once the consultant agrees to a specific submittal, you share the full profile.

    📡 Channels

    ChannelBest for
    Your client networkWarmest leads; send a hotlist and call
    VMS and MSP portalsFormal reqs with defined rate cards
    Other vendors and prime suppliersSub-vendor opportunities
    Job boards and professional networksFinding new reqs and clients
    Direct outreach to hiring managersTargeted, with a specific consultant for a likely need
    Example: matching channel to consultant
    A niche SAP consultant is best marketed by calling three clients known to run SAP and by checking the VMS for SAP reqs. A generalist Java developer can go on the broad hotlist. Narrow skills deserve narrow, personal outreach.

    ✍️ Right to represent

    Right to Represent (RTR) is the candidate's confirmation that your firm may submit them for a specific role. It prevents duplicate submissions and protects the consultant from being sent without consent.

    Good RTR practiceWhy
    Get permission per reqConsultant knows exactly where they are going
    Record the date and timeSettles who submitted first if two vendors submit
    Confirm rate, location, start date, contract typePrevents surprises after submission
    Do not submit the same person through two vendorsDuplicate submissions harm the consultant
    Example: a duplicate problem
    Two vendors submit the same consultant for the same req. The client rejects both, saying "we don't take duplicates". The consultant loses the chance. A time-stamped RTR with one vendor would have avoided it.

    🚀 Fast-turnaround submittals

    MinutesAction
    0–10Read the req; confirm must-haves, rate cap, start date, location
    10–25Match against the bench; call the best-fit consultant
    25–40Confirm interest, availability, rate, authorization, RTR
    40–60Tailor (truthfully) and submit with a short note

    Example: speed with accuracy
    You submit within 50 minutes, and the note says what you confirmed and what is a gap. The hiring manager notices the honesty, books an interview the same afternoon, and trusts your next submittal more. Fast and honest wins.

    ⚖️ Work authorization and fairness

    • Ask every candidate the same standard question: legally authorized to work, and any sponsorship need now or later.
    • Do not exclude or favour people by citizenship, national origin or accent.
    • Some work authorizations limit contract type or employer. Confirm with legal or immigration counsel instead of guessing.
    • Never share personal details (such as passport or tax numbers) in marketing materials.
    Example: the same question for everyone
    Your script says, "Are you legally authorized to work in the US, and will you need sponsorship now or in future?" You read it the same way to every candidate. It keeps you compliant and treats everyone fairly.

    🛡️ Ethics and accuracy

    ✅ Acceptable tailoring

    • Reorder skills to match the req
    • Highlight relevant real projects
    • Rewrite for clarity and keywords the consultant truly has

    🚫 Never

    • Add skills or years the person does not have
    • Invent employers, titles or certifications
    • Hide a known gap that matters to the req
    Example: the line you do not cross
    The req needs Kubernetes and the consultant has only used it for a week in a lab. You may highlight the lab honestly ("hands-on lab experience"). You must not write "3 years of Kubernetes". A false profile is found out at interview, and it ends client trust.

    Bench rate rose from 15% to 25% in a month, and average bench days from 10 to 22. You check the cause: profiles are too generic and submittals take 5 hours. You rewrite profiles and cut time-to-submit to 90 minutes. Next month bench rate falls back to 15%.

    Module 07 · Contractor Redeployment

    Contractor Redeployment

    The cheapest placement is the one you make before the current assignment ends. Learn how to track end dates, hold honest extension conversations and line up the next assignment, while keeping contractors loyal and the paperwork correct.

    🔄 What redeployment is

    Redeployment means moving a consultant from an assignment that is ending to their next one, with little or no gap. It protects margin, keeps consultants happy and saves the cost of recruiting someone new.

    Example: redeploy vs recruit
    Finding a new consultant for a req takes about two weeks of sourcing and screening effort. Moving an existing consultant whose project is ending takes one phone call and a quick submittal. Redeployment is faster, cheaper and builds loyalty.

    📅 Tracking end dates

    ConsultantClientEnd dateDays leftStatus
    A. RaoBank X30 Nov56Extension likely
    M. ChenRetail Y15 Nov41Redeploy now
    S. PatelTelco Z31 Oct26Urgent

    Keep one roll-off calendar and review it weekly. Anything under 60 days with no extension confirmed goes on the redeployment list.

    Example: the calendar catches a problem
    The weekly review shows S. Patel's contract ends in 26 days and the client has not mentioned an extension. You call the client the same day and learn the project is wrapping up. Because you caught it early, you have almost four weeks to line up the next role.

    ⏱️ The 90-60-30 playbook

    Read left to right, then continue on the next row.
    Days before endAction
    90Informal check-in: how is it going, what does the consultant want next?
    60Ask the client directly about extension plans
    45If no extension is confirmed, start marketing the consultant
    30Submit to matching reqs; interview in parallel with the current assignment
    14Confirm the next start date and paperwork
    Example: the playbook in action
    At day 60 the client says, "We may extend, but budget approval is pending." You tell the consultant honestly and, at day 45 with no answer, you begin submitting to other reqs as a back-up. At day 30 the consultant has two interviews. The extension is declined, and the next start date is already agreed.

    🤝 Extension conversations

    🏢 With the client

    • Ask early: "What is the plan after the current end date?"
    • Listen for budget, scope change or team changes
    • Offer a clear extension proposal with the same or adjusted rate

    🧑‍💻 With the consultant

    • Ask what they want: extension, new skills, new client
    • Be honest about the client's signals
    • Discuss rate expectations before the client asks
    Example: a rate-change extension
    The client wants to extend by 6 months, but only at a lower rate because the project is in maintenance mode. You explain this plainly to the consultant. She declines the cut and you redeploy her on a better-paid project, while the client gets a new consultant at its rate.

    🔎 Redeployment sources

    SourceWhy it works
    Same client, new projectThe client already knows and trusts the consultant
    Other teams at the same clientReferrals from the current hiring manager
    Your existing client networkWarm relationships and known reqs
    VMS and MSP portalsSteady flow of formal reqs
    Internal reqs from colleaguesOpen reqs your firm already holds
    Example: asking the right question
    At month 5 you ask the hiring manager, "Do any other teams here need someone with this skill?" She introduces you to a colleague with an open req. The consultant moves to the next team without a day on the bench.

    🎓 Upskilling to redeploy

    If market demand for a consultant's skills is falling, a little training can open many more reqs.

    Current skillAdjacent skillResult
    On-premise Java developerSpring Boot and AWS basicsCloud-ready Java roles
    Manual QA testerTest automation (e.g. Selenium, Python)Automation roles pay more and fill faster
    Windows server adminAzure and infrastructure as codeCloud infrastructure roles
    Example: a small investment
    A manual tester has been on the bench for three weeks. You agree a two-week plan: an automation course and a small portfolio project. After that, the consultant matches 12 open reqs instead of 2, and is placed in the second week.

    💬 Keeping contractors engaged

    • Check in regularly, even when nothing is wrong.
    • Be honest about market signals and client plans; do not over-promise.
    • Pay accurately and on time; payment problems end loyalty fastest.
    • Listen to goals: some want stability, some want challenge, some want remote.
    • Ask for referrals: happy contractors know other talented people.
    Example: loyalty pays back
    A contractor you treated well on a difficult assignment later refers two senior engineers and brings a new client contact. The relationship you built during a hard moment produced more placements than a cold search would.

    ⚖️ Compliance on redeployment

    CheckWhy
    Re-check contract model for the new clientDifferent client policies may require W2 or C2C
    Reassess classificationA new role may look more or less like employment
    Reassess IR35 status (UK)Each engagement is assessed on its own facts
    New paperwork: SOW, rate, start dateAvoid working without a signed assignment
    Benefits continuity (W2)Avoid gaps in coverage between assignments
    Non-solicit and conversion termsThe new client may have its own rules
    Example: a status that changed
    A UK contractor was outside IR35 on a fixed project. The next assignment embeds her in a client team with daily direction. The status must be assessed again, and it may now be inside. You tell her early and share the rate options.

    👋 Offboarding and conversions

    • Offboarding: return equipment, remove access, final timesheet and invoice, exit feedback.
    • Conversions: if the client wants to hire the consultant permanently, check the contract's conversion fee and minimum term.
    • Non-solicit clauses: never encourage a consultant to leave for the client's direct payroll in breach of the contract.
    • Feedback: record what went well; it helps the next placement.
    Example: a clean conversion
    After 5 months the client asks to hire a consultant. You check the agreement: conversion allowed after 6 months, or earlier with a fee. You tell the client the options. The consultant is hired at month 6, no fee is disputed, and the client sends you three new reqs.

    Of 10 assignments ending, 6 were extended and 3 of the remaining 4 were redeployed within 14 days. The extension rate is 60% and the redeployment rate is 75%. Only one consultant went to the bench, and that case is the focus of the next review.

    Module 08 · Advanced Practice

    Advanced: Terms, Risk & Judgement

    What separates a good contract recruiter from a trusted staffing partner: reading contract terms, handling hard conversations, protecting cash flow and staying compliant under pressure.

    📝
    This module explains common commercial terms in plain English. It is not legal advice. Have your legal team review actual contracts.

    📑 Contract terms that matter

    TermWhat it meansWatch out for
    Termination noticeHow much warning either side must give to end the assignmentShort notice from the client with long notice owed to the consultant
    Conversion feeFee the client pays if it hires your contractor directlyFee falling to zero too quickly
    Non-solicitRestricts hiring each other's people for a periodBroad wording that blocks normal business
    Pay-when-paidContractor is paid only after the client pays youLong client payment terms can hurt the consultant
    Indemnity and insuranceWho covers losses and what insurance is requiredInsurance limits C2C consultants cannot meet
    IP and confidentialityWho owns the work product and what must stay privateUnclear ownership of code or documents
    Example: a hidden problem
    A contract says the client may end the assignment with one day's notice, while the consultant must give 30 days' notice. The imbalance is unfair and may deter good candidates. You raise it with your legal team before the consultant signs, not after.

    🎭 Hard scenarios

    ScenarioBest response
    Client wants a UK contractor "outside IR35" but directs the work daily at fixed hours on siteShare the facts, say they point inside, suggest the client reviews its determination with specialist advice; do not pressure anyone
    Consultant insists on 1099 but works full time under a manager for one clientFlag misclassification risk to compliance; offer W2 or a properly structured alternative
    Consultant's "company" has no registration or insuranceRequest registration, W-9 and certificate of insurance; pause until verified
    Client wants to hire a contractor before the minimum termCheck conversion and non-solicit terms; discuss options openly with both sides
    Consultant on a work visa is offered C2CAsk legal or immigration counsel what is permitted; treat all candidates alike
    Contract ends in 45 days and no extension is plannedStart redeployment now; tell the consultant honestly
    Example: a calm response under pressure
    A client says, "Just put outside IR35 on the paperwork." You reply, "I can't decide the status. The facts of the role will, and the facts you've given me point inside. If you want, I can help you share the details with your compliance team." Calm, factual, and protective of everyone.

    🪜 Tiers and rate compression

    When a req passes through an MSP and one or more sub-vendors, each layer takes a share. By the time it reaches the consultant, the available rate can be well below what the client pays.

    Read left to right, then continue on the next row.
    LayerTakesRemaining (illustrative)
    Client bill rate-$100.00
    MSP fee (3%)$3.00$97.00
    Prime vendor margin (10%)$9.70$87.30
    Sub-vendor margin (10%)$8.73$78.57
    Example: why the req will not fill
    The client pays $100, yet only $78.57 reaches the sub-vendor's consultant side. If strong candidates want $85, nobody applies. A smart recruiter spots this at intake and either asks for a different route or tells the client the truth about the market rate.

    💼 Financial health

    RiskCauseMitigation
    Cash-flow gapPaying consultants weekly while clients pay at net 45 or 60Working capital, shorter terms, invoice financing
    Bad debtClient does not payCredit checks, clear terms, prompt follow-up
    Margin squeezeTiers and rate capsKnow the floor margin before submitting
    Compliance penaltiesMisclassification or payroll errorsProcess, training and audits
    Example: a margin floor in action
    Your floor is 15% gross margin on W2 and 12% on C2C. A req arrives with a rate cap that leaves only 8% on C2C. You decline politely and explain why, instead of taking an unprofitable contract that tightens cash for the whole business.

    🕵️ Verifying claims

    ClaimHow to verify
    "My company is registered and insured"Registration details, W-9, certificate of insurance, bank account name match
    "I can send a substitute"Is it in the contract and would the client really accept one?
    "Outside IR35 for my last role"Ask for the status determination statement and its reasons
    "I have 8 years of Kubernetes"Check the timeline and ask for project detail
    "I am free to start Monday"Confirm notice period and any other offers
    Example: a polite verification
    A contractor says his last role was outside IR35. You ask, "Could you share the status determination statement?" He sends it with the client's reasons. You note it, but remember the new engagement needs its own assessment.

    Example: diagnosing a month
    Fill rate fell to 20%, and 6 of 10 lost reqs had caps below your floor. You stop chasing low-cap reqs, focus on clients with workable caps and raise your fill rate to 35% the next month, with healthier margins.

    ⚖️ Ethical practice

    • Be transparent about rates, deductions and what the contractor will take home.
    • Never advise someone to misstate their status to save tax.
    • Treat every candidate the same way, including on work authorization.
    • Protect personal data: share only what is needed, and only with consent.
    • Do not submit someone without a valid right to represent.
    Example: the reputation test
    If a contractor read your notes and your emails about them, would you be comfortable? If yes, you are likely doing it right. Reputation spreads quickly in a small contractor community, and trust is the main asset in this business.

    📖 Full case study

    Req: Senior Java developer, 6-month contract, London hybrid, client wants "outside IR35", start in 2 weeks.

    1. Intake: you ask who directs the work, whether a substitute is acceptable, and whether the role is a fixed project or cover for a staff role. The client says the developer will join the team's daily stand-ups and take assigned tickets.
    2. Flag: these facts lean inside. You share them, politely, with the client's compliance contact, who runs its own assessment and issues a status decision with reasons.
    3. Result: the decision is inside IR35. You tell candidates up front and give a rate breakdown for umbrella and agency payroll.
    4. Bench match: two consultants on your bench match the skills and agree to the inside rate. RTR confirmed, you submit within 90 minutes.
    5. Outcome: one starts in 12 days. At day 120 you hold the first extension conversation, and at day 150 the client extends by 6 months.
    ✅
    Why it worked: early honest questions, no pressure on the status, transparent rates, fast and accurate submittals, and a redeployment-minded relationship that led to an extension.
    Example: what you would tell the client
    "The facts you described point inside IR35, so I suggest your compliance team issues the determination. I already have two consultants who accept an inside rate and can start in two weeks, with right to represent confirmed."

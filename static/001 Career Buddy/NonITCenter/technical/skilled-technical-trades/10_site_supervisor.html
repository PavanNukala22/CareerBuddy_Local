    Module 01 · Basic

    What is a Site Supervisor? Role, Responsibilities & Fundamentals

    Learn the complete field role: people, drawings, safety, quality, resources, progress, coordination and documentation.

    Site Supervisor

    A Site Supervisor is the field-level coordinator who turns approved project requirements into safe, organized and measurable execution. The role connects engineering information with crews, subcontractors, materials, equipment, inspections, schedules and reporting.

    Site executionSafetyQualityPlanningCoordinationProgressDrawingsReporting

    🧭 What is Site Supervisor?

    Site supervision is the practical control of field work so the right activity is performed by the right people, in the right sequence, using approved information, resources and controls.

    Translate requirements
    Read drawings, specifications, method statements and work instructions.
    Lead the crew
    Allocate work, clarify expectations, monitor competence and performance.
    Control safety
    Identify hazards, verify controls, coordinate permits and stop unsafe work.
    Control quality
    Verify workmanship, measurements, inspections and acceptance criteria.
    Control resources
    Coordinate labor, materials, tools, equipment and access.
    Report reality
    Record progress, constraints, inspections, manpower and actions.
    💡
    Simple rule: control the field before problems become incidents, defects, delays or rework.

    🌅 Daily Routine

    1. Review previous-day progress, issues, incidents and priorities.
    2. Confirm drawings, methods, permits, work fronts, manpower and equipment.
    3. Brief the crew and confirm task-specific controls.
    4. Allocate work with clear quantities, sequence and interfaces.
    5. Walk work fronts and verify safety, quality, housekeeping and resources.
    6. Coordinate inspections, measurements, hold/witness points and technical queries.
    7. Measure actual progress and update the daily report.
    8. Escalate constraints with owners and due dates.
    9. Close the shift with status, incomplete work and handover information.

    Module 02 · Planning

    Site Planning, Scheduling & Work Front Readiness

    Convert project plans into ready-to-execute field work.

    🗺️ Work Package Planning

    A work package is a manageable group of activities with defined scope, location, resources, sequence and completion criteria.

    ElementSupervisor question
    ScopeExactly what physical work is required?
    LocationWhich area, grid, level or chainage?
    InputsAre drawings, materials, permits and prerequisites ready?
    ResourcesHow many people, tools and machines are required?
    SequenceWhat must happen first and what can run in parallel?
    Quality gatesWhat must be inspected or released?
    CompletionWhat evidence proves the package is complete?

    📅 Scheduling & Dependencies

    Schedules state when work is planned; dependencies determine when work is actually ready.

    Dependency

    Downstream work waits for required prerequisite work or release.

    Constraint

    A condition preventing planned work: missing material, drawing, access, permit, inspection, manpower or equipment.

    FIELD EXAMPLE

    Painting is planned, but surface preparation has not been released. Do not report painting as started; record the constraint and recover the sequence compliantly.

    🚜 Resource Readiness

    People
    Competence, headcount and shift coverage.
    Materials
    Correct type, quantity, identification, storage and release.
    Plant & equipment
    Capacity, status, maintenance and operator competence.
    Tools
    Suitability, condition and calibration where required.
    Access & utilities
    Safe access, lighting, power, water and work-front clearance.
    Information
    Latest drawings, method, risk controls and inspection requirements.

    🚧 Work Front Readiness

    1. Correct location and boundary identified.
    2. Prerequisite work completed and released.
    3. Latest approved information at the point of work.
    4. Materials inspected, identified and stored correctly.
    5. Plant and tools available and fit for purpose.
    6. Access, lighting, barricading and housekeeping adequate.
    7. Permits and task-specific controls active.
    8. Inspection/test points understood by the crew.

    🗓️ Look-Ahead Planning

    HorizonFocus
    DailyExact work fronts, manpower, quantities, permits, inspections and constraints.
    WeeklyUpcoming activities, interfaces, material/equipment needs and recovery actions.
    MilestoneCritical sequence, testing, commissioning and handover readiness.
    ✓
    Ask not only “What are we doing tomorrow?” but also “What could prevent tomorrow's work from starting?”

    Module 03 · Technical Execution

    Construction Drawings, Technical Information & Execution

    Read field information confidently, perform practical checks and know when technical clarification is required.

    📐 Construction Drawings

    Before use, verify drawing number, title, revision/status, units, dimensions, levels, notes, details, sections and references.

    ElementField significance
    Revision blockConfirms current information and identifies changes.
    DimensionsControls size, location, spacing and geometry.
    LevelsControls vertical position and interfaces.
    Sections/detailsReveal information not visible in the main plan.
    NotesMay define materials, tolerances and workmanship.
    ReferencesConnect related sheets, schedules and specifications.
    ⚠️
    Never invent an unclear detail from memory. Raise an RFI/technical query and keep the response controlled.

    🖥️ AutoCAD

    AutoCAD is a supporting execution tool for reading, checking and communicating drawing information. Useful supervisor activities include layer awareness, measuring geometry, reviewing details, comparing revisions and preparing controlled markups.

    Layer awareness
    Identify disciplines and relevant proposed/existing elements.
    Measure
    Use drawing geometry and dimensions to understand field requirements.
    Revision review
    Recognize changed locations and details.
    Field markup
    Communicate locations and interfaces clearly while following document-control rules.

    🧮 Measurements & Calculations

    Supervisors may verify dimensions, quantities, levels, slopes, areas, volumes and productivity. Always confirm units and acceptance criteria.

    SLOPE

    A 120 mm fall over 20 m gives 120 ÷ 20 = 0.006, or 0.6%.

    VOLUME

    A 5 m × 4 m × 0.15 m pad has a geometric volume of 3.0 m³, subject to the project's measurement rules.

    🧰 Method Statements

    A method statement should turn approved requirements into an executable sequence with resources, controls, inspections and completion criteria.

    1. Confirm scope and prerequisites.
    2. Prepare the area and resources.
    3. Brief the crew on sequence and hazards.
    4. Execute in defined stages.
    5. Inspect/test at planned control points.
    6. Correct issues before the next controlled stage.
    7. Housekeep and hand over.

    ❓ RFI & Change Control

    A strong RFI identifies the exact drawing/detail, location, field condition, impact, evidence and requested clarification. When a change is approved, update controlled information and brief affected teams before execution.

    Module 04 · Safety & Quality

    Safety Leadership, Quality Control & Problem Prevention

    Make safe and right-first-time execution part of everyday supervision.

    🦺 Safety Leadership

    Safety leadership means identifying hazards, verifying controls, listening to workers, stopping unsafe work and planning production around safe execution.

    Pre-task briefing
    Task steps, hazards, controls, interfaces and emergency response.
    Field observation
    Check whether actual behavior matches planned controls.
    Stop-work
    Pause work when an immediate uncontrolled risk exists.
    Learning
    Use observations, near misses and incidents to improve controls.

    ⚠️ Risk Assessment

    1Identify
    2Assess
    3Control
    4Brief
    5Verify
    6Review

    Use the hierarchy of controls where applicable: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrative controls, then PPE.

    🔐 Permits & High-Risk Work

    WorkKey supervisor checks
    Hot workPermit, ignition controls, fire watch and gas testing where required.
    Confined spaceEntry authorization, atmosphere testing, ventilation, communication and rescue.
    Working at heightSafe access, fall protection and rescue arrangements.
    LiftingLift plan, capacity/status, competent personnel, exclusion zone and communication.
    ElectricalIsolation/authorization, competent persons and verification of controls.
    ⛔
    A permit is not just paperwork. Verify the physical controls described by the permit.

    🔍 Quality Control

    Quality control is built into execution through inspection, measurement, verification and documentation.

    ControlExample
    IncomingVerify identification, quantity, condition and required certificates/status.
    Pre-startConfirm prerequisites, dimensions, surfaces and setting-out.
    In-processCheck critical steps before they become inaccessible.
    Hold pointDo not proceed until the required release occurs.
    FinalConfirm acceptance criteria and records are complete.

    🛠️ NCR, RCA & CAPA

    StepAction
    IdentifyState requirement, actual condition and evidence.
    ContainTag, segregate, stop or otherwise prevent uncontrolled use.
    DispositionFollow approved rework, repair, replacement or use-as-is process.
    VerifyInspect/test corrected work and record result.
    LearnFor recurrence, identify root cause and implement corrective action.
    ✓
    Correction fixes the immediate condition; corrective action addresses the cause to reduce recurrence.

    Module 05 · People & Resources

    Team Supervision, Coordination & Resource Control

    Coordinate crews, subcontractors, tools, equipment and interfaces without losing control.

    👥 Team Supervision

    Set the standard, check understanding, observe execution and give timely feedback. Effective supervisors coach instead of waiting for failure.

    Effective

    • Clear instructions
    • Checks competence
    • Delegates with accountability
    • Corrects early
    • Recognizes good work

    Weak

    • Assumes understanding
    • Waits for failures
    • Blames individuals
    • Reports late
    • Allows informal workarounds

    🧑‍🔧 Work Allocation

    Allocate using competence + priority + location + workload + equipment availability.

    FactorQuestion
    CompetenceIs the person trained/authorized?
    PriorityDoes it affect a milestone or critical sequence?
    LocationCan the team work efficiently?
    WorkloadIs the shift allocation realistic?
    ResourcesAre required tools, plant and materials ready?

    🔗 Cross-Team Coordination

    Interfaces between civil, structural, mechanical, electrical, finishing and commissioning teams are common sources of delay and rework. Make prerequisites, responsibilities and handoffs explicit.

    Example

    Mechanical installation may require civil completion, survey acceptance, anchor-bolt release, clear access and lifting arrangements. Coordinate these before mobilizing the crew.

    🧰 Materials, Tools & Equipment

    Materials
    Specification, identification, quantity, storage and release.
    Tools
    Suitability, condition, safe use and calibration where needed.
    Equipment
    Capacity, inspection, maintenance and operator authorization.
    Maintenance
    Planned servicing reduces breakdowns and unsafe conditions.

    🔧 Troubleshooting & Maintenance

    1. Make the situation safe and isolate energy where required.
    2. Define the symptom and when it started.
    3. Check obvious causes: power, settings, connections, consumables and environment.
    4. Use procedures/manuals and competent technicians.
    5. Repair, test and verify before return to service.
    6. Record recurring faults and schedule preventive action.

    Module 06 · Data & Reporting

    Progress Monitoring, Reporting, Excel/ERP & KPIs

    Turn field observations into reliable data for decisions and recovery.

    📈 Progress Measurement

    Measure physical accomplishment, not effort alone. Define completion before comparing actual with plan.

    Planned quantityTarget output for the period
    Actual quantityVerified physical output
    VarianceActual − planned under the project convention
    Completion %Actual ÷ planned × 100 when comparable
    EXAMPLE

    Plan 200 m; actual 180 m → 90% completion.

    📝 Daily Site Reporting

    A strong report answers: What happened? Where? How much? Who? What stopped us? What is next?

    BlockTypical content
    WorkActivities, locations, quantities, status
    ManpowerCrews, subcontractors, shifts
    EquipmentMajor plant, utilization, breakdowns
    MaterialsReceipts, shortages, status
    SafetyToolbox, observations, incidents, permits
    QualityInspections, tests, NCRs, rework
    ConstraintsCause, impact, owner, due date

    📊 Excel & ERP

    Useful fields include Activity ID, location, planned/actual dates, planned/actual quantities, responsible person, status, constraint, action owner and due date. Consistent naming is essential for reliable analysis.

    📌 KPI Dashboard

    KPIPurpose
    Schedule varianceShows ahead/behind plan.
    ProductivityRelates output to labor/equipment input.
    Rework/NCRShows quality losses.
    Safety indicatorsCombines proactive controls and outcomes.
    Inspection backlogShows release bottlenecks.
    Equipment downtimeShows lost capacity and maintenance needs.
    Constraint ageingShows unresolved future risk.

    🔎 Trend Analysis & Escalation

    Watch for increasing rework, falling productivity, growing inspection backlog, repeated shortages and ageing constraints.

    Decision-ready escalation

    Fact: work is 8% behind plan for two periods. Cause: shared access. Impact: downstream testing may slip. Recommendation: approve a coordinated access window and resequence ready work.

    Module 07 · Advanced Leadership

    Advanced Supervision, Problem-Solving & Expert Leadership

    Move from reacting to issues toward predicting, preventing and improving site execution.

    🧠 Advanced Problem-Solving

    Use: define → contain → collect evidence → identify causes → choose action → implement → verify effectiveness.

    5 Why
    Explore causes beyond the visible symptom.
    Fishbone / 6M
    Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, Environment.
    Pareto
    Focus on the vital few causes with the largest impact.
    Trend analysis
    Detect deterioration or improvement over time.

    🛠️ RCA & CAPA

    LevelQuestionExample
    SymptomWhat happened?Repeated dimensional rework.
    Immediate causeWhat directly produced it?Incorrect setting-out reference.
    System causeWhy was the error possible?No independent verification.
    Corrective actionWhat changes the process?Add controlled verification.
    EffectivenessDid it work?Monitor subsequent work.

    🏗️ Design Review & Constructability

    Advanced supervisors contribute field knowledge to design review: access, sequence, tolerances, installation space, lifting, inspection access, interfaces, temporary works and maintainability.

    🔍
    Can this be safely and practically built as designed, inspected as required and maintained afterward?

    🎯 Risk-Based Supervision

    Prioritize attention for high-consequence or high-uncertainty work: critical lifts, hidden work, complex interfaces, first-time activities, tight tolerances and milestone-critical tasks.

    🏆 Expert Leadership

    1. Set clear standards and explain why they matter.
    2. Delegate responsibility with boundaries and escalation triggers.
    3. Coach at the point of work.
    4. Use evidence instead of blame.
    5. Protect safety and quality under schedule pressure.
    6. Make risks and decisions visible.
    7. Turn lessons into better methods and controls.

    Module 08 · Practice

    Practice: Run a Site Work Package

    Apply planning, drawings, safety, resources, quality, coordination and reporting in one realistic scenario.

    🏗️ Practice Scenario

    Mechanical equipment installation

    The team must install equipment on a prepared foundation. The latest drawing is available, but one interface detail is awaiting an RFI response. The foundation survey is complete, anchor-bolt inspection is not released, lifting equipment and riggers are available, and another contractor has stored material inside the planned lifting exclusion zone.

    1️⃣ Readiness Task

    ✍️ List the conditions you would verify before allowing the lift and installation to start.
    Model answer: Current approved information and resolved RFI; released anchor-bolt inspection; approved lift plan; competent operator/riggers; equipment capacity/status; clear exclusion zone; suitable access/ground; permit/controls; toolbox briefing; communication method; inspected lifting accessories; agreed sequence.

    2️⃣ Coordination Task

    ✍️ How would you handle the material stored in the lifting zone?
    Model answer: Do not set up the lift in the obstructed zone. Coordinate removal/relocation with the responsible contractor, re-check the exclusion area, brief the crew and record the constraint if it affects the schedule.

    3️⃣ Practice MCQ

    Q. Anchor-bolt inspection is not released but the crew is ready. What should you do?
    • A Install anyway
    • B Hold the affected activity, coordinate inspection/release and resequence ready work if possible
    • C Sign the inspection yourself
    • D Remove the inspection requirement

    🎤 Interview Preparation

    How do you start a new work activity?
    Confirm scope, latest information, prerequisites, method/risk controls, permits, resources, competence, work-front readiness, inspection points and crew briefing.
    How do you handle schedule pressure?
    Identify the cause, protect safety/quality controls, find compliant recovery options and escalate early with evidence.
    What if the drawing is unclear?
    Do not guess. Raise an RFI/technical query, control the affected work and communicate the written response.
    How do you manage subcontractors?
    Set clear scope and standards, coordinate interfaces, verify controls, monitor work objectively and escalate through agreed processes.
    What makes a Site Supervisor effective?
    Planning discipline, technical understanding, safety leadership, quality awareness, communication, resource control and early problem-solving.

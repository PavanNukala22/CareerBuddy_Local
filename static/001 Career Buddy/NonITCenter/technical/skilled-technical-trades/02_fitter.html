    Fitter Fundamentals & the 10 Roles

    Start from zero: where the Fitter department sits in Skilled & Technical Trades, what its ten roles do, which skills each role needs, and the words every workshop conversation uses.

    🧭 What is the Fitter department?

    Skilled & Technical Trades is a skill group made up of 10 departments. Fitter is one of them. A fitter builds, assembles, installs, aligns, maintains and repairs mechanical equipment: pumps, gearboxes, fans, conveyors, compressors and machine tools. The department has 10 roles, from Engineer to Analyst. This course teaches what each role does, which skills it needs, and how the roles work together safely.

    Engineers choose fits, loads and fastening, and draw assemblies.
    🔧
    Assemble and repair
    Technicians build, align, overhaul and repair equipment.
    📏
    Measure and check
    Inspectors and quality engineers prove the work is within limits.
    📅
    Plan and manage
    Supervisors, coordinators, planners and analysts keep jobs on track.
    EXAMPLE · One shaft, four people

    An Engineer decides the shaft fit and coupling. A Technician fits the bearing and aligns the coupling. An Inspector measures runout and checks torque marks. An Analyst later reports how often that machine stopped. One shaft, four different roles.

    📖 Key terms (what is what)

    ShaftRotating bar that carries power, for example from motor to pump.
    BearingPart that supports a shaft and lets it turn with low friction.
    CouplingJoins two shafts so one drives the other.
    AlignmentMaking two shaft centrelines meet in line within a tolerance.
    Soft footMachine foot that does not sit flat, so tightening it distorts the machine.
    ShimThin metal plate used to adjust height or position.
    TorqueTurning force on a bolt or shaft, measured in N·m.
    ToleranceAllowed variation from a nominal size, for example 25.000 ± 0.02 mm.
    RunoutHow much a rotating surface wobbles, measured with a dial gauge.
    Gasket / sealParts that stop leaks between surfaces or around a shaft.
    PMPreventive maintenance: scheduled work done before failure.
    LOTOLockout/Tagout: lock and tag all energy sources before work.
    EXAMPLE · Reading a work order

    "Isolate P-101, LOTO motor and suction/discharge valves, change DE bearing, check soft foot, realign to 0.05 mm, torque coupling bolts to 60 N·m, run and record vibration."
    Decoded: lock off every energy source, replace the drive-end bearing, check the machine sits flat, realign the shafts, tighten bolts to a set torque, run and measure vibration.

    Mechanical Theory, Drawings & Standards

    Know what you are working with: components, torque and power, fasteners, limits and fits, materials, lubrication, mechanical drawings, standards and documents.

    "I changed the drive-end bearing, checked the key, refitted the coupling and realigned the set." Every part named: bearing, key, coupling, alignment. If you can explain each one, you can follow workshop conversations.

    🔄 Force, torque and power

    Torque is a turning effect: force × distance from the centre (N·m). Power is how fast work is done: P = torque × angular speed. A drive that must deliver power at a given speed needs a certain torque.

    QuantityFormulaUnit
    Torque from a forceT = F × rNewton-metre (N·m)
    Angular speedω = 2π × rpm ÷ 60Radians per second
    PowerP = T × ωWatt (W)
    Torque from powerT = P ÷ ωN·m
    EXAMPLE · Torque of a pump drive

    A 45 kW pump runs at 1,480 rpm. ω = 2π × 1,480 ÷ 60 ≈ 155 rad/s. T = 45,000 ÷ 155 ≈ 290 N·m. The coupling must be rated above that, with a service factor.

    🔩 Fasteners and torque

    A bolt works like a spring: tightening stretches it, and that tension (preload) clamps the joint. Too little and it loosens; too much and it stretches or breaks.

    TopicWhat to knowExample
    Bolt gradeNumber shows strength; higher is stronger (8.8, 10.9, 12.9).Replacing a 10.9 bolt with an 8.8 weakens the joint.
    Torque valueSet by size, grade and lubrication; follow the specification.M16 grade 8.8 flange bolts at the specified N·m.
    Tightening sequenceTighten in a cross pattern, in steps (for example 30%, 60%, 100%).Flange tightened across the diagonals.
    LubricationOiled or dry threads change torque for the same bolt tension.Use the torque value for the condition stated.
    ReuseStretched or damaged bolts and locking nuts are replaced.New nylon-insert nuts on a guard bracket.
    ⚠️
    A torque wrench only works if it is calibrated and set correctly. A wrong setting or an old calibration makes every bolt wrong.
    EXAMPLE · Why the cross pattern

    Tightening a flange bolt by bolt in a circle pulls one side down first and leaves the gasket uneven. Cross-pattern tightening in steps squeezes the gasket evenly, so the flange does not leak.

    📏 Limits, fits and tolerances

    No part is made exactly to size. A tolerance is the allowed variation. A fit describes how a shaft sits in a hole: loose, snug or tight.

    Fit typeWhat happensTypical use
    ClearanceShaft is always smaller than the hole; it turns or slides.Shaft in a plain bearing.
    TransitionCould be a small clearance or a small interference.Locating pins, gear on shaft with key.
    InterferenceShaft is larger than the hole; it must be pressed or heated into place.Bearing inner ring on a shaft.
    EXAMPLE · Reading a fit

    A hole is Ø25 H7 (25.000 to 25.021 mm). A shaft is Ø25 g6 (24.980 to 24.993 mm). The shaft is always smaller than the hole: smallest clearance = 25.000 − 24.993 = 0.007 mm, largest = 25.021 − 24.980 = 0.041 mm. A clearance fit.

    🛢️ Materials and lubrication

    TopicWhat to knowExample
    SteelStrong and tough; shafts, bolts, gears.Medium-carbon steel shaft.
    Cast ironGood for housings; brittle under shock.Pump casing.
    Bronze / brassLow friction; bushes and wear rings.Bush in a slow-moving joint.
    GreaseThick lubricant that stays in place.Rolling bearings.
    OilFlows, cools and carries debris away.Gearbox sump.
    💡
    Right lubricant, right amount, right interval. Too much grease in a bearing overheats it just like too little.
    EXAMPLE · The wrong grease

    Two incompatible greases are mixed in one bearing. The thickeners react, the grease softens and runs out, and the bearing runs hot. A lubrication schedule that names one grease per point prevents it.

    Drawings show what the part or assembly looks like and how big it is. Specifications say what material, finish, standard and test apply. You need both.

    Precision Measurement, Alignment & Mechanical Safety

    The skills every one of the ten roles shares: measuring accurately, reading instruments, calibration, shaft alignment, safe isolation of all energy sources and lifting basics.

    📏 Measuring instruments

    Precision measurement means measuring small sizes and movements accurately and repeatably. Choose an instrument whose resolution is about one tenth of the tolerance you must check.

    InstrumentMeasuresTypical resolution
    Steel ruleLength1 mm
    Vernier / digital caliperOutside, inside, depth0.02 to 0.01 mm
    MicrometerOutside diameter or thickness0.01 mm (0.001 mm digital)
    Dial gauge / dial indicatorRunout, movement, alignment offsets0.01 mm
    Feeler gaugeGaps such as soft foot and coupling gap0.02 mm steps
    Bore gaugeInternal diameter0.01 mm
    Straightedge and levelFlatness and levelnessDepends on the tool
    Torque wrenchBolt tightening torqueSet to N·m
    Laser alignment toolShaft alignment offset and angle0.001 mm
    EXAMPLE · Choosing the tool

    A bearing seat must be within ±0.01 mm. A vernier caliper reads only to 0.02 mm, so it cannot judge it. Use a micrometer or bore gauge with 0.01 mm resolution or better.

    🔍 Reading a micrometer

    On a metric micrometer the sleeve shows whole and half millimetres, and the thimble shows hundredths.

    StepWhat you readExample
    1Whole millimetres on the sleeve12 mm
    2Half-millimetre line visible?+ 0.50 mm
    3Thimble division on the datum line+ 0.37 mm
    TotalAdd them12 + 0.50 + 0.37 = 12.87 mm
    💡
    Before each use: clean the faces, close them gently with the ratchet and check the zero. A 0.01 mm zero error shifts every reading.
    EXAMPLE · A zero error

    The micrometer reads +0.02 mm with the faces closed. A shaft reads 24.99 mm. The true size is 24.99 − 0.02 = 24.97 mm, which may be outside tolerance.

    🎯 Good measuring habits and calibration

    Do the same routine every time.
    HabitWhy it matters
    Let parts and tools reach the same temperatureMetal expands; a warm shaft reads larger
    Measure in the specified places and directionsA shaft can be oval or tapered
    Hold tools square to the partTilting causes cosine error and false readings
    Repeat at least twiceA single reading may be a slip
    Record the value, not just “OK”The number proves the result and shows trends
    Use only tools in calibration dateAn uncalibrated tool may hide a failure
    ⚠️
    If an instrument is found out of calibration, stop using it, tag it, and review measurements taken since the last good calibration.
    EXAMPLE · A missed due date

    A dial gauge is a month past its calibration date after 20 shaft readings. The quality engineer lists those readings, rechecks the shafts with a calibrated gauge, and accepts only those that agree.

    🔄 Shaft alignment

    Two coupled shafts must meet along one centreline. Misalignment overloads bearings and seals and is a top cause of early failure.

    MisalignmentWhat it isHow it shows
    Parallel (offset)Centrelines are side by side but parallel.Vibration at 2× running speed; hot coupling
    AngularCentrelines meet at an angle.Axial vibration; bearing and seal wear
    CombinedBoth at once, the usual case.Mix of the above
    Always check soft foot first.
    EXAMPLE · Why soft foot first

    If one foot does not sit flat, tightening it bends the machine and changes the alignment you just set. Fix soft foot first with shims (feeler gauge shows the gap), then align. Typical offset limits for a 1,500 rpm drive are a few hundredths of a millimetre; always use the equipment maker’s limits.

    🔒 Safe isolation and mechanical safety

    Mechanical equipment stores energy in more than one way. Safe isolation removes all energy sources and proves it.

    Energy typeExampleHow to make safe
    ElectricalMotor supplyIsolate, lock, tag, prove dead
    Mechanical / storedSpring, flywheel, raised loadRelease, block or support it
    Hydraulic / pneumaticPressurised lineClose valves, vent and drain, lock
    Process fluidPump suction and dischargeClose and lock valves, drain, vent
    GravitySuspended componentSupport with stands or blocks
    Each worker applies their own lock.
    📝
    Common injuries: pinch and crush points, entanglement in rotating parts, struck-by loads, hot surfaces and oil injection. Guards stay on; loose clothing and jewellery stay off.
    EXAMPLE · The hidden energy

    A fitter locks out the motor but not the discharge valve. When the casing is opened, the pipe pressure sprays out. Isolating every energy source, not just the electrical one, would have prevented the injury.

    🏗️ Lifting basics

    TopicWhat to knowExample
    WLL / SWLMaximum load a sling or hoist is rated to lift.A 2-tonne sling must not lift 3 tonnes.
    Sling angleWider angles increase tension in each leg.Two legs at 120° carry far more than at 60°.
    Centre of gravityHook goes above it so the load stays level.Motor-pump set lifted with the hook over the balance point.
    Lifting planA written plan for any complex lift.Plan names crane, slings, signaller and exclusion zone.
    Rigger and signallerTrained people direct and attach loads.Fitters do not lift without a competent rigger.
    ⚠️
    Never walk under a suspended load. Only trained, authorised people rig loads and give crane signals.
    EXAMPLE · Why the angle matters

    A 2-tonne load on two slings at 60° (included angle) puts about 1.15 tonnes in each leg. At 120° each leg carries about 2 tonnes, so a sling that was fine at 60° may be overloaded at 120°.

    Design & Delivery Roles

    Engineer, Senior Engineer and Project Engineer: what each owns, what every required skill means with an example, and what a day on the job looks like.

    📐 Fitter Engineer: what they do

    The Fitter Engineer designs and engineers mechanical assemblies and installations. They choose fits and tolerances, calculate loads, torque and power, define fastening and alignment requirements, and document assembly methods so fitters can build the same way every time. In this skill group the title normally means an engineer-level role close to the fitting trade, usually a diploma or degree holder focused on design and engineering support.

    Required skills

    engineeringtechnical designcalculationsproblem-solvingtechnical documentationmechanical drawingsequipment maintenanceprecision measurement
    📍
    Where they sit: Sits in the design office with regular workshop and site visits. Reports to a Senior Engineer or engineering manager, and hands the design to the Project Engineer and the fitting crews.
    EXAMPLE · Turning a need into a design

    Need: "Install a 45 kW pump on a new base." The Engineer picks the coupling size, checks shaft power and torque, defines the base plate and shim arrangement, sets the alignment tolerance and issues an assembly procedure. The crew can build without guessing.

    🧠 Fitter Senior Engineer: what they do

    The Fitter Senior Engineer is the mechanical authority. They carry out failure and vibration analysis, review other people’s designs, solve faults others could not, and guide junior engineers. Seniority here means judgement and accountability, not only years served.

    Required skills

    advanced technical analysisdesign reviewtroubleshootingleadershipreportingmechanical drawingsequipment maintenanceprecision measurement
    📍
    Where they sit: Reports to the engineering manager. Reviews the Engineer’s work, advises the Project Engineer on site, and is called in when equipment keeps failing.
    EXAMPLE · The called-in expert

    A pump set destroys a coupling every three months. Fitters replaced it three times. The Senior Engineer finds a soft foot and pipe strain pulling the casing out of line. The fix is base correction and pipe supports, not another coupling.

    🧭 Fundamentals

    What is the Fitter department, and where does it sit?
    It is one of ten departments in the Skilled & Technical Trades group. It has ten roles (Engineer, Senior Engineer, Project Engineer, Supervisor, Coordinator, Planner, Quality Engineer, Inspector, Technician, Analyst) and all share three skills: mechanical drawings, equipment maintenance and precision measurement.
    Explain torque and power with an example.
    Torque is force × distance (N·m). Power is torque × angular speed. A 45 kW drive at 1,480 rpm has ω ≈ 155 rad/s, so torque ≈ 45,000 ÷ 155 ≈ 290 N·m.
    What is a clearance fit versus an interference fit?
    Clearance: the shaft is always smaller than the hole, so it turns or slides. Interference: the shaft is larger, so it is pressed or heated into place and held by friction, for example a bearing inner ring on a shaft.
    Why must bolts be tightened to a specified torque in a set pattern?
    Torque sets bolt preload, which clamps the joint. Too little and it loosens; too much and it stretches. A cross pattern in steps squeezes gaskets evenly so flanges do not leak.
    What is soft foot and how do you find it?
    A machine foot that does not sit flat, so tightening it distorts the casing and spoils alignment. Loosen one bolt at a time and measure the lift with a dial gauge, or measure the gap with feeler gauges. Fix it with shims before aligning.
    Describe safe isolation on mechanical equipment.
    Identify every energy source (electrical, process fluid, stored, gravity), stop and isolate, lock and tag, release stored energy, prove zero energy, then work. Each worker applies their own lock.
    What is the difference between a drawing and a specification?
    A drawing shows layout, sizes and tolerances. A specification states material, finish, standard and tests. You need both to build and check work correctly.
    What are the three skills in all ten roles, and why?
    Mechanical drawings, equipment maintenance and precision measurement. Drawings say what to build, maintenance keeps it working, and precise measurement proves it is right.

    📐 Engineering roles

    Engineer · Senior Engineer · Project Engineer

    How do you choose a coupling for a drive?
    Find torque from power and speed, apply a service factor for the load type, check bore sizes, speed rating and misalignment capacity, then pick the size above the required torque.
    What do you check when a bearing keeps spinning on its shaft?
    Shaft and housing sizes against the drawing, the fit class, wear on the seat, and the retaining method. Specify a tighter fit or a locking method and repair the seat.
    Which analyses have you used to find the cause of repeated failures?
    Vibration spectrum analysis (1× unbalance, 2× misalignment), visual failure analysis of the broken part, load and fit checks, and review of lubrication and installation history.
    What does a design review catch?
    Errors on paper: missing thermal-growth allowance, no jacking bolts for alignment, wrong fits, unreachable lubrication points, inconsistent drawings. Catching them before manufacture is cheap.
    How do you manage a late vendor delivery on a project?
    Check whether it is on the critical path, ask for part delivery, resequence work that does not need it, consider extra crews, update the schedule and tell the client with a recovery plan.
    What do you need before handing over a pump installation?
    As-built drawings, alignment and torque records, calibration records for instruments used, manuals, lubrication schedule, spare-parts list, and a signed commissioning report.

    👷 Site, planning and coordination roles

    Supervisor · Coordinator · Planner

    How do you allocate work in your crew?
    Match each task to the person’s skill and authorisation: experienced fitters on bearing fits and alignment, apprentices on cleaning and preparation with supervision, and riggers for lifts. Check the plan again at midday.
    What do you do if someone skips a lock?
    Stop the work at once, make sure the equipment is properly isolated and proven at zero energy, remind the crew of the rule, record it as a safety observation and report it.
    How do you stop crews building from old drawings?
    Keep a drawing register, issue revision notices, remove superseded copies from site, and confirm with the supervisor that the crew uses the current revision.
    How do you resolve a clash between two trades over a crane?
    Bring both teams together, check priorities and the critical path, agree a new slot, record it and update the look-ahead.
    How do you find the critical path?
    Link activities by dependency and find the longest chain from start to finish. Any delay on that chain delays the finish.
    What do you do when actual progress is behind plan?
    Find where the delay is, test recovery options (more people, longer shifts, resequencing), show the effect on the finish date and agree the action.

    ✅ Quality and inspection roles

    Quality Engineer · Inspector

    Walk me through an RCA you led.
    Describe the defect, collect data, ask why repeatedly or use a fishbone, find the root cause with evidence, then agree corrective and preventive actions and check they worked.
    What goes into an inspection and test plan?
    Activities in order, hold and witness points, acceptance criteria, references, responsibilities and the record forms.
    How do you check a pump set against its drawing?
    Confirm drawing revision, check part numbers, bolt grades and torque marks, guard, shim condition, coupling gap, then alignment and runout readings against limits, and record the values.
    Why must instruments be calibrated?
    An uncalibrated instrument may read wrongly, so a pass could hide a fail. Calibration certificates are part of the test record.
    What is the difference between RCA and CAPA?
    RCA finds why a defect happened. CAPA corrects it (corrective action) and prevents it happening again (preventive action).
    When did you reject work and how did you handle it?
    State the standard, show the reading, hold the work calmly, raise an NCR, agree the fix and re-inspect before release.

    🔧 Technician and analyst roles

    Technician · Analyst

    A pump suddenly vibrates strongly. What do you do?
    Stop it safely, isolate and prove zero energy, then inspect base bolts, soft foot, coupling and alignment, bearings and impeller, fix the cause and restart under permit.
    What preventive maintenance have you carried out?
    Greasing and oil changes, belt tension, alignment checks, bolt checks, vibration and temperature readings, with schedule and records.
    How do you measure shaft runout?
    Mount a dial gauge on a rigid base with the tip on the shaft surface, turn the shaft slowly by hand and read the total swing. Compare with the limit.
    Which KPIs have you tracked and why do they matter?
    MTBF shows reliability, MTTR shows how fast repairs are, PM compliance shows if scheduled work is done. Together they show where to improve.
    How do you check data quality?
    Look for missing values, duplicates, inconsistent equipment tags and mismatched dates, and check against source records.
    Preventive versus corrective maintenance?
    Preventive is scheduled work done before failure (greasing, alignment checks). Corrective is repair after something has failed. Good plants shift effort from the second to the first.

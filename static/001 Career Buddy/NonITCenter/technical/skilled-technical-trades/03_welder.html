    Welder Fundamentals & the 10 Roles

    Start from zero: where the Welder department sits in Skilled & Technical Trades, what its ten roles do, which skills each role needs, and the words every welding conversation uses.

    🧭 What is the Welder department?

    Skilled & Technical Trades is a skill group made up of 10 departments. Welder is one of them. Welders join metal using heat, pressure or both: structural frames, pipework, tanks, vessels, machine parts and repairs. The department has 10 roles, from Engineer to Analyst. This course teaches what each role does, which skills it needs, and how the roles work together safely.

    Inspectors and quality engineers prove welds are sound.

    Supervisors, coordinators, planners and analysts keep jobs on track.

    📖 Key terms (what is what)

    WeldA joint made by melting and fusing metals, usually with added filler.
    Filler metalExtra metal added to the joint, such as an electrode or wire.
    Heat-affected zone (HAZ)Base metal next to the weld that was heated but not melted.
    PreheatHeating the metal before welding to slow cooling.
    Interpass temperatureMetal temperature between weld passes, kept within a limit.
    Root passFirst weld bead in a joint, made at the root.
    WPSWelding procedure specification: written instructions for welding a joint.
    PQRProcedure qualification record: the test that proves a procedure works.
    Welder qualificationTest and certificate showing a welder can weld to a procedure.
    NDTNon-destructive testing: finding defects without damaging the weld.
    DistortionUnwanted movement or bending caused by weld shrinkage.
    Hot workWelding, cutting or grinding that makes sparks or heat.
    EXAMPLE · Reading a welding instruction

    "Weld joint J-14 to WPS-03: preheat 100 °C, E7018 baked electrodes, max interpass 250 °C, inspect root, then fill and cap, MT after 24 hours."
    Decoded: use the approved procedure, warm the steel first, use dry low-hydrogen electrodes, keep it from overheating between passes, check the root, test for cracks after waiting.

    Welding Science, Procedures & Codes

    Know what you are working with: processes, joints and positions, weld symbols, heat and metallurgy, filler metals, procedures and qualification, codes and documents.

    🔥 Welding processes

    A welding process is the method used to make the arc and add filler. Each suits different materials, thicknesses and places.

    ProcessHow it worksTypical use
    MMA / SMAW (stick)Flux-coated consumable electrode; flux makes shielding gas and slag.Site repairs, structural steel, outdoors
    MIG / MAG (GMAW)Continuous wire fed through a gun with shielding gas.Fabrication shops, thin to medium steel
    TIG (GTAW)Non-consumable tungsten electrode with argon shielding; filler added by hand.Thin material, stainless, pipe roots
    Flux-cored (FCAW)Tubular wire filled with flux, with or without gas.Thick structural steel, high deposition
    Submerged arc (SAW)Arc buried under granular flux, usually automatic.Long straight welds on thick plate
    Oxy-fuel cuttingPlasma cuttingSpot weldingBrazingOrbital weldingLaser welding
    EXAMPLE · Choosing a process

    A 3 mm stainless tube needs a clean, precise weld: TIG. A 25 mm structural beam outdoors needs high deposition and wind tolerance: flux-cored. A quick site repair with no gas bottle: stick.

    🧩 Joints and positions

    JointWhat it isTypical weld
    ButtTwo plates edge to edge in the same plane.Groove weld
    T-jointOne plate at 90° to another.Fillet or groove weld
    LapTwo overlapping plates.Fillet weld
    CornerTwo plates meeting at the corner of a box.Fillet or groove weld
    EdgePlates side by side, welding the edges.Thin sheet work
    PositionMeaning
    1G / 1FFlat
    2G / 2FHorizontal
    3G / 3FVertical
    4G / 4FOverhead
    5GPipe fixed horizontal, welded all round
    6GPipe fixed at 45°, the hardest test position
    EXAMPLE · Why position matters

    A welder qualified in 1G (flat) cannot automatically weld overhead. The molten pool behaves differently against gravity, so qualification covers the position tested and those it allows.

    ✏️ Weld symbols and weld maps

    Drawings use weld symbols instead of words. Each weld on a weld map gets a number so it can be tracked from welding to NDT.

    Part of the symbolWhat it tells you
    Reference lineThe horizontal line the symbol sits on.
    ArrowPoints to the joint; the arrow side is the side it touches.
    TriangleFillet weld; below the line means arrow side, above means the other side (AWS style).
    Number beside the triangleLeg size of the fillet, for example 8 mm.
    Circle at the bendWeld all round.
    FlagField weld (made on site).
    TailReference to the procedure or note.
    💡
    Always check the revision number of the drawing and weld map. A weld size that changed in a new revision is easy to miss.
    EXAMPLE · Reading a symbol

    A triangle below the line with 8 to its left and a tail reading WPS-03: an 8 mm fillet on the arrow side, made to procedure WPS-03. A circle at the bend would add "all round".

    🌡️ Heat, metallurgy and preheat

    Welding heats metal to melting and then cools it quickly. How fast it cools decides how hard and tough the weld and the heat-affected zone (HAZ) become.

    TopicWhat to knowFormula or example
    Arc energy (heat input)More heat input means slower cooling and wider HAZ.kJ/mm = V × I × 60 ÷ (1000 × travel speed in mm/min)
    Carbon equivalent (CE)Higher CE means steel hardens more easily and cracks more easily.CE = C + Mn/6 + (Cr+Mo+V)/5 + (Ni+Cu)/15
    PreheatWarming before welding slows cooling and lets hydrogen escape.Thicker plate or higher CE needs more preheat
    Interpass temperatureLimits the temperature between passes to protect toughness.A stated maximum, such as 250 °C
    Hydrogen crackingNeeds hydrogen, a hard microstructure and stress together.Damp electrodes + fast cooling + restraint
    EXAMPLE · Worked numbers

    Heat input: 24 V, 200 A, 250 mm/min gives 24 × 200 × 60 ÷ (1000 × 250) = 1.15 kJ/mm. Carbon equivalent: C 0.20, Mn 1.20, Cr 0.10, Mo 0.05, V 0.02, Ni 0.10, Cu 0.15 gives 0.20 + 0.20 + 0.034 + 0.017 ≈ 0.45. A CE near 0.45 on thick plate usually calls for preheat.

    🧪 Filler metals and shielding gases

    ItemWhat it isExample
    E7018 electrodeLow-hydrogen stick electrode for strong structural steel welds.Baked and kept in a heated oven until used
    ER70S-6 wireSolid wire for MIG/MAG on carbon steel.Used with argon/CO₂ mix
    ArgonInert gas; does not react with the weld.TIG on stainless, MIG on aluminium
    CO₂ or argon/CO₂ mixActive gas used with MAG on steel.Fabrication of mild steel frames
    FluxCovers and protects the weld, forms slag.Stick, flux-cored, submerged arc
    Matching fillerStrength and chemistry matched to the base metal.Wrong filler can make a weak or brittle weld
    ⚠️
    Low-hydrogen electrodes absorb moisture from the air. Store, bake and issue them exactly as the maker and the procedure say. Damp electrodes are a top cause of porosity and cracking.
    EXAMPLE · The open packet

    A welder leaves a packet of low-hydrogen electrodes on the bench overnight in a damp shop. In the morning the welds show porosity and one crack. The electrodes should have been returned to the heated oven or a holding quiver.

    Weld Quality, NDT & Hot-Work Safety

    Defects and their causes, visual inspection and gauges, NDT methods, distortion control, hot-work permits, fumes, arc eye and gas cylinder safety.

    ⚠️ Weld defects: what, why and how to prevent

    A defect is an imperfection beyond the acceptance limit. Knowing the cause tells you the fix.

    DefectWhat it looks likeCommon causesPrevention
    PorositySmall gas holes in the weldDamp consumables, draughts, dirty metal, poor gas coverDry consumables, wind screens, cleaning, gas flow check
    Lack of fusionWeld metal not fused to base metal or previous beadLow current, wrong angle, fast travelCorrect parameters and technique
    Lack of penetrationRoot not fully weldedSmall gap, low current, wrong joint prepRight root gap and settings
    UndercutGroove melted into base metal at the toeCurrent too high, fast travel, wrong angleLower current, steady travel
    Slag inclusionSlag trapped inside the weldPoor cleaning between passesChip and brush each pass
    CracksLines in the weld or HAZHydrogen, fast cooling, restraint, wrong fillerPreheat, dry consumables, correct filler, sequence
    OverlapWeld metal lies on the surface without fusingToo slow travel, low heatCorrect speed and current
    Arc strike / spatterMarks and droplets outside the weldStriking the arc off the joint, wrong settingsStrike in the joint, correct settings
    EXAMPLE · Cause tells you the fix

    Porosity in welds made outdoors only points to wind disturbing the shielding gas. A wind screen and a gas flow check fix it. Grinding and rewelding without changing the cause would repeat the defect.

    📏 Visual inspection and weld gauges

    Visual testing (VT) is the first and cheapest check. It should be done before any other NDT.

    GaugeMeasuresTypical use
    Fillet weld gaugeLeg length and throat of fillet weldsChecking 8 mm fillets
    Bridge cam gaugeReinforcement height, undercut depth, misalignmentGroove weld profile
    Hi-lo gaugeMisalignment between pipe or plate edgesFit-up check
    Steel rule / tapeLength and spacingWeld length and position
    Magnifier and torchSurface cracks and small defectsClose visual checks
    Temperature stick / pyrometerPreheat and interpass temperatureBefore and between passes
    Do the same routine every time.
    ⚠️
    Gauges and thermometers must be in calibration. A wrong temperature reading can hide a missed preheat.
    EXAMPLE · A missed undersize

    A fillet should be 8 mm. The inspector uses the fillet gauge and reads 6 mm. Without the gauge it looked fine. The weld is marked, an NCR is raised, and it is built up and re-inspected.

    🔬 Non-destructive testing (NDT)

    NDT finds defects without damaging the weld. Each method sees different things.

    MethodWhat it findsWorks onLimits
    VT (visual)Surface defects and sizeAny weldOnly the surface
    PT (dye penetrant)Surface-breaking cracks and poresAny non-porous metalSurface-breaking only; needs cleaning
    MT (magnetic particle)Surface and near-surface cracksMagnetic steels onlyNot for stainless or aluminium
    UT (ultrasonic)Internal defects using soundMany metals, thick sectionsNeeds skilled operator and clean surface
    RT (radiography)Internal defects using X-rays or gamma raysMany metalsRadiation hazard; exclusion zone needed
    📝
    Radiography uses ionising radiation. Only trained, authorised staff work with it, and the area is cordoned off during exposure.
    EXAMPLE · Choosing a method

    A carbon-steel weld needs a check for surface cracks: MT is quick and sensitive. A stainless weld needs the same check: MT cannot be used, so PT is chosen. A thick butt weld needs a check for internal flaws: UT or RT.

    📐 Controlling distortion and residual stress

    Weld metal shrinks as it cools and pulls the part out of shape. Planning the sequence and holding the part reduce this.

    TechniqueHow it helpsExample
    Balanced sequenceWelds on opposite sides cancel shrinkage.Alternate sides of a beam web
    Back-steppingShort welds in the reverse direction reduce build-up of stress.Long seams welded in segments
    Clamps and jigsHold parts in position while welding.Fixture for a frame
    Smaller weldsLess weld metal means less shrinkage.Do not oversize fillets
    PreheatEven heating reduces stress gradients.Thick plate
    PWHTPost-weld heat treatment relieves stress and softens HAZ.Pressure vessels, thick steel
    EXAMPLE · The bowed plate

    A flange plate bows after welding one side only. Welding both sides alternately in a balanced sequence with clamps keeps it flat. Straightening afterwards is costly and can crack the steel.

    🛡️ Hot-work safety

    Welding makes sparks, heat, fumes and ultraviolet light. Safe hot work controls all of them.

    Never start before the permit is signed.
    HazardExampleControl
    FireSparks ignite rubbish or oilPermit, clear area, fire watch, extinguisher
    Fumes and gasesWelding fume, shielding gas build-upFume extraction, ventilation, respirator where needed
    Arc eyeUltraviolet light burns the eyesCorrect lens shade, screens for others nearby
    BurnsHot metal, spatterGloves, jacket, boots, no open pockets
    Electric shockDamaged cable or wet floorCheck cables, dry gloves, insulated mat
    Confined spaceWelding inside a tankPermit, ventilation, gas checks, standby person
    ⚠️
    Fire watch continues after the work stops. Sparks can smoulder for a long time. The site procedure sets the period, often 30 minutes or more.
    EXAMPLE · The quick job

    A welder starts a quick repair next to a stack of cardboard without a permit. Sparks land in the pile. A permit, a cleared area and a fire watch would have caught it.

    ⛽ Equipment and gas cylinder safety

    ItemSafe practiceWhy
    Welding cablesCheck insulation and connections before each shift.Damaged cables cause shock and fire
    Earth clampClamp to clean metal close to the work.Poor earth causes arcing and unstable welds
    Gas cylindersStore upright, chained, valves closed and capped, away from heat.A falling or heated cylinder is a serious hazard
    Regulators and hosesCheck for leaks and wear; use flashback arrestors for fuel gas.Leaks cause fire or gas build-up

    EXAMPLE · The leaning cylinder

    A gas cylinder stands unchained next to the welding bay. It is knocked over, the valve breaks off and the cylinder becomes a missile. Securing it upright is a basic rule, not a formality.

    📐 Welder Engineer: what they do

    The Welder Engineer designs welded joints and structures. They choose joint types and weld sizes, calculate strength and heat input, set out what a welding procedure must contain, and document the design so welders can build it correctly. In this skill group the title normally means an engineer-level role close to the welding trade, usually a diploma or degree holder focused on design and engineering support.

    Required skills

    engineeringtechnical designcalculationsproblem-solvingtechnical documentationtechnical knowledgeproblem-solvingdocumentation
    🔁
    In the role’s required-skill list, problem-solving appears twice: once as a role skill and once as one of the three shared skills. It counts as one skill in the table below, and it matters twice as much in this role.
    📍
    Where they sit: Sits in the design or welding-engineering office with regular workshop and site visits. Reports to a Senior Engineer or engineering manager, and hands the design to the Project Engineer and the welding crews.
    EXAMPLE · Turning a need into a welded design

    Need: "Fabricate a steel platform for a new machine." The Engineer chooses full-penetration butt welds for the main beam splices and fillet welds for stiffeners, sizes the welds, marks them on a weld map with a unique number each, and lists the material, process and preheat the procedure must cover.

    🧭 Fundamentals

    What is the Welder department, and where does it sit?
    It is one of ten departments in the Skilled & Technical Trades group. It has ten roles (Engineer, Senior Engineer, Project Engineer, Supervisor, Coordinator, Planner, Quality Engineer, Inspector, Technician, Analyst) and all share three skills: technical knowledge, problem-solving and documentation.
    Name the main welding processes and where each is used.
    Stick (SMAW) for site and structural work, MIG/MAG for shop fabrication, TIG for thin or stainless material and pipe roots, flux-cored for thick structural steel, and submerged arc for long automatic welds on thick plate.
    What are WPS, PQR and welder qualification?
    The WPS is the written procedure. The PQR is the record of the test weld that proves the procedure works. Welder qualification is the test and certificate showing a welder can follow it within stated limits.
    Why is preheat used?
    Preheat slows cooling, which reduces hardening in the heat-affected zone and lets hydrogen escape, so the risk of cracking falls. Thicker plate and higher carbon equivalent need more.
    What is hydrogen cracking and how do you prevent it?
    Cracking that needs hydrogen, a hard microstructure and stress together. Prevent it with dry low-hydrogen consumables, preheat, controlled interpass temperature and a good joint fit-up to reduce restraint.
    Name common weld defects and their causes.
    Porosity from gas contamination or damp consumables, lack of fusion from low heat or poor technique, undercut from high current or fast travel, slag inclusion from poor cleaning, cracks from hydrogen, fast cooling or restraint.
    Which NDT method would you choose for surface cracks and for internal flaws?
    Surface cracks: MT on magnetic steel, PT on non-magnetic or other metals. Internal flaws: UT or radiography. Visual testing always comes first.
    What are the three skills in all ten roles, and why?
    Technical knowledge, problem-solving and documentation. Knowledge keeps welds sound, problem-solving fixes what goes wrong, and documentation proves what was done.

    📐 Engineering roles

    Engineer · Senior Engineer · Project Engineer

    How do you choose between a fillet weld and a full-penetration butt weld?
    Look at the load path and joint type. Butt welds with full penetration carry the full plate strength and suit main splices; fillets suit T-joints and lap joints where the load is lower or shared. Check weld size, access for welding and inspection, and the code.
    How do you reduce distortion at the design stage?
    Keep welds small and symmetrical, balance them about the neutral axis, avoid unnecessary welds, allow for shrinkage and plan a balanced welding sequence with jigs.
    What would you look for in a failed weld?
    The fracture surface, cracking location, hardness and microstructure in the heat-affected zone, preheat and interpass records, consumable condition and the procedure used.
    What does a design review catch in welded designs?
    Welds the welder or inspector cannot reach, missing weld symbols, undersized welds, unclear procedures, conflicting revisions and joints that will distort badly.
    How do you manage an NDT delay on a project?
    Check which welds are critical, book a second NDT company or shift, prioritise critical joints, resequence downstream work such as painting, update the schedule and inform the client.
    What goes into the final dossier?
    Weld maps, procedure and qualification records, weld logs, inspection and NDT reports, material certificates, calibration records, NCRs with closure, and as-built drawings.

    👷 Site, planning and coordination roles

    Supervisor · Coordinator · Planner

    How do you allocate welders to joints?
    Match each joint’s process, position, material and thickness to the welder’s qualification. Put the most demanding joints with the most qualified people and check the qualification list before work starts.
    What do you check before hot work starts?
    Signed permit, area cleared of flammables, fire watch and extinguisher in place, ventilation or extraction working, gas cylinders secured, welding cables checked, and PPE correct.
    How do you stop welders using old procedures or drawings?
    Keep a procedure and drawing register, issue revision notices, remove superseded copies from the shop, and confirm with the Supervisor that the crew uses the current revision.
    How do you resolve a clash between NDT and painting?
    Bring both teams together, check which welds are accepted and which are not, agree an order so paint is applied only after acceptance, record it and update the look-ahead.
    How do you estimate welding time?
    Take weld length and size from the drawing, use deposition rates for the process and position, add preparation, cleaning, preheat and inspection time, and add allowance for repairs.
    What do you do when repairs are making progress fall behind?
    Find which defect and procedure cause the repairs, bring in quality to fix the cause, add repair capacity, update the forecast and show the effect on the finish date.

    ✅ Quality and inspection roles

    Quality Engineer · Inspector

    Walk me through an RCA you led.
    Describe the defect, collect data, ask why repeatedly or use a fishbone, find the root cause with evidence, agree corrective and preventive actions and check they worked.
    What goes into an inspection and test plan for welding?
    Activities in order (fit-up, root, final visual, NDT), hold and witness points, acceptance criteria and code references, responsibilities and record forms.
    How do you inspect a fillet weld?
    Check the weld ID and drawing revision, measure leg and throat with a fillet gauge, look at profile, undercut, overlap, cracks and spatter, and record the values and the result.
    Why must gauges and thermometers be calibrated?
    A wrong gauge could pass an undersize weld, and a wrong thermometer could hide a missed preheat. Calibration certificates are part of the record.
    What is the difference between RCA and CAPA?
    RCA finds why a defect happened. CAPA corrects it (corrective action) and prevents it happening again (preventive action).
    When did you reject work and how did you handle it?
    State the code and the reading, mark the weld, raise an NCR, agree the repair and re-inspect before release.

    🔥 Technician and analyst roles

    Technician · Analyst

    A MIG arc is unstable and spattery. What do you check?
    Gas flow and leaks, wire feed and tension, contact tip wear, earth clamp and polarity, voltage and current settings, and whether the joint is clean.
    How do you prepare a joint before welding?
    Cut or bevel to the specified preparation, clean oil, rust and paint from the joint and nearby area, set the root gap and alignment, tack weld as the procedure says, and preheat if required.
    What preventive maintenance have you carried out on welding equipment?
    Checking cables and connections, torch liners and tips, gas hoses and regulators, machine filters and earth clamps, on a schedule with records.
    Which KPIs have you tracked and why do they matter?
    First-time pass rate and repair rate show weld quality, welds per day shows productivity, consumable use shows cost. Together they show where to improve.
    How do you check data quality?
    Look for missing values, duplicates, inconsistent weld IDs and mismatched dates, and check against the weld log and NDT reports.
    Preventive versus corrective maintenance?
    Preventive is scheduled work done before failure. Corrective is repair after something has failed. Good plants shift effort from the second to the first.

    Fabricator Fundamentals & the 10 Roles

    Start from zero: where the Fabricator department sits in Skilled & Technical Trades, what its ten roles do, which skills each role needs, and the words every shop conversation uses.

    🧭 What is the Fabricator department?

    Skilled & Technical Trades is a skill group made up of 10 departments. Fabricator is one of them. Fabricators turn flat plate, sections, pipe and sheet into finished parts and structures: they mark out, cut, bend, roll, drill, fit up and assemble frames, platforms, tanks, guards and enclosures. The department has 10 roles, from Engineer to Analyst. This course teaches what each role does, which skills it needs, and how the roles work together safely.

    📐
    Design and draw
    Engineers choose parts, bends, joints and tolerances, and issue cut lists.
    🛠️
    Cut, form and assemble
    Technicians mark out, cut, bend, fit up and assemble parts.
    📏
    Measure and check
    Inspectors and quality engineers prove parts are within tolerance.
    📅
    Plan and manage
    Supervisors, coordinators, planners and analysts keep jobs on track.
    EXAMPLE · One bracket, four people

    An Engineer decides its thickness, bend and hole positions. A Technician cuts and bends it. An Inspector measures flange length and angle. An Analyst later reports how often brackets like it were scrapped. One bracket, four different roles.

    📖 Key terms (what is what)

    FabricationMaking parts and structures by cutting, forming and assembling metal.
    Cut listList of parts to cut, with sizes, quantities and material.
    NestingArranging parts on a sheet or plate to waste as little as possible.
    KerfWidth of material removed by a cut.
    Bend allowanceLength of material used in a bend, needed to calculate a flat pattern.
    SpringbackThe part partly returning toward its old shape after bending.
    Fit-upPositioning and holding parts together before joining them.
    Jig / fixtureA device that holds parts in the correct position.
    Tack weldSmall temporary weld that holds parts in position.
    DatumThe reference point or surface that dimensions are measured from.
    ToleranceAllowed variation from a nominal size.
    SquarenessHow close a corner is to 90°; checked with equal diagonals.
    EXAMPLE · Reading a shop instruction

    "Cut 4 × 500 mm from 60 × 40 RHS, bend flange to 90° with 3 mm radius, fit up in jig J-12, check diagonals ±2 mm, then release to welding."
    Decoded: cut four lengths of tube, form the bend to the specified radius, hold the parts in a fixture, check squareness within tolerance, pass to the welders only when it is right.

    Fabrication Science, Drawings & Standards

    Know what you are working with: materials and machines, weight, nesting and kerf, cutting processes, bending, fit-up and distortion, tolerances, drawings, standards and documents.

    🧰 What fabricators work with

    Material / shapeWhat it isTypical use
    PlateFlat steel, 6 mm and thickerBase plates, gussets, heavy panels
    SheetThin flat metal, under about 3 to 6 mmGuards, covers, enclosures
    Angle, channel, flat barRolled sections with simple profilesBrackets, supports, stiffeners
    I / H-beamDeep structural sectionsMain beams and columns
    RHS / SHS tubeRectangular or square hollow sectionFrames and tables
    PipeRound hollow sectionHandrails, supports, process piping
    MachineWhat it does
    SawCuts sections and bar to length
    ShearCuts sheet and plate in straight lines
    Plasma / oxy-fuel / laser tableCuts shapes from plate or sheet
    Press brakeBends sheet and plate along straight lines
    Plate rollRolls plate into curves and cylinders
    Drill / punchMakes holes
    EXAMPLE · Choosing the stock

    A guard panel from 2 mm sheet can be laser-cut and press-brake bent. A base plate from 20 mm steel plate is plasma-cut or oxy-fuel cut and drilled. The material and thickness decide the machines, so the Engineer must think about the shop when designing.

    ⚖️ Weight, nesting and cutting plans

    Fabricators need to know how heavy parts are and how much material a job needs. Both start with simple arithmetic.

    ItemFormulaExample
    Steel weightVolume × 7,850 kg/m³3,000 × 1,500 × 10 mm plate = 0.045 m³ → 353 kg
    Material utilisation (nesting yield)Part area ÷ sheet area × 1003.6 m² of parts on a 4.5 m² sheet = 80%
    Cutting plan with kerfSum of part lengths + (cuts × kerf)4 × 500 mm from bar with 3 mm kerf
    EXAMPLE · The kerf trap

    Four 500 mm pieces from a 2,000 mm bar with a 3 mm saw kerf: pieces total 2,000 mm, and three cuts remove another 9 mm, so you need 2,009 mm. A 2,000 mm bar is 9 mm short, so the last piece comes out 491 mm. Plan with kerf or the last part is wrong.

    ✂️ Cutting processes

    ProcessHow it cutsGood forWatch out for
    SawToothed bladeSections, bar, tubeBurrs; blade wear
    ShearTwo blades slice the sheetStraight cuts in sheet and thin plateEdge distortion; limited thickness
    Oxy-fuelBurns steel with oxygen and a fuel flameThick carbon-steel plateHeat, slow, carbon steel only
    PlasmaElectric arc and fast gas jetAny conductive metal, medium thicknessHeat-affected edge, fumes, bevel
    LaserFocused laser beamAccurate cuts in sheet and thinner plateHigh cost, reflective metals
    WaterjetHigh-pressure water with abrasiveAny material, no heatSlow, messy, cost per hour
    💡
    Heat-cut edges are harder. If the part will be bent, welded or machined, the cut quality and heat effect must suit what happens next.
    EXAMPLE · Choosing a process

    A 25 mm carbon-steel base plate with simple outline: oxy-fuel or plasma. A thin stainless enclosure with tight corners: laser. A heat-sensitive material that must stay unaffected: waterjet.

    🔧 Bending and forming

    Bending stretches the outside of the metal and compresses the inside. Between them is the neutral axis, which does not change length. The flat length needed must allow for this.

    TopicWhat to knowFormula or example
    Bend allowance (BA)Length of material in the bendBA = (π ÷ 180) × angle × (r + K × t)
    K-factorWhere the neutral axis sits in the thicknessAbout 0.33 to 0.5 for typical air bending
    V-die openingRule of thumb for mild steelAbout 8 × thickness
    Minimum inside radiusToo tight a radius cracks the outsideOften about 1 to 1.5 × thickness for mild steel
    Grain directionBending across the rolling grain is saferAvoid bends parallel to the grain on tight radii
    SpringbackMetal partly springs back after bendingOverbend slightly to hit the angle
    EXAMPLE · Bend allowance

    A 90° bend in 3 mm sheet with a 3 mm inside radius and K = 0.33: BA = 1.5708 × (3 + 0.33 × 3) = 1.5708 × 3.99 ≈ 6.27 mm. For 3 mm mild steel, a V-die of about 8 × 3 = 24 mm is a good starting point.

    🧩 Fit-up, jigs and distortion

    Fit-up means placing parts in their correct positions before they are joined. The quality of the fit-up decides the quality of the finished structure.

    TopicWhat to knowExample
    Jig / fixtureHolds parts accurately and repeatablyA frame jig holds all corners square
    Tack weldsSmall temporary welds to hold partsFour tacks per joint before full welding
    Gap and alignmentJoint gaps and edge alignment per the drawing2 mm root gap on a groove joint
    Squareness checkEqual diagonals mean a square cornerA 2.4 m × 1.8 m frame has 3.0 m diagonals
    Distortion controlBalanced welding sequence, clamps, small weldsWeld opposite sides in turn
    EXAMPLE · Equal diagonals

    A rectangular frame is 2.4 m × 1.8 m. Its diagonals should both be √(2.4² + 1.8²) = √9.0 = 3.0 m. If one is 3.012 m and the other 2.988 m, the frame is out of square by 24 mm in the diagonals and must be corrected before welding.

    📏 Tolerances and stack-up

    A tolerance is the allowed variation. When several parts are joined, their tolerances add up.

    MethodHow it worksExample: 4 parts each ±0.5 mm
    Worst caseAdd all tolerances directly±0.5 × 4 = ±2.0 mm
    Statistical (root sum of squares)Combine as √(Σ tolerance²)√(4 × 0.5²) = ±1.0 mm
    StandardWhat it covers
    ISO 2768General tolerances when none are shown
    ISO 13920General tolerances for welded constructions
    ISO 9013Thermal cutting quality
    💡
    Dimension from a datum, not part to part. Chains of dimensions accumulate error along the length.
    EXAMPLE · Why the datum matters

    A 6 m beam is made from 12 parts, each marked from the previous one with ±1 mm error. The end can be out by up to ±12 mm. Measuring each from one datum edge keeps every position within ±1 mm of where it should be.

    Measurement, Machine Care & Shop Safety

    Measuring and marking tools, datum habits, dimensional checks and calibration, machine care, guarding and isolation, and shop hazards.

    📏 Measuring and marking tools

    ToolMeasures or doesTypical use
    Steel tapeLengthOverall lengths and hole positions
    Steel ruleShort lengthsMarking and short checks
    Engineer’s square / combination squareRight angles and straightnessChecking corners and marking
    Digital angle gauge / protractorAnglesBend angle checks
    Vernier / digital caliperSize, thickness, hole diameterAccurate small dimensions
    Spirit level / digital levelLevel and plumbSetting up frames and machines
    Scriber, punch, chalk lineMarking outTransferring dimensions to metal
    Laser tracker / total stationLarge-scale positionsChecking big frames
    EXAMPLE · Choosing the tool

    Checking hole pitch to ±0.5 mm: use a caliper or a calibrated rule against a datum, not a long tape. Checking the length of a 6 m beam to ±2 mm: a calibrated tape is suitable. The tool must match the tolerance.

    🎯 Marking out and measuring habits

    Do the same routine every time.
    HabitWhy it matters
    Mark every position from one datumAvoids adding up errors along the length
    Check tapes and squares for damageA bent hook or worn end gives a wrong reading
    Check squareness with equal diagonalsQuick, accurate and independent of the square
    Allow for kerf and grindingThe cut removes material
    Measure twice before cuttingA single reading may be a slip
    Record the value, not just “OK”The number proves the result and shows trends
    EXAMPLE · Using one datum

    A plate needs four holes at 100 mm pitch. Marking each from the previous one lets errors accumulate. Marking them at 0, 100, 200 and 300 mm from one edge keeps each position independent, so errors stay at a single reading’s size.

    🌡️ Dimensional checks and calibration

    A measurement is only as good as the tool and the conditions. Steel also changes size with temperature.

    TopicWhat to knowExample
    CalibrationTools are compared with a reference on a scheduleTape and caliper certificates with a due date
    TemperatureSteel expands about 12 µm per metre per °C6 m × 12 µm × 10 °C = 0.72 mm
    Sag and supportLong tapes and beams sag if unsupportedSupport the tape along its length
    Flatness checkStraightedge and feeler gauges on a datum surfaceChecking a base plate before welding
    RecordMeasured value, tool ID and dateDimensional report line
    ⚠️
    If a tool is found out of calibration, stop using it, tag it, and review measurements taken since the last good calibration.
    EXAMPLE · The warm beam

    A 6 m beam is measured at 30 °C and the drawing assumes 20 °C. The beam is about 0.72 mm longer when warm. For ±2 mm tolerance this is acceptable, but for ±0.5 mm it would not be. Know when temperature matters.

    EXAMPLE · The drifting back-gauge

    After a collision the press-brake back-gauge is 1 mm out. Every flange comes out 1 mm short. A first-off check and a calibration record catch it after one part, not after a hundred.

    EXAMPLE · The stored energy

    A hydraulic press brake is switched off, but the ram can still sink under its own weight. Isolating the motor is not enough. The ram must be supported or lowered and pressure released before anyone works in the die area.

    Design & Delivery Roles

    Engineer, Senior Engineer and Project Engineer: what each owns, what every required skill means with an example, and what a day on the job looks like.

    📐 Fabricator Engineer: what they do

    The Fabricator Engineer designs fabricated assemblies. They break a product into parts, choose materials and thicknesses, design joints and bends, set tolerances, make parts easy to cut and nest, and produce the drawings and cut lists the shop works from. In this skill group the title normally means an engineer-level role close to the fabrication trade, usually a diploma or degree holder focused on design and engineering support.

    Required skills

    engineeringtechnical designcalculationsproblem-solvingtechnical documentationmechanical drawingsequipment maintenanceprecision measurement
    📍
    Where they sit: Sits in the design or engineering office with regular shop visits. Reports to a Senior Engineer or engineering manager, and hands the design to the Project Engineer and the shop crews.
    EXAMPLE · Turning a need into a fabrication design

    Need: "Build a steel frame with guard panels for a new machine." The Engineer chooses sections and plate thickness, designs the joints and bends, sets tolerances on the critical dimensions, checks that parts nest well on standard plate, and issues fabrication drawings, a bill of materials and cut lists.

    🧠 Fabricator Senior Engineer: what they do

    The Fabricator Senior Engineer is the fabrication authority. They analyse distortion, tolerance stack-up and structural behaviour, review other people’s designs, solve repeated shop problems and guide junior engineers. Seniority here means judgement and accountability, not only years served.

    Required skills

    advanced technical analysisdesign reviewtroubleshootingleadershipreportingmechanical drawingsequipment maintenanceprecision measurement
    📍

    🧭 Fundamentals

    What is the Fabricator department, and where does it sit?
    It is one of ten departments in the Skilled & Technical Trades group. It has ten roles (Engineer, Senior Engineer, Project Engineer, Supervisor, Coordinator, Planner, Quality Engineer, Inspector, Technician, Analyst) and all share three skills: mechanical drawings, equipment maintenance and precision measurement.
    How do you work out the weight of a steel plate?
    Weight = volume × density. Steel is about 7,850 kg/m³. A 3,000 × 1,500 × 10 mm plate has a volume of 0.045 m³, so it weighs about 353 kg.
    What is kerf and why does it matter in a cutting plan?
    Kerf is the width of material removed by the cut. Four 500 mm pieces from a bar with 3 mm kerf need 4 × 500 + 3 × 3 = 2,009 mm. Ignoring kerf makes the last part short.
    How do you calculate bend allowance?
    BA = (π ÷ 180) × angle × (inside radius + K × thickness). For a 90° bend in 3 mm sheet with a 3 mm radius and K = 0.33, BA ≈ 6.27 mm.
    What is springback and how do you handle it?
    After bending, the metal partly returns toward its original shape. Overbend slightly, bend a test piece, measure it, then adjust before running the batch.
    How do you check a frame is square?
    Measure both diagonals; they must be equal. For a 2.4 m × 1.8 m frame both should be 3.0 m. A difference means the frame is out of square.
    What is a tolerance stack-up?
    Several parts, each with its own tolerance, combine. Worst case adds them directly; statistical stack-up combines them as the square root of the sum of squares. Dimensioning from a datum avoids accumulating error.
    What are the three skills in all ten roles, and why?
    Mechanical drawings, equipment maintenance and precision measurement. Drawings say what to make, maintenance keeps machines accurate, and measurement proves the result.

    📐 Engineering roles

    Engineer · Senior Engineer · Project Engineer

    How do you choose a cutting process for a part?
    Consider material, thickness, edge quality, tolerance, heat effect, quantity and cost. Laser suits accurate thin parts, plasma and oxy-fuel suit thicker plate, waterjet suits heat-sensitive materials, saws suit sections.
    A flange will not fit in the press-brake tooling. What do you do?
    Check flange length against the die and punch, lengthen the flange or change the bend radius, consider a different tool or forming method, and update the drawing and cut list.
    How do you predict and control distortion?
    Review heat input, restraint and sequence; keep welds small and balanced, use jigs, and plan a cutting and welding order. Check with trial assemblies on critical products.
    What does a design review catch in a fabrication design?
    Parts the machines cannot make, impossible tolerances, missing datums, poor nesting, access problems for welding and inspection, and conflicting drawings and cut lists.
    How do you manage a late material delivery?
    Check which parts depend on it, ask for part delivery, start work that uses other material, consider alternative grades if approved, update the schedule and inform the client.
    What goes into the final dossier?
    Drawings and revisions, material certificates, dimensional reports, welder and procedure records, inspection and NCR records, calibration records and as-built information.

    👷 Site, planning and coordination roles

    Supervisor · Coordinator · Planner

    How do you allocate work on a shift?
    Match each task to the operator’s machine skill, put the most skilled on tight-tolerance work, use apprentices on supervised tasks, and re-check the plan midday against output.
    What do you check before a shift starts?
    Guards and light curtains, machine daily checks, drawings and cut lists at the right revision, material available and marked, tools in calibration, and the crew briefed.
    How do you stop the shop using old drawings?
    Keep a drawing and cut-list register, issue revision notices, remove superseded copies, and confirm with the Supervisor that operators use the current revision.
    How do you resolve a clash for a machine?
    Check priorities and the critical path, move work to another machine or subcontract, agree the new slot, record it and update the look-ahead.
    How do you plan machine servicing?
    Use the maintenance schedule and downtime data, choose quiet periods, make sure spares and tools are ready, and plan alternative capacity.
    What do you do when progress falls behind plan?
    Find the bottleneck, test recovery options (extra shift, subcontract, resequencing), show the effect on the finish date and agree the action.

    ✅ Quality and inspection roles

    Quality Engineer · Inspector

    Walk me through an RCA you led.
    Describe the defect, collect data, ask why repeatedly or use a fishbone, find the root cause with evidence, agree corrective and preventive actions and check they worked.
    What goes into an inspection and test plan for a frame?
    Activities in order (material check, cutting, fit-up, dimensions, finish), hold and witness points, acceptance criteria, responsibilities and record forms.
    How do you inspect a frame against its drawing?
    Check revision, material marks, overall dimensions, diagonals, hole pitch, flatness and joint gaps with calibrated tools, record the values and mark pass or hold.
    Why must measuring tools be calibrated?
    An uncalibrated tool may read wrongly, so a pass could hide a fail. Calibration certificates are part of the record.
    What is the difference between RCA and CAPA?
    RCA finds why a defect happened. CAPA corrects it (corrective action) and prevents it happening again (preventive action).
    When did you reject work and how did you handle it?
    State the drawing requirement and the reading, mark the part, raise an NCR, agree the fix and re-inspect before release.

    🛠️ Technician and analyst roles

    Technician · Analyst

    A bent flange is 2° under. What do you do?
    Check the angle with a calibrated gauge, recognise springback, adjust the press-brake depth to overbend slightly, bend a test piece, measure it, then run the batch.
    How do you mark out a part accurately?
    Clean the metal, choose one datum edge, mark each position from it with a scriber, cross-check dimensions and diagonals, and allow for kerf and grinding.
    What preventive care do you give machines?
    Daily guard and oil checks, lubrication, consumable and blade changes, back-gauge and tooling checks, and reporting any fault at once, recorded on the check sheet.
    Which KPIs have you tracked and why do they matter?
    OEE shows how well machines are used, material utilisation shows waste, rework and first-pass yield show quality, and on-time delivery shows service. Together they show where to improve.
    How do you check data quality?
    Look for missing values, duplicates, inconsistent part numbers and mismatched dates, and check against shift records.
    Preventive versus corrective maintenance?
    Preventive is scheduled work done before failure. Corrective is repair after something has failed. Good shops shift effort from the second to the first.

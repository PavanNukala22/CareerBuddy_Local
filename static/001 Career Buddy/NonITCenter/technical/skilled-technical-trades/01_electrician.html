    Electrician Fundamentals & the 10 Roles

    Start from zero: where the Electrician department sits in Skilled & Technical Trades, what its ten roles do, which skills each role needs, and the words every site conversation uses.

    🧭 What is the Electrician department?

    Skilled & Technical Trades is a skill group made up of 10 departments. Electrician is one of them. The department covers everyone who designs, installs, inspects, maintains, plans and measures electrical systems, and it has 10 roles, from Engineer to Analyst. In this course you learn what each role does, which skills it needs, and how the roles work together safely.

    ✅

    Electrical Theory, Drawings & Standards

    Know what you are working with: voltage, current, power, supply types, equipment, protection, drawings, specifications, codes and licences.

    💧 What electricity does

    Three ideas explain almost everything. A water pipe is the easiest way to picture them.

    IdeaWater-pipe pictureUnitSymbol
    VoltageWater pressureVoltV
    CurrentHow much water flowsAmpere (amp)I
    ResistanceA narrow pipe that resists flowOhm (Ω)R
    💡
    Ohm’s law: V = I × R. If you know two of the three, you can find the third. Anyone who works with circuits uses this every day.
    EXAMPLE · Ohm’s law in one line

    A 12 V battery drives a heater with 4 Ω of resistance. Current = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A. Double the resistance to 8 Ω and the current halves to 1.5 A.

    ⚡ Power and energy

    Power is how fast energy is used: P = V × I (watts). Energy is power over time (kilowatt-hours, kWh), which is what a bill measures.

    QuantityFormulaUnit
    Power (single phase)P = V × IWatt (W) or kilowatt (kW)
    EnergykWh = kW × hoursKilowatt-hour
    Three-phase currentI = P ÷ (√3 × V × power factor)Ampere
    EXAMPLE · Sizing a protective breaker

    A 3-phase 20 kW motor load runs at 415 V with power factor 0.9. Current ≈ 20,000 ÷ (1.732 × 415 × 0.9) ≈ 31 A. An engineer then selects a breaker and cable rated above that current (for example a 40 A breaker) after checking voltage drop and cable derating. Understanding these steps tells you why drawings, calculations and testing matter in every role.

    🔌 AC and DC, single-phase and three-phase

    🏠 Single-phase AC

    • Two conductors plus earth
    • Homes, shops, small offices
    • Lights, sockets, small appliances

    🏭 Three-phase AC

    • Three live conductors plus earth
    • Factories, large buildings, data centres
    • Large motors, pumps, heavy machines
    TypeWhat it isWhere you see it
    ACCurrent that reverses direction many times a second (50 or 60 Hz).Grid supply, building wiring.
    DCCurrent flowing one way only.Batteries, solar panels, EV chargers, control circuits.
    EXAMPLE · Why a plant uses three-phase

    A packaging line has a 30 kW motor. On three-phase it runs smoothly with thinner cables than a single-phase supply would need. Someone who has only wired houses may not have met this at scale, which is why industrial work needs extra training.

    📶 Voltage levels

    Voltage level changes the risk, training and licence. Exact limits differ by country, but the bands below are widely used.

    BandTypical rangeTypical useWho works here
    Extra-lowBelow about 50 VControls, alarms, telecomMost technicians
    Low voltage (LV)Up to about 1,000 VBuildings, panels, motorsMost electricians and engineers
    Medium voltage (MV)About 1 kV to 33 kVPlant and campus distribution (for example 11 kV)Authorised, specially trained staff
    High voltage (HV)Above about 33 kVTransmission and substations (for example 132 kV)Utility and specialist teams
    ⚠️
    LV experience does not automatically cover MV. Working on MV needs specific training and written authorisation, and a different level of risk control.
    EXAMPLE · A voltage step-up

    An electrician with 9 years of building wiring (230/400 V) is asked to help on 11 kV switchgear. The basics carry over, but the person must first complete MV training and be authorised. Skill at one level does not replace authorisation at the next.

    🛡️ Protection and earthing

    Every safe installation is built on two ideas. Earthing gives fault current a safe path to the ground. Protection (breakers, fuses, RCDs) detects the fault and disconnects fast.

    A fault is cleared before a person is hurt.
    📝
    An arc flash is an explosive release of energy from an electrical fault. Higher-energy boards need labels, special PPE and strict procedures. Every electrical worker should be able to explain why it matters.
    EXAMPLE · Why earthing is tested

    An inspector finds a motor frame not bonded to earth. Everything still works, so nobody noticed. If the motor winding failed, the frame would become live. This is why testing and electrical safety are required in every one of the ten roles.

    Testing & Electrical Safety

    The skills every one of the ten roles shares: testing, instruments and calibration, safe isolation, permits, PPE and arc-flash awareness.

    🔬 Testing: proving the installation works and is safe

    Testing means measuring with instruments. It is different from inspection, which is looking and comparing. Both are needed before energising.

    TestWhat it provesInstrument
    ContinuityA conductor is unbroken end to end.Multimeter or low-resistance tester
    Insulation resistanceInsulation is healthy and not leaking.Insulation tester (megger)
    PolarityLive and neutral are connected correctly.Multimeter or installation tester
    Earth-loop impedanceA fault will trip protection fast enough.Loop tester
    RCD trip testThe RCD trips within the required time.RCD tester
    ThermographyHot spots from loose or overloaded connections.Thermal camera
    Typical order for a new circuit. Always follow the project test procedure.
    EXAMPLE · Why order matters

    Insulation resistance is measured before the circuit is energised. If a cable was nicked during pulling, the megger shows low resistance. Energising first would turn a cheap repair into a fault or fire.

    🎯 Instruments and calibration

    A reading is only as good as the instrument. Calibration compares an instrument with a known reference. Each calibrated instrument has a certificate with a due date.

    InstrumentMeasuresCheck before use
    MultimeterVoltage, current, resistanceValid calibration; test on a known live source
    Clamp meterLoad current without breaking the circuitJaws closed and clean; correct range
    Insulation testerInsulation resistanceRight test voltage; leads intact
    Loop testerEarth-fault loop impedanceCalibration label in date
    Torque screwdriverTerminal tightening torqueCalibration and correct setting
    ⚠️
    If an instrument is found out of calibration, stop using it, tag it, and review results taken since the last good calibration.
    EXAMPLE · A missed due date

    A torque screwdriver is a month past its due date. Fifty terminations were tightened with it. The quality engineer lists those terminations and has them re-torqued with a calibrated tool before energising.

    🔒 Safe isolation step by step

    Switching a breaker off is not isolation. Safe isolation makes sure nobody can energise the equipment and proves it is dead.

    Each worker applies their own lock.
    StepWhat to doWhy
    IdentifyUse drawings and labels to find every source.Many machines have more than one supply.
    Switch offOpen the isolator or breaker.Removes the supply.
    Lock and tagPersonal padlock and tag on each isolation point.Nobody can switch it back on.
    Prove the testerCheck tester on a known live source.A faulty tester can show “dead” wrongly.
    Test deadTest between all conductors and to earth.Confirms zero energy.
    Re-prove and workCheck tester again, then start.Confirms the tester still works.
    EXAMPLE · The colleague’s lock

    Two electricians work on one motor. Both apply their own padlock. One finishes and removes only their lock; the motor stays locked off until the second finishes. A single shared lock would have failed that second person.

    🛡️ Safety controls and arc flash

    ControlMeaningExample
    LOTOLock and tag energy sources off.Personal padlock on a motor isolator.
    Permit to workSigned approval for specific hazardous work.Permit for work inside a switchboard.
    PPEProtective clothing and equipment.Insulated gloves, arc-rated face shield.
    Earthing and RCDSafe path for fault current and fast trip on leakage.RCD trips if a drill cable is cut.
    Arc-flash labelWarns of incident energy and PPE needed.Label on the front of a 400 A board.
    📝
    An arc flash is an explosive release of energy from an electrical fault through the air. It causes severe burns and pressure injuries. Higher-energy boards need labels, special PPE and strict procedures.
    💡
    Safety vs electrical safety: general safety is PPE, housekeeping, working at height. Electrical safety adds isolation, LOTO, arc-flash and shock control. All ten roles need electrical safety.
    EXAMPLE · The one-minute rule

    Before touching a conductor ask: What is the energy source? Is it isolated and locked? Have I proven it dead? Do I have the right PPE and permit? If any answer is “no”, stop.

    Design & Delivery Roles

    Engineer, Senior Engineer and Project Engineer: what each owns, what every required skill means with an example, and what a day on the job looks like.

    📐 Electrician Engineer: what they do

    The Electrician Engineer designs and engineers electrical systems. They size cables and protection from calculations, produce drawings and specifications, and document the design so site teams can build it. In this skill group the title normally means an engineer-level role close to the electrical trade, usually a diploma or degree holder focused on design and engineering support.

    Required skills

    engineeringtechnical designcalculationsproblem-solvingtechnical documentationelectrical drawingstestingelectrical safety
    📍
    Where they sit: Sits in the design office, with regular site visits. Reports to a Senior Engineer or engineering manager, and hands the design to the Project Engineer and site teams.
    EXAMPLE · Turning a need into a design

    Client: "We need power for a new workshop with four CNC machines." The Engineer lists loads, picks a supply arrangement, sizes cables and breakers, draws the single-line diagram and issues a cable schedule. The site team can now build without guessing.

    EXAMPLE · Preventive vs corrective

    A breaker is tightened and scanned with a thermal camera every quarter to catch heat early: preventive. The same breaker is replaced after it fails and the line stops: corrective. Plants want more of the first and less of the second.

    🔀 Look-alike skills

    Several skills sound the same but differ in meaning. Telling them apart is a core skill.

    PairDifferenceQuick test question
    Safety vs electrical safetySafety is general site safety (PPE, heights). Electrical safety adds isolation, LOTO, arc flash and shock control."How do you isolate and prove dead?"
    Inspection vs testingInspection is looking and comparing. Testing is measuring with instruments."What did you look at, and what did you measure?"
    Planning vs schedulingPlanning is the logic; scheduling is the calendar and critical path."What is the critical path on your last job?"
    Monitoring vs trackingMonitoring is watching daily; tracking is recording versus plan."How do you know you are behind?"
    Coordination vs cross-team coordinationCoordination aligns parties on one job; cross-team coordination aligns several trades or departments."Which trades did you align?"
    Documentation vs technical documentationDocumentation is records in general; technical documentation is design and test papers."Which documents did you write yourself?"

    🧭 Fundamentals

    What is the Electrician department, and where does it sit?
    It is one of ten departments in the Skilled & Technical Trades group. It has ten roles (Engineer, Senior Engineer, Project Engineer, Supervisor, Coordinator, Planner, Quality Engineer, Inspector, Technician, Analyst) and all of them share three skills: electrical drawings, testing and electrical safety.
    Explain Ohm’s law with an example.
    V = I × R. A 24 V supply across a 6 Ω load drives 24 ÷ 6 = 4 A. Power is P = V × I, so the load uses 96 W.
    Why do we earth installations?
    To give fault current a safe, low-resistance path so the protective device trips quickly and exposed metal does not stay live.
    What is the difference between a breaker, a fuse and an RCD?
    A breaker trips on overload or short circuit and can be reset. A fuse melts once and must be replaced. An RCD trips when current leaks to earth, protecting people from shock.
    What is LOTO, and when do you use it?
    Lockout/Tagout. Before any work on equipment that could start or hold energy: isolate, lock, tag, prove dead. Use it every time, however short the job.
    Describe safe isolation.
    Identify the circuit, isolate it, lock and tag, prove dead (test the tester on a known source, test the circuit, test the tester again), then work. Restore only after the area is clear and permission is given.
    What is the difference between a drawing and a specification?
    A drawing shows layout and detail. A specification states materials, standards and performance. You need both to build and check work correctly.
    What are the three skills in all ten roles, and why?
    Electrical drawings, testing and electrical safety. Drawings say what to build, testing proves it works, safety keeps people alive while it is built and used.

    ✅ Quality and inspection roles

    Quality Engineer · Inspector

    Walk me through an RCA you led.
    Strong answer: Defect, data collected, method (5 whys or fishbone), root cause, evidence.
    Weak answer: Names a cause with no method or evidence.
    What is the difference between corrective and preventive action?
    Strong answer: Corrective fixes this defect; preventive stops it occurring elsewhere or again.
    Weak answer: Treats them as the same thing.
    What goes into an inspection and test plan?
    Strong answer: Activities, hold points, acceptance criteria, responsibilities, record forms.
    Weak answer: "A checklist."
    Walk me through how you inspect a panel against its drawing.
    Strong answer: Drawing revision, ratings, cable sizes, labels, torque, earth, records, sign-off.
    Weak answer: "I look if it seems fine."
    Which instruments have you used and how do you know they are accurate?
    Strong answer: Names instruments, mentions calibration certificates and checks.
    Weak answer: Cannot name any.
    When did you reject work, and how did the crew react?
    Strong answer: Specific case, standards cited, calm handling, re-inspection.
    Weak answer: Avoids conflict; never rejected anything.
    What is the difference between RCA and CAPA?
    RCA finds why a defect happened. CAPA corrects it (corrective action) and prevents it happening again (preventive action).
    Why must test instruments be calibrated?
    An uncalibrated instrument may read wrongly, so a pass could be a hidden fail. Calibration certificates are part of the test record.

    🔧 Technician and analyst roles

    Technician · Analyst

    A motor will not start. What do you do first?
    Strong answer: Safe isolation and basic checks, then work from supply to load methodically.
    Weak answer: "Replace the motor" or "reset until it holds".
    What preventive maintenance have you carried out?
    Strong answer: Specific tasks and intervals: thermography, torque checks, cleaning, test records.
    Weak answer: "General maintenance".
    Describe your isolation and LOTO routine.
    Strong answer: Identify source, isolate, lock and tag, test, prove dead, then start.
    Weak answer: Skips testing or locks.
    Which KPIs have you tracked and why do they matter?
    Strong answer: Names KPIs (for example MTTR, fault rate, compliance), explains what each drives.
    Weak answer: Lists metrics with no use.
    Show me a decision that came from your analysis.
    Strong answer: Describes data, finding, recommendation and what changed.
    Weak answer: "I send reports; others decide."
    How do you check data quality?
    Strong answer: Missing values, duplicates, inconsistent tags, source checks.
    Weak answer: "I trust the system."
    What is MTTR and how do you calculate it?
    Mean time to repair: total repair time divided by number of repairs. For 12 hours over 4 faults, MTTR is 3 hours.
    Preventive versus corrective maintenance?
    Preventive is scheduled work done before failure (thermography, tightening, cleaning). Corrective is repair after something has failed. Plants aim to do more of the first.

    🏗️ Scenario: Warehouse electrical fit-out

    Your company is installing the electrical system for a 5,000 m² logistics warehouse: a 400 A main panel, six sub-panels, 1.2 km of cable tray and 24 motors for conveyors. The client wants energisation in 12 weeks. In week 5 these things happen: switchgear delivery slips by 10 days; an inspector finds a 63 A breaker where the drawing shows 32 A; a technician starts work on a sub-panel with no personal lock applied; and a conveyor motor trips on start-up.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Design and calculations

    ✍️ List the inputs an Engineer needs before sizing the cable for a 30 kW conveyor motor.
    Model answer: Running current (from power, voltage, power factor), cable length and route, installation method, ambient temperature and grouping with other cables, allowed voltage drop, fault level at the panel, and the protective device type. Then select cable and breaker, and record them in the cable schedule.
    Q. A cable carries the right current but runs 120 m. What else must be checked before it is accepted?
    • A Voltage drop and fault-current withstand
    • B The brand printed on the cable drum
    • C The supplier’s warranty length
    • D The weight of the empty drum

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Planning and coordination

    ✍️ Switchgear slips by 10 days. What should the Planner and Project Engineer do first?
    Model answer: Check whether the switchgear is on the critical path. If yes, the finish date moves unless recovered. Ask the vendor for part delivery, re-sequence work that does not need the switchgear (tray, cable pulling), consider extra crews, update the schedule and inform the client with a recovery plan.
    Q. A task has 5 days of float. What does that mean?
    • A It must start 5 days early
    • B It can slip 5 days without delaying the project finish
    • C It needs 5 extra electricians
    • D It sits on the critical path

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Site safety

    ✍️ A technician starts work on a sub-panel without applying a personal lock. What do you do as Supervisor?
    Model answer: Stop the work immediately. Make sure the circuit is properly isolated, locked, tagged and proven dead. Remind the team of the LOTO rule, record the breach as a safety observation, report it to the safety officer and check that all other crews have applied their locks. Do not let the job continue until it is done correctly.
    Q. Why does every worker apply a personal lock during isolation?
    • A Locks make the panel look tidy
    • B The equipment stays locked off until each worker has removed their own lock
    • C One lock is cheaper than several
    • D Locks replace the need to test dead

    4️⃣ Task 4 · Inspection and quality

    ✍️ The Inspector finds a 63 A breaker where the drawing shows 32 A. What happens next?
    Model answer: Hold the panel. Raise an NCR with photos and drawing revision. The breaker is replaced to match the approved drawing (or the design is formally reviewed and changed by the Engineer), then the panel is re-inspected. The Quality Engineer runs RCA to find why the wrong breaker was fitted and adds a preventive action, such as a rating check at panel receipt.
    Q. What does a hold point in an inspection and test plan mean?
    • A Work may continue if nobody attends
    • B Work must stop until the inspector releases it
    • C A point used only for filing records
    • D A point that applies to suppliers only

    5️⃣ Task 5 · Testing and commissioning

    ✍️ Before energising the new feeders, which tests do you run and in what order?
    Model answer: Visual inspection, continuity of protective conductors, insulation resistance, polarity, earth fault loop impedance, RCD trip tests, then functional tests of motors and controls. Record every reading with date, instrument and its calibration certificate.
    Q. An insulation test on one new cable reads very low. What is the right response?
    • A Energise it, since the others passed
    • B Do not energise; find and repair the damage, then retest
    • C Change the test voltage until it passes
    • D Record a pass to save time

    6️⃣ Task 6 · Maintenance and data

    ✍️ A conveyor motor trips on start-up. Describe your fault-finding steps as the Technician.
    Model answer: Isolate and lock out. Check supply, protection and overload settings. Inspect starter, contactor and cable terminations. Use the schematic to trace the control circuit. Test motor winding insulation. Find the cause (for example a failed contactor coil or loose termination), repair, test, restart under permit and record the repair.
    Q. A thermal scan of a panel shows one termination far hotter than its neighbours carrying the same load. What does it suggest?
    • A A loose or damaged connection that needs attention
    • B The panel is working perfectly
    • C The label is in the wrong place
    • D The drawing revision is out of date

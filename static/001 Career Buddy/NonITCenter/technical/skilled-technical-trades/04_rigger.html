    Rigger Fundamentals & the 10 Roles

    Start from zero: where the Rigger department sits in Skilled & Technical Trades, what its ten roles do, which skills each role needs, and the words every lifting conversation uses.

    🧭 What is the Rigger department?

    Skilled & Technical Trades is a skill group made up of 10 departments. Rigger is one of them. Riggers plan, attach, lift, move and place heavy loads safely using cranes, hoists, slings, jacks and other equipment: machines, vessels, steel beams, pipework and plant. The department has 10 roles, from Engineer to Analyst. This course teaches what each role does, which skills it needs, and how the roles work together safely.

    📐
    Plan and calculate
    Engineers work out weights, angles, ratings and lift designs.
    🪝
    Rig and lift
    Technicians sling loads and guide lifts safely.
    🔍
    Inspect and verify
    Inspectors and quality engineers check gear and set-ups.
    📅
    Plan and manage
    Supervisors, coordinators, planners and analysts keep lifts safe and on time.
    EXAMPLE · One load, four people

    An Engineer works out the weight and the sling arrangement. A Technician slings the load and guides the lift. An Inspector checks the slings and shackles before use. An Analyst later reports how often similar gear failed inspection. One load, four different roles.

    LoadThe item being lifted or moved.
    Centre of gravity (CoG)The balance point of the load.
    WLLWorking load limit: the maximum load a piece of equipment is rated to lift.
    SlingWire rope, chain or synthetic strap used to attach the load to the hook.
    ShackleU-shaped link with a pin, used to connect slings and lifting points.
    Spreader beamA beam that spreads sling legs so they hang vertically.
    Tag lineA rope used to control rotation of a hanging load.
    Signaller / banksmanThe person who directs the crane operator.
    Lift planWritten plan describing how a lift will be carried out safely.

    OutriggerExtendable leg that spreads a crane’s support on the ground.
    Exclusion zoneArea under and around the load where nobody may stand.

    EXAMPLE · Choosing the sling

    Lifting a polished machine housing: a synthetic sling protects the finish. Lifting rough steel plate with sharp edges: a wire rope or chain sling with edge protection. The wrong choice either damages the load or the sling.

    ⚖️ Load weight and centre of gravity

    Every lift starts with two questions: how heavy is it, and where does it balance? Guessing either is how lifts fail.

    MaterialApproximate densityUse
    Steel7,850 kg/m³Plates, beams, vessels
    Aluminium2,700 kg/m³Light structures
    Concrete2,400 kg/m³Blocks and slabs
    Water1,000 kg/m³ (1 tonne per m³)Tanks, filled vessels
    💡
    Weight = volume × density. Remember to add the weight of the rigging and hook block, and the weight of any liquid still inside the load.
    EXAMPLE · Weight of a plate

    A steel plate is 6 m × 2 m × 25 mm. Volume = 6 × 2 × 0.025 = 0.3 m³. Weight = 0.3 × 7,850 = 2,355 kg (about 2.4 tonnes). The hook must sit directly above the plate’s centre of gravity so it hangs level.

    📐 Sling angles and tension

    When sling legs spread, each leg pulls harder than a share of the load. The flatter the angle (measured from the horizontal), the higher the tension.

    Angle of leg to horizontalTension factor (1 ÷ sin θ)Tension per leg, 10 t load, 2 legs
    90° (vertical)1.005.0 t
    60°1.155.8 t
    45°1.417.1 t
    30°2.0010.0 t
    ItemFormulaNote
    Tension per legT = W ÷ (n × sin θ)n = number of legs carrying the load; θ = angle to the horizontal
    Four-leg slingAssume only 2 or 3 legs carryA rigid load is rarely shared equally
    ⚠️
    Many procedures set a minimum of 45° to the horizontal. Below that, tension grows quickly and a sling that looked adequate can be overloaded.
    EXAMPLE · Why shallow is dangerous

    A 10 t load on two legs at 30° to the horizontal puts 10 t in each leg, twice the 5 t each leg carries when hanging vertically. A pair of 6 t slings that were fine at 60° (5.8 t each) would be dangerously overloaded at 30°.

    🏷️ Working load limit and hitches

    The working load limit (WLL) is the most that equipment is rated to lift in normal use. It is far below its breaking load, because a safety factor is built in.

    HitchHow the sling is usedEffect on rating
    Straight (vertical)Sling hangs straight from hook to load.Full WLL
    ChokeSling is wrapped around the load and through itself.Reduced (often about 80% of straight WLL)
    BasketSling goes under the load with both ends on the hook.Up to twice straight WLL when legs are vertical, less as angles flatten
    TypeTypical design factorNote
    Wire rope slingAbout 5 : 1Follow the standard and the maker’s data
    Chain slingAbout 4 : 1Grade of chain matters
    Synthetic slingAbout 7 : 1Sensitive to cuts and chemicals
    ⚠️
    Never lift above the WLL of any item in the chain: sling, shackle, hook and lifting point. The weakest one decides.
    EXAMPLE · The weakest link

    A 10 t sling is joined to a 4.75 t shackle on a lug. The shackle, not the sling, limits the lift. If the calculated tension in that leg is 5.8 t, the shackle is overloaded and must be replaced with a larger one.

    🏗️ Cranes, load charts and ground

    A crane’s lifting capacity falls as the radius grows. The load chart gives capacity at each radius and configuration. Capacity is also limited by the ground under it.

    ItemWhat to knowExample
    RadiusHorizontal distance from the crane’s centre to the hook.12 m radius may allow 11 t; 20 m may allow only 5 t
    Gross loadLoad plus rigging plus hook block (and any block/ball).8.0 + 0.3 + 0.7 = 9.0 t
    UtilisationGross load ÷ chart capacity.9.0 ÷ 11 ≈ 82%
    Outriggers and matsFully extended on firm mats to spread load.Never lift on retracted outriggers
    Ground bearing pressureLoad on a support ÷ its area.40 t on a 4 m² mat ≈ 98 kPa
    📝
    Many sites require extra planning when a lift uses more than a set share of the chart capacity (often 75% to 90%). Follow your site’s rule and the crane maker’s chart.
    EXAMPLE · Checking a lift against the chart

    A load weighs 8.0 t. Slings and shackles weigh 0.3 t and the hook block 0.7 t, so the crane lifts 9.0 t. At 12 m radius the chart allows 11 t, so utilisation is about 82%. If the crane must reach 18 m, the chart may fall below 9 t and the lift is not allowed.

    Rigging Inspection, Signals & Lift Safety

    Inspecting slings and hardware, pre-use and periodic checks, signals, the lift-day sequence, hazards and controls, and moving loads without a crane.

    🔎 Inspecting slings and rigging hardware

    Rigging gear wears and gets damaged. Inspection compares its condition with discard criteria. If it fails, it is removed from service, not repaired by guesswork.

    ItemCondemn if you see…Why
    Wire rope slingBroken wires beyond the limit, kinks, birdcaging, heavy corrosion, heat damage, reduced diameterStrength is lost and may fail without warning
    Chain slingStretched, bent, nicked or gouged links, worn links, heat damageChain can fail suddenly when deformed
    Synthetic slingCuts, abrasion, chemical damage, melting, knots, damaged stitching, core showingLoad-bearing fibres are weakened
    ShackleBent body or pin, wear, wrong pin (such as a bolt), deformationOnly the correct pin keeps its rating
    HookOpened throat, twisting, cracks, missing or faulty latchAn opened throat shows overload
    Any itemIllegible label or missing certificateRating cannot be proved
    EXAMPLE · A bolt for a pin

    A shackle has a bolt where the pin should be. It looks tight, but a bolt is not made to take the load in a shackle. The inspector condemns the shackle, tags it out of service, and the supervisor finds why spare pins were missing.

    ✅ Pre-use and periodic inspection

    Frequent / competent-person inspectionAt set intervals during useA trained inspector
    Periodic thorough examinationAt intervals set by law or standard (often 6 or 12 months)A competent examiner, with a certificate
    Always do the same routine in the same order.
    ToolUsed to measure

    ⚠️

    EXAMPLE · Colour and tag

    Many sites use a colour code or tag to show that gear has passed its latest examination. A sling with last quarter’s colour or no tag is not used until it is checked, even if it looks perfect.

    📡 Signals and communication

    One person gives signals to the crane operator, using standard hand signals or a dedicated radio channel.

    SignalMeaning
    Raise loadHoist the hook up
    Lower loadLower the hook
    Travel / swingMove the load in the direction shown
    StopStop all movement
    Emergency stopStop immediately, can be given by anyone who sees danger
    RuleWhy
    One signaller at a timeTwo voices give conflicting orders
    Stay where the operator can see youSignals must not be missed
    Repeat radio instructions clearlyAvoids mishearing

    EXAMPLE · Stop means stop

    During a lift, a labourer walks into the exclusion zone. The nearest rigger shouts and signals stop. The operator stops at once. The rule is simple: anyone can stop a lift; only the planned team restarts it.

    💡
    The test lift costs a minute and prevents most accidents. A load that tilts at 100 mm off the ground is far easier to correct than one at 5 m.
    EXAMPLE · The test lift

    A load starts to tilt as it leaves the ground. The rigger signals stop and lowers it. The hook was a little off the centre of gravity. The rigger shortens one leg and tries again. The second test lift is level, and the main lift continues.

    ⚠️ Hazards and controls

    HazardExampleControl
    Dropped loadSling fails or hook opensInspect gear, correct ratings, exclusion zone
    Crushing / pinchHands between load and supportKeep hands clear; use tag lines and tools
    Swinging loadLoad spins or swaysTag lines, slow travel, controlled landing
    Overload / tippingCrane lifts beyond chartCheck load chart and radius, include rigging weight

    ⚠️
    Never stand under a suspended load. Never ride the load or the hook. Stay out of the exclusion zone.
    EXAMPLE · Wind and surface area

    A flat panel of 2 t has a large surface area and catches wind much more than a compact 2 t block. The same gust that is harmless for the block can swing the panel. Always judge wind with the load’s shape, not just its weight.

    🛞 Moving loads without a crane

    Not every load is lifted by a crane. Jacks, skates and rollers move heavy items short distances.

    MethodUseKey rule
    Hydraulic jackRaise a load a few hundred millimetresSupport with packing as you go; never work under a load on a jack alone
    Skates / rollersSlide a machine into positionKeep the path clear and level; control with pull or push gear
    Cribbing / packingBuild a stable stack under a loadCross-layer timber, level and stable
    Winch / pullingPull a load along a pathCheck anchor points and rated gear
    GantryLift in a fixed place with a frame and hoistRated, level and on firm ground
    EXAMPLE · Jacking safely

    A machine is raised 150 mm with a jack. The crew builds a cribbing stack as it rises. Only then does anyone work close to it, because a jack can fail or slip. The stack, not the jack, holds the load while people are nearby.

    Design & Delivery Roles

    Engineer, Senior Engineer and Project Engineer: what each owns, what every required skill means with an example, and what a day on the job looks like.

    📐 Rigger Engineer: what they do

    The Rigger Engineer designs lifting and moving arrangements. They work out load weight and centre of gravity, calculate sling tensions, choose slings, shackles and spreader beams, design lifting lugs, and document the arrangement so riggers can lift safely. In this skill group the title normally means an engineer-level role close to the rigging trade, usually a diploma or degree holder focused on design and engineering support.

    Required skills

    engineeringtechnical designcalculationsproblem-solvingtechnical documentationtechnical knowledgeproblem-solvingdocumentation
    🔁
    In the role’s required-skill list, problem-solving appears twice: once as a role skill and once as one of the three shared skills. It counts as one skill in the table below, and it matters twice as much in this role.
    📍
    Where they sit: Sits in the lifting-engineering office with regular site visits. Reports to a Senior Engineer or engineering manager, and hands the lift design to the Project Engineer and the rigging crews.
    EXAMPLE · Turning a need into a lifting design

    Need: "Lift a 25-tonne vessel onto its foundation." The Engineer confirms the weight and centre of gravity, chooses a four-leg sling set and a spreader beam, calculates tension in each leg at the planned angle, checks the lifting lugs, and issues a lift drawing and rigging schedule.

    🧭 Fundamentals

    What is the Rigger department, and where does it sit?
    It is one of ten departments in the Skilled & Technical Trades group. It has ten roles (Engineer, Senior Engineer, Project Engineer, Supervisor, Coordinator, Planner, Quality Engineer, Inspector, Technician, Analyst) and all share three skills: technical knowledge, problem-solving and documentation.
    How do you find the weight and centre of gravity of a load?
    Use drawings, nameplates, vendor data or a weigh check. For simple shapes, volume × density (steel is about 7,850 kg/m³). Add rigging and hook block weight for the crane. Place the hook above the centre of gravity so the load hangs level.
    Why does sling angle matter?
    Tension in each leg is W ÷ (n × sin θ), where θ is the angle to the horizontal. As the angle gets flatter, tension rises sharply, for example 5 t per leg when vertical but 10 t per leg at 30° for a 10 t load on two legs.
    What is a working load limit (WLL)?
    The maximum load a piece of equipment is rated to lift in normal use, set well below breaking load by a safety factor. The weakest item in the chain (sling, shackle, hook, lug) decides the limit.
    How do you check that a lift is within a crane’s capacity?
    Add the load, rigging and hook block to get the gross load, find the radius, read the capacity on the load chart for that configuration, and compare. Also check the ground and outrigger loads.
    What goes into a lift plan?
    Load weight and centre of gravity, crane and radius, rigging and angles, ground and outrigger loads, wind limit, exclusion zone, communication, roles, and emergency actions.
    When do you condemn a sling or shackle?
    When it meets the discard criteria: cuts, kinks, broken wires beyond the limit, stretched or bent parts, heat damage, illegible label, wrong pin, or missing certificate. If in doubt, condemn.
    What are the three skills in all ten roles, and why?
    Technical knowledge, problem-solving and documentation. Knowledge keeps lifts safe, problem-solving fixes what goes wrong, and documentation proves what was done.

    📐 Engineering roles

    Engineer · Senior Engineer · Project Engineer

    How do you choose between a spreader beam and a plain sling set?
    Look at the sling angle and the load. A spreader beam keeps legs vertical, reducing leg tension and squeezing forces on the load. If angles would be shallow or the load is fragile, use a spreader beam.
    A load’s centre of gravity is off-centre. What do you do?
    Locate the centre of gravity, put the hook above it, and use unequal sling lengths or a lifting beam so the load hangs level. Recalculate leg tensions because the leg nearer the centre carries more.
    What studies does a critical lift need?
    Weight check, crane chart and radius, ground-bearing check, wind limit, load sharing for tandem lifts, lug and structural checks, and clearance to obstacles and power lines.
    What does a design review of a lift plan catch?
    Missing rigging weight, shallow sling angles, under-rated shackles, no ground check, unclear signals, conflicting revisions and missing contingency steps.
    How do you manage a crane delay on a project?
    Check which lifts depend on it, ask for a smaller crane for light lifts, resequence work, consider extra shifts, update the schedule and inform the client with a recovery plan.
    What goes into the final dossier?
    Lift plans, risk assessments, crane and equipment certificates, rigging schedules, pre-use checklists, inspection records, lift logs, NCRs with closure, and as-built information.

    👷 Site, planning and coordination roles

    Supervisor · Coordinator · Planner

    How do you allocate roles on lift day?
    Match each person’s competence to the role: qualified crane operator, rigger or slinger, signaller and lift supervisor. Confirm roles in the briefing so everyone knows who does what.
    What do you check before a lift starts?
    Lift plan and roles briefed, equipment inspected and in certificate, wind and ground checked, crane set up on mats with outriggers extended, exclusion zone marked, communication tested.
    How do you stop crews using old lift plans?
    Keep a plan register, issue revision notices, remove old copies from site, and confirm with the Supervisor that the crew uses the current revision.
    How do you resolve a clash between a lift and another activity?
    Bring both teams together, check priorities and the critical path, agree a new slot, record it and update the look-ahead.
    How do you plan for weather?
    Check forecasts and the wind limit, include stand-by days, prepare an alternative sequence for light lifts, and monitor wind on site with an anemometer.
    What do you do when delays push the schedule back?
    Find which lifts are on the critical path, test recovery options (extra shift, second crane for light lifts), show the effect on the finish date and agree the action.

    ✅ Quality and inspection roles

    Quality Engineer · Inspector

    Walk me through an RCA you led.
    Describe the defect, collect data, ask why repeatedly or use a fishbone, find the root cause with evidence, agree corrective and preventive actions and check they worked.
    What goes into an inspection and test plan for a lift?
    Activities in order, hold and witness points (rigging inspection, crane set-up, test lift), acceptance criteria and references, responsibilities and record forms.
    How do you inspect a sling set before a lift?
    Check labels and certificates, look for cuts, kinks, wear and heat damage, check hooks and latches and shackle pins, measure if in doubt, then pass or condemn and record the equipment IDs.
    Why must certificates and tags be checked?
    They prove the equipment passed its periodic thorough examination within the required interval. Without them, the rating and condition cannot be trusted.
    What is the difference between RCA and CAPA?
    RCA finds why a defect happened. CAPA corrects it (corrective action) and prevents it happening again (preventive action).
    When did you condemn gear and how did the crew react?
    State the criterion and the measurement, tag the item out of service, explain calmly, record it and arrange a replacement so the job continues.

    🪝 Technician and analyst roles

    Technician · Analyst

    A load tilts as it leaves the ground. What do you do?
    Signal stop and lower it, check the hook position against the centre of gravity and the sling lengths, re-rig, then test lift again before continuing.
    How do you choose a sling for a load?
    Consider weight, shape, surface and edges, then choose wire rope, chain or synthetic with the right rating for the hitch and angle, and add edge protection where needed.
    What preventive care do you give lifting gear?
    Clean and dry after use, store slings off the ground and away from chemicals and heat, lubricate wire rope, use edge protectors, and inspect before every use.
    Which KPIs have you tracked and why do they matter?
    Lifts without incident, failed-inspection rate and near-miss rate. Together they show whether lifting is getting safer and where to improve.
    How do you check data quality?
    Look for missing values, duplicates, inconsistent equipment IDs and mismatched dates, and check against inspection records.
    Preventive versus corrective maintenance?
    Preventive is scheduled work done before failure. Corrective is repair or replacement after something has failed. Good sites shift effort from the second to the first.

    ✍️ The vessel weighs 25 t and uses four-leg slings at 60° to the horizontal. Assuming only two legs carry the load, what is the tension per leg, and what must you check?

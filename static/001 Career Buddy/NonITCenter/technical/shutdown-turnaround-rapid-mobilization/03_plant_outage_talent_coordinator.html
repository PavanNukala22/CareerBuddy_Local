    Course Overview

    Plant Outage Talent Coordinator: overview, objectives and prerequisites.

    🧭 Course Overview

    This course teaches how a Plant Outage Talent Coordinator finds, checks and schedules the core mechanical crafts for a plant outage. It covers four skills: sourcing rotating-equipment fitters, sourcing high-pressure welders qualified in 6G and TIG, sourcing boilermakers and tracking outage shift rosters.

    The role matters because these crafts do the work that decides whether a plant restarts safely. A poorly aligned pump fails within weeks. A weld that fails radiography must be cut out and redone. A boiler tube leak can force a second shutdown. The coordinator who finds truly qualified people and keeps the roster accurate protects quality, safety and the schedule.

    Each skill has four levels, formulas and worked examples in the main lessons. The extra material adds tools, testing, good practice, projects, troubleshooting and interview practice. Figures are illustrative and use US dollars. Codes, union rules and site rules differ by country and client, so always confirm the current requirements with the client's quality and safety teams.

    Who this course is for

    • Industrial recruiters who want to specialise in mechanical crafts
    • Outage and shutdown coordinators at staffing agencies
    • Maintenance planners and supervisors who build crews
    • Site HR and contractor coordinators at power, refinery and chemical plants

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    When you finish this course you will be able to:

    1. Explain what rotating-equipment fitters do and how to screen their skills.
    2. Calculate fitter headcount from equipment counts and hours per job.
    3. Explain 6G and TIG qualifications and what welder continuity means.
    4. Calculate weld test pass rates, repair rates and the number of welders to test.
    5. Explain what boilermakers do on boilers, vessels and heat exchangers.
    6. Plan boilermaker crews for tube and exchanger work.
    7. Build an outage shift roster for day and night shifts.
    8. Calculate roster coverage, overtime hours and fatigue limits.
    9. Track attendance, swaps and releases against the roster every shift.
    10. Report craft gaps early and fix them before they hit the schedule.
    11. Apply the main data protection, certificate fraud and timesheet controls used in outage staffing.

    Level 1 · Foundations · Fundamentals

    📘 Fundamentals: What rotating-equipment fitters do and why they matter

    Rotating-equipment fitters, also called millwrights or rotating mechanics, work on machines that spin: pumps, compressors, fans, blowers, gearboxes and turbines. In an outage they strip, inspect, repair and reassemble these machines, replace seals and bearings and align shafts.

    They matter because rotating equipment keeps a plant moving. A pump that is poorly aligned or wrongly assembled can vibrate, leak and fail soon after restart. The coordinator must find fitters with real experience on the equipment types in the job list, not just a general mechanical background.

    TermMeaning
    Rotating equipmentMachines that spin, such as pumps and compressors
    MillwrightCommon name for a rotating-equipment fitter
    AlignmentMaking two shafts line up within tolerance
    Mechanical sealDevice that stops fluid leaking along a shaft
    BearingPart that supports a spinning shaft
    OverhaulFull strip, inspect, repair and rebuild
    Fitter-hours = Number of machines × Hours per machine
    🧪
    Practical Example: The job list has 18 centrifugal pumps at 32 hours each and 4 compressors at 160 hours each. Fitter-hours = 18 × 32 + 4 × 160 = 576 + 640 = 1,216 fitter-hours.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Treating every mechanic as a rotating-equipment fitter
    • Ignoring the equipment type in the job list
    • Forgetting time for alignment and testing after assembly

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    The list has 30 pumps at 28 hours each and 2 fans at 60 hours each. How many fitter-hours are needed?

    Show answer
    30 × 28 + 2 × 60 = 840 + 120 = 960 fitter-hours.
    Level 1 · Foundations · Core Concepts

    🧭 Core Concepts: Equipment types, skill levels and screening questions

    Fitters range from helpers to lead hands and specialists. A helper supports the work. A journeyman can overhaul common pumps alone. A lead fitter runs a crew and signs off quality. A specialist handles large compressors, steam turbines or gearboxes, often with a vendor representative.

    Screen by asking about the exact equipment, the alignment method used, the seal types fitted and the tolerances achieved. Ask for references from supervisors who saw the work.

    LevelTypical workScreening focus
    HelperLifting, cleaning, parts handlingSafety record, reliability
    Journeyman fitterPump overhauls, seals, bearingsPump types, seal fitting, alignment method
    Lead fitterCrew lead, sign-offCrew size led, quality records
    Compressor specialistLarge compressorsCompressor models, clearances, run tests
    Turbine specialistSteam or gas turbinesTurbine types, vendor work, documentation
    Crew mix share = Fitters at a level ÷ Total fitters × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: A crew of 40 fitters has 4 lead fitters, 24 journeymen and 12 helpers. Journeyman share = 24 ÷ 40 × 100 = 60 percent and helper share = 12 ÷ 40 × 100 = 30 percent. The client asked for helpers to be no more than 30 percent, so the mix is within limits.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Asking only "are you a fitter?" instead of equipment-specific questions
    • Filling journeyman roles with helpers to meet numbers
    • Having no lead fitter per crew

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    A crew has 50 fitters with 5 leads, 30 journeymen and 15 helpers. What share are helpers?

    Show answer
    15 ÷ 50 × 100 = 30 percent.
    Level 2 · Core concepts · Intermediate Concepts

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Sourcing fitters step by step

    Start with the equipment list and convert it to fitter-hours and headcount by level. Search your pool for fitters with matching equipment history, then reach out to past crews, union halls where used, partner agencies and specialist networks.

    Screen each candidate with a skills checklist and a reference from a supervisor. For critical machines, ask the client whether a practical test or vendor sign-off is needed. Record each fitter's equipment experience in the tracker so you can match them to jobs.

    StepOutput
    1. Read the equipment listMachines by type and count
    2. Convert to fitter-hoursHours by level
    3. Calculate headcountFitters by shift and level
    4. Search pool and networksCandidate list
    5. Screen and referenceQualified fitters
    6. Tag equipment experienceMatched crew list
    Fitter headcount = Fitter-hours ÷ (Hours per shift × Shifts × Productivity factor)
    🧪
    Practical Example: Fitter-hours are 2,880 over 15 shifts of 12 hours with a productivity factor of 0.8. Headcount = 2,880 ÷ (12 × 15 × 0.8) = 2,880 ÷ 144 = 20 fitters.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Starting sourcing before the equipment list is known
    • Skipping supervisor references for specialist roles
    • Not recording which equipment each fitter knows

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    Fitter-hours are 1,728 over 12 shifts of 12 hours with productivity 0.75. How many fitters are needed?

    Show answer
    12 × 12 × 0.75 = 108. 1,728 ÷ 108 = 16 fitters.
    Level 3 · Intermediate · Intermediate Concepts

    📊 Intermediate Concepts: Fitter quality KPIs: rework, vibration and supervisor ratings

    After the outage, measure the quality of the fitters you supplied. Rework rate shows how many jobs had to be opened again. Startup vibration checks show whether alignment was good. Supervisor ratings show reliability and skill.

    Use these results to rate each fitter in your pool. The best fitters are invited first next time. Fitters with poor results are retrained or not rehired for that work.

    KPIFormula or meaningTypical target
    Rework rateJobs reopened ÷ Jobs completedUnder 3 percent
    Startup vibration passMachines within limits ÷ Machines startedOver 95 percent
    Supervisor ratingAverage score out of 54 or higher
    On-time completionJobs on time ÷ Jobs completedOver 90 percent
    Repeat hire rateFitters rehired ÷ Fitters suppliedTracked yearly
    Rework rate = Jobs reopened ÷ Jobs completed × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: Fitters completed 120 pump jobs and 5 had to be reopened for seal leaks. Rework rate = 5 ÷ 120 × 100 = 4.2 percent, above the 3 percent target. Three of the five jobs were done by one crew, so that crew's seal training is reviewed.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Rating fitters on attendance only
    • Not linking rework to the fitter or crew
    • Never sharing quality results with the client

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    Fitters completed 80 jobs and 2 were reopened. What is the rework rate?

    Show answer
    2 ÷ 80 × 100 = 2.5 percent.
    Level 4 · Expert · Advanced Concepts

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: specialist scarcity, vendor reps and critical machines

    At expert level you plan for the machines that can stop a restart: large compressors, turbines and critical pumps. Specialists for these are scarce and often booked months ahead by several plants. You reserve them early and agree with the client which jobs need original equipment manufacturer (OEM) representatives.

    You also manage the mix of agency fitters and vendor staff. Make sure roles are clear: who leads, who signs off and who supplies tools. Plan backups for each specialist, because one sick specialist can hold up a critical machine.

    RiskControl
    Specialist booked by another plantReserve early with a deposit or retention agreement
    Unclear vendor roleWritten scope for OEM representative and agency crew
    One specialist per critical machineName a backup for each
    Fitters unfamiliar with a modelPre-outage briefing and manuals
    Poor documentationAlignment and clearance records required for sign-off
    Critical machine cover = Qualified specialists available ÷ Critical machines needing a specialist
    🧪
    Practical Example: Four critical compressors each need a specialist on every shift, so 8 specialist shifts are needed each day. You have 10 specialists. Cover = 10 ÷ 8 = 1.25, giving two spare specialists for illness or overtime.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Assuming specialists will be free when the date is close
    • Letting vendor and agency staff argue about who signs off
    • Accepting a machine with no alignment record

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    Three critical machines each need one specialist on day and night shift. You have 7 specialists. What is the cover?

    Show answer
    Needed = 3 × 2 = 6. Cover = 7 ÷ 6 = 1.17.

    Level 1 · Foundations · Fundamentals

    📘 Fundamentals: What high-pressure welders do and why qualification matters

    High-pressure welders join pipe and fittings that will carry steam, gas or hydrocarbons at high pressure and temperature. A failed weld can cause a leak, fire or explosion, so every welder must be qualified to an approved welding procedure, and their welds are inspected, often by radiography.

    TIG, also called gas tungsten arc welding (GTAW), gives very clean, controlled welds and is often used for the root pass on pressure pipe. The 6G test position has the pipe fixed at a 45 degree angle, so the welder must weld in every position around it. A 6G qualification is widely seen as the hardest common pipe test.

    TermMeaning
    TIG or GTAWGas tungsten arc welding, precise process for roots
    Stick or SMAWShielded metal arc welding, common for fill and cap
    6G positionPipe fixed at 45 degrees, all positions in one test
    WPSWelding procedure specification the work must follow
    WPQWelder performance qualification record
    Radiography (RT)X-ray or gamma inspection of welds
    Weld repair rate = Welds rejected ÷ Welds inspected × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: Radiography inspected 400 welds and 10 were rejected. Repair rate = 10 ÷ 400 × 100 = 2.5 percent. Many clients set a limit of about 2 to 5 percent, so this crew is within an acceptable range.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Accepting a welder's word instead of a qualification record
    • Assuming a 6G welder is qualified for every material and process
    • Ignoring the repair rate when rating welders

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    250 welds were inspected and 9 were rejected. What is the repair rate?

    Show answer
    9 ÷ 250 × 100 = 3.6 percent.
    Level 1 · Foundations · Core Concepts

    🧭 Core Concepts: Qualifications, continuity and the weld test

    A welder's qualification is tied to a process, material group, thickness and diameter range and position. Codes such as ASME Section IX set the rules in many industries. A welder qualified in 6G with TIG on carbon steel may still need a new test for stainless steel or a different process.

    Continuity means the welder has used the process recently enough to keep the qualification active, often within six months under common code rules. Most clients also require a site weld test before work, so plan test booth time and expect some failures.

    ItemWhat to check
    ProcessTIG, stick or combination on the record
    MaterialCarbon steel, stainless, chrome-moly, nickel alloys
    Position6G covers all positions for pipe in many codes
    Diameter and thickness rangeMatches the job pipe sizes
    Continuity logRecent use of the process recorded
    Client site testBooth time, coupon and inspection
    Welders to test = Welders required ÷ Expected test pass rate
    🧪
    Practical Example: The job needs 36 TIG/stick welders on chrome-moly pipe. The expected site test pass rate is 75 percent. Welders to test = 36 ÷ 0.75 = 48 welders.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Not checking the material group on the record
    • Accepting records with no continuity log
    • Booking too few test booths for the number of welders

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    You need 42 welders and the expected test pass rate is 70 percent. How many must you test?

    Show answer
    42 ÷ 0.7 = 60 welders.
    Level 2 · Core concepts · Intermediate Concepts

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Sourcing and testing welders step by step

    Get the welding scope from the client: materials, pipe sizes, processes and the welding procedures to be used. Convert weld inches or joint counts into welder-hours and headcount. Then search pools for welders with matching qualifications and continuity.

    Pre-screen with records and references, then book site tests early so failures can be replaced. Track each welder from contact to test to pass to arrival. Record every result so pass rates by source can guide the next search.

    StepOutput
    1. Get welding scope and proceduresMaterials, sizes, processes
    2. Convert to welder-hoursHours and headcount
    3. Search for matching recordsCandidate list
    4. Check WPQ and continuityPre-qualified list
    5. Book and run site testsPass and fail results
    6. Replace failures fastFinal welder list
    Welder-hours = Joints × Hours per joint
    🧪
    Practical Example: The scope has 320 joints of 6-inch chrome-moly pipe at 5.5 hours per joint including preheat and post-weld wait. Welder-hours = 320 × 5.5 = 1,760. Over 10 shifts of 12 hours at 0.8 productivity, each welder gives 96 productive hours, so 1,760 ÷ 96 = 18.3, so 19 welders.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Testing welders on the first day of the outage with no time to replace failures
    • Not checking procedures for preheat and heat treatment time
    • Losing test results in paper files

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    The scope has 200 joints at 4.8 hours each. How many welder-hours is that?

    Show answer
    200 × 4.8 = 960 welder-hours.
    Level 3 · Intermediate · Intermediate Concepts

    📊 Intermediate Concepts: Welder KPIs: test pass rate, repair rate and source quality

    Measure test pass rate by source and by material, repair rate by welder, and the time from contact to pass. A source with a low pass rate costs booth time, travel and delays, even if it gives many candidates.

    Keep every welder's results in your pool record. Welders with low repair rates are invited first. Share repair data with the client inspector so the client sees you track quality.

    KPIFormulaTypical target
    Test pass rateWelders passing ÷ Welders testedTracked by source
    Repair rateRejected welds ÷ Inspected weldsUnder client limit, often 2 to 5 percent
    Contact to pass timeDays from contact to test passUnder 7 days
    Welder retentionWelders finishing the outage ÷ Welders startedOver 95 percent
    Cost per qualified welderSourcing and test cost ÷ Welders passedTracked per outage
    Cost per qualified welder = Total sourcing and test cost ÷ Welders who passed
    🧪
    Practical Example: Sourcing and testing cost $28,800, covering 48 tests including travel and coupons. 36 welders passed. Cost per qualified welder = 28,800 ÷ 36 = $800.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Measuring only how many welders were found
    • Not tracking pass rate by source
    • Hiding high repair rates from the client

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    Testing cost $18,000 and 30 welders passed. What is the cost per qualified welder?

    Show answer
    18,000 ÷ 30 = $600.
    Level 4 · Expert · Advanced Concepts

    🔐 Advanced Concepts: Expert: exotic materials, heat treatment crews and fraud risk

    At expert level you source for hard work: stainless, duplex, chrome-moly and nickel alloys, small-bore high-pressure lines and orbital welding. These welders are scarce, so you build pools in advance and track their qualifications by material.

    You also guard against fraud. Fake or borrowed qualification papers exist. Verify records with the issuing company or test centre, check the welder's identity at the test, and never let one welder test for another. Coordinate with heat treatment and inspection crews, because welds on some alloys cannot be accepted without them.

    RiskControl
    Fake or borrowed certificatesVerify with issuer, ID check at test
    Proxy testingPhoto ID and witness at the booth
    Too few alloy weldersPool by material, early reservations
    Heat treatment delaysBook post-weld heat treatment crews with welders
    High repair rates on alloysPre-job practice coupons and supervision
    Weld inches per welder per shift = Total weld inches completed ÷ Welder shifts worked
    🧪
    Practical Example: Welders completed 21,600 weld inches over 180 welder shifts. Weld inches per welder per shift = 21,600 ÷ 180 = 120. The client benchmark for this pipe is 110, so productivity is above target.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Accepting copies of certificates without verification
    • Not booking heat treatment crews alongside welders
    • Treating all stainless welders as equal regardless of alloy

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    Welders completed 15,000 weld inches over 125 welder shifts. What is the output per welder per shift?

    Show answer
    15,000 ÷ 125 = 120 weld inches.

    Level 1 · Foundations · Fundamentals

    📘 Fundamentals: What boilermakers do and why they matter

    Boilermakers build and repair boilers, pressure vessels, tanks, towers and heat exchangers. In an outage they open vessels, replace boiler tubes, pull and reinstall exchanger tube bundles, fit internals in towers and do heavy rigging, burning and fitting work.

    They matter because this work is heavy, dangerous and often on the critical path. Exchanger bundle work and boiler tube repairs are common causes of overrun. Boilermakers often work in confined spaces and at height, so safety training and experience are as important as trade skill.

    TermMeaning
    Pressure vesselClosed container holding fluid under pressure
    Heat exchangerEquipment that moves heat between fluids
    Tube bundleSet of tubes pulled out of an exchanger for cleaning or repair
    Boiler tubeTube that carries water and steam in a boiler
    Tower internalsTrays and packing inside a column
    Confined spaceEnclosed area with limited entry and exit
    Boilermaker-hours = Units × Hours per unit
    🧪
    Practical Example: The scope has 14 exchanger bundles at 48 hours each and 2 tower tray jobs at 220 hours each. Boilermaker-hours = 14 × 48 + 2 × 220 = 672 + 440 = 1,112 hours.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Using general labourers for boilermaker work
    • Ignoring confined space and height experience
    • Forgetting rigging and crane time in exchanger jobs

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    The scope has 10 bundles at 40 hours and 3 vessel inspections at 60 hours. How many boilermaker-hours?

    Show answer
    10 × 40 + 3 × 60 = 400 + 180 = 580 hours.
    Level 1 · Foundations · Core Concepts

    🧭 Core Concepts: Boilermaker roles, crews and union or contract rules

    Boilermaker crews usually include a foreman, journeyman boilermakers, apprentices and sometimes a rigging specialist. Exchanger crews often work with crane operators and bundle extractor operators. Tube work may need welders qualified on boiler tubes.

    In some regions boilermakers are hired through union halls with set crew ratios, rates and rules. In others they come through agencies or direct hire. Know which rules apply to the client site and follow them exactly.

    RoleTypical work
    ForemanLeads crew, plans tasks, safety
    Journeyman boilermakerFit-up, burning, rigging, tube work
    ApprenticeSupports journeymen under supervision
    Rigging specialistPlans and leads heavy lifts
    Tube welderWelds boiler tubes to procedure
    Crew size = Journeymen + Apprentices + Foreman
    🧪
    Practical Example: The site rule is one foreman per 10 journeymen and no more than one apprentice per 4 journeymen. For 20 journeymen you need 2 foremen and up to 5 apprentices. Crew size = 20 + 5 + 2 = 27.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Breaking crew ratio rules to save cost
    • Sending apprentices to lead tasks alone
    • Forgetting crane and extractor operators in exchanger planning

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    The rule is one foreman per 8 journeymen and one apprentice per 4 journeymen. For 24 journeymen, what is the full crew size?

    Show answer
    Foremen = 24 ÷ 8 = 3. Apprentices = 24 ÷ 4 = 6. Crew = 24 + 6 + 3 = 33.
    Level 2 · Core concepts · Intermediate Concepts

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Sourcing boilermakers step by step

    Start from the scope: exchangers, vessels, boiler tubes and tower work. Convert to hours and headcount, then decide the source: union hall, agency pool, past crews or partners. Screen for the exact work, confined space and height training, rigging experience and safety record.

    Book foremen first because they shape the crew. Then fill journeymen and apprentices in the right ratio. Confirm safety training certificates such as confined space entry and working at height before mobilization.

    Boilermaker-hours are 2,304 over 16 shifts of 12 hours at 0.75 productivity. How many boilermakers?

    Show answer

    Level 3 · Intermediate · Intermediate Concepts

    📈 Intermediate Concepts: Boilermaker KPIs: bundle cycle time, safety and productivity

    Track bundle cycle time from isolation to return to service, tube leak test results, safety observations and hours per bundle against the estimate. These show whether crews are skilled and well led.

    Report results by crew and foreman. A crew that pulls bundles faster without incidents is a strong candidate for the next outage.

    KPIFormula or meaningTypical target
    Bundle cycle timeHours from pull to reinstallAgainst estimate
    Hours per bundleActual hours ÷ Bundles completedAt or below estimate
    Leak test passUnits passing first test ÷ Units testedOver 95 percent
    Safety observationsPositive and negative findingsZero serious events
    Productivity factorEarned hours ÷ Actual hoursOver 0.8
    Hours per bundle = Actual boilermaker-hours ÷ Bundles completed
    🧪
    Practical Example: A crew used 760 hours on 16 bundles. Hours per bundle = 760 ÷ 16 = 47.5. The estimate was 48, so the crew is on target.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Judging crews only on speed and not on leak tests or safety
    • Not separating crane delays from crew performance
    • Missing the foreman's role in results

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    A crew used 630 hours on 14 bundles. What are the hours per bundle?

    Show answer
    630 ÷ 14 = 45 hours.
    Level 4 · Expert · Advanced Concepts

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: heavy lifts, confined space risk and peak crew planning

    At expert level you plan boilermaker crews around the heavy lift plan and confined space rules. Lifts need certified riggers and signal persons. Confined spaces need entrants, attendants and rescue cover. These roles must be in the crew plan, not found on the day.

    You also plan the peak. Exchanger and tower work often peaks in the first half of execution. Work with planners to stagger bundle pulls so crews and cranes are not overloaded, and keep a reserve of journeymen for emergent tube leaks.

    RiskControl
    Lifts without certified riggersName riggers and signal persons per lift
    Confined space with no attendantAttendant and rescue team in the roster
    Crane conflictsBundle pull sequence agreed with planners
    Emergent tube leaksReserve journeymen and tube welders
    Fatigue in heavy workRotations and rest breaks
    Confined space roles = Entrants + Attendants + Rescue team members
    🧪

    📘 Fundamentals: What an outage roster is and why tracking matters

    An outage roster shows which worker is on which shift, crew and area for every day of the outage. It turns the staffing plan into a daily reality. Tracking compares who is rostered with who actually turned up, and records swaps, absences and releases.

    It matters because the roster drives safety, cost and progress. Supervisors need to know who they have. Payroll needs it to check timesheets. Safety needs it to know who is on site in an emergency. An outdated roster means missing people, paid ghosts and confused crews.

    TermMeaning
    RosterList of workers by shift, crew and day
    RotationPattern of days on and days off
    AttendanceWorkers who actually arrived for the shift
    SwapAgreed change of shift between workers
    CoverageRostered workers ÷ Required workers
    Muster listList used to account for people in an emergency
    Roster coverage = Rostered workers ÷ Required workers × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: Night shift needs 140 workers and the roster shows 133. Coverage = 133 ÷ 140 × 100 = 95 percent. The 7 gaps are welders and fitters, so they are flagged to the sourcing team.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Keeping the roster in several versions
    • Not updating the roster after swaps
    • Forgetting the roster is also used for emergency muster

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    Day shift needs 220 and the roster shows 209. What is the coverage?

    Show answer
    209 ÷ 220 × 100 = 95 percent.
    Level 1 · Foundations · Core Concepts

    🧭 Core Concepts: Shift patterns, rotations and hours rules

    Outages usually run two 12-hour shifts, day and night. Rotations vary: some outages run straight through with no days off for 14 or 21 days, others use patterns such as 14 on and 7 off, or 6 on and 1 off. The pattern must follow the site's hours and fatigue rules.

    Hours rules often limit hours per day, hours per week and consecutive shifts, and set minimum rest between shifts. Check these rules before building the roster, not after.

    PatternMeaningNote
    12-hour day and nightTwo shifts per 24 hoursHandover at shift change
    7 on, 0 offEvery day for the outageNeeds strict fatigue checks
    6 on, 1 offOne rest day per weekNeeds extra workers to cover rest days
    14 on, 7 offRotation for long outagesNeeds relief crews
    Night premiumExtra pay for nightsCheck contract rules
    Workers needed with rest days = Daily requirement × Days in cycle ÷ Working days in cycle
    🧪
    Practical Example: A craft needs 30 workers on day shift every day. The pattern is 6 on and 1 off. Workers needed = 30 × 7 ÷ 6 = 35 to cover every day with each worker taking a rest day.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Building a pattern that breaks site fatigue rules
    • Forgetting that rest days need extra workers
    • Mixing patterns in one crew without planning handovers

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    A craft needs 24 workers every day with a 6 on, 1 off pattern. How many workers are needed?

    Show answer

    Start from required headcount by craft, shift and day. Place lead hands and foremen first, then assign workers to crews and areas. Balance experience across day and night shifts so night shift is not left with weaker crews.

    Publish the roster to supervisors, workers and payroll from one source. Update it every shift for absences, swaps and releases, and lock past days once timesheets are approved.

    Level 4 · Expert · Advanced Concepts

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: fatigue management, payroll reconciliation and emergency muster

    At expert level the roster is a safety and control tool. Use it to flag workers near fatigue limits, such as too many consecutive shifts or too little rest. Block assignments that break the rules.

    Reconcile the roster with gate records and timesheets every week to stop ghost hours and paid absences. Keep the roster accurate in real time so the emergency muster list is right. In an emergency, every missing name must be accounted for.

    AreaExpert control
    FatigueAutomatic flags for consecutive shifts and rest
    PayrollWeekly roster, gate and timesheet reconciliation
    MusterReal-time roster linked to gate records
    SwapsApproval rules and log

    🧪

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Letting supervisors override fatigue rules informally
    • Paying from timesheets without checking gate records
    • Leaving released workers on the muster list

    Outage talent coordination handles personal data and safety-critical records: IDs, qualification records, weld test results, safety certificates, rosters, gate data and pay details. Fake certificates or wrong rosters can put unsafe people on safety-critical work or leave people unaccounted for in an emergency.

    Security here means protecting personal data, verifying qualifications, preventing proxy testing and ghost hours, and keeping the roster accurate for emergency muster. Confirm exact legal and site duties with your privacy, quality and safety teams.

    Fake or borrowed qualificationUnsafe welds and equipmentVerify with issuer and witness tests
    Proxy weld testUnqualified welder on pressure pipePhoto ID check at the booth

    Roster data leakPersonal and site security riskRole-based access to the roster
    Wrong muster listPeople missed in an emergencyLive roster linked to gate data

    🛡️ Security for: Rotating-equipment fitter sourcing

    • Store fitter records and references in the tracking system with access limits.
    • Do not share supervisor references with other candidates or agencies.
    • Verify specialist vendor certificates before the worker reaches site.

    🛡️ Security for: High-pressure welder sourcing (6G/TIG)

    • Verify every welder qualification with the issuer or test centre.
    • Check photo ID at every weld test and record the witness.
    • Keep weld test and repair results confidential to quality staff and the welder.

    🛡️ Security for: Boilermaker sourcing

    • Check safety certificates against issuer records, not just copies.
    • Restrict access to union or agency member data to staff who need it.
    • Keep lift and confined space role lists accurate and controlled.

    🛡️ Security for: Outage shift roster tracking

    • Limit roster editing to named coordinators and log every change.
    • Lock past days once timesheets are approved.
    • Share the muster list only with the site emergency team and supervisors.

    Verified qualification rate = Qualifications verified with issuer ÷ Qualifications on file × 100
    🧪

    Security checklist

    • Qualifications are verified with the issuer before work.
    • Photo ID is checked at every test.

    Show model answer
    The welder passed a test with the pipe fixed at 45 degrees, welding all positions around it. It is a demanding test, but the qualification still applies only to its process, material and range.

    What is welder continuity?

    Show model answer
    A record showing the welder has used the process recently enough to keep the qualification active, often within six months under common code rules.

    Half your welders fail the site test. What do you do?

    Show model answer
    Replace failures from the reserve, check which sources and materials failed, tighten pre-screening and offer practice coupons where allowed.

    How do you prevent proxy testing?

    Show model answer
    Check photo ID at the booth, have a witness sign the test record and link the record to the welder's verified identity.

    You need 30 welders and the pass rate is 75 percent. How many do you test?

    Show model answer
    30 ÷ 0.75 = 40 welders.

    💬 Interview Questions: Boilermaker sourcing

    What work do boilermakers do in an outage?

    Show model answer
    Open vessels, pull and reinstall exchanger bundles, repair boiler tubes, fit tower internals and do heavy rigging, burning and fit-up.

    Why book foremen first?

    Show model answer
    Foremen shape and lead the crew, plan tasks and own safety. Good foremen also bring reliable journeymen with them.

    Exchanger work is running behind. What do you check?

    Show model answer
    Hours per bundle by crew, crane availability, permit delays and whether the pull sequence overloads crews. Then agree fixes with planners.

    Why are confined space roles in the crew plan?

    Show model answer
    Entry needs entrants, attendants and rescue cover. If these roles are not planned, work stops or people take unsafe shortcuts.

    Bundles need 50 hours each and there are 12 bundles. How many hours?

    Show model answer
    12 × 50 = 600 hours.

    💬 Interview Questions: Outage shift roster tracking

    Why does an outage roster matter beyond scheduling?

    Show model answer
    It drives payroll checks, safety supervision and the emergency muster list, so errors affect cost and lives.

    How do rest days change headcount?

    Show model answer
    Each worker is off some days, so you need extra workers to cover every day. For 6 on, 1 off, multiply daily need by 7 ÷ 6.

    Night shift absence is rising in one crew. What do you do?

    Show model answer
    Check reasons, fatigue, conditions and supervision for that crew, fix the cause and fill gaps from the reserve.

    How do you stop ghost hours?

    Show model answer
    Reconcile roster, gate records and timesheets every week and lock approved days.

    A worker works 7 shifts of 12 hours with 40 straight-time hours. How much overtime?

    Show model answer
    84 − 40 = 44 hours.

    📚 Course Summary: what you covered

    Rotating-equipment fitter sourcing

    • Convert the equipment list into fitter-hours and headcount.
    • Screen on exact equipment, seals and alignment.
    • Keep lead fitters per crew and reserve specialists early.
    • Rate fitters on rework and vibration results.

    High-pressure welder sourcing (6G/TIG)

    • Check process, material, position, range and continuity.
    • Test enough welders to cover the expected pass rate.
    • Track pass and repair rates by source and welder.
    • Verify certificates and stop proxy testing.

    Boilermaker sourcing

    • Convert bundles, vessels and tubes into crews.
    • Book foremen first and follow ratio rules.
    • Plan riggers, attendants and rescue roles.
    • Track hours per bundle and safety results.

    Outage shift roster tracking

    • Build one roster from headcount by craft and shift.
    • Add workers to cover rest days and check fatigue rules.
    • Track attendance every shift and reconcile with gate records.
    • Keep the muster list live.

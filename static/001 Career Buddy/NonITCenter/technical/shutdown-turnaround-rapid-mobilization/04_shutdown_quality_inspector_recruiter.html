    Course Overview

    Shutdown Quality Inspector Recruiter: overview, objectives and prerequisites.

    🧭 Course Overview

    This course teaches how a Shutdown Quality Inspector Recruiter finds and verifies the people who prove that a plant is safe to restart. It covers three skills: sourcing non-destructive testing (NDT) inspectors at Level II and Level III, screening hydro-test supervisors and verifying flange management inspectors.

    The role matters because inspection is the last line of defence before hydrocarbons, steam and chemicals flow again. A missed crack, a badly run pressure test or a leaking flange can cause a fire, a release or a second shutdown. The recruiter who checks every certificate and every claim protects lives, the plant and the client's licence to operate.

    Each skill has four levels, formulas and worked examples in the main lessons. The extra material adds tools, testing, good practice, projects, troubleshooting and interview practice. Figures are illustrative and use US dollars. Inspection codes, certification schemes and radiation rules differ by country and client, so always confirm current requirements with the client's quality and inspection leads.

    Who this course is for

    • Technical recruiters moving into inspection and quality roles
    • Shutdown staffing coordinators who fill inspection packages
    • Quality and inspection managers who build shutdown teams
    • Contractor compliance staff who verify inspector credentials

    Level 1 · Foundations · Fundamentals

    📘 Fundamentals: What NDT inspectors do and why levels matter

    Non-destructive testing (NDT) finds defects in welds, pipes, vessels and equipment without damaging them. Common methods are visual testing (VT), magnetic particle testing (MT), dye penetrant testing (PT), ultrasonic testing (UT), radiographic testing (RT) and eddy current testing (ET). Advanced methods include phased array UT (PAUT) and time-of-flight diffraction (TOFD).

    Inspectors are certified by level. Level I carries out tests under supervision. Level II sets up equipment, performs tests and interprets results against standards. Level III writes procedures, approves techniques and oversees the programme. A shutdown needs mostly Level II inspectors, led by one or more Level III.

    TermMeaning
    VT, MT, PTVisual, magnetic particle and dye penetrant testing for surface flaws
    UT and PAUTUltrasonic testing for internal flaws and wall thickness
    RTRadiography using X-rays or gamma rays
    Level IIPerforms and interprets tests to a procedure
    Level IIIWrites procedures and oversees the NDT programme
    Inspection pointOne location or weld to be tested
    Inspector-shifts = Inspection points ÷ Points per inspector per shift
    🧪
    Practical Example: The plan has 1,200 UT thickness points and a Level II inspector covers 150 points a shift. Inspector-shifts = 1,200 ÷ 150 = 8. Over 4 shifts that is 2 UT inspectors.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Sending Level I inspectors to interpret results alone
    • Ignoring which methods each inspector is certified in
    • Forgetting that a Level III is needed to approve procedures

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    There are 900 MT points and an inspector covers 60 a shift. How many inspector-shifts are needed?

    Show answer
    900 ÷ 60 = 15 inspector-shifts.
    Level 1 · Foundations · Core Concepts

    🧭 Core Concepts: Certification schemes, experience hours and eye tests

    There are two main types of certification scheme. Employer-based schemes, such as those following ASNT SNT-TC-1A, are run by the employer under a written practice. Central schemes, such as ISO 9712 programmes like PCN, are run by an independent body. Clients state which schemes they accept.

    Every certification depends on training hours, experience hours, exams and an eye test. Near vision tests are usually needed every year. A certificate is valid only for the method, level and sometimes the sector shown on it, and only while the eye test and renewal dates are current.

    ItemWhat to check
    Method and levelMatches the scope, such as UT Level II
    SchemeAccepted by the client
    SectorSuch as welds, castings or in-service inspection
    Expiry or renewal dateWithin date for the shutdown
    Eye testUsually within the last 12 months
    Experience logHours in the method
    Certificate validity days = Expiry date − Last shutdown day
    🧪
    Practical Example: An inspector's UT certificate expires on 30 June. The shutdown ends on 12 June. Validity days = 18 days after the shutdown, so the certificate covers the job. If the eye test expired on 1 June, the inspector could not work after that date.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Checking the certificate but not the eye test date
    • Assuming all schemes are accepted by every client
    • Accepting a certificate for the wrong sector

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    A certificate expires on 20 May and the shutdown ends on 28 May. Is the inspector covered for the whole job?

    Show answer
    No. It expires 8 days before the end, so it must be renewed or the inspector released before 20 May.
    Level 2 · Core concepts · Intermediate Concepts

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Sourcing NDT inspectors step by step

    Start with the inspection and test plan. Count points by method, convert to inspector-shifts and headcount by level, and add a Level III. Then search pools and inspection company partners for the right methods and schemes.

    Verify every certificate with the issuer or the employer's written practice, check the eye test and experience log, and call references from inspection leads. For radiography, check radiation safety cards and licences required by local law.

    StepOutput
    1. Read the inspection and test planPoints by method
    2. Convert to headcountInspectors by method and level
    3. Add Level III coverProgramme lead
    4. Search pools and partnersCandidate list
    5. Verify certificates and eye testsVerified list
    6. Check radiation licences for RTRT crew cleared
    Inspector headcount = Inspector-shifts ÷ Shifts available
    🧪
    Practical Example: The plan needs 48 PAUT inspector-shifts and the inspection window is 12 shifts. Headcount = 48 ÷ 12 = 4 PAUT inspectors. Add one reserve because PAUT inspectors are scarce, giving 5.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Sourcing by job title instead of by method
    • Forgetting radiation safety cards for RT crews
    • Not adding reserve for scarce advanced methods

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    The plan needs 60 UT inspector-shifts in a 10-shift window. How many UT inspectors are needed?

    Show answer
    60 ÷ 10 = 6 inspectors.
    Level 3 · Intermediate · Intermediate Concepts

    📊 Intermediate Concepts: Inspector KPIs: verification, findings and report quality

    Measure how many inspectors were verified before arrival, how often their reports are accepted first time and how many findings are confirmed by a second inspector or by later repair. These show whether you supplied competent people.

    Track findings carefully. Few findings may mean good equipment or a weak inspector. A Level III review of a sample of reports gives a fair view.

    KPIFormulaTypical target
    Pre-arrival verificationVerified before arrival ÷ Inspectors supplied100 percent
    Report acceptanceReports accepted first time ÷ Reports submittedOver 95 percent
    Confirmed findingsFindings confirmed ÷ Findings reportedTracked by inspector
    Re-inspection ratePoints re-inspected ÷ Points inspectedUnder 5 percent
    Level III audit passReports passing audit ÷ Reports auditedOver 95 percent
    Report acceptance rate = Reports accepted first time ÷ Reports submitted × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: Inspectors submitted 240 reports and 228 were accepted first time. Acceptance = 228 ÷ 240 × 100 = 95 percent. Most rejected reports came from one inspector who used the wrong acceptance standard, so the Level III briefs them again.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Judging inspectors by how fast they finish
    • Treating zero findings as proof of quality
    • Skipping Level III audits during busy shifts

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    160 reports were submitted and 150 were accepted first time. What is the acceptance rate?

    Show answer
    150 ÷ 160 × 100 = 93.75 percent.
    Level 4 · Expert · Advanced Concepts

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: advanced methods, radiation safety and certificate fraud

    At expert level you source scarce advanced methods such as PAUT, TOFD and corrosion mapping, often months ahead. You check that inspectors hold the specific qualifications the client wants, sometimes including client-specific performance demonstrations.

    You also manage risk. Radiography uses ionising radiation, so crews need licences, dosimeters, exclusion zones and night working plans. Certificate fraud exists, including altered dates and borrowed certificates, so verify every certificate with the issuer and check photo ID on arrival.

    RiskControl
    Fake or altered certificateIssuer verification and photo ID
    Expired eye testCheck date before travel
    Radiation exposureLicensed crews, dosimeters, exclusion zones
    Scarce PAUT or TOFDEarly reservation with inspection partners
    Weak interpretationLevel III review and performance demonstration
    Fraud catch rate = Invalid certificates found before arrival ÷ Invalid certificates found in total × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: Over a year, 10 invalid certificates were found. 9 were caught in pre-arrival verification and 1 was found on site. Fraud catch rate = 9 ÷ 10 × 100 = 90 percent. The one on site had a valid number but another person's name, so photo ID checks are now made against the issuer database.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Accepting certificate scans without issuer checks
    • Letting RT crews work without exclusion zone plans
    • Booking PAUT inspectors only weeks before the job

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    12 invalid certificates were found and 11 were caught before arrival. What is the fraud catch rate?

    Show answer
    11 ÷ 12 × 100 = 91.7 percent.

    Level 1 · Foundations · Fundamentals

    📘 Fundamentals: What a hydro-test is and why supervisors matter

    A hydrostatic test, or hydro-test, fills piping or a vessel with water and raises the pressure above its normal operating level to prove it is strong and leak-free. It is used after new construction, repairs and many weld replacements before the system goes back into service.

    The supervisor plans and controls the test. Pressure testing stores energy, so a failure can throw parts or release water at high force. A competent supervisor follows the procedure, sets exclusion zones, uses calibrated gauges, controls pressure increase and keeps accurate records.

    TermMeaning
    Hydro-testPressure test using water
    Pneumatic testPressure test using air or gas, with higher stored energy
    Test packSet of drawings, records and limits for one test
    Test pressurePressure applied during the test
    Hold timeTime the pressure is held for inspection
    Exclusion zoneArea kept clear of people during testing
    Test pressure = Design pressure × Test factor
    🧪
    Practical Example: A line has a design pressure of 250 psi. The test procedure sets a factor of 1.5. Test pressure = 250 × 1.5 = 375 psi. The actual value must follow the code and procedure, which may adjust for temperature.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Treating a hydro-test as routine plumbing
    • Using gauges with no calibration record
    • Letting people stand near a test under pressure

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    Design pressure is 180 psi and the test factor is 1.5. What is the test pressure?

    Show answer
    180 × 1.5 = 270 psi.
    Level 1 · Foundations · Core Concepts

    🧭 Core Concepts: Test packs, procedures and the supervisor's checklist

    Each test is run from a test pack. It contains isometric drawings, the test boundaries, the list of welds, the test pressure and hold time, gauge and relief valve details, and sign-off sheets. The supervisor checks that all welds in the boundary are complete and inspected before the test.

    Screen supervisors by asking how they prepare a pack, check boundaries and blinds, choose gauge ranges, vent air, raise pressure in steps and depressurise safely. Ask for examples of tests they stopped and why.

    Checklist itemWhy it matters
    All welds complete and inspectedNo untested joints in the boundary
    Blinds and isolations listedTest does not leak into live systems
    Calibrated gauges in the right rangeAccurate reading of pressure
    Relief valve set correctlyProtects against over-pressure
    Air vented before pressurisingReduces stored energy
    Exclusion zone setProtects people
    Gauge range check = Test pressure ÷ Gauge full-scale reading
    🧪
    Practical Example: Test pressure is 375 psi and the gauge reads up to 600 psi. Ratio = 375 ÷ 600 = 0.625. Many procedures want the test pressure to sit in the middle part of the gauge range, often about 1.5 to 4 times the test pressure for full scale, so a 600 psi gauge is suitable.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Starting a test with incomplete welds in the boundary
    • Using a gauge where the test pressure is near the top of the scale
    • Not venting trapped air

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    Test pressure is 300 psi and the gauge reads up to 1,000 psi. What is the ratio and is the full scale within 1.5 to 4 times the test pressure?

    Show answer
    Ratio = 300 ÷ 1,000 = 0.3. Full scale is 1,000 ÷ 300 = 3.3 times the test pressure, which is within 1.5 to 4.
    Level 2 · Core concepts · Intermediate Concepts

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Screening hydro-test supervisors step by step

    Start with the number of test packs, the systems and any pneumatic tests. Decide how many supervisors are needed by shift. Screen with a structured interview, a scenario test on a real pack, references from site test coordinators and checks of any required certificates or company authorisations.

    Confirm that each supervisor knows the client's procedure and codes used on site. Many clients require a site competence check before the supervisor can sign test records.

    StepOutput
    1. Count test packs and typesPacks by system and test type
    2. Calculate supervisor headcountSupervisors by shift
    3. Structured interviewScores on procedure and safety
    4. Scenario test on a sample packPractical score
    5. References and authorisationsVerified history
    6. Client competence checkApproved to sign records
    Supervisor headcount = Test packs ÷ (Packs per supervisor per shift × Shifts)
    🧪
    Practical Example: There are 96 test packs to complete in 8 shifts. A supervisor can complete 3 packs a shift. Headcount = 96 ÷ (3 × 8) = 4 supervisors.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Hiring on years of experience without a scenario test
    • Skipping references from test coordinators
    • Letting new supervisors sign records before the client check

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    There are 120 packs in 10 shifts and a supervisor completes 4 a shift. How many supervisors?

    Show answer
    120 ÷ (4 × 10) = 3 supervisors.
    Level 3 · Intermediate · Intermediate Concepts

    📈 Intermediate Concepts: Hydro-test KPIs: first-time pass, throughput and record quality

    Track how many packs pass on the first attempt, how many packs are completed per shift and how many records are complete when handed over. Failed tests may mean leaking joints, poor preparation or wrong boundaries.

    Rate each supervisor on these results and on safety. A supervisor with fast throughput but missing records is a risk at handover and audit.

    KPIFormulaTypical target
    First-time pass ratePacks passing first test ÷ Packs testedOver 90 percent
    ThroughputPacks completed ÷ ShiftsAs per plan
    Record completenessComplete packs ÷ Packs handed over100 percent
    Gauge calibration complianceGauges in date ÷ Gauges used100 percent
    Safety eventsIncidents during testingZero
    First-time pass rate = Packs passing first test ÷ Packs tested × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: Supervisors tested 80 packs and 72 passed first time. Pass rate = 72 ÷ 80 × 100 = 90 percent. Five of the eight failures were flange leaks, so the flange team is asked to check bolting before tests.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Counting a retest pass as first-time pass
    • Handing over packs with missing signatures
    • Using gauges past their calibration date

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    150 packs were tested and 141 passed first time. What is the pass rate?

    Show answer
    141 ÷ 150 × 100 = 94 percent.
    Level 4 · Expert · Advanced Concepts

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: pneumatic tests, stored energy and stop-work authority

    At expert level you source supervisors who can run pneumatic tests safely, where the client allows them. Gas stores far more energy than water at the same pressure, so pneumatic tests need larger exclusion zones, extra approvals and very careful pressure steps.

    The best supervisors use stop-work authority without hesitation. In screening, ask for real cases where they stopped a test. Check that they understand how to calculate or obtain safe distances from the procedure and engineering team, rather than guessing.

    AreaExpert practice
    Pneumatic test approvalEngineering and safety sign-off before test
    Exclusion distanceSet from procedure or engineering calculation
    Pressure stepsRaised in stages with checks at each step
    Stop-work authorityUsed whenever conditions are unsafe
    RecordsPressure chart, gauge numbers, witnesses and times
    Pressure step size = (Test pressure − Starting pressure) ÷ Number of steps
    🧪
    Practical Example: A pneumatic test goes from 0 to 120 psi in 5 steps. Step size = (120 − 0) ÷ 5 = 24 psi. The supervisor holds and checks at 24, 48, 72, 96 and 120 psi as the procedure requires.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Running a pneumatic test with a hydro-test exclusion zone
    • Raising pressure in one go
    • Choosing supervisors who have never stopped a test

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    A test goes from 0 to 200 psi in 4 steps. What is the step size?

    Show answer
    (200 − 0) ÷ 4 = 50 psi.

    Level 1 · Foundations · Fundamentals

    📘 Fundamentals: What flange management is and why it matters

    Flange management is the system that makes sure every bolted flange joint opened in a shutdown is put back together correctly. It covers joint lists, gaskets, bolts, lubrication, tightening method, torque or tension values, tagging and inspection. Many sites follow guidance such as ASME PCC-1 for bolted flange joint assembly.

    It matters because a large share of leaks at restart come from flanges. A wrong gasket, uneven tightening or missed bolt can leak hydrocarbons or steam. Flange inspectors verify each joint, and bolting technicians must be trained and qualified.

    TermMeaning
    Bolted flange jointTwo flanges, a gasket and bolts
    GasketSeal between flange faces
    TorqueTurning force applied to a nut
    TensioningStretching the bolt with a hydraulic tool
    Joint tagLabel showing the joint's status and history
    Flange inspectorVerifies assembly and records
    Joint completion rate = Joints closed and verified ÷ Joints opened × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: The shutdown opened 1,400 joints. By day 18, 1,260 were closed and verified. Completion = 1,260 ÷ 1,400 × 100 = 90 percent, so 140 joints remain before restart.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Treating flange work as simple bolt tightening
    • Not tracking every joint opened
    • Letting untrained workers tighten critical joints

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    900 joints were opened and 828 are verified. What is the completion rate?

    Show answer
    828 ÷ 900 × 100 = 92 percent.
    Level 1 · Foundations · Core Concepts

    🧭 Core Concepts: Bolting methods, joint tags and qualifications

    Joints are tightened by hand torque, hydraulic torque wrenches or hydraulic tensioners, following a pattern and passes set in the procedure. Critical joints often need calibrated tools and recorded values. Each joint is tagged through its life: opened, gasket replaced, tightened, inspected and leak tested.

    Bolting technicians are often qualified through training and practical assessment, for example following the qualification approach in ASME PCC-1 Appendix A or similar industry schemes. Flange inspectors should know gaskets, bolting patterns, tool calibration and records.

    ItemWhat to check
    Bolting technician trainingCourse record and practical assessment
    Tool calibrationCertificates for torque and tension tools
    Tightening procedurePattern, passes and final values
    Joint tag systemStatus at each stage
    Gasket and bolt checkCorrect type, size and material
    Inspector competenceExperience and client approval
    Final torque per pass = Target torque × Pass percent
    🧪
    Practical Example: Target torque is 600 ft-lb. The procedure uses passes at 30, 60 and 100 percent. Pass values = 600 × 0.3 = 180, 600 × 0.6 = 360 and 600 × 1.0 = 600 ft-lb, followed by check passes as required.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Accepting technicians with no practical assessment
    • Using tools with expired calibration
    • Skipping passes to save time

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    Target torque is 450 ft-lb with passes at 30, 70 and 100 percent. What are the pass values?

    Show answer
    135, 315 and 450 ft-lb.
    Level 2 · Core concepts · Intermediate Concepts

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Verifying flange inspectors and bolting crews step by step

    Start from the joint list and its critical joints. Convert to bolting crews and flange inspectors per shift. Then verify each person's training records, practical assessments, tool competence and references.

    On arrival, run a short practical check or witness the first joints. Record each person's qualification and approval on the tracker so supervisors know who may sign off critical joints.

    StepOutput

    2. Calculate crews and inspectorsHeadcount by shift
    3. Verify training and assessmentsVerified records
    4. Check tool competenceTools each person may use
    5. ReferencesConfirmed past flange work
    6. Witness first jointsSite approval
    Flange inspectors needed = Joints to verify ÷ (Joints per inspector per shift × Shifts)
    🧪

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Not separating critical and non-critical joints
    • Approving inspectors without witnessing their work
    • Missing tool competence in the records

    There are 800 joints to verify in 10 shifts and an inspector verifies 16 a shift. How many inspectors?

    Show answer

    Level 3 · Intermediate · Intermediate Concepts

    📊 Intermediate Concepts: Flange KPIs: tagging, leak rate and rework

    Track joints tagged at each stage, leaks found at leak test or start-up, rework joints and the time joints wait for verification. Leak rate is the main measure of flange crew quality.

    Link leaks to crews, gaskets and methods. If one crew or one joint class leaks more, retrain, change methods or move work.

    KPIFormulaTypical target
    Tagging complianceJoints correctly tagged ÷ Joints checked100 percent
    Leak rateJoints leaking ÷ Joints testedAs low as possible, often under 1 percent
    Rework rateJoints reworked ÷ Joints closedUnder 2 percent
    Verification waitHours from tightening to verificationUnder 8 hours
    Calibration complianceTools in date ÷ Tools used100 percent
    Leak rate = Joints leaking ÷ Joints tested × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: At leak test, 1,250 joints were tested and 10 leaked. Leak rate = 10 ÷ 1,250 × 100 = 0.8 percent. Six of the ten were from one crew using the wrong gasket size, so their gasket checks are reviewed.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Not linking leaks to crew and method
    • Allowing tags to be filled in later from memory
    • Measuring speed but not leaks

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    2,000 joints were tested and 14 leaked. What is the leak rate?

    Show answer
    14 ÷ 2,000 × 100 = 0.7 percent.
    Level 4 · Expert · Advanced Concepts

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: critical joints, start-up leaks and joint integrity programmes

    At expert level you help the client build a full joint integrity programme: joint risk ranking, approved procedures, qualified people, calibrated tools, tagging, leak testing and lessons learned. You supply people who fit that programme, not just bolting labour.

    Critical joints, such as those in high pressure, high temperature or toxic service, need senior technicians, recorded values and inspector sign-off. Start-up leaks are costly and dangerous, so plan a hot bolting or leak response team only where the client's procedures allow it.

    AreaExpert control
    Risk rankingCritical joints flagged for senior crews
    ProceduresApproved method per joint class
    PeopleQualified technicians and inspectors only
    RecordsValues, tools and names per joint
    Start-upLeak response plan under client procedures
    Critical joint coverage = Critical joints with senior technician and inspector sign-off ÷ Critical joints × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: There are 180 critical joints. 174 have senior technician work and inspector sign-off recorded. Coverage = 174 ÷ 180 × 100 = 96.7 percent. The 6 remaining joints are signed off before the unit is allowed to start up.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Letting junior crews close critical joints alone
    • Planning no response to start-up leaks
    • Losing joint records after the shutdown

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    There are 240 critical joints and 228 are fully signed off. What is the coverage?

    Show answer
    228 ÷ 240 × 100 = 95 percent.

    Security & Compliance

    Data protection, access, fraud, safety and legal risk for this role. Each skill has a security question in the Assessment.

    🔐 Security & Compliance: the big picture

    Inspection staffing handles personal data and safety-critical credentials: IDs, NDT certificates, eye test results, radiation licences and dose records, references and pay details. It also controls who may sign records that allow a plant to restart. A fake certificate or a careless record can lead to a missed defect or a failed test.

    Security here means protecting personal and health data, verifying every credential, preventing borrowed or altered certificates, controlling who may sign records and following radiation and pressure safety rules. Confirm the exact legal duties with your privacy, quality and radiation safety teams.

    Security riskWhy it mattersControl
    Fake or altered NDT certificateMissed defectsIssuer verification and photo ID
    Eye test or dose data leakHealth data breachRestricted access and secure storage
    Unauthorised record sign-offInvalid test or inspectionApproval log and named signatories
    Radiation exposureHealth and legal riskLicensed crews and dosimetry
    Forged joint tagsHidden leak riskField checks against records

    🛡️ Security for: NDT Level II/III inspector sourcing

    • Verify every NDT certificate with the issuer or the employer's written practice.
    • Store eye test and radiation dose records as health data with strict access.
    • Check photo ID against the certificate holder on arrival.

    🛡️ Security for: Hydro-test supervisor screening

    • Keep the list of approved supervisors controlled and up to date.
    • Allow only approved supervisors to sign test records.
    • Store test records securely so they cannot be changed after sign-off.

    🛡️ Security for: Flange management inspector verification

    • Verify bolting training records with the training provider.
    • Allow only approved inspectors to sign off critical joints.
    • Keep joint records in a controlled system with an audit trail.

    🧪 Measuring security

    Credential verification rate = Credentials verified with the issuer ÷ Credentials on file × 100
    🧪
    Worked example: For a shutdown, 140 inspection credentials are on file and 137 have been verified with the issuer. Verification rate = 137 ÷ 140 × 100 = 97.9 percent. The three unverified people do not sign any records until verification is complete.

    Security checklist

    • Every credential is verified with the issuer before work.
    • Photo ID is checked against certificate holders.
    • Health data such as eye tests and dose records is restricted.
    • Only approved people sign test and joint records.
    • Records are locked after sign-off.
    • Staff are trained on privacy and fraud each year.

    Interview Preparation

    Five questions per skill with model answers: concept, scenario and numeric.

    💬 Interview Questions: NDT Level II/III inspector sourcing

    What is the difference between Level II and Level III in NDT?

    Show model answer
    Level II sets up and performs tests and interprets results to a procedure. Level III writes and approves procedures, selects techniques and oversees the NDT programme.

    What is the difference between employer-based and central certification?

    Show model answer
    Employer-based schemes are run by the employer under a written practice. Central schemes are run by an independent body. Clients state which they accept.

    An inspector's eye test expires mid-shutdown. What do you do?

    Show model answer
    Arrange a new eye test before the expiry date or plan a replacement from that date. The inspector cannot work with an expired eye test.

    How would you spot a fake NDT certificate?

    Show model answer
    Verify the number with the issuer, check the name matches photo ID, check dates and method, and question certificates that cannot be verified.

    1,500 UT points at 150 per shift in a 5-shift window. How many inspectors?

    Show model answer
    1,500 ÷ 150 = 10 inspector-shifts. 10 ÷ 5 = 2 inspectors.

    💬 Interview Questions: Hydro-test supervisor screening

    What is a hydro-test?

    Show model answer
    A pressure test using water to prove piping or a vessel is strong and leak-free before service.

    Why are pneumatic tests more dangerous?

    Show model answer
    Gas stores much more energy than water at the same pressure, so a failure releases far more energy. They need larger exclusion zones and extra approvals.

    What do you check in a test pack before testing?

    Show model answer
    All welds complete and inspected, boundaries and blinds, test pressure and hold time, calibrated gauges in range, relief valve setting and the exclusion zone.

    A supervisor says they have never stopped a test. Is that a concern?

    Show model answer
    It can be. I would ask for examples of unsafe conditions they found and what they did, because good supervisors use stop-work authority.

    Design pressure is 220 psi and the factor is 1.5. What is the test pressure?

    Show model answer
    220 × 1.5 = 330 psi.

    💬 Interview Questions: Flange management inspector verification

    Why does flange management matter in a shutdown?

    Show model answer
    Many restart leaks come from flanges. Managing every joint with the right gasket, bolts, method, values, tags and inspection prevents leaks.

    What is the difference between torque and tensioning?

    Show model answer
    Torque turns the nut to create bolt load. Tensioning stretches the bolt with a hydraulic tool and then sets the nut, which can give more even load.

    Leaks are higher for one crew. What do you do?

    Show model answer
    Check their gaskets, tools, method and records, retrain or reassign them and re-inspect their joints.

    How do you verify a bolting technician?

    Show model answer
    Check training and practical assessment records with the provider, check tool competence, take references and witness their first joints.

    12 of 1,600 joints leaked. What is the leak rate?

    Show model answer
    12 ÷ 1,600 × 100 = 0.75 percent.

    Course Overview

    Mechanical Integrity Staffing Lead: overview, objectives and prerequisites.

    🧭 Course Overview

    This course teaches how a Mechanical Integrity Staffing Lead finds and vets the specialists who keep pressure equipment and machinery fit for service. It covers three skills: vetting valve repair technicians, sourcing turbine overhaul engineers and screening heat exchanger specialists.

    The role matters because mechanical integrity work protects the plant from leaks, fires and breakdowns. A relief valve that does not lift, a turbine rebuilt with the wrong clearances or an exchanger with leaking tubes can all cause serious harm. The staffing lead who supplies truly competent specialists helps the plant restart safely and run reliably.

    Each skill has four levels, formulas and worked examples in the main lessons. The extra material adds tools, testing, good practice, projects, troubleshooting and interview practice. Figures are illustrative and use US dollars. Codes, repair shop accreditations and client rules differ by country, so always confirm current requirements with the client's mechanical integrity and reliability leads.

    Who this course is for

    • Technical recruiters supplying maintenance and reliability specialists
    • Shutdown staffing coordinators covering valves, machinery and exchangers
    • Mechanical integrity and reliability engineers who build outage teams
    • Contractor managers who vet specialist service companies

    Level 1 · Foundations · Fundamentals

    📘 Fundamentals: What valve repair technicians do and why vetting matters

    Valve repair technicians strip, inspect, repair and reassemble valves. They replace seats, discs, packing and gaskets, lap sealing faces, repair actuators and test valves for shell strength and seat leakage. In a shutdown, hundreds of valves may be removed and sent to a field shop or repaired in place.

    Vetting matters because valves control and protect the process. A gate valve that does not seal can make isolation unsafe. A pressure relief valve (PRV), also called a safety valve or PSV, that does not open at its set pressure can let a vessel over-pressure. Technicians must be skilled, and relief valve work often requires an accredited repair organisation.

    TermMeaning
    Gate, globe and ball valvesCommon isolation and control valves
    Check valveStops reverse flow
    Control valveAdjusts flow, often with an actuator
    Pressure relief valve (PRV or PSV)Opens to protect equipment from over-pressure
    Seat leakage testChecks the valve seals when closed
    Set pressurePressure at which a relief valve opens
    Technician-hours = Valves × Hours per valve
    🧪
    Practical Example: The valve list has 200 isolation valves at 5 hours each and 40 relief valves at 8 hours each. Technician-hours = 200 × 5 + 40 × 8 = 1,000 + 320 = 1,320 hours.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Treating relief valves like ordinary valves
    • Hiring general fitters for valve shop work
    • Ignoring test bench capacity when planning

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    There are 150 valves at 4 hours and 30 relief valves at 9 hours. How many technician-hours?

    Show answer
    150 × 4 + 30 × 9 = 600 + 270 = 870 hours.
    Level 1 · Foundations · Core Concepts

    🧭 Core Concepts: Valve types, skills and relief valve accreditation

    Different valves need different skills. Isolation valves need seat and packing work. Control valves need actuator and positioner skills, often with instrument technicians. Relief valves need precise set pressure testing and records. Many regions require relief valve repairs to be done by an accredited organisation, such as one holding a National Board VR certificate in the United States.

    When vetting, check the technician's valve types, the tests they have run, the test standards used and whether they worked under an accredited relief valve programme. Ask for references from shop supervisors.

    Valve workKey skillsCheck
    Isolation valvesSeats, packing, lappingValve types and sizes handled
    Control valvesActuators, positioners, trimActuator brands and calibration
    Relief valvesSet pressure, blowdown, recordsAccredited programme and test records
    Check valvesDiscs, hinges, seatsInspection experience
    Shop testingShell and seat testsTest standards used
    Test bench capacity = Benches × Tests per bench per shift
    🧪
    Practical Example: The field shop has 3 relief valve test benches and each bench handles 6 valves a shift. Capacity = 3 × 6 = 18 valves a shift. With 90 relief valves, the shop needs 90 ÷ 18 = 5 shifts.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Sending relief valves to a shop without the needed accreditation
    • Vetting control valve technicians without actuator questions
    • Planning more valves than test benches can handle

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    There are 4 benches doing 5 valves a shift each, and 100 relief valves. How many shifts are needed?

    Show answer
    Capacity = 4 × 5 = 20 a shift. 100 ÷ 20 = 5 shifts.

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Vetting valve technicians step by step

    Start from the valve list. Split by valve type and work type: in-place repair, shop repair, relief valve testing and control valve work. Convert to hours and headcount. Then source technicians by type and check their records.

    Vet with a skills checklist, references from shop supervisors and, for relief valves, proof that they work under the accredited programme the client requires. Witness the first repairs and tests on site before full release.

    Valve technician headcount = Technician-hours ÷ (Hours per shift × Shifts × Productivity factor)
    🧪
    Practical Example: Technician-hours are 1,320 over 11 shifts of 12 hours at 0.8 productivity. Headcount = 1,320 ÷ (12 × 11 × 0.8) = 1,320 ÷ 105.6 = 12.5, so 13 technicians.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Using one headcount for all valve types
    • Releasing technicians without witnessing early work
    • Missing references from shop supervisors

    Technician-hours are 864 over 9 shifts of 12 hours at 0.8 productivity. How many technicians?

    Show answer

    Level 3 · Intermediate · Intermediate Concepts

    📊 Intermediate Concepts: Valve KPIs: test pass rate, rework and turnaround time

    Track how many valves pass their first test, how many must be reworked and how long valves take from removal to return. For relief valves, track set pressure test results and record completeness.

    Link results to technicians. A technician with a high first-test pass rate and complete records is a strong pick for the next outage.

    KPIFormulaTypical target
    First-test pass rateValves passing first test ÷ Valves testedOver 95 percent
    Rework rateValves reworked ÷ Valves repairedUnder 3 percent
    Shop turnaroundHours from receipt to returnAs per plan
    Record completenessValves with full records ÷ Valves returned100 percent
    Relief valve set accuracyValves set within tolerance ÷ Relief valves tested100 percent
    First-test pass rate = Valves passing first test ÷ Valves tested × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: The shop tested 240 valves and 228 passed first time. Pass rate = 228 ÷ 240 × 100 = 95 percent. Most failures were seat leaks on large gate valves, so lapping work on those valves goes to the most experienced technicians.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Counting retest passes as first-test passes
    • Returning valves without complete records
    • Not linking rework to technicians

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    180 valves were tested and 171 passed first time. What is the pass rate?

    Show answer
    171 ÷ 180 × 100 = 95 percent.
    Level 4 · Expert · Advanced Concepts

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: relief valve programmes, traceability and specialist shops

    At expert level you help the client run a relief valve programme with full traceability: each valve's tag, set pressure, as-found and as-left tests, repairs and the name of the technician and the accredited shop. Missing records can mean a valve must be retested before start-up.

    You also decide when to use specialist shops instead of field crews. Large control valves, exotic materials and relief valves often go to approved shops. Plan transport, turnaround times and witness visits so shop work does not delay restart.

    AreaExpert practice
    TraceabilityTag, set pressure, tests and names on every record
    As-found testingRecord condition before repair
    Accredited shopsUse only approved shops for relief valves
    Shop turnaroundAgree times and track daily
    Witness visitsClient inspectors witness key tests
    Relief valve traceability = Relief valves with complete records ÷ Relief valves returned × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: 120 relief valves were returned and 117 have complete records with as-found and as-left results. Traceability = 117 ÷ 120 × 100 = 97.5 percent. The three valves without records are retested before installation.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Skipping as-found tests to save time
    • Using shops without the required accreditation
    • No daily tracking of shop turnaround

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    90 relief valves were returned and 88 have complete records. What is the traceability?

    Show answer
    88 ÷ 90 × 100 = 97.8 percent.

    Level 1 · Foundations · Fundamentals

    📘 Fundamentals: What a turbine overhaul is and why it needs specialists

    A turbine overhaul opens a steam or gas turbine to inspect and repair its parts. Work includes removing casings, lifting the rotor, inspecting blades, seals and bearings, measuring clearances, repairing or replacing parts, reassembling and aligning the machine and then supporting start-up and run tests.

    It needs specialists because turbines are high-speed, high-value machines. Wrong clearances or poor alignment can cause vibration, rubs or failure. Overhauls are often on the critical path and are led by the original equipment manufacturer (OEM) or a qualified service company, with skilled agency staff in support.

    TermMeaning
    Steam turbineTurns steam energy into shaft power
    Gas turbineBurns fuel to drive a shaft
    RotorThe spinning shaft with blades
    CasingThe outer shell of the turbine
    ClearanceGap between moving and fixed parts
    OEM field service engineerManufacturer's specialist on site
    Overhaul crew-hours = Tasks × Hours per task (summed across tasks)
    🧪
    Practical Example: A steam turbine overhaul has casing removal 240 hours, rotor removal and inspection 360 hours, bearing and seal work 180 hours and reassembly and alignment 420 hours. Crew-hours = 240 + 360 + 180 + 420 = 1,200 hours.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Treating a turbine overhaul like a pump overhaul
    • Not knowing who leads: OEM or service company
    • Forgetting time for run tests after reassembly

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    Tasks take 300, 400, 200 and 500 hours. What are the total crew-hours?

    Show answer
    300 + 400 + 200 + 500 = 1,400 hours.
    Level 1 · Foundations · Core Concepts

    🧭 Core Concepts: Turbine roles: OEM engineers, technical advisors and crafts

    A turbine overhaul team usually has an OEM or service company technical advisor or field service engineer, a turbine supervisor, millwrights or turbine mechanics, riggers, crane operators, NDT inspectors and sometimes machinists. Each role has a clear scope.

    When sourcing, find out who the client has contracted and which roles you must supply. Agency staff often supply turbine mechanics, riggers and helpers working under the technical advisor's direction.

    RoleTypical scope
    Technical advisor or field service engineerDirects the technical work and signs off clearances
    Turbine supervisorLeads crews and schedule
    Turbine mechanic or millwrightDisassembly, measurement, reassembly
    Rigger and crane operatorLifts casings and rotors
    NDT inspectorInspects blades, rotors and casings
    MachinistOn-site machining and fitting
    Specialist cost = Day rate × Days on site + Travel and living cost
    🧪
    Practical Example: A technical advisor costs $2,200 a day for 18 days, plus $4,500 travel and living. Cost = 2,200 × 18 + 4,500 = 39,600 + 4,500 = $44,100.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Supplying mechanics with no turbine experience
    • Leaving riggers out of the turbine plan
    • Not agreeing who signs off clearances

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    An engineer costs $1,900 a day for 15 days plus $3,000 travel. What is the cost?

    Show answer
    1,900 × 15 + 3,000 = 28,500 + 3,000 = $31,500.
    Level 2 · Core concepts · Intermediate Concepts

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Sourcing turbine engineers step by step

    Get the turbine type, model, scope and dates from the client. Ask early, because OEM engineers and experienced turbine mechanics are booked months ahead. Agree with the OEM or service company which roles you will supply.

    Screen candidates on the turbine models they have worked on, the tasks they performed, the measurements they took and who they reported to. Take references from technical advisors. Check lifting and safety training for riggers and confined space or height training where needed.

    StepOutput
    1. Get model, scope and datesTurbine work plan
    2. Agree roles with OEM or service companyRole split
    3. Calculate headcount by role and shiftCrew plan
    4. Source by model experienceCandidate list
    5. Screen and referenceVerified turbine crew
    6. Pre-job briefingCrew briefed on model and procedure
    Turbine crew headcount = Crew-hours ÷ (Hours per shift × Shifts × Productivity factor)
    🧪
    Practical Example: Crew-hours are 1,200 over 10 shifts of 12 hours at 0.75 productivity. Headcount = 1,200 ÷ (12 × 10 × 0.75) = 1,200 ÷ 90 = 13.3, so 14 people on the turbine crew across shifts.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Sourcing turbine mechanics a week before the outage
    • Screening without asking about the turbine model
    • Skipping the pre-job briefing

    Crew-hours are 1,080 over 9 shifts of 12 hours at 0.8 productivity. How many people?

    Show answer

    Level 3 · Intermediate · Intermediate Concepts

    📈 Intermediate Concepts: Turbine KPIs: schedule, vibration and rework

    Measure whether the overhaul finished on schedule, whether vibration at start-up was within limits and whether any work had to be reopened. Also measure safety, especially during heavy lifts.

    Rate each supplied person based on the technical advisor's feedback. The best turbine mechanics are scarce, so keep them close with early bookings for the next outage.

    KPIFormula or meaningTypical target
    Schedule performancePlanned days ÷ Actual days1.0 or better
    Start-up vibrationWithin OEM limitsPass on first run
    ReworkTasks reopened ÷ Tasks completedZero for critical tasks
    Lift safetyLifts without incident100 percent
    Advisor ratingAverage score out of 54 or higher
    Schedule performance = Planned overhaul days ÷ Actual overhaul days
    🧪
    Practical Example: The overhaul was planned for 21 days and took 23. Schedule performance = 21 ÷ 23 = 0.91, so it ran 9 percent long. The delay came from late arrival of a specialist machinist, so machinists are booked earlier next time.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Judging success only by finishing on time
    • Not asking the technical advisor for ratings
    • Ignoring lift safety in the review

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    An overhaul was planned for 18 days and took 20. What is the schedule performance?

    Show answer
    18 ÷ 20 = 0.9.
    Level 4 · Expert · Advanced Concepts

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: critical path risk, OEM contracts and knowledge retention

    At expert level you manage the turbine as a critical path risk. Name backups for key roles, plan shift handovers so measurements are not lost and track parts and tools that the OEM must supply. A single missing specialist or part can delay the whole restart.

    You also protect knowledge. Experienced turbine people are retiring and hard to replace. Build a pool, pair younger mechanics with experts and capture lessons learned from each overhaul.

    RiskControl
    Key specialist unavailableNamed backup and early booking
    Lost measurements at handoverWritten clearance records each shift
    OEM parts lateDaily parts tracking with the OEM
    Role confusionWritten split of OEM and agency scope
    Skills ageing outMentoring and pool development
    Critical role cover = Qualified people available ÷ Critical role slots per day
    🧪
    Practical Example: The turbine needs a technical advisor and 2 lead mechanics on each of 2 shifts, so 6 critical slots a day. You have 7 qualified people. Cover = 7 ÷ 6 = 1.17, giving one spare for illness or overtime limits.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • No backups for critical turbine roles
    • Verbal handovers of clearance readings
    • Ignoring parts tracking because it is the OEM's job

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    There are 8 critical slots a day and 10 qualified people. What is the cover?

    Show answer
    10 ÷ 8 = 1.25.

    Level 1 · Foundations · Fundamentals

    📘 Fundamentals: What heat exchanger specialists do and why it matters

    Heat exchanger specialists work on equipment that transfers heat between fluids. In a shutdown they open exchangers, pull tube bundles, clean them by hydro-blasting or chemical cleaning, test tubes for thinning and leaks, plug or replace damaged tubes, replace gaskets and reassemble and pressure test the unit.

    It matters because exchanger work is often on the critical path, and fouled or leaking exchangers reduce plant output and can mix process streams. Specialists include bundle extractor operators, hydro-blasters, tube testers, retubing crews and exchanger fitters.

    TermMeaning
    Shell-and-tube exchangerTubes inside a shell carry two fluids
    Tube bundleRemovable set of tubes
    Hydro-blastingCleaning with very high pressure water
    Tube testingEddy current or ultrasonic checks of tubes
    PluggingSealing a damaged tube so it is out of service
    RetubingReplacing tubes in the bundle
    Tube plugging percent = Plugged tubes ÷ Total tubes × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: A bundle has 1,200 tubes. 54 were already plugged and testing finds 18 more to plug. Plugging = (54 + 18) ÷ 1,200 × 100 = 6 percent. If the client's limit is 10 percent, the bundle can stay in service with a plan to retube later.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Treating exchanger work as general cleaning labour
    • Not knowing the client's plugging limit
    • Forgetting extractor and crane crews

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    A bundle has 900 tubes and 45 are plugged. What is the plugging percent?

    Show answer
    45 ÷ 900 × 100 = 5 percent.
    Level 1 · Foundations · Core Concepts

    🧭 Core Concepts: Exchanger roles, cleaning methods and tube testing

    Exchanger work involves several specialist roles. Bundle extractor operators pull and reinsert bundles. Hydro-blasting crews clean tubes and shells using equipment at very high pressure, which is dangerous and needs trained operators. Tube testing technicians use eddy current, IRIS ultrasonic or other methods. Exchanger fitters handle gaskets, bolting and reassembly.

    Screen each role separately. Hydro-blasters need high-pressure water jetting training and safety records. Tube testers need NDT certificates in the right method. Fitters need flange and bolting skills.

    RoleKey skillsCheck
    Bundle extractor operatorPulling and setting bundlesEquipment type and lift experience
    Hydro-blasterHigh-pressure water jettingJetting training and safety record
    Tube testerEddy current, IRIS, other methodsNDT certificate in method
    Exchanger fitterGaskets, bolting, reassemblyFlange skills and references
    Retubing crewTube removal and expansionRetubing projects and tooling
    Cleaning throughput = Tubes cleaned ÷ Crew shifts
    🧪
    Practical Example: A hydro-blasting crew cleaned 3,600 tubes in 6 crew shifts. Throughput = 3,600 ÷ 6 = 600 tubes per crew shift.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Using untrained workers for hydro-blasting
    • Sending tube testers without the right NDT method
    • Not separating roles in the crew plan

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    A crew cleaned 2,800 tubes in 4 crew shifts. What is the throughput?

    Show answer
    2,800 ÷ 4 = 700 tubes per crew shift.
    Level 2 · Core concepts · Intermediate Concepts

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Screening exchanger specialists step by step

    Start with the exchanger list: number of units, tube counts, cleaning method, testing scope and likely repairs. Convert to crews by role. Then screen each candidate on the equipment and methods they have used, their safety training and references.

    For hydro-blasting, check training and the safety record closely, because injuries from high-pressure water can be severe. For tube testing, verify NDT certificates with the issuer. For fitters, check flange and bolting competence.

    StepOutput

    2. Convert to crews by roleCrew plan by shift

    4. Screen hydro-blastersTrained, safe crews

    6. Screen fittersApproved fitters
    Hydro-blasting crews needed = Tubes to clean ÷ (Tubes per crew shift × Shifts available)
    🧪

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Planning one crew type for all exchanger work
    • Skipping safety record checks for hydro-blasters
    • Not verifying tube tester certificates

    📊 Intermediate Concepts: Exchanger KPIs: bundle cycle time, test results and leaks

    Track bundle cycle time from isolation to return, tubes tested and plugged, leak test results after reassembly and safety events during hydro-blasting. Compare with the plan to see whether crews and methods worked.

    Rate each crew and specialist. Fast crews that leave leaks or unsafe practice are not the best choice, even if they save time.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Counting units tested twice as one pass
    • Not recording plug locations
    • Valuing speed over safety in jetting crews

    Level 4 · Expert · Advanced Concepts

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: retube decisions, specialist contractors and emergent repairs

    At expert level you help the client plan for emergent exchanger work. Tube testing may show that a bundle passes the plugging limit and must be retubed or replaced. Retubing needs specialist crews and tools, and sometimes a shop. Have contractors on standby when a high plugging result is likely.

    You also coordinate specialist contractors, such as tube testing companies and jetting firms, so they arrive in the right order: pull, clean, test, repair, reassemble and test. Gaps between steps waste days on the critical path.

    SituationExpert response
    Plugging above limitRetube crew or replacement bundle plan
    Testing finds many defectsExtra repair crews from standby list
    Jetting firm lateBackup contractor agreed in advance
    Steps out of orderSequenced schedule with handover points
    Repeated foulingLessons learned on cleaning method
    Tubes that can still be plugged = Plugging limit × Total tubes − Tubes already plugged
    🧪
    Practical Example: A bundle has 1,000 tubes and the limit is 10 percent. 82 tubes are already plugged. Tubes that can still be plugged = 0.1 × 1,000 − 82 = 100 − 82 = 18. If testing finds 25 bad tubes, the limit is exceeded and the retube plan is triggered.

    ⚠️ Common mistakes

    • Waiting for test results before thinking about retubing
    • Booking contractors with no sequence
    • Ignoring repeated fouling patterns

    ✍️ Hands-on Practice

    A bundle has 1,400 tubes, a 10 percent limit and 120 plugged. How many more can be plugged?

    Show answer
    0.1 × 1,400 − 120 = 140 − 120 = 20 tubes.

    🏗️ Real-World Project: Vetting a valve team for a refinery outage

    Skill: Valve repair technician vetting

    The valve list has 240 isolation valves at 5 hours each and 60 relief valves at 8 hours each. There are 10 shifts of 12 hours at 0.8 productivity. The field shop has 3 relief valve benches doing 5 valves a shift. Of 300 valves tested, 285 passed first time. 60 relief valves were returned and 57 had complete records.

    ItemData
    Isolation valves240 at 5 hours
    Relief valves60 at 8 hours
    Shifts10 of 12 hours
    Productivity0.8
    Benches3 at 5 valves a shift
    Results285 of 300 first pass, 57 of 60 traceable

    Your tasks

    1. Calculate technician-hours and headcount.
    2. Calculate the shifts needed for relief valve bench tests.
    3. Calculate the first-test pass rate.
    4. Calculate relief valve traceability.
    5. Suggest one control before installation.

    Check your work

    Show worked solution
    1) Hours = 240 × 5 + 60 × 8 = 1,200 + 480 = 1,680. Hours per technician = 12 × 10 × 0.8 = 96. Headcount = 1,680 ÷ 96 = 17.5, so 18 technicians. 2) Bench capacity = 3 × 5 = 15 a shift. 60 ÷ 15 = 4 shifts. 3) Pass rate = 285 ÷ 300 × 100 = 95 percent. 4) Traceability = 57 ÷ 60 × 100 = 95 percent. 5) Example: retest the 3 relief valves without complete records before they are installed.

    Security & Compliance

    Data protection, access, fraud, safety and legal risk for this role. Each skill has a security question in the Assessment.

    🔐 Security & Compliance: the big picture

    Mechanical integrity staffing handles personal data and safety-critical records: IDs, certificates, accreditation details, test records, references and pay details. These records prove that valves, turbines and exchangers are fit for service. A fake accreditation or edited test record can hide a dangerous fault.

    Security here means protecting personal data, verifying certificates and shop accreditations, keeping test and clearance records accurate and locked, preventing fraud in day rates and timesheets and following safety rules for high-pressure work. Confirm the exact legal duties with your privacy, quality and safety teams.

    Security riskWhy it mattersControl
    Fake shop accreditationUnsafe relief valve repairsCheck accreditation register and scope
    Edited test recordsHidden faultsLocked records with audit trail
    Fake NDT or jetting certificatesUnsafe workIssuer verification
    Inflated day rates or daysCost fraudTimesheets matched to gate records
    Specialist data leakPrivacy breachAccess limits on pool data

    🛡️ Security for: Valve repair technician vetting

    • Check relief valve shop accreditation on the official register.
    • Lock valve test records after sign-off and keep an audit trail.
    • Limit access to technician records to staff who need them.

    🛡️ Security for: Turbine overhaul engineer sourcing

    • Keep OEM technical documents confidential under the client's agreements.
    • Match specialist day rates to signed timesheets and gate records.
    • Store clearance records in a controlled system.

    🛡️ Security for: Heat exchanger specialist screening

    • Verify jetting training and NDT certificates with issuers.
    • Keep plug maps and test reports in a controlled system.
    • Share exchanger records only with the client's integrity team.

    🧪 Measuring security

    Record lock rate = Signed records locked against editing ÷ Signed records × 100
    🧪
    Worked example: For the outage, 820 valve, turbine and exchanger records were signed. 812 were locked in the controlled system and 8 were still in editable spreadsheets. Lock rate = 812 ÷ 820 × 100 = 99 percent. The 8 records are moved and locked before handover.

    Security checklist

    • Accreditations are checked on official registers.
    • Certificates are verified with issuers.
    • Signed test and clearance records are locked.
    • Day rates and days are matched to timesheets and gate records.
    • Specialist pool data is access-controlled.
    • Staff are trained on privacy and fraud each year.

    💬 Interview Questions: Valve repair technician vetting

    What is a pressure relief valve and why is it special?

    Show model answer
    It opens at a set pressure to protect equipment from over-pressure. Its repair and testing must be precise and is often limited to accredited organisations, so vetting is stricter.

    How do you vet a control valve technician?

    Show model answer
    Ask about actuator and positioner types, calibration, trim work and tests run, and take references from shop supervisors or instrument leads.

    The valve shop has a queue of relief valves. What do you do?

    Show model answer
    Check bench capacity, add benches or shifts, prioritise valves by start-up sequence and update the client on timing.

    Why record as-found tests?

    Show model answer
    They show the valve's condition before repair, which helps the client judge reliability and inspection intervals.

    200 valves at 6 hours each over 10 shifts of 12 hours at 0.8 productivity. How many technicians?

    Show model answer
    1,200 hours ÷ 96 = 12.5, so 13 technicians.

    💬 Interview Questions: Turbine overhaul engineer sourcing

    What does a turbine overhaul involve?

    Show model answer
    Removing casings, lifting and inspecting the rotor, repairing blades, seals and bearings, measuring clearances, reassembling, aligning and supporting run tests.

    Who usually leads the technical work?

    Show model answer
    An OEM or service company technical advisor or field service engineer, with agency mechanics, riggers and inspectors working under their direction.

    A key turbine mechanic falls ill. What do you do?

    Show model answer
    Call the named backup, check the pool and OEM for cover, and tell the client and advisor at once with any impact on the schedule.

    How do you screen a turbine mechanic?

    Show model answer
    Ask about models worked on, tasks done, measurements taken and who they reported to, and take a reference from a technical advisor.

    An advisor costs $2,000 a day for 14 days plus $4,000 travel. What is the cost?

    Show model answer
    2,000 × 14 + 4,000 = 28,000 + 4,000 = $32,000.

    💬 Interview Questions: Heat exchanger specialist screening

    What roles are needed for exchanger work?

    Show model answer
    Bundle extractor operators, hydro-blasters, tube testers, exchanger fitters and sometimes retubing crews, plus riggers and crane operators.

    Why screen hydro-blasters closely?

    Show model answer
    High-pressure water jetting can cause severe injuries. Operators must be trained and have a good safety record.

    Testing shows a bundle above the plugging limit. What happens?

    Show model answer
    The bundle must be retubed or replaced as the client decides. A standby retube crew or spare bundle plan avoids a long delay.

    How do you verify a tube tester?

    Show model answer
    Check the NDT certificate for the method, such as eddy current, verify it with the issuer and check the eye test date.

    A bundle has 1,500 tubes and 60 are plugged. What is the plugging percent?

    Show model answer
    60 ÷ 1,500 × 100 = 4 percent.

    What is Welding Inspection? Role, Responsibilities & Fundamentals

    Start from zero: what quality means for welded work, how QA and QC differ, how the ten welding-inspection roles fit together, and the welding basics every inspector must know.

    🧭 What is Welding Inspection (QA/QC)?

    A weld is often hidden inside a pipe, pressure vessel or structure, and failure can be catastrophic. Welding inspection is the systematic checking of materials, procedures, welders, welding work and finished welds against the drawings, specification, code and contract, and recording the evidence.

    QA (Quality Assurance)QC (Quality Control)
    FocusProcess and systemProduct and weld
    NaturePreventiveDetective
    Welding examplesWPS approval, welder qualification, ITP, supplier approval, auditsVisual check, NDT, dimensions, hydrotest, NCRs
    Question asked"Are we welding the right way?""Is this weld acceptable?"
    💡
    Memory aid: QA builds the fence; QC checks nothing escaped. A good welding inspection professional does both.

    🔥 Welding Basics Every Inspector Must Know

    Welding joins metals by fusion, usually with a filler metal and protection from air (flux or shielding gas).

    ProcessNameKey points
    SMAWStick / MMAFlux-coated electrode; portable; slag must be removed; low-hydrogen electrodes need baking
    GTAWTIGNon-consumable tungsten, inert gas; clean, precise; used for root passes and stainless
    GMAWMIG/MAGContinuous wire with shielding gas; fast; risk of lack of fusion
    FCAWFlux-coredTubular wire; high deposition; slag
    SAWSubmerged arcArc under flux; thick plate, high quality, automated
    TopicTypes
    Joint typesButt, T, corner, lap, edge
    Weld typesGroove (butt) weld, fillet weld, plug/slot weld
    Positions1G/1F flat, 2G/2F horizontal, 3G/3F vertical, 4G/4F overhead, 5G/6G pipe fixed
    EXAMPLE · Reading a weld

    A pipe butt weld with a V-groove: root gap 2–3 mm, root pass by GTAW, fill and cap by SMAW. The inspector checks fit-up, root pass, interpass cleaning and the final cap.

    ⚠️
    Safety first: fumes, arc flash, hot work permits, confined spaces and radiography exclusion zones are inspection hazards too. Wear the correct PPE.

    WPSWelding Procedure Specification: written instructions to weld
    PQRProcedure Qualification Record: test evidence proving the WPS
    WPQ/WQTWelder qualification: proves the welder’s skill
    MTCMaterial Test Certificate: chemistry and mechanical properties
    ITPInspection and Test Plan
    NDTNon-destructive testing

    Heat inputEnergy per length of weld (kJ/mm)

    ✍️ Explain QA vs QC using a pipe welding example.
    Model answer: QA: approving the WPS, qualified welders and ITP before work. QC: visual and NDT inspection of the finished weld. QA prevents; QC detects.

    Codes, Procedures, Materials & Documentation

    Know exactly what you inspect against: codes, WPS/PQR/WPQ, drawings, weld symbols, materials and records.

    🏛️ Where Requirements Come From

    Order of authority: law/regulation → contract → code/standard → project specification → drawing. If two documents conflict, raise a Technical Query; never guess.

    Code / StandardUsed for
    ASME Section IXWelding and brazing qualification (WPS/PQR/WPQ)
    ASME B31.3 / B31.1Process / power piping
    ASME Section VIIIPressure vessels
    AWS D1.1Structural steel welding
    API 1104Cross-country pipelines
    ISO 3834Quality requirements for fusion welding
    ISO 15614 / ISO 9606Procedure and welder qualification (European/ISO)
    ISO 5817Weld imperfection quality levels B, C, D
    ISO 9712 / SNT-TC-1ANDT personnel certification

    📋 WPS, PQR and WPQ

    DocumentPurposeInspector checks
    WPSTells the welder how to weldCorrect WPS for the joint, current revision
    PQRProves the WPS with testsTest results meet code; WPS is backed by a PQR
    WPQQualifies the welderValid for process, material, thickness, position; not expired
    • Essential variables (process, base metal group, filler class, thickness range, preheat, PWHT) need requalification if changed.
    • Non-essential variables can change with a WPS revision.
    • Welder continuity: qualification lapses if the welder does not use the process within the period required by the code.
    💡
    Always check the welder ID against the welder’s qualification before the first weld, not after the report.

    📐 Drawings & Weld Symbols

    The welding symbol tells weld type, size, length, finish and where to weld. Reference line with arrow: arrow side below the line, other side above it; flag = field weld; circle = weld all around; tail = WPS/process note.

    EXAMPLE

    A fillet symbol with 6 on the arrow side means a 6 mm leg fillet on the arrow side. Check the actual leg with a fillet gauge.

    🧱 Materials, Consumables & Traceability

    • Verify base metal and filler against MTC (heat number, grade, chemistry, mechanicals) at receiving.
    • PMI (positive material identification) confirms alloy grades such as stainless or Cr-Mo.
    • Low-hydrogen electrodes: bake and hold in a heated oven; flux-cored and SAW flux stored dry.
    • Transfer heat numbers when cutting pipe so traceability is not lost.
    • Use a weld map / isometric to link every joint to welder, WPS, filler batch, NDT and date.
    ⚠️
    Wrong material or wet electrodes cause defects that no later inspection can fully fix. Check before welding.

    🗂️ Documentation & Document Control

    If it is not recorded, it did not happen. Use controlled documents (latest revision only), signed reports and a final quality dossier with MTCs, WPS/PQR/WPQ, NDT reports, calibration certificates, NCRs and test certificates.

    • Reports must be factual, dated, legible, traceable and signed.
    • Obsolete drawings must be removed from the work area.
    • Technical documentation: clear, short, evidence-based; state requirement, finding and conclusion.

    Module 03 · Intermediate

    Inspection Stages, Visual Examination & Measurement

    Perform inspection correctly at every stage, with calibrated tools and objective records.

    🔍 The Three Stages of Weld Inspection

    StageChecks
    Before weldingDocuments (WPS, WPQ), material ID, joint preparation and cleanliness, fit-up gap, alignment, preheat, consumable storage, equipment settings
    During weldingParameters (current, voltage, travel speed), preheat and interpass temperature, root pass quality, interpass cleaning, sequence, shielding gas flow, welder compliance
    After weldingVisual examination, dimensions, NDT, PWHT records, repairs, pressure test, marking and final records
    EXAMPLE · Real scenario

    A pipe spool root is welded with a 5 mm gap instead of 2–3 mm. Caught during fit-up inspection, it is corrected in minutes. Found after RT, it is a repair costing hours and an NCR.

    💡
    The cheapest place to find a defect is before welding.

    👁️ Visual Examination (VT) and Gauges

    VT is the first and most used method. Good lighting (usually ≥ 500 lux), a clean surface and close access are required.

    ToolMeasures
    Weld gauge / fillet gaugeLeg length, throat, reinforcement
    Hi-Lo gaugeMisalignment of pipes or plates
    Bridge cam gaugeUndercut depth, reinforcement, bevel angle
    Vernier / micrometerThickness, diameter, small dimensions
    Tape / steel ruleLength, spacing
    Mirror, torch, borescopeHidden or internal surfaces
    • Typical VT findings: cracks, porosity, undercut, overlap, spatter, incomplete fill, excess reinforcement, arc strikes.
    • Cracks are never acceptable in any common code.

    🌡️ Preheat, Interpass and PWHT

    • Preheat slows cooling, reduces hardness and hydrogen cracking risk; check it with temperature sticks or a contact pyrometer.
    • Interpass temperature has a maximum to protect toughness.
    • PWHT (post-weld heat treatment) relieves residual stress; review the chart for soak temperature, time, heating and cooling rate.
    • Thicker, higher-carbon-equivalent steels need higher preheat.
    EXAMPLE

    WPS requires 150 °C minimum preheat. A temperature stick reads 90 °C. Stop work, reheat, record the temperature and only then release.

    🧰 Calibration, Checklists & Sampling

    • Every measuring instrument must show valid calibration with a label and register entry.
    • If a tool is found out of calibration: quarantine, assess the effect on past measurements, re-check where necessary, record it.
    • Checklists keep inspections consistent; include item, requirement, result, accept/reject, signature.
    • Sampling (e.g. 10% RT) follows the code or spec; extra samples apply if rejects are found.
    • Mark inspection status clearly: accepted, rejected, on hold.
    ✍️ A fillet gauge is overdue for calibration. What do you do?
    Model answer: Remove it from use and label it, use a valid gauge, assess previous measurements since the last valid calibration, re-inspect if needed, raise an NCR if there is impact, and update the register.

    NDT, Mechanical Testing & the Inspection Test Plan

    Choose the right test for the right flaw, and plan inspection with hold and witness points.

    📡 Non-Destructive Testing (NDT) Methods

    MethodFindsNotes
    VTSurface flawsFirst method, lowest cost
    PT (Penetrant)Surface-breaking flaws on any non-porous metalClean, apply penetrant, dwell, remove, develop
    MT (Magnetic particle)Surface/near-surface flaws in ferromagnetic steelNot for austenitic stainless
    RT (Radiography)Volumetric flaws: porosity, slag, lack of penetrationRadiation safety zone; film or digital
    UT (Ultrasonic)Planar flaws (cracks, lack of fusion), thicknessNeeds skilled operator
    TOFD / PAUTAccurate flaw sizing, recordableReplaces RT in many projects
    OthersFerrite, hardness, leak, PMIUsed for special alloys and service
    💡
    Rule of thumb: surface = PT/MT, volumetric = RT, planar = UT/TOFD/PAUT.
    EXAMPLE

    Suspected toe cracks on a carbon steel nozzle weld: use MT. Same on stainless: use PT. Suspected lack of fusion in a thick butt weld: use UT/PAUT.

    🔨 Destructive Tests (Procedure Qualification)

    TestShows
    TensileStrength of the joint
    Bend (face/root/side)Ductility and soundness
    Charpy impactToughness at the design temperature
    Hardness surveyRisk of hard zones / cracking
    Macro/micro examinationPenetration, fusion, HAZ, defects

    💧 Pressure & Leak Testing

    • Hydrotest usually at 1.5× design pressure per code; check gauge calibration, venting, safety and hold time.
    • Pneumatic test stores more energy and is more hazardous: strict safety control.
    • Record gauge IDs, pressures, hold times, results and witnesses.

    📋 Inspection & Test Plan (ITP)

    CodeMeaningEffect
    H — HoldStop until inspected and releasedMandatory
    W — WitnessParty notified; may attendProceed if absent after notice
    R — ReviewDocument reviewRecords check
    S — SurveillanceRandom monitoringNo notice
    EXAMPLE · ITP extract

    Material receiving (H) → Fit-up (H, client W) → Weld visual (H) → NDT 10% RT (R) → PWHT (R) → Hydrotest (H).

    ⚠️
    Welding past a hold point without release is a process nonconformance even if the weld looks good.

    Weld Defects, NCR, Repair, Root Cause & CAPA

    Identify defects, control nonconformance, repair properly, find true root causes and stop recurrence.

    🧩 Common Weld Defects: Cause and Prevention

    DefectTypical causePrevention
    PorosityMoisture, contamination, gas lossDry consumables, clean joint, check gas flow
    UndercutHigh current, wrong angleCorrect parameters and technique
    Lack of fusionLow heat, poor manipulationRight settings, clean bevel
    Incomplete penetrationSmall gap, large root faceCorrect fit-up
    Slag inclusionPoor interpass cleaningClean between passes
    Cracks (hot/cold)Hydrogen, restraint, high hardness, low preheatPreheat, low-hydrogen consumables
    OverlapLow travel speed or heatControl technique
    DistortionUneven heat inputBalanced sequence, fixtures, low heat input
    📝
    Hydrogen cracking needs three things together: hydrogen, tensile stress and a hard microstructure. Remove any one to prevent it.

    🚩 Nonconformance and the NCR Process

    Flow: Identify → Contain → Raise NCR → Disposition → Correct → Verify → Close.

    • An NCR states the requirement, the actual finding, location/ID and evidence.
    • Disposition options: Rework/repair, Use-as-is (needs engineering/client approval), Reject/scrap, Return to supplier.
    • Never let the inspector alone approve use-as-is on a code-governed weld.

    🛠️ Weld Repair

    • Locate and mark the defect (use NDT results).
    • Remove by grinding or gouging; confirm removal with PT/MT.
    • Re-weld using an approved WPS and qualified welder.
    • Re-inspect with the same method plus the original NDT; extend NDT if repairs are frequent.
    • Limit repair attempts per the code/spec; excessive repairs need engineering review.
    EXAMPLE

    RT shows slag at a pipe weld. Mark, gouge, confirm by PT, rewelded by a qualified welder, then RT again. Record everything against the same weld number with an R1 suffix.

    🧠 Root Cause Analysis (RCA) Tools

    ToolUse
    5 WhysAsk "why?" repeatedly until the system cause is found
    Fishbone (Ishikawa)Group causes: Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, Environment
    ParetoFind the few defects causing most losses
    Fault tree / 8DComplex or customer-level problems
    EXAMPLE · 5 Whys

    Porosity → Gas coverage lost → Wind at the site → No windshield → Not in the job plan → Root cause: planning checklist lacks a weather-protection step.

    ♻️ CAPA: Correction, Corrective & Preventive Action

    TypeMeaning
    CorrectionFix the specific weld (repair)
    Corrective actionRemove the cause so it does not recur (e.g. revise the checklist, retrain)
    Preventive actionStop a potential problem elsewhere (apply the fix on other projects)
    • Assign owner and due date.
    • Verify effectiveness after 30–90 days using data. If the same NCR returns, reopen RCA.
    • Training alone is rarely a sufficient corrective action.

    Module 06 · Expertise

    Planning, Coordination, Data, Audits, Design Review & Leadership

    Work at expert level: plan and coordinate inspection across teams, turn data into decisions, audit and lead.

    📅 Planning, Scheduling, Coordination & Supervision

    • Plan inspection from the project schedule and ITP; send notifications within the agreed notice period.
    • Allocate resources by critical path, hold-point deadlines, inspector qualification and NDT equipment availability.
    • Track progress in Excel/ERP: welds completed, inspected, accepted, repaired, pending.
    • Coordinate with production, planning, NDT, client and subcontractors. Use short daily meetings and a shared hold-point register.
    • Supervise: brief the team, check safety (permits, PPE), review reports, coach inspectors, escalate issues early.
    EXAMPLE

    Three spools hit the same hydrotest hold on Friday but only one test pump is available. The planner and coordinator re-sequence tests and notify the client in advance, avoiding idle crews.

    📈 KPIs, Trend Analysis & Dashboards

    KPIMeaning
    Weld repair rateRepaired welds ÷ welds inspected × 100
    First Pass YieldWelds accepted first time ÷ inspected × 100
    NCR rate / ageingNCRs per period; average days open
    CAPA on-time closureCAPAs closed on time ÷ due × 100
    Hold-point complianceHold points released before work ÷ total (leading indicator)
    COPQRework + scrap + delays + penalties
    EXAMPLE

    18 of 600 welds repaired = 3% repair rate. If the target is ≤ 2%, break the data down by welder, process, position and defect to find the driver.

    • Excel: pivot tables, filters, charts. Power BI: dashboards with slicers by project, welder, defect.
    • A dashboard should answer one question and end with an action owner.
    • Distinguish leading indicators (predict) from lagging ones (report results).

    🔎 Audits & ISO 3834

    • First-party (internal), second-party (customer), third-party (certification).
    • Audit flow: plan, checklist, opening meeting, evidence collection, findings, closing meeting, report, follow-up.
    • Major findings: systemic failure or requirement not met affecting product/system. Minor: isolated lapse.
    • ISO 3834 evaluates a fabricator’s welding quality capability (personnel, equipment, WPS, inspection, NDT, records).

    🧪 Design Review & Advanced Technical Analysis

    • Check weldability: joint design, access for welding and for inspection, material compatibility, distortion control, NDT feasibility.
    • Review calculations: weld size, throat, strength; confirm against the code.
    • For service issues use failure analysis: fractography, metallography, hardness, chemistry.
    • Advanced NDT (PAUT/TOFD) for accurate flaw sizing and fitness-for-service decisions.

    🌟 Leadership & Quality Culture

    • Lead by example: follow every hold point yourself.
    • Give everyone stop-work authority for safety and quality.
    • Treat NCRs as learning, not blame; reward early reporting.
    • Mentor juniors, share lessons learned across projects, and report clearly to management.
    💡
    Culture shows when schedule pressure is high. Experts protect the hold point.

    Practice: Run Welding Inspection on a Pipe Spool Project

    Apply everything end to end: requirements, planning, inspection, NCR/CAPA and reporting.

    0 / 0 correct

    🏗️ Scenario

    You inspect 300 carbon steel spools to ASME B31.3. The ITP requires 10% RT and a hydrotest. In week 3: repair rate is 9% (target ≤ 3%), two joints were welded without fit-up release, and a Hi-Lo gauge is a month past calibration.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Before welding

    ✍️ List the documents and checks needed before production welding.
    Model answer: Approved drawings (latest), project spec, ASME B31.3, WPS/PQR, welder qualifications, approved ITP, NDT procedures and personnel, MTCs, consumable control, calibration register, NCR procedure.
    Q. Two joints were welded without fit-up release. What first?
    • A Ignore
    • B Raise an NCR, hold related work and evaluate the welds (extra NDT if needed)
    • C Delete the hold point
    • D Blame the client

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Calibration and measurement

    ✍️ What do you do about the overdue Hi-Lo gauge?
    Model answer: Quarantine it, use a calibrated gauge, assess measurements taken since the last valid calibration, re-check affected joints if necessary, raise an NCR where there is impact, and update the calibration register.

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Root cause and CAPA

    ✍️ Repair rate is 9%. How do you investigate and prevent recurrence?
    Model answer: Break down repairs by welder, process, position, defect, shift and consumable batch (Pareto). Use 5 Whys/fishbone on the top defect, correct the cause (e.g. fit-up control, consumable baking, coaching), add a hold-point check, set owners and due dates, then verify the repair rate falls over 30–60 days.
    Q. Most repairs are porosity on outdoor welds. Best corrective action?
    • A Reject all welders
    • B Add wind shielding and gas coverage checks to the work plan
    • C Skip RT
    • D Increase current

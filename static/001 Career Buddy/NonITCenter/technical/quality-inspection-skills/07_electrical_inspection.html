    What is Electrical Inspection? Role, Responsibilities & Fundamentals

    Start from zero: what quality means for electrical installations and equipment, how QA and QC differ, how the ten electrical-inspection roles fit together, and the basics every inspector must know.

    🧭 What is Electrical Inspection (QA/QC)?

    Electrical inspection is the systematic checking and testing of electrical materials, equipment and installations (cables, switchgear, transformers, motors, earthing, lighting, control panels) against the drawings, specification, standard and contract, and recording the evidence. It covers visual checks, correct installation, measurements, functional tests and electrical safety.

    QA (Quality Assurance)QC (Quality Control)
    FocusProcess and systemProduct and installation
    NaturePreventiveDetective
    Electrical examplesITP, calibration system, supplier approval, audits, proceduresInsulation test, earth test, termination check, test reports, NCRs
    Question asked"Are we working the right way?""Is this installation acceptable?"
    💡
    Memory aid: QA builds the system that prevents errors; QC checks and tests the installation to catch them.

    ⚡ Electrical Basics Every Inspector Must Know

    An electrical installation moves energy from a source (utility or generator) through transformers, switchgear and cables to loads such as motors, lighting and heaters, with protection and earthing making it safe.

    QuantityUnitRemember
    Voltage (V)VoltElectrical "pressure" that drives current
    Current (I)AmpereFlow of charge; heats conductors
    Resistance (R)Ohm (Ω)Ohm's law: V = I × R
    Power (P)Watt (kW)Single phase P = V × I × cos φ; three phase P = √3 × V × I × cos φ
    FrequencyHertz50 Hz or 60 Hz supply
    TopicRemember
    Voltage levelsLow voltage (LV) up to 1 kV AC; medium and high voltage above (exact limits follow the standard)
    Three-phaseThree conductors 120° apart; correct phase sequence decides motor rotation
    ProtectionFuses, breakers and relays interrupt faults; RCDs detect earth leakage
    EarthingGives faults a low-impedance path so protection operates and touch voltage stays safe
    EXAMPLE · Real scenario

    A new 415 V feeder to a pump motor is cabled and terminated. Before energising, the inspector checks cable size and tag, termination tightness, phase identification and earthing, then measures insulation resistance and records it.

    📝
    Safety first: assume every conductor is live until proved dead. Isolation, lock-out/tag-out, PPE and permits apply to inspection and testing too.

    Drawings, Standards, Protection, Cables & Documentation

    Know exactly what you inspect against: electrical drawings, standards, ratings, earthing, cable data and records.

    🏛️ Where Requirements Come From

    Order of authority: law/regulation → contract → standard → project specification → drawing. If two documents conflict, raise a Technical Query; never guess.

    📐 Reading Electrical Drawings

    DrawingWhat it showsInspector uses it to
    Single-line diagram (SLD)Sources, transformers, breakers, buses, feeders and ratingsVerify equipment ratings and the supply path
    Schematic / control diagramControl and protection logic, contacts, relays, interlocksPlan functional and interlock tests
    Wiring / termination diagramTerminal numbers and wire numbersCheck each termination
    Layout / routing drawingEquipment, tray and conduit routes, earthing layoutCheck physical installation
    Cable scheduleCable tag, size, core, from–to, lengthMatch tag, size and route

    • Symbols and legend: use the standard symbol list (e.g. IEC 60617).
    • Revision control: inspect only to the latest approved revision; mark red-line changes for as-built.
    💡
    Trace one feeder end to end: SLD → cable schedule → wiring diagram → field termination. A mismatch anywhere is a nonconformance or a query.

    🛡️ Protection, Ratings and Earthing Systems

    Earthing systemIdea
    TN-S / TN-C-SNeutral and protective earth from source; fault current returns by metal path, so breakers or fuses operate
    TTInstallation has its own earth electrode; RCD protection is normally required
    ITIsolated or high-impedance source; first fault is monitored, second fault trips
    RatingCheck
    IP code (IEC 60529)First digit solids/dust, second digit water; must meet the area requirement
    Ex markingZone, gas group and temperature class must match the hazardous area
    Short-circuit ratingBreaking capacity must be at or above the prospective fault level
    Protection settingsOverload, short-circuit and earth-fault settings per the coordination study
    EXAMPLE · Real scenario

    A breaker rated 25 kA breaking capacity is installed where the study shows 31 kA fault level. It is nonconforming, because it may fail to interrupt a fault safely.

    🔗 Cables, Materials, Certificates & Traceability

    • Verify cable type, size, voltage grade and core count against the cable schedule and datasheet.
    • Check test certificates (factory tests, type tests) and match batch or drum numbers to the delivery.
    • Inspect for transport damage: cut sheath, crushed ends, moisture in end caps, drum condition.
    • Check bending radius, support spacing, segregation of power and signal cables, glands and lugs.
    • Segregate and tag rejected, on-hold and approved material.

    🗂️ Documentation & Document Control

    • Controlled documents: drawings, procedures, ITP, checklists, calibration records and test formats.
    • Records must be legible, dated, signed and traceable to the equipment, circuit and the instrument used.
    • Never use obsolete revisions; remove them from the work area.
    • Keep a final data book: test reports, certificates, NCRs, red-line drawings and as-built records.

    Inspection Stages, Test Instruments & Calibration

    Inspect and test correctly at every stage, with calibrated instruments and objective records.

    🔍 The Stages of Electrical Inspection

    StageChecks
    ReceivingDocuments, nameplate data, certificates, visual and transport damage
    InstallationMounting, cable routing, glands, lugs, labels, earthing, tightness of terminations
    Pre-commissioningInsulation, continuity, polarity, earth and protection tests while de-energised
    CommissioningEnergisation, phase sequence, voltage and current readings, functional and interlock tests
    HandoverPunch list closed, as-built drawings, test records and data book delivered
    💡
    The cheapest place to find an error is before energising: complete pre-commissioning checks first.

    📏 Electrical Test Instruments

    InstrumentTypical useNote
    Digital multimeterVoltage, resistance, continuityCheck category rating (CAT III / IV)
    Clamp meterLoad current without breaking the circuitClamp one conductor only
    Insulation tester (megger)Insulation resistance at 250 / 500 / 1000 V DC and higherIsolate first and discharge after
    Low-resistance ohmmeterContact and bonding resistance, µΩ–mΩUses high test current
    Earth testerEarth electrode and soil resistanceFall-of-potential method
    Loop impedance testerEarth fault loop impedance ZsVerify disconnection time
    RCD testerTrip current and timeTest at rated and 5× rated current
    Phase sequence meterPhase rotationNeeded before motor run
    Thermal imagerHot spots on live equipmentNeeds adequate load
    Relay test setSecondary injection of protection relaysVerify pickup and trip time
    Power quality analyserVoltage, harmonics, power factorRecord over a period
    📝
    Use instruments with the correct measurement category and a valid calibration label. Inspect leads and probes before every use.

    🧪 Good Testing Practice

    • Isolate, prove dead and secure before any de-energised test.
    • Disconnect or protect electronic devices (drives, PLC cards, surge protectors) before high-voltage insulation tests.
    • Note temperature and humidity: insulation resistance falls as temperature and moisture rise.
    • Discharge the stored charge after an insulation test, especially on long cables.
    • Record actual values with units, instrument ID and date, not only pass/fail.
    • Retest after any rework and attach the new record to the old one.

    🛠️ Calibration, Acceptance Criteria & Sampling

    • Calibration is traceable to national or international standards; each instrument has an ID, due date and status label.
    • If an instrument is found out of calibration, quarantine it and evaluate results since the last good calibration.
    • Sampling: test 100% of critical circuits (protection, earthing, emergency); sample low-risk repetitive items only as the ITP allows.
    • Use a checklist tied to the drawing revision for every equipment type.
    TestTypical acceptance idea (always follow the project standard)
    Insulation resistance, circuits up to 500 VTest at 500 V DC; at least 1 MΩ (IEC 60364-6)
    Insulation resistance, SELV / PELVTest at 250 V DC; at least 0.5 MΩ

    ⛑️ Electrical Safety

    The five safety rules for work on installations: disconnect completely, secure against reconnection, verify absence of voltage, earth and short-circuit where required, protect against adjacent live parts.

    • LOTO: each person fits a personal lock and tag on the isolation point.
    • Prove dead: test the tester on a known source, test the circuit, test the tester again.
    • Stored energy: discharge capacitors and long cables; release trapped mechanical energy in breakers.
    • Arc flash: know the incident energy and boundaries, and wear the rated PPE.
    • Permits: hold a permit to work and a clear scope; never work alone on live equipment.
    ⚠️
    If you are not sure a circuit is dead, treat it as live. Stop and ask.

    📋 Inspection & Test Plan (ITP) and Commissioning

    • The ITP lists each activity, the acceptance criteria, the reference document and who attends.
    • Hold point (H): work cannot continue until the inspector releases it. Witness (W): inspector may attend. Review (R): records are reviewed.
    • Typical hold points: earthing before backfill, cable insulation test before energising, protection settings before commissioning.
    • Functional tests (interlocks, trips, alarms, run test) follow an approved procedure and are recorded and signed.
    • Pre-commissioning is complete only when the punch list is cleared or accepted in writing.

    Nonconformance, Root Cause Analysis & CAPA

    Control nonconforming work, find true root causes and stop recurrence.

    🧩 Common Electrical Nonconformances

    IssueTypical causePrevention
    Loose terminationsWrong torque, no re-torqueCalibrated torque tools, torque marking, thermography
    Low insulation resistanceMoisture, damaged cableProtect cable ends, test before and after pulling
    Wrong cable size or tagDrawing mismatch, poor labellingCable schedule check, tag verification
    Poor earthing / high resistanceBad joints, dry soil, missing bondsJoint inspection, test before backfill
    Incorrect protection settingStudy not applied or outdatedSettings sheet with sign-off, relay test
    Wrong phase sequenceCores swapped at terminationPhase sequence test before motor run
    IP or Ex noncomplianceMissing gland or plug, wrong equipmentArea classification check, gland inspection

    🚩 Nonconformance and the NCR Process

    • Identify, tag and isolate the item immediately; do not energise nonconforming equipment.
    • Raise an NCR: description, circuit or tag, requirement, measured value, location, evidence.
    • Disposition: use-as-is (needs engineering/client approval), rework, repair, replace or return to vendor.
    • Re-inspect and retest after rework and close the NCR with verified evidence.

    🔎 Root Cause Analysis (RCA) Tools

    ToolUse
    5 WhysAsk why repeatedly until the process cause is reached
    Fishbone (Ishikawa)Group causes: man, machine, method, material, measurement, environment
    Pareto chartRank defects to attack the vital few
    FMEARank risks with RPN = Severity × Occurrence × Detection
    8D reportStructured team problem solving for customer issues

    ✅ CAPA: Correction, Corrective & Preventive Action

    LevelMeaningExample
    CorrectionFix this nonconformanceRe-terminate the 14 loose lugs at MCC-2
    CorrectiveRemove the root causeAdd torque-mark verification to the termination checklist
    PreventiveStop it in other placesApply the same check to all panels and use thermography at handover
    💡
    Verify effectiveness: the defect rate must fall and stay down for the monitoring period (for example 30–60 days) before closing CAPA.

    Module 06 · Expertise

    Planning, Data, KPIs, Audits & Leadership

    Work at expert level: plan across teams, turn data into decisions, audit and lead.

    📅 Planning, Scheduling, Coordination & Supervision

    • Plan inspections from the project schedule and ITP; give hold-point notices within the agreed time.
    • Allocate resources by critical path, inspector skill and test-equipment availability (relay set, megger, thermal imager).
    • Track progress in Excel/ERP: circuits received, inspected, tested, accepted, rejected, pending.
    • Coordinate with construction, commissioning, the utility, the client and the safety team through short daily meetings and a shared outage plan.
    • Supervise: brief the team, check permits and isolations, review test records, coach inspectors, escalate early.
    EXAMPLE · Real scenario

    A shutdown window allows only 8 hours for transformer testing, but the relay test set is also needed for the switchgear. The planner re-sequences the tests and books a second set so no isolation time is wasted.

    📊 KPIs & Dashboards

    MeasureMeaning
    First pass yieldCircuits passing tests the first time ÷ total tested
    Test completion %Tests completed ÷ tests planned
    NCR closure timeDays from NCR raised to closed
    Punch list closure rateItems closed ÷ items raised
    On-time inspectionInspections done by due date ÷ planned
    MTBF / MTTRMean time between failures / to repair
    Power factor / harmonic levelPower quality indicators from analyser data
    • Show trends on charts and dashboards (Excel/Power BI); act on patterns, not single readings.
    • A falling trend of insulation resistance on one feeder is an early warning even if it is still above the limit.

    🧾 Audits & Quality System

    • Internal audits verify that work follows ISO 9001 procedures; findings are graded and tracked to closure.
    • Supplier audits and factory acceptance tests reduce incoming risk.
    • Management review uses KPIs, audit results and CAPA status to set improvement goals.

    🧠 Design Review & Advanced Technical Analysis

    • Review designs for testability and maintainability: can each critical circuit actually be isolated and tested?
    • Check short-circuit level, cable sizing, voltage drop and protection coordination studies against the installed equipment.
    • Assess harmonics and power factor where drives and non-linear loads are present.
    • Analyse failures with data (test trends, thermography, event logs) before changing the design.
    • Record lessons learned and update checklists and ITPs.

    🏆 Leadership & Quality Culture

    • Lead by example: follow the procedure, measure honestly, report problems early.
    • Coach inspectors; share findings openly without blame.
    • Balance schedule pressure with quality and safety: never release a hold point without evidence.
    • Build a culture where anyone can stop the job for a safety or quality concern.

    Practice: Run Electrical Inspection on a Substation and Pump Motor Project

    Apply everything end to end: requirements, testing, analysis, earthing, NCR/CAPA and reporting.

    0 / 0 correct

    🏗️ Scenario

    You inspect a 415 V motor control centre (MCC) and a 55 kW pump motor feeder in a new substation. In week 3: the pump motor winding reads 0.6 MΩ at 500 V DC, the insulation tester is a month past its calibration date, and the earth fault loop impedance on one feeder is above the limit.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Before inspection

    ✍️ List the documents and checks needed before starting inspection and testing.
    Model answer: Latest approved drawings (SLD, schematics, cable schedule), purchase order/spec, ITP and checklists, equipment datasheets and certificates, approved test procedures and formats, calibration register with valid instruments, inspector competence and permits, NCR procedure, and an isolation and LOTO plan.
    Q. The insulation tester is a month past calibration. What first?
    • A Keep using it, it is probably fine
    • B Stop using it, tag it, and evaluate results taken since its last good calibration
    • C Remove the due date label
    • D Ask the operator to estimate the error

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Measurement and analysis

    ✍️ The motor winding reads 0.6 MΩ. What does it mean and what do you do?
    Model answer: It is below the minimum acceptable value, so the motor must not be energised. Disconnect the motor from the cable and retest each separately, check temperature and humidity, look for moisture or contamination, dry out or clean the winding, retest and record the polarization index, and raise an NCR if it still fails.
    Q. Which action is the best first step before blaming the motor?
    • A Disconnect the cable and retest the winding and the cable separately
    • B Energise the motor to dry it
    • C Change the colour of the lead
    • D Increase the test voltage to maximum

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Earthing, root cause and CAPA

    ✍️ The loop impedance on one feeder is too high. How do you investigate and prevent recurrence?
    Model answer: Check joints and terminations in the protective conductor, continuity of the earth path, cable length and size, and the protective device rating. Repair and retest, record before and after values, use 5 Whys to find why it was released out of limit, add an earth path test hold point and a signed record to the ITP, and retest the whole group of similar circuits.
    Q. Why does a high earth fault loop impedance matter?
    • A The protective device may not disconnect the fault in the required time
    • B The cable will be shinier
    • C The motor will run faster
    • D Nothing, it is only paperwork

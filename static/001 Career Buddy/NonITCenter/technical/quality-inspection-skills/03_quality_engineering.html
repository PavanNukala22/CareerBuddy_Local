    What is Quality Engineering? The Role & Fundamentals

    Start from zero: what quality engineering is, how it differs from QA, QC and inspection, the types of quality engineers, their responsibilities and the product lifecycle they support.

    🧭 What is Quality Engineering?

    Quality Engineering (QE) applies engineering methods, statistics and problem-solving to design quality into products and processes — so that defects are prevented, variation is controlled and customers get reliable products at the right cost.

    FunctionMain questionTypical activities
    InspectionIs this item good?Measure, examine, accept/reject
    Quality ControlIs the output meeting requirements?Inspection, testing, release
    Quality AssuranceIs our system preventing problems?Procedures, audits, training
    Quality EngineeringHow do we design, control and improve processes so defects can't happen?FMEA, control plans, SPC, capability, MSA, root cause, design review, supplier development
    💡
    A quality engineer is the engineer of the quality system: they turn requirements into controls, data into decisions and problems into permanent fixes.

    Customer QECustomer complaints and requirements8D reports, warranty analysis

    Requirements, Design Review, Tolerances & Standards

    Turn customer needs into clear, measurable and achievable requirements — and catch problems on paper before they reach production.

    🎯 From Customer Needs to CTQs

    Figure 2.1 · CTQ flow-down
    EXAMPLE · CTQ flow-down

    Customer need: "the valve must never leak". CTQs: seat flatness ≤ 0.01 mm, seat roughness Ra ≤ 0.8 μm, hydrotest 1.5× rated pressure with zero leakage. Each CTQ gets a measurement method and control in the control plan.

    📐 Drawings, Specifications & GD&T

    ElementQE checks
    Title block & revisionLatest approved revision, material, general tolerance
    Dimensions & tolerancesAre they functional and achievable by the process?
    GD&T & datumsDatums reflect how the part is located and used; feasible to measure
    Special characteristicsSafety/critical symbols clearly marked
    Notes & specificationsHeat treatment, coating, testing, packaging requirements
    Referenced standardsCorrect edition and clauses

    🧮 Tolerance Analysis & Engineering Calculations

    MethodHowWhen
    Worst caseAdd all tolerances: ± (t1 + t2 + …)Safety-critical, small volumes
    RSS (statistical)± √(t1² + t2² + …)High-volume, capable processes
    Monte CarloSimulate distributionsComplex assemblies
    EXAMPLE · Stack-up

    Four parts each ±0.05 mm. Worst case = ±0.20 mm. RSS = ±√(4 × 0.05²) = ±0.10 mm. If the assembly gap must be ±0.15 mm, worst case fails but RSS passes — only acceptable if each process is capable and centred.

    • Other common QE calculations: test pressures, sample sizes, capability indices, defect rates (PPM, DPMO), cost of quality.

    🗃️ Technical Documentation

    • QEs write procedures, work instructions, FMEAs, control plans, test reports, deviation requests and engineering change documentation.
    • Good technical documents are specific, measurable, controlled and traceable.
    • Engineering changes must go through change control: impact assessment, FMEA/control plan updates, customer approval where required, re-validation.

    ⚠️ FMEA — Failure Mode & Effects Analysis

    ColumnQuestion
    Function / stepWhat should it do?
    Failure modeHow could it fail?
    EffectWhat happens to the customer? → Severity (S) 1–10
    CauseWhy would it fail? → Occurrence (O) 1–10
    ControlsHow do we prevent/detect it? → Detection (D) 1–10
    RiskRPN = S × O × D, or AIAG-VDA Action Priority (High/Medium/Low)
    ActionsWhat will reduce the risk? Owner, date, re-rating
    EXAMPLE · PFMEA line
    StepFailure modeEffectSCauseODetectionDRPN
    Drill Ø10 holeOversize holeLoose bolt, joint failure8Worn drill4Final go/no-go gauge5160
    💡
    Reduce severity only by design change; reduce occurrence by preventing causes; improve detection last — prevention beats detection.

    Measurement Systems, SPC & Process Capability

    Prove that measurements can be trusted and that processes are stable and capable — the statistical core of quality engineering.

    📏 Measurement System Analysis (MSA)

    StudyAnswers
    BiasIs the average reading close to the true value?
    LinearityIs bias consistent across the range?
    StabilityDoes the system drift over time?
    Gauge R&RHow much variation comes from the gauge (repeatability) and operators (reproducibility)?
    Attribute agreementDo inspectors agree on pass/fail?
    %GRR (of tolerance or study variation)Decision
    < 10%Acceptable
    10–30%May be acceptable depending on application and cost
    > 30%Unacceptable — improve the measurement system
    • Number of distinct categories (ndc) should be ≥ 5.
    • Calibration proves accuracy against standards; MSA proves the whole system (gauge + method + people + environment) is fit for use.
    • Inspection tools and equipment must be maintained and verified (technicians' preventive maintenance).

    📊 Variation & Statistical Process Control (SPC)

    🌫️ Common cause

    Natural, random variation built into the process. Reduce it only by changing the process.

    ⚡ Special cause

    Unusual, assignable variation (tool break, new material lot). Find and remove it.

    ChartDataUse
    X̄-R / X̄-SVariables in subgroupsMachined dimensions
    I-MRIndividual variablesBatch processes, low volume
    p / npProportion / count defectivePass/fail inspection
    c / uCount of defectsDefects per unit
    • Control limits = mean ± 3σ of the process — not spec limits.
    • Signals: a point beyond limits, 7–8 points in a row on one side, 6 increasing/decreasing, cycles or trends (Western Electric / Nelson rules).
    • React with the control plan's reaction plan; never adjust a stable process for common-cause noise (over-adjustment increases variation).

    🎯 Process Capability

    IndexFormulaMeaning
    Cp(USL − LSL) ÷ 6σPotential capability — spread only
    Cpkmin(USL − μ, μ − LSL) ÷ 3σActual capability — spread and centring
    Pp / PpkSame, using overall (long-term) σPerformance over time
    EXAMPLE · Calculate Cpk

    LSL 9.90, USL 10.10, mean 10.04, σ 0.02. Cp = 0.20 ÷ 0.12 = 1.67. Cpk = min(0.06, 0.14) ÷ 0.06 = 1.00. Spread is fine but the process is off-centre — centre it to raise Cpk.

    CpkInterpretation
    < 1.00Not capable — defects expected
    1.00–1.33Marginal
    ≥ 1.33Capable (common minimum)
    ≥ 1.67Required for many critical/safety characteristics
    ⚠️
    Capability is only meaningful for a stable process (in statistical control) with a trustworthy measurement system.

    📈 Data Analysis & Dashboards

    • Use Excel/Power BI for Pareto charts, trend lines, histograms, control charts and KPI cards.
    • Segment data by line, shift, supplier, product, defect type.
    • Pair numbers with action: every red KPI needs an owner and next step.
    • Sampling: ISO 2859-1 (attributes) and ISO 3951 (variables) for acceptance sampling; use statistics, not habit.

    Nonconformance, Root Cause, DOE & CAPA

    Lead structured problem-solving from first containment to verified, permanent corrective action.

    🚩 Nonconformance Management

    Figure 5.1 · From nonconformance to CAPA
    • Disposition requires engineering judgement: function, safety, fit, reliability — supported by analysis or test.
    • Use-as-is and repair dispositions often need customer deviation/concession approval.
    • Track NCR cost (scrap, rework, sorting) to feed cost of quality.

    🧠 Structured Problem Solving: 8D & DMAIC

    8D stepContent
    D0Emergency response
    D1Form the team
    D2Describe the problem (5W2H, Is/Is-not)
    D3Interim containment
    D4Root cause — occurrence and escape (why not detected)
    D5Choose and verify permanent corrective actions
    D6Implement and validate
    D7Prevent recurrence — update FMEA, control plan, standards
    D8Recognise the team

    🔁 8D

    Best for customer complaints and specific problems needing containment and documentation.

    📐 DMAIC

    Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve, Control — data-driven Six Sigma projects to reduce chronic variation.

    🔬 Root Cause Tools & Troubleshooting

    ToolUse
    5 WhysDrill to a controllable cause (do separate chains for occurrence and escape)
    Fishbone (6M)Brainstorm causes
    Is / Is-notNarrow the problem by comparing where it is and isn't
    ParetoPrioritise the vital few
    Scatter / correlationTest relationships between variables
    Hypothesis testsProve differences statistically (t-test, chi-square)
    • Troubleshooting rule: confirm the root cause by turning the problem on and off.
    • Ask both "why did it occur?" and "why didn't our controls detect it?"

    🧪 Design of Experiments (DOE)

    DOE changes several process factors together in a planned way to find which ones affect the output and their best settings — far more efficient than changing one factor at a time.

    EXAMPLE · 2-factor DOE
    RunTemperaturePressureDefect %
    1LowLow4.0
    2HighLow1.5
    3LowHigh3.8
    4HighHigh1.2

    Temperature has a large effect (≈ 2.5% reduction); pressure a small one. Set temperature high, confirm with a validation run, then update the control plan.

    ✅ CAPA & Supplier Corrective Action

    ActionExample
    CorrectionSort and rework affected lots
    Corrective actionPoka-yoke fixture prevents wrong orientation
    Preventive actionApply fixture concept to similar parts
    VerificationZero recurrence in 3 months; Cpk ≥ 1.33
    StandardiseUpdate PFMEA, control plan, work instruction, training
    • Strength of controls: eliminate > error-proof > automate > detect > train > warn.
    • For supplier issues, issue a SCAR and require an 8D with evidence.

    Reliability, Cost of Quality, Improvement & Leadership

    Work at expert level: predict reliability, quantify quality costs, run improvement programmes, audit processes and lead the quality function.

    ⏳ Reliability Engineering Basics

    ReliabilityProbability a product performs its function for a given time under stated conditions.
    MTBFMean Time Between Failures (repairable items) = total operating time ÷ failures.
    MTTFMean Time To Failure (non-repairable items).
    Bathtub curveInfant mortality → useful life (random failures) → wear-out.
    Accelerated life testingHigher stress to reveal failures faster.
    EXAMPLE · MTBF

    10 pumps run 2,000 h each (20,000 h total) with 4 failures → MTBF = 20,000 ÷ 4 = 5,000 h.

    🚀 Lean Six Sigma & Continual Improvement

    • Six Sigma: reduce variation; 6σ ≈ 3.4 DPMO.
    • Lean: remove waste — defects, over-processing, waiting, motion, inventory.
    • Kaizen events and suggestion systems for daily improvement.
    • Error-proofing (poka-yoke) to make defects impossible.
    • Lessons learned fed into design reviews, FMEAs and standards.

    🔎 Audits & Compliance

    Audit typeFocus
    System auditQMS vs ISO 9001/IATF/AS9100
    Process auditIs the process run per control plan and work instructions?
    Product auditDoes finished product meet all requirements (customer view)?
    Layered process audits (LPA)Short, frequent checks by multiple management layers
    Supplier auditSupplier capability and compliance

    🧑‍💼 Leadership, Project Execution & Safety

    • Lead launches and projects: quality gates, risk registers, clear owners.
    • Supervise and develop teams: competence matrix, coaching, fair work allocation.
    • Report to management: KPIs, cost of quality, risks, decisions needed.
    • Integrate safety: special characteristics often protect user safety; never trade safety for schedule.
    • Influence without authority: data, collaboration with design/production/purchasing, and customer focus.

    Module 07 · Interview Preparation

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer.

    🧭 Role & Fundamentals

    What does a quality engineer do differently from a QC inspector?
    An inspector checks whether products meet requirements. A quality engineer designs and improves the controls: translates requirements into CTQs, builds FMEAs and control plans, proves measurement and process capability, leads root cause and CAPA, and drives improvement.
    Explain worst-case vs RSS tolerance stack-up.
    Worst case adds all tolerances and guarantees fit even at extremes; RSS takes the square root of the sum of squares, assuming independent, centred, capable processes, and gives a tighter realistic estimate.
    What is the difference between verification and validation?
    Verification proves the design meets specified requirements; validation proves the product meets the user's needs in intended use.
    How do you approach a design review?
    Check requirements, CTQs, tolerances and GD&T for function and manufacturability, review DFMEA and lessons learned, and ask how each critical feature will be measured and controlled.

    📊 Planning, FMEA & Statistics

    Walk me through building a PFMEA.
    Map the process flow, list functions and failure modes per step, rate severity of effects, identify causes and occurrence, list prevention/detection controls and detection ratings, prioritise with RPN or action priority, assign actions, re-rate, and link to the control plan.
    What is a control plan?
    A document listing each process step and characteristic with specification, measurement method, sample size/frequency, control method (e.g. SPC) and reaction plan.
    Explain Cp vs Cpk.
    Cp compares spec width with process spread (potential). Cpk also considers centring — the distance from the mean to the nearest spec limit divided by 3σ. Cpk ≤ Cp always.
    What is Gauge R&R and acceptable limits?
    It quantifies measurement variation from repeatability and reproducibility. Under 10% is acceptable, 10–30% conditionally, above 30% unacceptable; ndc should be at least 5.
    Control limits vs specification limits?
    Specification limits come from customer/design requirements; control limits are calculated from process data (±3σ) and show the process's natural variation.

    🛠️ Problem Solving & Leadership

    Describe the 8D process.
    D0 emergency response, D1 team, D2 problem description, D3 containment, D4 root cause for occurrence and escape, D5 choose and verify corrective actions, D6 implement and validate, D7 prevent recurrence by updating standards, D8 recognise the team.
    When would you use DOE?
    When several factors may affect an output and you need to find the significant ones and best settings efficiently, including interactions, instead of changing one factor at a time.
    How do you know a CAPA was effective?
    Define measurable criteria up front, monitor data for an adequate period, confirm no recurrence and improved capability, and update FMEA, control plan and work instructions.
    How would you reduce cost of poor quality?
    Quantify COPQ by category, Pareto the biggest losses, run root cause and improvement projects, shift spending to prevention (FMEA, poka-yoke, supplier development), and track savings.

    🏭 Scenario: Steel Mounting Bracket Launch

    Your plant is launching a stamped-and-welded steel mounting bracket for an automotive customer (IATF 16949). Critical characteristic ◆: hole position ±0.20 mm (seat-belt anchor). Trial run data: mean offset +0.08 mm, σ = 0.05 mm. Gauge R&R on the CMM fixture: 34%. PPAP due in 6 weeks. Two weld cracks were found in 300 trial parts.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Plan

    ✍️ List the APQP/PPAP deliverables you must prepare.
    Model answer: Process flow, DFMEA (from design), PFMEA, control plan, MSA studies, initial capability studies on special characteristics, dimensional results, material/performance test results (weld strength), work instructions, packaging, part submission warrant (PSW) and customer approvals.

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Measurement & Capability

    Q. Gauge R&R is 34%. What first?
    • A Run capability studies anyway
    • B Improve the measurement system before trusting capability data
    • C Ignore it
    • D Increase sample size only
    ✍️ Calculate Cp and Cpk for hole position (LSL −0.20, USL +0.20, mean +0.08, σ 0.05) and interpret.
    Model answer: Cp = 0.40 ÷ 0.30 = 1.33. Cpk = min(0.20−0.08, 0.08+0.20) ÷ 0.15 = 0.12 ÷ 0.15 = 0.80. The spread is acceptable but the process is off-centre; Cpk 0.80 is not capable for a critical characteristic (target ≥ 1.67). Centre the process (fixture/die adjustment), then re-study.

    3️⃣ Task 3 · FMEA & Control Plan

    ✍️ Write a PFMEA line and control plan entry for weld cracks.
    Model answer: PFMEA: Step: MIG weld bracket to base. Failure mode: weld crack. Effect: bracket detaches under load — safety (S=10). Cause: insufficient preheat/excess current/poor fit-up (O=4). Detection: visual 100% + periodic destructive peel test (D=5). Actions: parameter window via DOE, fixture improvement, error-proof current monitoring. Control plan: weld current and wire speed monitored each part with alarms; visual 100%; destructive peel test 1 per shift; reaction plan: stop, quarantine since last good test, NCR.

    4️⃣ Task 4 · Problem Solving

    Q. Which tool best finds the optimal welding current and wire-feed settings?
    • A 5 Whys only
    • B Design of Experiments
    • C Pareto chart only
    • D Customer survey
    ✍️ How will you prove the fix is effective before PPAP?
    Model answer: Run a validation lot (e.g. 300 parts) at the new settings: zero cracks in visual and destructive tests, Cpk ≥ 1.67 on hole position after centring with an acceptable MSA (<10%), update PFMEA/control plan, document in an 8D, and include results in the PPAP package.

    What is QA/QC? The Role, Responsibilities & Fundamentals

    Start from zero: what quality means, how QA and QC differ, what the QA/QC team does every day, and how the roles grow from technician to senior engineer.

    🧭 What is QA/QC?

    Quality means a product, structure or service meets every specified requirement — drawings, specifications, codes, the contract and the customer's needs — every time. The QA/QC function makes sure that happens and proves it with records.

    🛡️ Quality Assurance (QA)

    Prevents problems. Focuses on the process: procedures, quality plans, training, supplier approval, audits. Asks: "Are we doing the work the right way?"

    🔍 Quality Control (QC)

    Detects problems. Focuses on the product: inspection, measurement, testing, acceptance. Asks: "Is this product right?"

    QAQC
    FocusProcess / systemProduct / output
    NaturePreventive, proactiveDetective, reactive
    WhenBefore and during workDuring and after work
    Typical outputsQuality plans, procedures, ITPs, audit reportsInspection reports, test certificates, NCRs
    Main peopleQuality engineers, QA managersInspectors, technicians, QC engineers
    💡
    Simple memory aid: QA builds the fence; QC checks nothing escaped. A strong QA/QC professional does both.

    💼 The QA/QC Role — What You Actually Do

    Whether you work in manufacturing, construction, oil & gas, power, pharma or infrastructure, the QA/QC role has the same core purpose: confirm work meets requirements, stop nonconforming work, and improve the process so it doesn't happen again.

    📐 Reading Drawings & Specifications

    Revision blockWhat changed in each revision — always inspect to the latest approved revision

    Drawing: Ø40.00 +0.025 / -0.000. Acceptable range = 40.000 to 40.025 mm. A measurement of 40.030 mm is nonconforming, even though it "looks fine".

    Inspection, Measurement & Testing

    Perform inspections correctly: choose the right stage and method, use calibrated tools, sample sensibly, stay safe and record objective evidence.

    🔍 The Inspection Procedure

    An inspection procedure defines what to inspect, how, with which equipment, against what acceptance criteria, who may do it and how to record it.

    Figure 3.1 · Inspection cycle

    🗂️ Inspection by Stage

    TypeWhenTypical checks
    Receiving / incomingMaterial arrivesQuantity, damage, marking, heat numbers vs Material Test Certificates (MTCs)
    Source / vendor inspectionAt supplier before dispatchWitness tests, check documents
    First Article Inspection (FAI)First item of a new run or after a changeFull dimensional and functional check
    In-processDuring workFit-up before welding, surface prep before painting
    FinalBefore releaseDimensions, visual, tests, documentation complete
    Pre-dispatch / packingBefore shippingPreservation, packing, marking, shipping documents

    📏 Measurement Tools & Calibration

    ToolMeasuresTypical resolution
    Steel rule / tapeLength1 mm
    Vernier caliperLength, ID, OD, depth0.02 mm
    MicrometerPrecise OD/ID0.01–0.001 mm
    Height gauge / dial indicatorHeight, runout, flatness0.01 mm
    Bore gaugeInternal diameters0.001–0.01 mm
    Thread / plug / ring gaugesGo / No-Go limitsAttribute
    Weld gaugesFillet size, undercut, reinforcement0.5 mm
    DFT gaugeCoating thickness1 μm
    Hardness testerHardness (HRC, HB, HV)—
    CMMComplex geometry, GD&T~0.001 mm
    • Rule of thumb: instrument resolution ≈ 1/10 of the tolerance band.
    • Every instrument has a unique ID, calibration certificate, status label and due date.
    • Calibration must be traceable to national/international standards (e.g. NPL India, NIST).
    • If an instrument is found out of calibration, evaluate all product measured since its last good calibration.
    • MSA / Gauge R&R checks whether measurement variation is small enough to trust results (<10% good, 10–30% conditional, >30% unacceptable).

    🧪 Testing Methods: NDT & Destructive

    MethodDetectsNotes
    Visual (VT)Surface defectsFirst and most common inspection
    Liquid Penetrant (PT)Surface-breaking cracksAny non-porous material
    Magnetic Particle (MT)Surface / near-surface cracksFerromagnetic materials only
    Ultrasonic (UT)Internal defects, thicknessNeeds skilled operator; no radiation
    Radiography (RT)Internal volumetric defects (porosity, slag)Radiation safety controls required
    Pressure / leak testLeaks, strengthHydrotest, pneumatic test
    Destructive testsMechanical propertiesTensile, bend, impact, hardness, macro
    📌
    NDT personnel must hold valid qualifications (ISO 9712 or ASNT Level I/II/III). Results must be interpreted by an appropriately qualified level.

    📊 Checklists & Sampling

    • Checklists convert requirements into clear checks so nothing is missed — include reference, acceptance criteria, result and sign-off.
    • 100% inspection: critical, safety-related or small-quantity items.
    • Sampling (ISO 2859-1 / ANSI Z1.4): large lots; choose inspection level and AQL → sample size and accept/reject numbers.
    • Sampling is only valid if the process is stable — otherwise inspect more.
    EXAMPLE · AQL sampling

    Lot 1,200 pcs, general inspection level II, AQL 1.0 → sample 80 pcs. Accept the lot if ≤ 2 defectives, reject if ≥ 3.

    🦺 Safety, Status Marking & Records

    • Inspectors work around cranes, hot work, height, radiation and pressure — follow permits, PPE, barricades and exclusion zones.
    • Never sign off what you didn't see; never inspect an unsafe area.
    • Mark status clearly: accepted (green), rejected (red), hold (yellow).
    Inspection report must includeItem ID and traceability, drawing/spec and revision, method, instrument ID and calibration status, measured results, acceptance decision, inspector name/signature, date, photos where useful.

    Nonconformance, Troubleshooting, Root Cause & CAPA

    Control nonconforming work, troubleshoot systematically, find true root causes and make sure problems never come back.

    🚩 Nonconformance & the NCR Process

    A nonconformance is any failure to meet a requirement — product, process, material, documentation or supplier. ISO 9001 clause 8.7 requires nonconforming outputs to be identified and controlled.

    Figure 5.1 · NCR workflow

    🔴 Weak NCR

    "Weld bad. Fix it."

    🟢 Strong NCR

    "Spool SP-14 weld W3: visual inspection per ASME B31.3 found undercut 1.2 mm vs max 0.8 mm over 40 mm length (photo P-77, gauge WG-03). Spool tagged red in hold area B."

    🗂️ Disposition Types

    DispositionMeaningApproval
    ReworkRestore to full conformance with the original requirementQA; re-inspect
    RepairMake acceptable for use, not to original requirementEngineering + usually client
    Use-as-isAccept without changeEngineering justification + client concession
    ScrapCannot be usedQA; prevent reuse
    Return to vendorSupplier nonconformancePurchasing + SCAR
    RegradeUse for a less demanding applicationEngineering; keep traceability
    📌
    A Material Review Board (MRB) — QA, engineering, production and sometimes the client — decides significant dispositions.

    🧰 Troubleshooting Systematically

    1. Define the problem precisely: what, where, when, how many (Is / Is-not).
    2. Contain to protect the customer and stop more defects.
    3. Gather data: measurements, records, photos, process parameters, recent changes.
    4. List possible causes (fishbone 6M: man, machine, method, material, measurement, environment).
    5. Test causes with evidence; eliminate the ones data rules out.
    6. Confirm root cause — can you turn the problem on and off?

    🧠 Root Cause Analysis Tools

    ToolUse
    5 WhysAsk why repeatedly to reach a controllable cause
    Fishbone (Ishikawa)Brainstorm causes in 6M categories
    Pareto chartPrioritise the vital few problems (80/20)
    Is / Is-notNarrow where and when the problem occurs
    8DStructured team approach for customer/supplier problems
    FMEARate risks by severity, occurrence, detection to prevent failures
    EXAMPLE · 5 Whys — repeated undercut
    1. Why undercut? → Travel speed too high.
    2. Why? → Welder rushing to meet daily target.
    3. Why? → Target set without considering weld size.
    4. Why not detected earlier? → No in-process visual check after root/hot pass.
    5. Root causes: unrealistic targets + missing in-process check.

    🛠️ CAPA: Correction, Corrective & Preventive Action

    TermMeaningExample
    CorrectionFix the immediate problemGrind and re-weld undercut areas
    Corrective actionRemove root cause of existing problemRevise targets; add in-process visual check after hot pass
    Preventive actionRemove cause of a potential problemApply same controls to other welding crews
    • Prefer strong controls: error-proofing > automation > checklists > training > reminders.
    • Every action has an owner and a due date.
    • Verify effectiveness with data (e.g. no recurrence in next 50 welds) before closing.
    • Update procedures, ITPs, FMEAs and training records so the learning sticks.
    Q. Which is a true corrective action?
    • A Re-weld the defective joint
    • B Tell the welder to be careful
    • C Add a mandatory in-process check and revise unrealistic targets
    • D Close the NCR quickly

    Quality Data, Audits, Design Review & Leadership

    Work at expert level: turn quality data into decisions, audit systems, review designs, lead teams and drive continual improvement.

    📈 KPIs & Data Analysis

    KPIFormula / meaning
    First Pass Yield (FPY)Units passing first inspection ÷ units inspected × 100
    Weld repair rateRepaired welds ÷ welds inspected (by length or count) × 100
    NCR rateNCRs per period or per 1,000 units
    NCR ageingAverage days NCRs remain open
    CAPA on-time closureCAPAs closed by due date ÷ total due × 100
    Repeat NCR %NCRs with a recurring root cause ÷ total NCRs
    Supplier PPMDefective parts per million received
    Cost of poor quality (COPQ)Scrap + rework + warranty + penalties
    EXAMPLE · Calculating FPY

    240 spools inspected, 216 accepted first time → FPY = 216 ÷ 240 × 100 = 90%.

    📊 Trends, SPC & Dashboards

    • Trend analysis: plot defects over time to see if things improve or worsen.
    • Pareto analysis: focus on the few defect types causing most problems.
    • Control charts (SPC): show whether a process is stable; investigate points outside control limits or non-random patterns.
    • Process capability: Cp/Cpk compare process spread to tolerance; Cpk ≥ 1.33 is a common minimum target.
    • Excel / Power BI dashboards: pivot tables, filters by project/supplier/defect type, clear charts and action owners.
    💡
    A good dashboard answers one question ("Where are our quality losses coming from?") and leads to an action — not just a collection of charts.

    🔎 Audits

    TypeByPurpose
    First-party (internal)Own organisationCheck QMS compliance and effectiveness
    Second-partyCustomer of supplierApprove/monitor suppliers
    Third-partyCertification bodyISO certification
    • Plan → prepare checklist → opening meeting → collect evidence (interviews, records, observation) → findings → closing meeting → report → CAPA follow-up.
    • Grade findings: major nonconformity, minor nonconformity, observation / opportunity for improvement.

    📝 Design Review & Advanced Technical Analysis

    • QA/QC engineers review drawings and specifications for inspectability, clear tolerances, correct standards and realistic acceptance criteria.
    • Use calculations to verify requirements — e.g. hydrotest pressure, weld sizes, tolerance stack-ups.
    • Feed lessons learned from NCRs back into design reviews so the same issues aren't designed in again.
    • Advanced analysis: failure analysis, metallurgical review, statistical analysis of process data.
    EXAMPLE · Hydrotest pressure

    Design pressure 20 bar; piping code requires 1.5 × design pressure (simplified) → test pressure = 30 bar, held for the specified time with no leaks.

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer.

    🧭 Role & Fundamentals

    What is the difference between QA and QC?
    QA is process-focused and preventive — quality plans, procedures, training, audits and supplier control. QC is product-focused and detective — inspection, measurement and testing of the actual work. QA builds the system; QC verifies the output.
    What are the main responsibilities of a QA/QC engineer?
    Understand requirements, prepare quality plans and ITPs, coordinate and perform inspections, ensure calibrated tools and qualified people, review documentation, raise and close NCRs, drive root cause and CAPA, track KPIs and report to management.
    Why is traceability important?
    It links each item to its materials, processes, inspections and people, so if a problem is found you can identify affected items, prove conformance and comply with codes and contracts.
    How do you handle conflicting requirements between a drawing and a specification?
    Do not assume. Raise a Technical Query or RFI to the engineer/client, hold affected work if necessary, and proceed only with a documented answer.

    🔍 Inspection & Planning

    Explain an ITP and hold vs witness points.
    An Inspection and Test Plan lists every inspection/test in sequence with references, acceptance criteria, records and each party's involvement. A hold point stops work until released; a witness point needs notification, but work can proceed if the party doesn't attend after proper notice.
    How do you choose a measuring instrument?
    Match the measurement type, range and accuracy; aim for resolution around one-tenth of the tolerance; confirm calibration status and suitability (e.g. temperature, access).
    What do you do if a gauge is found out of calibration?
    Remove it from use, assess the error, identify all products measured since the last good calibration, re-check affected items if needed, raise an NCR where appropriate and record the evaluation.
    How do you plan inspections on a busy project?
    Derive inspection activities from the ITP and project schedule, send notifications within agreed notice periods, allocate qualified inspectors by priority and location, track planned vs completed in Excel/ERP and escalate bottlenecks early.
    Which NDT method would you use to find internal porosity in a butt weld?
    Radiography (RT) is well suited to volumetric defects like porosity; ultrasonic testing (UT) is an alternative, especially for planar defects and thick sections.

    🛠️ NCR, CAPA, Data & Leadership

    Walk me through how you handle a nonconformance.
    Identify and contain (tag, segregate), raise an NCR with requirement vs actual and evidence, agree disposition with engineering/MRB/client, implement and re-inspect, analyse root cause, implement CAPA, verify effectiveness and close.
    Rework vs repair vs use-as-is?
    Rework restores full conformance with the original requirement. Repair makes the item fit for use but not to the original requirement and usually needs client approval. Use-as-is accepts it unchanged with engineering justification and often a client concession.
    Which KPIs would you track for a QA/QC department?
    First pass yield, NCR rate and ageing, repeat NCR percentage, CAPA on-time closure, weld repair rate, supplier PPM, inspection backlog and cost of poor quality.
    How do you build a quality culture as a QA/QC lead?
    Set clear standards, involve production early, coach rather than police, share data openly, recognise good quality, use stop-work authority responsibly and fix systems instead of blaming people.

    🏗️ Scenario: Pipe Spool Fabrication Project

    Your company fabricates 300 carbon-steel pipe spools to ASME B31.3 for a refinery client. The contract requires an approved ITP, 10% RT of butt welds, hydrotest and a final quality dossier. In week 3: weld repair rate is 9% (target ≤ 3%), two fit-ups were welded without the hold-point release, and a vernier used in receiving inspection is found one month past its calibration due date.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Requirements & Planning

    ✍️ List the key documents you need before fabrication starts.
    Model answer: Approved drawings (latest revision), project specification, ASME B31.3, WPS/PQR and welder qualifications, approved ITP and quality plan, NDT procedures and qualified NDT personnel, material specifications/MTC requirements, calibration register, inspection checklists and the NCR/CAPA procedure.
    Q. Fit-up is a Hold point for the contractor. Two joints were welded without release. What first?
    • A Ignore since welds look fine
    • B Raise an NCR for bypassing the hold point and assess the affected welds (extra NDT if needed)
    • C Remove the hold from the ITP
    • D Blame the client

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Inspection & Calibration

    ✍️ What do you do about the overdue vernier?
    Model answer: Withdraw and quarantine it, label "out of calibration", send for calibration, review all receiving inspections done with it since its due date, re-measure critical dimensions with a calibrated instrument if the error is significant, raise an NCR/record the impact assessment, and add calibration-due alerts to the register.

    3️⃣ Task 3 · NCR & Root Cause

    ✍️ Write a strong NCR description for one repaired weld.
    Model answer: Spool SP-112 weld W4: RT per ASME Sec V shows porosity cluster exceeding ASME B31.3 normal-service acceptance criteria (RT report RT-319, film 4). Weld marked for repair; spool tagged red in hold area C. Welder ID W-27, WPS-05.
    ✍️ Use a fishbone (6M) to list possible causes of the 9% repair rate.
    Model answer: Man: welder skill/fatigue, rushing. Machine: faulty gas regulator, worn torches. Method: incorrect parameters, missing interpass cleaning, no in-process check. Material: damp electrodes (poor baking/storage), contaminated pipe ends. Measurement: RT interpretation inconsistency. Environment: wind drafts disturbing shielding gas, humidity.

    4️⃣ Task 4 · CAPA & KPIs

    Q. Data shows 70% of repairs are porosity from two outdoor bays on windy days. Best corrective action?
    • A Retrain all welders only
    • B Install wind shields/enclosures, control electrode baking and add in-process visual checks; monitor repair rate by bay
    • C Increase RT to 100% forever
    • D Close the NCRs
    ✍️ Define how you will verify CAPA effectiveness and report it.
    Model answer: Track weekly weld repair rate by bay and welder on a dashboard (Excel/Power BI). Effectiveness criterion: repair rate ≤ 3% for 4 consecutive weeks with no repeat porosity NCRs from the affected bays. Report to the project manager and client in the weekly quality report with trend chart, Pareto of defects and CAPA status.

    5️⃣ Task 5 · Close-out

    ✍️ List what goes into the final quality dossier.
    Model answer: Approved ITP and quality plan, material test certificates with traceability (heat numbers), WPS/PQR and welder qualifications, fit-up and visual reports, NDT reports, repair records, hydrotest certificates, dimensional reports, calibration certificates of instruments used, closed NCRs and CAPA records, release notes and final acceptance certificate.

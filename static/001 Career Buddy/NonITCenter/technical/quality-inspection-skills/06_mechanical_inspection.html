    What is Mechanical Inspection? Role, Responsibilities & Fundamentals

    Start from zero: what quality means for mechanical parts and equipment, how QA and QC differ, how the ten mechanical-inspection roles fit together, and the basics every inspector must know.

    🧭 What is Mechanical Inspection (QA/QC)?

    Mechanical inspection is the systematic checking of materials, machined and fabricated parts, assemblies and equipment against the drawings, specification, standard and contract, and recording the evidence. It covers dimensions, form and position, surface finish, material properties, assembly, alignment and equipment condition.

    QA (Quality Assurance)QC (Quality Control)
    FocusProcess and systemProduct and part
    NaturePreventiveDetective
    Mechanical examplesITP, calibration system, supplier approval, audits, proceduresMeasuring parts, first-article reports, hardness checks, NCRs
    Question asked"Are we making it the right way?""Is this part acceptable?"
    💡
    Memory aid: QA builds the system that prevents errors; QC checks the product to catch them.

    ⚙️ Mechanical Basics Every Inspector Must Know

    Most mechanical parts are made by machining, casting, forging, fabrication and then assembled into equipment such as pumps, gearboxes, compressors and conveyors.

    GrindingFine finishing, tight toleranceSize, roundness, surface roughness
    Casting / forgingShapes metal by pouring or pressingSurface defects, dimensions, material certificate
    FabricationCutting, forming and joining plate/sectionSquareness, flatness, hole positions, fit-up
    AssemblyFitting parts into equipmentFits, clearances, torque, alignment, cleanliness

    Drawings, Tolerances, Standards, Materials & Documentation

    Know exactly what you inspect against: drawings, GD&T, fits, standards, material certificates and records.

    🏛️ Where Requirements Come From

    Order of authority: law/regulation → contract → standard → project specification → drawing. If two documents conflict, raise a Technical Query; never guess.

    StandardUsed for
    ISO 9001Quality management system
    ASME Y14.5 / ISO 1101Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T)
    ISO 286Limits and fits (e.g. H7/g6)
    ISO 1302 / ISO 21920Surface texture (roughness)
    EN 10204Material inspection certificates (2.1, 2.2, 3.1, 3.2)
    ISO 2859-1Sampling by attributes (AQL)
    ISO 17025Calibration laboratory competence
    ISO 20816Vibration severity of machines
    AS9102First article inspection report

    📐 Reading Mechanical Drawings

    • Title block: part number, revision, material, scale, units, general tolerances.
    • Views: front, top, side, sections and details; check the projection system.
    • Dimensions: linear, diameter (Ø), radius (R), chamfers, threads and notes.
    • Revision control: inspect only to the latest approved revision.
    💡
    If the drawing has no tolerance on a dimension, the general tolerance block applies.

    🎯 Tolerances, Fits and GD&T

    Fit typeResultExample
    ClearanceAlways a gap; parts move freelyH7/g6 sliding fit
    TransitionEither small gap or small interferenceH7/k6 location fit
    InterferenceAlways tight; press or shrink fitH7/p6 press fit
    GD&T groupCharacteristics
    FormFlatness, straightness, circularity, cylindricity
    OrientationPerpendicularity, parallelism, angularity
    LocationPosition, concentricity, symmetry
    RunoutCircular runout, total runout
    EXAMPLE · Real scenario

    A shaft of Ø40 h6 has limits 40.000 / 39.984 mm (tolerance 16 µm). A reading of 39.980 mm is outside the limit and the shaft is nonconforming.

    🔗 Materials, Certificates & Traceability

    • Verify the material test certificate (e.g. EN 10204 3.1) against the grade, heat number and drawing.
    • Match heat/batch numbers to the marking on the part; keep traceability from raw material to final assembly.
    • Use PMI or hardness checks when the certificate or marking is in doubt.
    • Segregate and tag rejected, on-hold and approved items.

    🗂️ Documentation & Document Control

    • Controlled documents: drawings, procedures, ITP, checklists, calibration records.
    • Records must be legible, dated, signed and traceable to the part and the instrument used.
    • Never use obsolete revisions; remove them from the work area.
    • Keep final data book: reports, certificates, NCRs and as-built records.

    Inspection Stages, Measuring Instruments & Calibration

    Measure correctly at every stage, with calibrated tools and objective records.

    🔍 The Stages of Mechanical Inspection

    StageChecks
    ReceivingDocuments, material certificate, marking, visual condition, sample dimensions
    In-processFirst-piece approval, key dimensions, tool wear, setup, process parameters
    FinalAll specified dimensions, form/position, surface finish, hardness, marking, cleanliness
    AssemblyFits, clearances, torque, alignment, rotation, leaks, functional test
    DispatchPreservation, packing, labels, release documents
    💡
    The cheapest place to find an error is before machining the next hundred parts: approve the first piece.

    📏 Precision Measuring Instruments

    InstrumentTypical useTypical resolution
    Vernier / digital caliperExternal, internal, depth0.02 mm / 0.01 mm
    MicrometerPrecise external diameters, thickness0.01 mm (digital 0.001 mm)
    Dial / bore gaugeBore diameter, roundness, runout0.01 mm or finer
    Height gauge + surface plateHeights, positions0.01 mm
    Go / No-Go gaugesFast pass/fail for holes, threadsPass/fail
    Feeler, radius, thread gaugesGaps, radii, thread pitchFixed leaves
    CMM3D size, form and positionMicrometre level
    Roughness testerSurface roughness Ra0.01 µm
    📝
    Rule of thumb: the instrument resolution should be at most 1/10 of the tolerance being checked.

    🧪 Good Measuring Practice

    • Clean part and instrument; let both reach room temperature (20 °C).
    • Zero or set the instrument with a reference before use.
    • Measure at several positions and in two directions; record actual values, not only pass/fail.
    • Avoid excessive force; use the ratchet on a micrometer.
    • Avoid parallax and cosine error; keep the instrument square to the feature.

    🛠️ Calibration, MSA & Sampling

    • Calibration is traceable to national/international standards; each instrument has an ID, due date and status label.
    • If an instrument is found out of calibration, quarantine it and evaluate items measured since the last good calibration.
    • Gauge R&R (MSA): below 10% of tolerance is acceptable, 10–30% conditional, above 30% unacceptable.
    • Sampling: 100% inspection for critical features; AQL sampling (ISO 2859-1) for large lots of low risk.
    • Use a checklist tied to the drawing revision for every part type.

    Equipment, Alignment, Maintenance, Testing & the ITP

    Inspect assembled equipment, choose the right test and plan inspection with hold and witness points.

    🔧 Assembly, Torque & Shaft Alignment

    • Check cleanliness, fits, clearances, seals and lubrication before closing an assembly.
    • Tighten bolts in a cross (star) pattern to the specified torque with a calibrated torque wrench.
    • Shaft alignment (e.g. pump–motor) is checked for parallel offset and angular error, preferably with a laser tool.
    • Correct soft foot (a base that rocks or distorts the frame) before final alignment.
    EXAMPLE · Real scenario

    After a motor is replaced, the pump vibrates. Dial or laser readings show 0.25 mm offset against a limit of 0.05 mm. Re-shimming and re-checking restores smooth running.

    📈 Maintenance Strategies

    TypeIdeaExample
    CorrectiveFix after failureReplace a failed bearing
    Preventive (PM)Fixed-interval serviceLubricate every 500 h
    Predictive (PdM)Act on condition dataVibration or oil analysis
    Reliability-centredChoose strategy by failure riskFMEA-based plan
    Vibration patternLikely cause
    High at 1× running speedUnbalance
    High at 2× speed (and axial)Misalignment
    Bearing defect frequenciesBearing wear
    Broadband / randomLooseness, lubrication or cavitation problems

    📡 Material & Non-Destructive Testing

    TestPurposeNotes
    Hardness (HRC, HBW, HV)Strength / heat treatment checkSurface prep required
    PT (penetrant)Surface-breaking cracksAny non-porous metal
    MT (magnetic particle)Surface/near-surface cracksFerromagnetic only
    UT (ultrasonic)Internal flaws, thicknessSkilled operator
    RT (radiography)Internal volumetric flawsRadiation safety
    PMIVerify alloy compositionPortable XRF/OES
    💡
    Rule of thumb: surface methods first, volumetric methods after, and choose the method by the flaw you expect.

    📋 Inspection & Test Plan (ITP) and FAI

    • The ITP lists each activity, the acceptance criteria, the reference document and who attends.
    • Hold point (H): work cannot continue until the inspector releases it. Witness (W): inspector may attend; Review (R): records are reviewed.
    • First Article Inspection (FAI): full measurement of the first production part against every drawing requirement before series production.
    • Functional tests (run test, leak test, pressure test) are done per approved procedure and recorded.

    Nonconformance, Root Cause Analysis & CAPA

    Control nonconforming parts, find true root causes and stop recurrence.

    🧩 Common Mechanical Nonconformances

    IssueTypical causePrevention
    Oversize/undersizeTool wear, wrong offsetFirst-piece check, tool life control
    Poor surface finishWorn tool, wrong feedParameter control, tool change plan
    Out-of-round / taperMachine wear, clamping distortionFixturing review, machine maintenance
    Wrong materialMix-up, missing traceabilityCertificate check, PMI, marking
    MisalignmentSoft foot, poor shimmingAlignment check, procedure
    Burrs / damageHandling, poor deburringHandling and packing rules
    Failed hardnessHeat treatment variationFurnace records, test pieces

    🚩 Nonconformance and the NCR Process

    • Identify, tag and segregate the part immediately; stop further work on the batch if needed.
    • Raise an NCR: description, quantity, drawing/spec requirement, measured value, location, evidence.
    • Disposition: use-as-is (needs engineering/client approval), rework, repair, scrap or return to vendor.
    • Re-inspect after rework and close the NCR with verified evidence.

    🔎 Root Cause Analysis (RCA) Tools

    ToolUse
    5 WhysAsk why repeatedly until the process cause is reached
    Fishbone (Ishikawa)Group causes: man, machine, method, material, measurement, environment
    Pareto chartRank defects to attack the vital few
    FMEARank risks with RPN = Severity × Occurrence × Detection
    8D reportStructured team problem solving for customer issues

    ✅ CAPA: Correction, Corrective & Preventive Action

    LevelMeaningExample
    CorrectionFix this nonconformanceRe-machine the 12 oversize shafts
    CorrectiveRemove the root causeAdd tool-wear offset check every 25 parts
    PreventiveStop it in other placesApply the same check to all similar lathes
    💡
    Verify effectiveness: the defect rate must fall and stay down for 30–60 days before closing CAPA.

    Module 06 · Expertise

    Planning, Statistics, KPIs, Audits & Leadership

    Work at expert level: plan across teams, turn data into decisions, audit and lead.

    📅 Planning, Scheduling, Coordination & Supervision

    • Plan inspections from the project schedule and ITP; give hold-point notices within the agreed time.
    • Allocate resources by critical path, inspector skill and gauge/CMM availability.
    • Track progress in Excel/ERP: parts received, inspected, accepted, rejected, pending.
    • Coordinate with production, maintenance, stores, supplier and client through short daily meetings.
    • Supervise: brief the team, check safety and permits, review reports, coach inspectors, escalate early.
    EXAMPLE · Real scenario

    Two shafts and a CMM program are due the same morning but only one CMM exists. The planner re-sequences the work and informs the client, so no crew is idle.

    📊 Process Capability, KPIs & Dashboards

    MeasureMeaning / formula
    Cp(USL − LSL) / 6σ: potential capability
    CpkMin[(USL − mean)/3σ, (mean − LSL)/3σ]; ≥ 1.33 usually required
    First pass yieldGood parts first time ÷ total parts
    PPMDefective parts per million
    OEEAvailability × Performance × Quality
    MTBF / MTTRMean time between failures / to repair
    On-time inspectionInspections done by due date ÷ planned
    • Show trends on control charts and dashboards (Excel/Power BI); act on signals, not single points.
    • Check measurement system first: a poor gauge makes good data meaningless.

    🧾 Audits & Quality System

    • Internal audits verify that work follows ISO 9001 procedures; findings are graded and tracked to closure.
    • Supplier audits and source inspection reduce incoming risk.
    • Management review uses KPIs, audit results and CAPA status to set improvement goals.

    🧠 Design Review & Advanced Technical Analysis

    • Review designs for inspectability: can each critical feature actually be measured?
    • Check tolerance stack-up and fit selection for assembly function.
    • Analyse failures with data (vibration, oil, wear patterns) before changing the design.
    • Record lessons learned and update checklists and ITPs.

    🏆 Leadership & Quality Culture

    • Lead by example: follow the procedure, measure honestly, report problems early.
    • Coach inspectors; share findings openly without blame.
    • Balance schedule pressure with quality: never release a hold point without evidence.
    • Build a culture where anyone can stop the job for a safety or quality concern.

    Practice: Run Mechanical Inspection on a Shaft and Pump Project

    Apply everything end to end: requirements, measurement, capability, alignment, NCR/CAPA and reporting.

    0 / 0 correct

    🏗️ Scenario

    You inspect a batch of 200 machined pump shafts, drawing tolerance Ø40 h6 (0 / −0.016 mm), and a pump–motor assembly. In week 3: batch Cpk is 0.9, a micrometer is a month past its calibration date, and the pump–motor alignment shows an offset above the limit.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Before inspection

    ✍️ List the documents and checks needed before starting inspection.
    Model answer: Latest approved drawing, purchase order/spec, ITP and checklist, material certificates (EN 10204 3.1), approved procedures, calibration register and valid instruments, inspector competence, NCR procedure, and a first-piece approval plan.
    Q. The micrometer is a month past calibration. What first?
    • A Keep using it, it is probably fine
    • B Stop using it, tag it, and evaluate parts measured since its last good calibration
    • C Remove the due date label
    • D Ask the operator to estimate the error

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Measurement and capability

    ✍️ Cpk is 0.9. What does it mean and what do you do?
    Model answer: The process is not capable (target ≥ 1.33): variation is too large or the mean is off-centre. Verify the measurement system (gauge R&R), check for tool wear or drift, centre the process, consider 100% inspection until capability improves, and raise an NCR/CAPA.
    Q. Which data should you check first before blaming the process?
    • A Operator age
    • B Colour of the shaft
    • C The measurement system (gauge condition and R&R)
    • D Packing material

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Alignment, root cause and CAPA

    ✍️ The pump–motor offset is above the limit. How do you investigate and prevent recurrence?
    Model answer: Check soft foot, pipe strain and shim condition, re-align with a laser tool, record before/after values, use 5 Whys on why it was released out of tolerance, add an alignment hold point and a signed record to the ITP, and verify vibration after restart.
    Q. Vibration is high at 2× running speed. Most likely cause?
    • A Unbalance
    • B Misalignment
    • C Electrical supply only
    • D Wrong paint colour

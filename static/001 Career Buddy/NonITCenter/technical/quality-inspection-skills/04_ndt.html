    What is NDT? The Role, Methods & Fundamentals

    Start from zero: what non-destructive testing is, why industry depends on it, the main methods, how NDT people are qualified and what each NDT role does.

    🧭 What is NDT?

    Non-Destructive Testing (NDT) — also called NDE (evaluation) or NDI (inspection) — is a group of methods used to find, measure and evaluate flaws or properties in materials and components without damaging them. After testing, the part can still be used.

    🔬 Non-destructive testing

    Part remains usable. Can test 100% of welds or components. Finds discontinuities and measures thickness. Examples: UT, RT, MT, PT.

    💥 Destructive testing

    Sample is cut, broken or deformed. Tests only samples. Measures mechanical properties. Examples: tensile, bend, impact, macro tests.

    Surface Methods: Visual, Penetrant & Magnetic Particle Testing

    Learn the three most-used surface methods step by step — principles, equipment, technique, limitations and acceptance.

    👁️ Visual Testing (VT)

    • First NDT method on every job — many defects are visible.
    • Direct VT: eye within ~600 mm (24 in) at ≥ 30° angle; remote VT: borescopes, cameras, drones.
    • Lighting ≥ 1,000 lux (≈ 100 fc) at the surface for typical code requirements.
    • Tools: weld gauges, magnifiers, mirrors, flashlights, measuring tapes, borescopes.
    Weld visual checksExample limit source
    CracksNot permitted (most codes)
    Undercut depthCode table (e.g. AWS D1.1, ISO 5817)
    Weld size/profile, reinforcementDrawing + code
    Porosity, spatter, arc strikesCode + specification

    🧴 Liquid Penetrant Testing (PT)

    Figure 2.1 · PT sequence
    Type / methodMeaning
    Type I / Type IIFluorescent (UV-A in darkened area) / visible red dye
    Method AWater-washable
    Method B / DPost-emulsifiable (lipophilic / hydrophilic)
    Method CSolvent-removable — common for field work
    • Works on any non-porous material; detects surface-breaking flaws only.
    • Temperature range typically ~10–52 °C (50–125 °F) unless procedure qualified otherwise.
    • Never spray solvent directly on the surface when removing excess (Method C) — it washes penetrant out of flaws.
    • Fluorescent PT: check UV-A intensity and ambient white light per procedure.

    🧲 Magnetic Particle Testing (MT)

    • Works only on ferromagnetic materials (carbon/low-alloy steel) — not on austenitic stainless or aluminium.
    • Detects surface and slightly sub-surface discontinuities.
    • Flaws are best detected when perpendicular to the magnetic field — inspect in two directions about 90° apart.
    • Equipment: yoke (AC/DC/permanent), prods, coils, central conductor, bench units.
    • Particles: dry or wet, visible (black/red on white contrast paint) or fluorescent (UV-A).
    CheckTypical requirement (ASME V example)
    AC yoke lifting power≥ 4.5 kg (10 lb) at maximum pole spacing
    DC / permanent yoke lifting power≥ 18 kg (40 lb)
    Field adequacyPie gauge / shim indicators
    After testDemagnetise if required; clean

    ⚖️ Choosing Between VT, PT & MT

    SituationBest choiceWhy
    Carbon steel weld, surface cracksMTMore sensitive to fine and near-surface cracks; faster on ferromagnetic
    Stainless steel weldPTMT not possible on austenitic stainless
    Aluminium casting surfacePTNon-ferromagnetic
    Painted surfaceRemove coating first (or use ET)Coatings block penetrant; thick coatings reduce MT sensitivity
    All weldsVT firstCheap, fast, catches gross defects

    Volumetric & Electromagnetic Methods: UT, RT & ET

    Understand how ultrasonic, radiographic and eddy-current testing work, how they are calibrated and how to use them safely.

    🔊 Ultrasonic Testing (UT) Principles

    • A transducer sends high-frequency sound (typically 1–10 MHz, often 2–5 MHz for welds) into the part through a couplant.
    • Sound reflects from flaws and the back wall; the instrument shows echoes on an A-scan (amplitude vs time).
    • Straight-beam (0°) longitudinal waves: thickness, laminations. Angle-beam shear waves (45°, 60°, 70°): weld inspection.
    • Thickness gauging: corrosion monitoring and wall-loss surveys.
    EXAMPLE · Thickness from time

    Steel longitudinal velocity ≈ 5,920 m/s. Round-trip time 6.0 μs → thickness = (5,920 × 6.0×10⁻⁶) ÷ 2 ≈ 17.8 mm.

    🎛️ UT Calibration & Evaluation

    ItemPurpose
    Calibration blocks (IIW/V1, V2/K2, step wedge)Range, probe index, beam angle, linearity
    Reference blocks with side-drilled holesBuild DAC/TCG sensitivity curve
    DAC / TCGCompensates amplitude loss with depth; sets reporting/rejection levels
    Transfer correctionAdjusts for surface/attenuation differences between block and part
    Scanning sensitivityTypically reference + 6 dB or as per code
    • Evaluate indications by amplitude (vs DAC), length (e.g. 6 dB drop technique), position and type.
    • UT is excellent for planar defects (cracks, lack of fusion) oriented across the beam.
    • Limitations: operator skill, coarse grains (austenitic welds), complex geometry, thin sections.

    ☢️ Radiographic Testing (RT)

    • X-rays or gamma rays pass through the part; denser/thicker areas absorb more. Flaws (voids) let more radiation through → darker areas on film.
    • Sources: X-ray tubes; gamma isotopes Ir-192 (common for steel ~10–60 mm), Se-75 (thinner), Co-60 (thick sections).
    • Image capture: film, computed radiography (CR) plates or digital detector arrays (DR).
    • Techniques: SWSI (single wall single image), DWSI, DWDI (elliptical/superimposed for small pipes).
    Quality checkMeaning
    IQI (wire or hole type)Proves required sensitivity is achieved
    Film densityTypically within 2.0–4.0 for code acceptance (check exact code)
    Location markers & IDTraceability of weld and position
    Geometric unsharpnessControlled by source size, distances
    💡
    RT is excellent for volumetric defects (porosity, slag) but can miss tight planar cracks not aligned with the beam.

    🛡️ Radiation Safety

    ⏱️
    Time
    Minimise exposure time.
    📏
    Distance
    Dose rate falls with the square of distance.
    🧱
    Shielding
    Lead, concrete, collimators.
    • ALARA — As Low As Reasonably Achievable.
    • Barricades, warning signs and lights at the controlled-area boundary; survey meters and personal dosimeters (TLD/EPD) mandatory.
    • Only licensed/authorised radiographers handle sources; follow the regulator (e.g. AERB in India).
    • Source retrieval checks with survey meter after every exposure.
    EXAMPLE · Inverse square law

    Dose rate 100 μSv/h at 2 m. At 4 m: 100 × (2/4)² = 25 μSv/h. Doubling distance quarters the dose rate.

    🌀 Eddy Current Testing (ET)

    • AC coil induces eddy currents in conductive materials; cracks, wall loss or conductivity changes alter the coil impedance.
    • Uses: surface cracks (even through thin paint), heat-exchanger tube inspection, conductivity/heat-treatment checks, coating thickness.
    • Shallow penetration (skin effect) — mainly surface/near-surface.
    MethodStrengthLimitation
    UTPlanar defects, depth sizing, thickness, one-side accessSkill-dependent, needs couplant
    RTPermanent image, volumetric defectsRadiation hazard, needs two-side access, may miss tight cracks
    ETFast, no couplant, through paintConductive materials only, shallow

    • Read drawings/isometrics for material, thickness, joint type, weld numbers and NDT symbols/extent.
    • A weld map links every weld number to welder ID, WPS, NDT method, extent and results — the backbone of traceability.
    • Method selection considers material, thickness, joint geometry, expected flaw type and orientation, access, code requirement, safety and schedule.

    Interpretation, Reporting, Repairs & NCR/CAPA

    Interpret indications correctly, write defensible reports, manage repairs and drive corrective action for recurring weld defects.

    🔍 Common Weld Discontinuities & How NDT Shows Them

    DiscontinuityCause (typical)Best methods / appearance
    PorosityMoisture, poor gas shielding, contaminationRT: dark rounded spots; UT: scattered low echoes
    Slag inclusionPoor interpass cleaningRT: irregular dark shapes; UT: echoes
    Lack of fusionLow heat input, poor techniqueUT: strong planar echo; RT: may appear as dark line if aligned
    Incomplete penetrationRoot gap/heat too smallRT: dark straight line at root; UT: root echo
    CracksHydrogen, restraint, hardness, hot crackingMT/PT on surface; UT for internal; RT if aligned
    UndercutExcess current/travel speedVT, RT dark edge line
    Burn-through / excess penetrationExcess heat at rootVT internal, RT light area

    ⚖️ Interpretation vs Evaluation

    🧩 Interpretation

    Decide what the indication is: relevant, non-relevant or false — and the likely discontinuity type.

    📏 Evaluation

    Measure the relevant indication (size, length, amplitude, density) and compare with acceptance criteria → accept or reject.

    • Only qualified Level II/III personnel interpret and evaluate for acceptance.
    • When unsure, use a complementary method (e.g. UT to size an RT indication, MT to confirm surface crack).

    🧾 NDT Reporting

    IdentificationProject, component, weld no., drawing/isometric, material, thickness, welder.
    TechniqueMethod, procedure no./rev, equipment and serial, probes/source, calibration details, sensitivity.
    ResultsIndications with location, size/type, evaluation against code clause.
    DecisionAccept / reject, extent covered, limitations (e.g. restricted access).
    PersonnelName, level, certificate no., signature, date; client/TPI witness.

    🔴 Weak report

    "W-17 RT done. Some porosity. Reject."

    🟢 Strong report

    "W-17, L-102, 12.7 mm CS, RT per PRC-RT-03 Rev 2, Ir-192, IQI wire 10 visible, density 2.6–3.1. Cluster porosity 0–110 mm, largest pore 3.5 mm exceeds ASME B31.3 Table 341.3.2 limits → REJECT. Repair R1 required."

    🔁 Repairs, Re-tests & Penalty Tests

    • Rejected welds: mark repair area, repair per approved repair WPS, then re-test with the same method (plus any additional code requirement).
    • Many codes require progressive / penalty examination: when a random-sample weld fails, test additional welds by the same welder (e.g. 2 more; if they fail, all welds represented).
    • Track repair cycles (R1, R2…) — repeated repairs may need engineering review.

    🛠️ NCR, Root Cause & CAPA for NDT Findings

    NCR triggerExample
    Defects beyond acceptanceRejected welds above target repair rate
    Process breachRT shot without valid IQI; unqualified technician signed report
    Equipment issueFlaw detector found out of calibration
    Missed inspectionWeld covered/insulated before NDT
    EXAMPLE · 5 Whys — high porosity rate
    1. Why porosity? → Gas shielding disrupted.
    2. Why? → Wind across open bay.
    3. Why no shelter? → Windshields not provided in field plan.
    4. Why not planned? → Site-condition risks not in the welding execution plan.
    5. CAPA: windshields + wind-speed check before welding; review execution plan template; monitor repair rate per welder weekly.

    🎯 NDT Reliability & Probability of Detection

    • POD: probability that a method detects a flaw of a given size — no method finds 100% of flaws.
    • a90/95: flaw size detected with 90% probability at 95% confidence — used in aerospace/fracture-mechanics design.
    • Reliability depends on method, procedure, equipment, human factors (fatigue, lighting) and flaw orientation.
    • Improve with complementary methods, recorded/automated scanning and independent review.

    📊 NDT Data, KPIs & Dashboards

    KPIFormula / use
    Weld repair (reject) rateRejected welds ÷ welds tested × 100 (or by length)
    Repair rate by welder / processFind welders/processes needing action
    NDT backlogWelds awaiting NDT — schedule risk

    EXAMPLE · Repair rate

    RT of 180 welds, 9 rejected → repair rate = 9 ÷ 180 × 100 = 5%. Pareto shows 6 of 9 rejects are porosity from 2 welders on the night shift → targeted action.

    • Use Excel/Power BI: pivot by welder, process, defect type, location; trend charts weekly; flag welders above threshold for retraining/requalification.

    📝 Design Review & Advanced Technical Analysis

    • Review designs for inspectability: access for probes and sources, weld geometry, surface finish, thickness transitions.
    • Calculations: exposure times, geometric unsharpness, beam paths/skip distances, coverage, dose rates and exclusion distances.
    • Fitness-for-service (e.g. API 579) uses NDT sizing data to decide if in-service flaws are acceptable.
    • Failure investigations combine NDT with metallurgy and fracture analysis.
    EXAMPLE · Skip distance

    Angle-beam 60° in 20 mm plate: full skip = 2 × t × tan 60° ≈ 2 × 20 × 1.732 ≈ 69 mm. Scan zone must cover the full weld volume within this path.

    🧑‍💼 Leadership, Audits & Continuous Improvement

    • Level III / senior engineers own the written practice, procedures, training and certification records.
    • Audit NDT vendors and crews: qualifications, calibration, procedure compliance, film/data review.
    • Lead the team: competence matrix, coaching, fair allocation, fatigue management, zero-harm radiation culture.
    • Report to management: repair trends, risks, schedule impact, CAPA status, and recommendations.

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer.

    🧭 NDT Fundamentals

    What is the difference between an indication, a discontinuity and a defect?
    An indication is any NDT response. A discontinuity is an actual interruption in the material. A defect is a discontinuity that exceeds the acceptance criteria and makes the part rejectable.
    Explain Level I, II and III.
    Level I performs tests and calibrations under written instructions. Level II sets up, calibrates, interprets and evaluates against codes and reports. Level III develops and approves procedures, interprets codes, selects methods and trains/examines personnel.
    Why can't MT be used on austenitic stainless steel?
    Austenitic stainless steel is non-ferromagnetic, so it cannot be magnetised to produce flux leakage. Use PT or ET instead.
    Why inspect with MT in two directions?
    Flaws are best detected when perpendicular to the magnetic field, so two inspections about 90° apart ensure cracks in any orientation are found.

    🔊 Methods & Techniques

    What is DAC and why is it used in UT?
    Distance Amplitude Correction is a curve built from reference reflectors at different depths. It compensates for amplitude loss with distance and sets recording and rejection levels.
    Compare UT and RT for weld inspection.
    RT gives a permanent image and is excellent for volumetric flaws like porosity and slag but has radiation hazards and may miss tight planar cracks. UT is better for planar flaws, depth sizing and one-side access, without radiation, but is more operator-dependent.
    What does an IQI prove on a radiograph?
    That the radiographic technique achieved the required sensitivity — the specified wire or hole is visible.
    Explain the three principles of radiation protection.
    Time (minimise exposure), distance (dose rate falls with the square of distance) and shielding (lead, concrete, collimators), under the ALARA principle.
    What is the role of a couplant in UT?
    It removes air between the probe and the part so sound energy transfers into the material.

    🛠️ Interpretation, Reporting & Leadership

    What must an NDT report contain?
    Component and weld identification, material and thickness, procedure and revision, equipment and calibration details, technique, indications with size and location, evaluation against the code clause, accept/reject, limitations, and personnel name, level and certificate.
    A random-sample weld fails RT. What happens?
    Mark and repair the weld, re-test it, and apply progressive (penalty) examination to additional welds by the same welder as required by the code; if those fail, examine all welds represented.
    How would you reduce a high weld repair rate?
    Analyse rejects by defect type, welder, process and location (Pareto), find root causes (e.g. wind affecting gas shielding, poor interpass cleaning), implement CAPA such as windshields, consumable control or retraining, and monitor repair rate weekly.
    What is POD?
    Probability of Detection — the likelihood that a method detects a flaw of a given size; it shows no NDT method is 100% reliable and guides method selection and design limits.

    🛢️ Scenario: Refinery Piping Shutdown

    During a refinery shutdown, your NDT team must examine 420 new butt welds on carbon-steel process piping (ASME B31.3 normal service, 6–25 mm wall) plus 30 stainless-steel small-bore welds. The project spec requires 10% RT of CS butt welds, 100% VT, and PT on stainless welds. The plant is operating nearby; work areas are congested. After week 1, RT shows 12 rejects in 60 welds, mostly porosity.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Plan Methods & Resources

    ✍️ Choose methods for CS and SS welds and list planning steps.
    Model answer: CS butt welds: 100% VT, 10% RT (or UT/PAUT where RT is impractical, if spec allows). SS small-bore: 100% VT + PT (non-magnetic). Planning: build weld map with welder IDs, select random sample per welder, schedule RT at night with exclusion zones, allocate Level II crews and Ir-192/X-ray sources, track requests and results in Excel.

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Radiation Safety

    Q. Dose rate is 400 μSv/h at 1 m from the source. Approximately what is it at 10 m?
    • A 40 μSv/h
    • B 4 μSv/h
    • C 0.4 μSv/h
    • D 100 μSv/h
    ✍️ List safety controls for RT in an operating plant.
    Model answer: Permit to work, radiation safety officer approval, barricades and signs at the calculated dose-rate boundary, warning lights/announcements, survey meters and personal dosimeters, collimators/shielding, night-time shooting, coordination with operations, source retrieval survey after each exposure, emergency procedure.

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Interpret & Report

    ✍️ A radiograph shows a straight dark line along the weld root centre. What is it likely to be and how do you confirm?
    Model answer: Likely incomplete penetration (or lack of root fusion). Confirm by reviewing fit-up/joint design and position, compare with root geometry, check from the ID if accessible (VT/borescope) or use UT for sizing; evaluate against B31.3 acceptance (incomplete penetration is limited/not permitted depending on service).

    4️⃣ Task 4 · NCR & CAPA

    Q. Reject rate is 12/60 (20%) and mostly porosity. Best first analysis?
    • A Increase RT to 100% for ever
    • B Pareto rejects by welder, location, process and conditions
    • C Stop all welding permanently

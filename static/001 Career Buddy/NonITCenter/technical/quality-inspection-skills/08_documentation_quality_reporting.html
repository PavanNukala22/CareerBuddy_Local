    What is Documentation & Quality Reporting? Role, Responsibilities & Fundamentals

    Start from zero: why documents and records matter, how QA and QC differ, how the ten documentation and quality-reporting roles fit together, and the basics everyone must know.

    🧭 What is Documentation & Quality Reporting?

    Documentation and quality reporting is the discipline of defining how work must be done (documents), proving what was actually done (records) and communicating the results (reports) so decisions are based on facts. If it is not recorded, it cannot be proved.

    QA (Quality Assurance)QC (Quality Control)
    FocusProcess and systemProduct and result
    NaturePreventiveDetective
    Documentation examplesQuality manual, procedures, ITP, audit plans, document controlInspection reports, test records, certificates, NCRs, punch lists
    Question asked"Are we working the right way?""Is this product acceptable?"
    💡
    Memory aid: documents say what to do, records prove what was done, reports tell people what it means.

    💼 The Documentation & Quality Reporting Role Family

    Quality Management System, Standards & Document Control

    Know the system your documents live in: ISO standards, document hierarchy, numbering, revision and distribution.

    🏛️ Standards and Where Requirements Come From

    Order of authority: law/regulation → contract → standard → project specification → drawing/procedure. If two documents conflict, raise a Technical Query; never guess.

    StandardUsed for
    ISO 9001Quality management system requirements
    ISO 9000Fundamentals and vocabulary
    ISO 10005Guidelines for quality plans
    ISO 19011Guidelines for auditing management systems
    ISO 10012Measurement management systems (calibration control)
    ISO 2859-1Sampling by attributes (AQL)
    ISO 15489Records management
    EN 10204Material inspection certificates (2.1, 2.2, 3.1, 3.2)
    ISO/IEC 17025Testing and calibration laboratory competence
    ISO 9001:2015 clauseTopic
    7.5Documented information
    8.7Control of nonconforming outputs
    9.1Monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation
    9.2Internal audit
    9.3Management review
    10.2Nonconformity and corrective action

    🗂️ Document Control

    • Unique number and title: every document has an ID, revision, date and status.
    • Review and approval before issue; the approver is recorded.
    • Distribution control: a distribution list or system shows who holds the current copy.
    • Withdrawal: obsolete versions are removed or marked obsolete.
    • External documents (client specs, standards) are logged and kept current.
    • Transmittals record what was sent, when and to whom.
    💡
    If the drawing or procedure in your hand has no revision or approval status, treat it as uncontrolled and ask.

    🧾 Records: Good Documentation Practice

    Principle (ALCOA)Meaning
    AttributableWho did it and when is clear
    LegibleReadable now and in the future
    ContemporaneousRecorded at the time the activity is done
    OriginalFirst record or a certified true copy
    AccurateCorrect, complete and consistent
    • Correct a paper error with one line, initials, date and reason; never use correction fluid or overwrite.
    • Do not leave blank fields; use "N/A" with initials.
    • Electronic records need access control, backup and an audit trail of changes.
    EXAMPLE · Real scenario

    A date on a test record is wrong. The inspector strikes it with a single line, writes the right date, initials, dates the correction and notes "typing error". The original entry remains readable.

    📐 Specifications, Drawings and Revisions

    • Title block: drawing number, revision, material, scale, approval status.
    • Inspect and report only to the latest approved revision; write the revision on the report.
    • Control red-line changes and convert them to as-built drawings at handover.
    • Use a Technical Query for unclear or conflicting requirements and keep the answer on file.

    📦 Retention, Storage and Handover

    • Retention periods are set by contract, regulation and company procedure.
    • Store records so they are protected from loss, damage and unauthorised change.
    • Final handover: indexed data book with reports, certificates, NCRs, as-built drawings and test records.

    Inspection Records, Checklists & Reports

    Create inspection documents that are clear, complete and usable as evidence.

    📋 The Inspection and Test Plan (ITP)

    • The ITP lists each activity, the acceptance criteria, the reference document, the records required and who attends.
    • Hold (H): work cannot continue until the inspector releases it. Witness (W): inspector may attend. Review (R): records are reviewed.
    • Each ITP step should point to a form or checklist so the record is defined in advance.
    StageTypical records
    ReceivingDelivery note check, certificate review, visual inspection report
    In-processFirst-piece report, process checks, hold-point release
    FinalFinal inspection report, test results, release note
    DispatchPacking check, release certificate, transmittal

    ✍️ Writing a Good Inspection Report

    SectionContent
    HeaderProject, item, tag or part number, drawing and revision, report number, date
    ReferenceProcedure, ITP step, standard and acceptance criteria
    EquipmentInstrument ID and calibration due date
    ResultsActual values with units, locations and sample size
    ConclusionAccepted, rejected or on hold, with NCR reference if needed
    ApprovalName, signature, date; reviewer or witness where required
    📝
    Record actual readings, not only "OK". Photos and sketches help but must be identified and dated.

    🧪 Checklists, Calibration and Measurement Records

    • Use a checklist tied to the drawing revision for each product type.
    • Every report shows the instrument ID and calibration status.
    • If an instrument is found out of calibration, quarantine it and evaluate results since its last good calibration.
    • Sampling follows the ITP or ISO 2859-1 (AQL); critical features are checked 100%.

    📣 Daily, Weekly and Monthly Quality Reports

    ReportAudienceTypical content
    Daily inspection logSite teamInspections done, accepted, rejected, open holds
    Weekly quality reportProject managerNCRs raised and closed, ageing, hold points, look-ahead
    Monthly quality reportManagement and clientKPIs, trends, CAPA status, audit results, risks
    • Start with a short executive summary: status, key issues and decisions needed.
    • Use tables and charts consistently; show target next to actual.
    • Every figure must be traceable to source records.

    Certificates, Traceability, NCR Records & Data Books

    Control the documents that prove material quality, track nonconformances and assemble the final data book.

    🏅 Material and Test Certificates

    EN 10204 typeMeaning
    2.1Declaration of compliance by the manufacturer
    2.2Test report with non-specific testing by the manufacturer
    3.1Inspection certificate with specific test results, validated by the manufacturer's authorised inspection representative independent of production
    3.2As 3.1 plus validation by an independent party (e.g. client or authorised inspector)
    • Check grade, heat or batch number, standard and test values against the order and drawing.
    • Match the certificate to the marking on the item; reject if they do not agree.

    🔗 Traceability

    • Traceability links item → material → process → inspection → instrument → record.
    • Use a traceability matrix from requirement to inspection activity to evidence.
    • Keep heat or batch numbers through machining and assembly; segregate approved, on-hold and rejected items.
    EXAMPLE · Real scenario

    A client asks which batch of bolts was used on pump P-101. Using the traceability record, the quality engineer finds the certificate, the receiving inspection report and the torque record in minutes.

    🚩 NCR Records and Control of Nonconforming Output

    • Identify, tag and segregate the item immediately.
    • The NCR records: description, quantity, requirement, actual value, location, evidence and reporter.
    • Disposition: use-as-is (needs documented engineering/client approval), rework, repair, scrap or return to vendor.
    • Re-inspect after rework and close the NCR with verified evidence.
    • Maintain an NCR register with status, owner, due date and age.
    ⚠️
    Use-as-is without documented approval is a hidden defect waiting to be found later.

    📘 Compiling the Data Book (MDR)

    SectionContents
    IndexDocument list with numbers and revisions
    DesignAs-built drawings, datasheets, approved specifications
    MaterialsCertificates and traceability records
    Inspection and testsReports, test results, hold-point releases
    QualityNCRs with closure evidence, calibration records, punch list
    HandoverRelease certificates, manuals, spare parts lists
    • Check completeness against the supplier document requirements list (SDRL).
    • Verify revisions, signatures and dates before issue; record the transmittal.

    Root Cause Analysis, CAPA & Quality Improvement

    Find true root causes, stop recurrence and prove the fix worked.

    🧩 Common Documentation Nonconformances

    IssueTypical causePrevention
    Wrong drawing revision usedObsolete copy in the work areaControlled distribution, remove obsolete copies
    Missing signature or dateRush, unclear responsibilityPre-handover record review, define signatories
    Certificate not matching itemMix-up, poor markingMarking and certificate check at receiving
    Uncalibrated instrument usedNo calibration status checkCalibration register, status labels
    Records altered or back-datedPressure or weak cultureGood documentation practice, audit trail, training
    NCR open too longNo owner or due dateAgeing review, escalation rule

    🔎 Root Cause Analysis (RCA) Tools

    ToolUse
    5 WhysAsk why repeatedly until a process cause is reached
    Fishbone (Ishikawa)Group causes: man, machine, method, material, measurement, environment
    Pareto chartRank defects to attack the vital few
    FMEARank risks with RPN = Severity × Occurrence × Detection
    8D reportD3 containment, D4 root cause, D5–D7 corrective and preventive action
    💡
    Stop at a cause in the process or system you can fix, not at a person to blame.

    ✅ CAPA: Correction, Corrective & Preventive Action

    LevelMeaningExample
    CorrectionFix this nonconformanceReplace the wrong-revision drawings in the workshop
    CorrectiveRemove the root causeControl distribution so only current revisions are printed
    PreventiveStop it in other placesApply the same rule to all projects and sites
    • Each CAPA has an owner, due date, action and evidence.
    • Verify effectiveness: the issue must not recur for a defined period (for example 30–60 days) before closure.

    ♻️ Lessons Learned and Continual Improvement

    • Record lessons at project milestones and at handover.
    • Feed lessons into procedures, checklists and ITP templates.
    • Use repeated NCRs with the same cause as evidence of a systemic issue.

    Module 06 · Expertise

    Planning, Data, KPIs, Audits & Leadership

    Work at expert level: plan documentation across teams, turn data into decisions, audit and lead.

    📅 Planning, Coordination & Supervision

    • Plan document submissions and inspections from the project schedule and the SDRL.
    • Allocate resources by workload, skills and deadlines.
    • Track progress in Excel/ERP: documents planned, submitted, approved, rejected, overdue.
    • Coordinate with engineering, production, suppliers and the client through short regular meetings.
    • Supervise: review records before issue, coach staff, escalate early.
    EXAMPLE · Real scenario

    Three suppliers are late with certificates before a shipment. The coordinator lists the missing documents, agrees dates with each supplier and informs the project manager, so shipment is not released without the required records.

    📊 KPIs, Trends & Dashboards

    MeasureMeaning
    NCR rateNCRs per inspection, per 1000 items or per work package
    NCR closure time and ageingDays open; share older than the target
    First pass yieldItems accepted first time ÷ items inspected
    Document approval cycle timeDays from submission to approval
    On-time report submissionReports issued on time ÷ planned
    CAPA effectivenessCAPA closed without recurrence ÷ CAPA closed
    Cost of poor qualityRework, scrap, repeat inspection and claims
    • Show trends and targets; average alone can hide a few very old NCRs.
    • A dashboard needs few meaningful KPIs, owners and actions.
    • Check the measurement system first: poor data gives poor reports.

    🧾 Audits & Management Review

    • Internal audits (ISO 19011 approach) check that work follows procedures; findings are graded and tracked to closure.
    • Supplier audits and document reviews reduce incoming risk.
    • Management review uses audit results, customer feedback, process performance, NCR/CAPA status and risks to set improvement actions.

    🧠 Advanced Technical Analysis & Design Review

    • Review documents for consistency between specification, drawing, ITP and test procedure.
    • Use traceability matrices to find requirements without evidence.
    • Analyse failures and NCR data with Pareto and trend charts before changing procedures.
    • Record lessons learned and update templates.

    🏆 Leadership & Quality Culture

    • Make accurate, timely records a visible expectation.
    • Encourage reporting of errors without blame.
    • Balance schedule pressure with quality: never pre-sign or release a hold point without evidence.
    • Build a culture where anyone can stop the job for a safety or quality concern.

    Practice: Build the Quality Records for a Pump Package Project

    Apply everything end to end: document control, records, NCR/CAPA, reporting and data book.

    0 / 0 correct

    🏗️ Scenario

    You manage the quality documents for a pump package. In week 3: an inspection report cites drawing Rev B while the approved drawing is Rev C, one NCR has been open for 45 days, a CAPA was closed without checking effectiveness, and the client wants the data book in two weeks.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Document control

    ✍️ The inspection report refers to drawing Rev B but Rev C is approved. What do you do?
    Model answer: Stop acceptance on that record, find out whether the item was checked against Rev B or Rev C, compare the changes between revisions, re-inspect to Rev C if affected, raise an NCR for the document-control failure, correct the record following good documentation practice, remove obsolete Rev B copies and review other reports for the same issue.
    Q. How should a signed paper record error be corrected?
    • A Single line through, initials, date and reason
    • B Correction fluid
    • C Overwrite the entry
    • D Tear out the page

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Reporting

    ✍️ The weekly report shows an average NCR closure time of 12 days. Why is this not enough and what else do you show?
    Model answer: An average can hide a few very old NCRs. Show ageing by band (for example 0–15, 16–30, over 30 days), list the oldest NCRs with owner and due date, show the trend against target, and state the decision needed to close them.
    Q. Best way to present NCR performance?
    • A Trend plus ageing, with owners and targets
    • B A single average number only
    • C Only the closed NCRs
    • D No numbers at all

    3️⃣ Task 3 · CAPA and data book

    ✍️ A CAPA was closed without an effectiveness check. How do you respond and prepare the data book?
    Model answer: Reopen the CAPA, define the effectiveness criteria and monitoring period, collect data and close only when the issue has not recurred. For the data book, build the index from the SDRL, check each document for revision, signature and date, confirm certificates match the traceability records, close or list open NCRs, and issue with a transmittal.
    Q. When is a CAPA properly closed?
    • A When the action is done and effectiveness is verified
    • B When the form is signed
    • C When the manager is on leave
    • D When the report is shorter

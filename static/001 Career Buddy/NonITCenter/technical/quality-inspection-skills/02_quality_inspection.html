    What is Quality Inspection? The Role & Fundamentals

    Start from zero: what inspection is, why it matters, what inspection professionals do, the types of inspection and the basic inspection process.

    🧭 What is Quality Inspection?

    Quality inspection is the activity of examining, measuring, testing or gauging a product, material or piece of work and comparing the result with specified requirements to decide whether it conforms — accept or reject — and recording the evidence.

    Figure 1.1 · Every inspection follows the same five steps
    💡
    Inspection does not make a product good — it proves whether it is good and stops bad product from moving forward. Prevention comes from fixing processes using inspection data.

    ⚖️ Inspection vs Testing vs QA vs QC

    TermMeaningExample
    InspectionExamining/measuring a characteristic against requirementsMeasuring hole diameter, checking weld profile
    TestingApplying a condition or load to evaluate performance or propertiesPressure test, tensile test, electrical test
    Quality Control (QC)All product-checking activities — inspection is its core toolDaily inspection, testing and release
    Quality Assurance (QA)System-level prevention: procedures, plans, audits, trainingWriting the inspection procedure; auditing inspectors

    Reading Drawings, Specifications & Inspection Standards

    An inspector is only as good as their understanding of the requirement. Learn to extract every inspectable characteristic and acceptance criterion.

    📐 Drawings for Inspectors

    Drawing elementWhat the inspector extracts
    Title blockPart no., revision, material, scale, units, general tolerance
    Revision tableExact changes — confirm you hold the latest approved revision
    DimensionsEvery size with its tolerance → inspection characteristics
    General tolerance noteDefault limits for untoleranced dimensions (e.g. ISO 2768-m)
    Surface finish symbolsRequired roughness, e.g. Ra 1.6 μm
    GD&T framesForm, orientation, location, runout requirements with datums
    Weld symbolsWeld type, size, length, side, contour/finish
    NotesCoatings, heat treatment, marking, special tests, critical characteristics
    EXAMPLE · Ballooning a drawing

    Before a first article inspection, the inspector numbers every characteristic (1, 2, 3…) on a copy of the drawing — called ballooning — and creates a matching inspection report so no dimension is missed.

    🎯 GD&T Essentials for Inspection

    Symbol typeExamplesHow it is inspected
    FormFlatness, straightness, circularitySurface plate + dial indicator, CMM
    OrientationPerpendicularity, parallelism, angularityRelative to datum using indicators, CMM
    LocationPosition, concentricityCMM, position gauges
    RunoutCircular / total runoutRotate on datum axis with dial indicator
    DatumsA, B, C reference featuresSet up the part on datums exactly as specified
    ⚠️
    Measuring from the wrong datum is one of the most common inspection mistakes — the result can be "in tolerance" but meaningless.

    📜 Standards & Acceptance Criteria

    TypeExamplesInspector uses it for
    Quality managementISO 9001 (and sector: IATF 16949, AS9100, ISO 13485)How inspection fits the QMS
    Product / construction codesASME VIII, ASME B31.3, AWS D1.1, API 650, IS 800Acceptance criteria for welds, dimensions, tests
    Test-method standardsASME Sec V (NDT), ASTM E8 (tensile), ISO 6507 (hardness)How to perform tests
    Sampling standardsISO 2859-1 / ANSI Z1.4 (attributes), ISO 3951 (variables)Sample sizes and acceptance numbers
    Tolerance standardsISO 2768 (general tolerances), ISO 1101 (GD&T)Default and geometric tolerances
    Personnel qualificationISO 9712, ASNT SNT-TC-1A, AWS CWIProving inspector competence
    💡
    Always cite the exact clause/table used as acceptance criteria in your report, e.g. "AWS D1.1 Table 8.1".

    🗃️ Inspection Documentation & Control

    • Use only controlled, current drawings, procedures and checklists; mark obsolete copies "superseded".
    • When requirements are unclear or conflict, raise a Technical Query (TQ) — never interpret silently.
    • Write technical documentation (inspection procedures, reports, TQs) in clear, specific, measurable language.
    • Keep records legible, signed, dated and retrievable for the required retention period.
    Q. A dimension has no tolerance written next to it. Where do you find its limits?
    • A Guess based on experience
    • B General tolerance note in the title block (e.g. ISO 2768)
    • C Ask production
    • D Any value is acceptable

    Measurement, Inspection Techniques & Equipment

    Master the inspector's toolkit: measurement principles, instruments, visual/weld/coating inspection, NDT, testing, equipment care and calibration.

    📏 Measurement Fundamentals

    AccuracyHow close a measurement is to the true value.
    Precision (repeatability)How close repeated measurements are to each other.
    ResolutionSmallest change an instrument can display.
    Measurement uncertaintyDoubt range around a result due to instrument, method, operator and environment.
    Reference temperatureDimensional measurements are referenced to 20 °C — large parts expand with heat.
    • Choose instrument resolution ≈ 1/10 of the tolerance.
    • Clean the part and the instrument; let parts reach room temperature.
    • Take repeated readings for critical characteristics; record the actual value, not just "OK".

    🧰 Inspection Tools & How to Use Them

    ToolUseGood practice
    Vernier / digital caliperOD, ID, depth, step (±0.02 mm)Zero before use; measure square to the feature
    MicrometerPrecise OD (±0.001–0.01 mm)Use ratchet for consistent force
    Bore gaugeInternal diametersSet with ring gauge/setting master; rock to find minimum
    Dial indicator / height gaugeHeight, runout, flatnessUse on a surface plate; check indicator travel
    Go/No-Go gaugesHoles, threads, slotsGo must enter; No-Go must not — never force
    Radius / feeler / weld gaugesRadii, gaps, weld sizeChoose correct blade; read at the right point
    Surface roughness testerRa/Rz valuesCorrect cut-off length and direction
    Hardness testerHRC, HB, HVVerify with test block daily
    DFT gaugeCoating thicknessCalibrate on shims over the substrate
    CMM / optical / laser scannersComplex geometry, GD&TProgrammed per datums; temperature controlled

    👁️ Visual, Weld & Coating Inspection

    InspectionWhat to checkTypical tools
    Visual (VT)Cracks, dents, burrs, corrosion, finish, markingAdequate lighting (≥ 500 lux general, ≥ 1000 lux close), magnifier, mirror
    Weld — beforeMaterial, WPS, welder qualification, fit-up, cleanliness, preheatFit-up gauge, thermometer/crayons
    Weld — duringParameters, interpass temperature, cleaningClamp meter, temp. sticks
    Weld — afterSize, profile, undercut, porosity, cracks, spatterFillet gauge, undercut gauge
    CoatingSurface prep grade (e.g. Sa 2½), profile, DFT, adhesion, holidaysReplica tape, DFT gauge, holiday detector

    🧪 NDT & Destructive Testing

    MethodFindsLimitations
    PT — liquid penetrantSurface-breaking cracksSurface defects only; clean surface needed
    MT — magnetic particleSurface/near-surface cracksFerromagnetic materials only
    UT — ultrasonicInternal defects, thicknessOperator skill; couplant; geometry
    RT — radiographyVolumetric internal defectsRadiation safety; planar cracks may be missed
    Hardness, tensile, bend, impactMechanical propertiesDestroys or marks the sample
    📌
    Inspectors may perform or witness NDT only within their qualification level (ISO 9712 / ASNT Level I, II, III).

    🔧 Equipment Operation, Maintenance & Troubleshooting

    • Daily checks: zero/verification on reference standards (e.g. hardness test block, gauge blocks).
    • Preventive maintenance: cleaning, battery/probe checks, lubrication, storage in cases.
    • Troubleshooting: inconsistent readings → check zero, cleanliness, probe/tip wear, temperature, operator technique, then calibration.
    • Remove damaged or doubtful equipment from use with a "Do not use" tag.

    🏷️ Calibration Control

    • Every instrument: unique ID, calibration certificate, status label with due date, entry in the calibration register.
    • Calibration must be traceable to national/international standards.
    • Out-of-tolerance found at calibration → evaluate all results since the last good calibration.
    • Measurement System Analysis: Gauge R&R (variables) and attribute agreement analysis (go/no-go, visual) prove inspection results are reliable.
    Q. A hardness tester gives a reading outside the value of its daily test block. First action?
    • A Continue testing
    • B Stop using it, investigate (indenter, setup), and evaluate recent results
    • C Adjust readings by hand
    • D Ignore the test block

    Inspection Planning, Sampling, Coordination & Supervision

    Run inspection as a well-planned operation: plans, checklists, sampling, scheduling, coordination, supervision, safety and progress tracking.

    🗺️ Inspection Plans, ITPs & Control Plans

    DocumentPurpose
    Quality / inspection planDefines what, how, when and by whom inspection happens for a product or project
    ITP (Inspection & Test Plan)Sequence of inspections with references, acceptance criteria, records and Hold/Witness/Review points
    Control plan (manufacturing)For each characteristic: method, frequency, sample size, reaction plan
    Inspection checklistStep-by-step checks the inspector completes and signs
    ITP codeMeaning
    H — HoldWork stops until inspection is performed and released
    W — WitnessParty notified; work proceeds if not attended after notice
    R — ReviewDocument review
    S — SurveillanceRandom monitoring

    ✅ Designing Good Checklists

    • One line per characteristic, with reference (drawing/clause), acceptance criteria, method/tool, result (actual value) and status.
    • Order checks the way the inspector physically works around the part.
    • Mark critical characteristics clearly.
    • Leave space for remarks, photos and sign-off.
    EXAMPLE · Checklist line
    #CharacteristicRequirementToolActualOK/NOK
    5Bore Ø40.000–40.025Bore gauge BG-0440.012OK
    6Ra bore≤ 1.6 μmRoughness tester RT-021.9NOK

    📊 Sampling Inspection

    ConceptMeaning
    LotBatch produced under the same conditions
    AQLAcceptable Quality Limit — worst average quality tolerated
    Inspection levelGeneral I, II (normal), III; special S-1…S-4
    Sample size code letterFrom lot size + inspection level
    Ac / ReAccept / reject numbers
    Switching rulesNormal ↔ tightened ↔ reduced based on recent results
    EXAMPLE · ISO 2859-1 example

    Lot 3,000 pcs, level II → code letter K → sample 125. AQL 1.0 → Ac 3, Re 4. Found 2 defectives → accept lot (defective pieces still rejected individually).

    ⚠️
    Sampling never applies to safety-critical characteristics where the contract or code demands 100% inspection.

    📅 Scheduling, Resources & Tracking

    • Build the inspection schedule from production/project plans and ITP hold/witness points.
    • Allocate inspectors by qualification, skill, location, workload; book equipment and labs.
    • Send inspection notifications within agreed notice periods.
    • Track in Excel/ERP: requests, status, backlog, reports issued, NCRs raised.
    • Monitor progress daily; escalate bottlenecks before they delay production.

    🤝 Coordination, Communication & Supervision

    🏭
    Production
    Agree timing, give fast clear feedback.
    🧑‍💼
    Client / TPI
    Notify witness points, share reports.
    🚚
    Vendors
    Source inspections, documentation, NCRs.
    👷
    Inspection team
    Daily brief, work allocation, report review, coaching.
    • Supervisors check a sample of reports for completeness and correctness before release.
    • Safety first: permits, PPE, working at height, confined spaces, radiation zones; inspectors stop if unsafe.
    • Communicate facts with evidence: "Spool 14 rejected — root gap 5 mm vs 2–3 mm (photo 112)."

    Defect Classification, NCR, Troubleshooting & CAPA

    Classify defects consistently, control rejected product, troubleshoot recurring problems and drive corrective action.

    🗂️ Defect Classification

    ClassMeaningExample
    CriticalLikely to cause unsafe conditions or failure of the product's primary functionCrack in a pressure-retaining weld
    MajorLikely to cause failure or materially reduce usabilityBore oversize affecting bearing fit
    MinorUnlikely to reduce usability; departure from standardsSmall cosmetic scratch on non-visible surface
    • Use defect catalogues and limit samples (approved good/bad examples) so all inspectors judge the same way.
    • Run attribute agreement studies to check inspectors are consistent.

    🚩 NCR & Control of Rejected Product

    Figure 5.1 · NCR flow from the inspector's view
    DispositionInspector's task
    ReworkRe-inspect to the original requirement
    RepairInspect to the approved repair procedure; record approval reference
    Use-as-isConfirm concession/approval is documented before release
    ScrapEnsure it is physically destroyed or marked to prevent reuse
    Return to vendorRecord and issue supplier NCR/SCAR

    🧰 Troubleshooting Inspection Problems

    1. Is the result real? Re-measure, check instrument, datum and method.
    2. Define the problem: what, where, when, how many (Is / Is-not).
    3. Check recent changes: material lot, operator, machine, tool, program, environment.
    4. Use data: when did it start, which lots, which machines?
    5. Confirm the cause by turning the problem on/off.

    🧠 Root Cause & CAPA

    ToolUse
    5 WhysDrill to a controllable cause
    Fishbone (6M)Brainstorm causes
    ParetoPrioritise defect types
    8DCustomer/supplier problems
    FMEAPrevent failures by risk ranking
    ActionExample
    CorrectionRework 12 oversized bores
    Corrective actionAdd in-process bore check every 5 pcs; replace worn tool holder
    Preventive actionApply same check to similar machines
    Effectiveness checkZero bore NCRs in next 10 lots

    Inspection Analytics, Effectiveness & Leadership

    Use inspection data to improve processes, prove inspection effectiveness, review designs and lead inspection teams.

    📈 Inspection KPIs

    KPIFormula / meaning
    Rejection rateRejected ÷ inspected × 100
    First Pass YieldAccepted first time ÷ inspected × 100
    Defects per unit (DPU)Total defects ÷ units inspected
    PPMDefective parts per million
    Inspection escape rateDefects found by customer ÷ total defects found
    Inspection backlog / turnaroundPending requests; time from request to report
    NCR closure timeAverage days to close NCRs
    EXAMPLE · Escape rate

    Inspection found 95 defects; the customer found 5 more. Escape rate = 5 ÷ 100 = 5% — a signal to strengthen inspection or process controls.

    📊 Data Analysis, SPC & Dashboards

    • Trend analysis: defects by week, line, supplier, inspector.
    • Pareto: the vital few defect types.
    • Control charts (X̄-R, p-chart) show process stability; act on out-of-control signals.
    • Cp / Cpk capability — Cpk ≥ 1.33 typical minimum; ≥ 1.67 for critical characteristics.
    • Excel / Power BI: pivot tables, slicers by product/supplier, clear KPI cards, owner and action columns.

    🎯 Inspection Effectiveness & Risk-Based Inspection

    • Inspection is never 100% effective — fatigue, lighting, and unclear criteria let defects escape.
    • Improve with clear criteria, limit samples, good lighting, error-proofed gauges and automation (vision systems, CMM).
    • Risk-based inspection: focus effort on critical characteristics, new suppliers, new processes, and areas with high escape history.
    • Reduce inspection where processes are proven capable (Cpk high, stable) — move effort to prevention.

    📝 Design Review & Advanced Technical Analysis

    • Review drawings for inspectability: measurable datums, realistic tolerances, clear acceptance criteria.
    • Perform calculations: tolerance stack-ups, test pressures, sample sizes, measurement uncertainty.
    • Lead failure analysis with labs (fractography, metallography).
    • Feed inspection findings back into design and process FMEAs.
    EXAMPLE · Tolerance stack-up

    Three spacers each 10.00 ± 0.05 mm stacked → total 30.00 ± 0.15 mm (worst case). If the assembly requires ± 0.10 mm, the design needs tighter spacer tolerances or selective assembly.

    🧑‍💼 Leadership, Audits & Reporting

    • Audit the inspection process itself: procedures followed, tools calibrated, records complete, inspectors qualified.
    • Build team competence: qualification matrix, training, coaching, rotation to avoid fatigue.
    • Report to management with KPIs, trends, risks and recommendations.
    • Promote "quality at source": operators self-inspect with clear criteria; inspectors verify and coach.
    Q. Customer finds 10 defects; inspection found 190. Escape rate?
    • A 5%
    • B 10%
    • C 19%
    • D 0.5%

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer.

    🔍 Role & Fundamentals

    What does a quality inspector do?
    Understands requirements from drawings, specifications and standards; prepares checklists and calibrated tools; inspects, measures and tests; decides accept/reject/hold; marks status; records objective evidence; raises NCRs and supports corrective action.
    Attribute vs variable inspection?
    Attribute inspection classifies items as pass/fail or counts defects (go/no-go, visual). Variable inspection records actual measured values (e.g. 25.03 mm), which allows trend and capability analysis.
    What are first-, second- and third-party inspection?
    First-party is by the manufacturer's own team, second-party by the customer, third-party by an independent inspection agency.
    How do you handle an untoleranced dimension?
    Apply the general tolerance specified in the title block or notes (e.g. ISO 2768-m). If none is specified, raise a technical query.

    📏 Measurement, Sampling & Planning

    How do you select an instrument?
    Match the characteristic and range, choose resolution about one-tenth of the tolerance, confirm calibration status, consider access and temperature, and use the correct datum setup.
    Explain AQL sampling.
    From lot size and inspection level get the sample size code letter and sample size; from the AQL get accept/reject numbers. If defectives ≤ Ac accept the lot, ≥ Re reject it. Defective pieces are always rejected individually.
    What is a hold point?
    A step in the ITP where work must stop until the specified inspection is performed and formally released by the named party.
    What would you do if your daily verification on a test block fails?
    Stop using the instrument, tag it, investigate setup and wear, recalibrate if needed and evaluate results taken since the last good verification.
    How do you ensure all inspectors judge visual defects the same way?
    Clear criteria, defect catalogues, approved limit samples, good lighting, training and attribute agreement studies.

    🛠️ NCR, Data & Leadership

    Classify critical, major and minor defects.
    Critical: likely to cause unsafe conditions or failure of primary function. Major: likely to cause failure or reduce usability. Minor: unlikely to reduce usability.
    What is an inspection escape and how do you reduce it?
    A defect that passes inspection and reaches the customer. Reduce with clear criteria, better methods and lighting, error-proof gauges, automation, training, and fixing process causes through CAPA.
    Which KPIs would you track?
    Rejection rate, first pass yield, defects per unit or PPM, escape rate, inspection backlog/turnaround, NCR closure time and repeat defects.
    How would you lead an inspection team?
    Set clear standards and priorities, allocate work by competence, review reports, coach, manage safety and fatigue, use data to improve and communicate openly with production and clients.

    ⚙️ Scenario: Valve Body Production

    Your plant machines valve bodies for an oil & gas customer. Requirements: drawing VB-300 Rev E, critical bore Ø50.000–50.025 mm, seat face flatness 0.01 mm, Ra ≤ 0.8 μm on seat, hydrotest 1.5× rated pressure, and customer witness (W) at final inspection. Lots of 500 pcs; AQL 0.65 for non-critical dimensions. Last month the customer reported 3 seat leaks on delivered valves.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Plan the Inspection

    ✍️ List the inspection stages and what you check at each.
    Model answer: Receiving: forging material grade vs MTC, heat number, visual and hardness. First article: full ballooned layout incl. GD&T on CMM. In-process: critical bore and seat flatness every 5 pcs, Ra on seat. Final: 100% critical characteristics, sampling (AQL 0.65) for non-critical, visual, hydrotest. Pre-dispatch: marking, preservation, packing, documents.
    Q. Which characteristics need 100% inspection rather than AQL sampling?
    • A Non-critical chamfers
    • B Critical bore, seat flatness, seat finish and hydrotest
    • C Paint colour only
    • D None

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Measurement

    ✍️ Choose instruments for the bore, flatness and roughness, with reasons.
    Model answer: Bore: bore gauge or air gauge with 0.001 mm resolution set on a ring master (tolerance 0.025 mm → resolution ≤ 0.0025). Flatness: CMM or optical flat/monochromatic light for 0.01 mm. Ra: calibrated roughness tester with correct cut-off on the seat face, verified on a roughness standard.

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Investigate the Seat Leaks

    Q. First step after the customer complaint?
    • A Blame the customer
    • B Contain: quarantine stock, check WIP and finished goods, notify customer, and raise an NCR
    • C Close the complaint
    • D Increase production
    ✍️ Use data and 5 Whys to propose a root cause and CAPA.
    Model answer: Data: all 3 leaks from lots made on machine M-7 in week 32; seat Ra measured 1.1–1.4 μm (above 0.8). Why rough? Worn finishing insert. Why not detected? Roughness checked only at first article, not in-process. Why? Control plan missing in-process Ra check. Corrective: replace insert, add tool-life limit, add Ra check every 10 pcs with SPC chart, update control plan. Preventive: apply to similar seats on other machines. Effectiveness: zero leak complaints in next 3 months and seat Ra Cpk ≥ 1.33.

    4️⃣ Task 4 · Report the Results

    ✍️ Calculate: 500 inspected, 470 accepted first time, 30 rejected; customer found 3 escapes. Report FPY, rejection rate and escape rate.
    Model answer: FPY = 470/500 = 94%. Rejection rate = 30/500 = 6%. Escape rate = 3/(30+3) ≈ 9.1% of all defects found. Present on a dashboard with Pareto of defect types and CAPA status.

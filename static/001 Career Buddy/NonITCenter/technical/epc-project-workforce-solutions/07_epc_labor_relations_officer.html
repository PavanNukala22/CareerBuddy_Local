    The Role & Labor Relations Basics

    Before you sit across the table from a union or settle a site dispute, understand what an EPC Labor Relations Officer owns, how an issue travels from complaint to resolution, and the vocabulary every labor conversation uses.

    🧭 What does this officer do?

    An EPC Labor Relations Officer is the neutral, fact-based link between project management and the workforce. You help negotiate agreements, settle disputes before they stop work, keep the project inside local labor law, and manage the relationship with unions. You are not a lawyer and you do not take sides. You are expected to prevent small problems from becoming stoppages.

    📜
    Negotiate
    Prepare and run collective bargaining so agreements are fair and affordable.
    ⚖️
    Resolve
    Handle grievances and site disputes through a clear, fair procedure.
    🏛️
    Comply
    Keep pay, hours, welfare and terminations inside local labor law.
    🤝
    Connect
    Run the day-to-day interface with unions and worker representatives.
    💡
    Example. 400 pipefitters say their overtime has been paid late for two months and warn they may stop work. Your job is to check the facts, meet their representatives, agree a payment date, and fix the payroll cut-off so it does not repeat.
    📝
    Two ways you meet this role. (1) You hire this officer for a client. (2) Your staffing firm supplies workers and you manage their labor relations. This course covers both: how the work is done, and how to vet someone who says they have done it.

    🛤️ How an issue travels

    Most labor trouble starts small. The earlier you catch it, the cheaper it is to fix.

    Read left to right, then continue on the next row.
    StageWhat happensTypical output
    Issue raisedA worker, crew or union flags a concern.Logged complaint
    Listen and fact-checkHear the story, check payroll, attendance, contract.Fact sheet
    MeetMeet representatives with the facts in hand.Agreed next steps
    ResolveFix the cause, confirm in writing.Signed note or minutes
    PreventChange the process that caused it.Updated procedure
    Example: one complaint, start to finish
    Monday: a crew says wages are 5 days late. Monday afternoon: payroll confirms a bank cut-off error. Tuesday: you meet the delegate and give a payment date. Wednesday: wages paid. Friday: payroll moves its cut-off two days earlier. Zero stoppage.

    📚 Key vocabulary

    You will hear these words every day. Learn them first and the rest of the course gets much easier.

    TermMeaningExample
    Collective bargainingNegotiation between employer and worker representatives on work terms.“Talks on the site wage scale start Monday.”
    CBACollective bargaining agreement: the written result of those talks.“Overtime rules are in clause 12 of the CBA.”
    Union / delegateOrganization of workers / a representative chosen by workers.“The pipefitters’ delegate asked for a meeting.”
    GrievanceA formal complaint about pay, conditions or treatment.“Grievance filed over rota changes.”
    MediationA neutral helps the sides reach their own agreement.“We asked a mediator to join.”
    ArbitrationA neutral hears both sides and decides.“The CBA sends this clause to arbitration.”
    Strike / work stoppageWorkers collectively stop work.“A 2-day stoppage cost the schedule.”
    No-strike clauseUnion promises not to strike on matters the agreement covers.“Clause 30 is the no-strike clause.”
    Bargaining unitThe group of workers an agreement covers.“All site craft below supervisor level.”
    Labor inspectorGovernment official who checks labor-law compliance.“An inspector visits the camp Thursday.”
    Example: decoding a real message
    “The delegate raised a grievance over rota changes; clause 12 says 7 days’ notice; if step 2 fails it goes to mediation.”
    Decoded: a worker representative filed a formal complaint that shift schedules were changed without the notice the agreement requires, and the agreed procedure moves to a neutral helper if the second step does not solve it.

    👥 Who you work with

    🏢 Management side

    • Project manager: owns schedule pressure.
    • HR and payroll: own contracts, pay and records.
    • Site safety and camp managers: see problems first.
    • Legal counsel: advises on law and risk.

    🧑‍🔧 Worker side

    • Union delegates and officials: formal representatives.
    • Crew foremen: informal voice of the crew.
    • Non-union workers: same fair treatment, same procedure.
    • Subcontractor workers: employed elsewhere, working on your site.
    📝
    Tip. Walk the site and the camp every week. Half of all disputes are visible on the ground (long queues, broken buses, late pay rumors) before anyone writes a complaint.
    Example: a walk that paid off
    On a camp walk you notice 60 people waiting 90 minutes for buses. You raise it with logistics, and the transport complaint that was building never reaches the union.

    📡 Signal, not syntax

    You do not need to be a lawyer. You need to hear whether a candidate has really done this work. Signal is evidence of ownership: numbers, constraints, trade-offs and honest failures.

    🔴 Low signal

    “I handled labor issues and worked with unions on a large project.”

    🟢 High signal

    “I resolved 90 grievances in a year, 80% at the first step, and negotiated a site wage agreement for 1,200 workers with no stoppage.”

    ✅
    Rule of thumb: ask how many, what was at stake, what you personally decided, and what went wrong. Real officers answer all four without hesitating.
    Example: the four questions in action
    Resume: “Negotiated with unions.”
    How many? Three CBAs. At stake? Wage scale and shift rules. Personal role? Led the management table. Went wrong? One round broke down over overtime, so we brought in a mediator.

    Labor Relations Fundamentals

    A plain-English map of why megaprojects are special for labor relations, who is employed by whom, and which rules sit above which.

    🏗️ Why megaprojects are different

    EPC megaprojects bring thousands of workers together for a short time, often far from home, under tight deadlines.

    👥
    Large crews
    Thousands of workers, many trades, many employers.
    🌍
    Many nationalities
    Different languages, expectations and legal status.
    🏕️
    Remote camps
    Living conditions become a labor issue.
    ⏱️
    Schedule pressure
    Every lost day is costly, so small disputes grow fast.
    💡
    Example. A late bus in a city is an annoyance. At a remote camp it is 600 people who cannot reach work, and a complaint that reaches the union by lunch.

    🧩 Who employs whom

    RelationshipMeaningLabor relations implication
    Direct hireEmployed by the main contractorYou own pay, discipline and grievances
    Subcontractor laborEmployed by a subcontractorSubcontractor is employer; you coordinate and monitor
    Agency / labor supplySupplied by a recruitment or manpower agencyCheck agency compliance, pay and fees
    Expatriate / TCNHired from outside the project countryPermits, travel, contracts and language matter
    Local hireResident of the project countryLocal law and community relations dominate
    Example: same site, three employers
    On one civil crew, 40 are direct hires, 30 belong to a subcontractor and 20 come through an agency. A wage complaint from the 30 is first raised with their employer, with you coordinating.

    🪜 Which rules rank highest?

    Several layers of rules apply at once. In general, higher layers override lower ones, and the worker gets the more protective rule. Always confirm the details in your country.

    LayerExampleNote
    1. National lawLabor code, wage rules, safety lawSets the minimum standard
    2. Sector or national agreementsIndustry-wide agreementsApply where the law or agreement extends them
    3. Site or company CBAYour project’s collective agreementCan improve on the law, not go below it
    4. Company policyHandbook and proceduresMust be consistent with the layers above
    5. Individual contractThe worker’s own contractCannot give less than the layers above
    Example: law versus agreement
    The CBA says overtime paid after 10 hours, but national law requires overtime after 8. You apply the more protective rule unless counsel confirms otherwise, and fix the CBA at the next renewal.

    ⚖️ Core worker rights and employer duties

    TopicWhat it means in practice
    PayCorrect wages paid on time with clear payslips
    Hours and restWorking hours, rest days and overtime within legal limits
    Safety and welfareSafe work, decent camp, food and transport
    Fair treatmentNo discrimination, harassment or forced labor
    RepresentationFreedom to join or not join a union and to bargain collectively
    📝
    Freedom of association and collective bargaining are core principles of the International Labour Organization (ILO). National laws differ on how they are applied, so always check local rules.
    Example: a rights check
    A worker says his passport is held by a supervisor. Holding passports against a worker’s will is a serious issue in many countries. You escalate it to HR and legal the same day.

    💸 The cost of a labor dispute

    Disputes cost more than the claim itself: lost hours, schedule delay, morale and reputation.

    ⚠️
    Illustrative arithmetic. A stoppage of 300 workers for 3 days of 8 hours at $15 an hour of paid time lost is 300 × 3 × 8 × 15 = $108,000, before any schedule delay claim.
    Example: the cheaper fix
    The disputed overtime claim is worth $60,000. Settling it early costs less than the $108,000 stoppage it may cause, and keeps trust. Compare both before you decide.

    🪤 Common beginner traps

    ❌ Mistake

    • “Labor relations means fighting the union.”
    • “If the CBA says it, the law does not matter.”
    • “Non-union workers have no rights.”

    ✅ Reality

    • It means fair, honest dealing that prevents disputes.
    • The CBA cannot go below the legal minimum.
    • All workers are covered by law, and deserve a fair procedure.
    Example: avoiding the “only union” trap
    A non-union crew raises a safety complaint. You use the same grievance procedure you would for union members, and the problem is solved in a day.

    Collective Bargaining Agreements

    How to prepare, negotiate, agree and administer a CBA that is fair to workers and affordable for the project.

    🗺️ The bargaining workflow

    A CBA is a written agreement between the employer and worker representatives on terms of work. Every step has an owner and an output.

    Read left to right, then continue on the next row.
    Example: exit rules between steps
    You do not table a wage offer until costing is approved by finance, the mandate is signed off by the project director, and legal has checked the draft language.

    📄 What a CBA usually contains

    SectionTypical contentWhy it matters
    Scope and partiesWho is covered and who signsAvoids coverage disputes
    Wages and allowancesScales, shift, travel and camp allowancesThe cost core
    Hours and overtimeNormal hours, rota, overtime rulesFrequent source of claims
    Leave and rotationAnnual leave, rest periods, travelAffects planning
    Safety and welfareCamp standards, PPE, transportPrevents welfare disputes
    Grievance procedureSteps, timelines, neutral helpThe dispute route
    Term and renewalStart, end, renewal talksPrevents a gap with no agreement
    No-strike / no-lockoutPeace during the agreement termProtects the schedule
    Example: a clause doing its job
    Clause 12 requires 7 days’ notice before a rota change. When a manager changes a rota with 2 days’ notice, the clause gives the delegate a clear, fair basis to object.

    🧮 Preparing: data and costing

    Never enter a room without numbers. Cost every proposal before you table or accept it.

    ItemIllustrative figure
    Base monthly wage2,500
    Union claim4% increase = 100 more per month
    New monthly wage2,600
    Workers covered500
    Annual cost of the claim100 × 12 × 500 = 600,000
    ✅
    Always annualize. A 100 monthly raise sounds small. For 500 workers it is 600,000 a year, which is why finance must approve the mandate first.
    Example: a data pack
    Your pack contains pay scales, overtime hours, absence rates, market wage data, the project’s remaining months, and the cost of each proposal. Both sides can argue from the same numbers.

    🤝 Negotiating well

    • Interests, not just positions: ask why a demand matters.
    • Package trades: trade across items, not one at a time.
    • Know your best alternative: what happens if talks fail?
    • Good faith: share relevant information and respond honestly.
    • Record everything: proposals, counter-offers and agreements in writing.
    💡
    Example trade. The union wants 4%. You offer 3% plus an attendance bonus. 3% of 2,500 = 75, so 75 × 12 × 500 = 450,000 a year, saving 150,000 versus the full claim, and the bonus gives workers something real.
    Example: finding the interest
    The union insists on a transport allowance. Asking why shows the real issue is a 90-minute bus delay. A fixed bus schedule solves it for less money.

    ✍️ Agree, ratify and implement

    • Ratify: confirm the union’s members and your management approve the deal as the law and rules require.
    • Communicate: explain the deal in plain language and the workers’ languages.
    • Train supervisors: they apply it every day.
    • Update payroll: set the new rates and effective date correctly.
    • Monitor: review in the first 30 days for misunderstandings.
    ⚠️
    A good deal badly implemented creates new disputes. Check payroll on the first pay day after any change.
    Example: a launch checklist
    Before the new overtime rule starts, supervisors get a one-page briefing, payroll tests 20 sample payslips, and notice boards carry the rule in three languages.

    🗓️ Administering the agreement

    TaskExample
    Interpretation questionsA joint committee agrees what “continuous shift” means
    Coverage questionsA new crew asks whether the CBA covers them
    Expiry calendarOpen renewal talks well before the end date
    RecordsKeep signed versions, minutes and side letters
    📝
    Expiry rule of thumb. If the CBA ends in 60 days and talks have not started, you are already late. Open talks now and agree a timeline.
    Example: an expiry caught early
    Your calendar flags a CBA ending on 31 March. You open talks on 1 January, agree a timeline in writing, and reach a deal before expiry. No gap, no uncertainty.

    Site Dispute Resolution

    How to sort, handle and prevent disputes: the grievance ladder, neutral help, work stoppages and root-cause fixes.

    🔍 Types of dispute

    TypeMeaningExample
    Individual grievanceOne worker’s complaintUnpaid overtime for one person
    Collective grievanceA group’s complaintA whole crew’s late wages
    Rights disputeDisagreement about how an existing agreement or law appliesWhat a clause on rota notice means
    Interest disputeDisagreement about new termsParties cannot agree the next wage scale
    Example: sorting before acting
    A crew says the rota change broke the CBA. That is a rights dispute, so you go to the grievance procedure. A demand for a new allowance is an interest dispute, so it goes to bargaining.

    🪜 The grievance ladder

    Most CBAs and company procedures set steps and timelines. Using them protects both sides.

    Read left to right, then continue on the next row.
    StepOwnerIllustrative timeline
    1. SupervisorLine supervisor2 working days
    2. Labor Relations OfficerYou3 working days
    3. Joint meetingDelegate + manager5 working days
    4. Senior managementProject director7 working days
    5. Neutral helpMediator or arbitratorPer agreement
    Example: solved at step 1
    A welder’s break time was cut. The supervisor restores it the same day and notes it. One minute of care avoids five levels of paper.

    🕊️ Mediation, conciliation and arbitration

    MethodWho decidesBest used when
    MediationThe parties, helped by a neutralBoth sides want a deal but are stuck
    ConciliationThe parties, with a (often official) conciliatorLaw or agreement offers a formal service
    ArbitrationThe arbitratorThe agreement sends the issue to a final decision
    💡
    A cooling-off period is a set waiting time before industrial action starts, used in some systems to allow further talks. Confirm whether your country or agreement has one.
    Example: choosing the method
    A pay-scale dispute stalls. Both sides still want a deal, so you propose a mediator. A one-day mediation produces a package that neither side could have reached alone.

    🚨 Handling a work stoppage: the first 24 hours

    If workers stop, your calm, factual response sets the tone.

    first-24-hours checklist1. Safety first: make equipment and areas safe; check no one is at risk
2. Listen: meet the representatives and hear the issue
3. Facts: confirm what happened with payroll, HR and the supervisor
4. Communicate: tell management and workers what you know and next steps
5. Advice: involve legal counsel on notices, pay and procedure
6. Record: keep a timeline of calls, offers and decisions
7. Resolve: agree a path back to work and a date to fix the cause
    ⚠️
    Do not punish a whole crew in anger, cut off food or camp services, or threaten dismissal before taking advice. These actions often turn a small dispute into a large one.
    Example: a calm response
    A 150-person crew stops over a cancelled rest day. You secure the work area, meet two delegates within the hour, and agree to restore the rest day next week. The crew is back at work by afternoon.

    📈 Root causes and early warning

    SignalWhat it may meanResponse
    Repeated complaints on late pay across crewsPayroll process problemReview cut-offs and bank timing
    Rising absence on one site areaPoor supervision or conditionsVisit and speak with the crew
    Notices appearing in the campOrganized concern buildingMeet delegates early
    Many requests for rota changesFatigue or planning issueReview schedule and rest rules
    Example: using an early-warning log
    Your log shows 14 complaints about the same bus route in one month. You raise it with logistics, fix the route, and complaints drop to zero.

    🔬 Worked example: the late-overtime dispute

    400 workers say overtime has been paid late. Payroll finds 12 overtime hours per person unpaid at a base rate of 10 an hour with a 1.5 multiplier (illustrative).

    StepActionNumber
    Owed per worker12 hours × 10 × 1.5180
    Owed in total400 workers × 18072,000
    MeetingDelegates and payroll agree a payment dateWithin 5 days
    PreventionMove payroll overtime cut-off two days earlierProcess fix
    ✅
    Why it worked: facts first, one meeting with the right people, a date in writing, and a process change so it does not happen again.

    Local Labor Law Compliance

    How to know what law applies, build a compliance map, keep records that stand up to inspection, and handle change.

    🏛️ What labor law covers

    AreaTypical questions
    Wages and paymentMinimum wage, payment timing, deductions, payslips
    Hours and overtimeDaily and weekly limits, overtime rate, rest days
    LeaveAnnual, sick, public holidays, special leave
    Termination and noticeNotice, severance, final settlement, fair process
    Safety and welfareAccommodation, food, transport, medical care
    Permits and contractsWork permits, contract language, sponsorship
    Equality and conductDiscrimination, harassment, forced labor
    Association and bargainingUnion rights, recognition, strike rules
    ⚠️
    Rules vary by country and sometimes by region. This course teaches the method. Always confirm the actual rules with HR, legal counsel or the labor authority.
    Example: same question, different answers
    Two projects in two countries ask how much notice is required before releasing a worker. The answers differ by country and contract, so you check each local rule, not your last project’s.

    🗺️ Build a compliance map

    A compliance map is a one-page table per jurisdiction listing the rule, the source and the owner.

    example compliance map (one row each)Topic:      Overtime
Rule:       Paid at a higher rate after the daily limit (confirm figure locally)
Source:     National labor code, article (to be filled)
Owner:      Payroll Manager
Checked by: Legal counsel, date, next review date
    Example: a map that saved a project
    The map shows rest-day work needs extra pay and written agreement. Supervisors are briefed, and a surprise inspection finds no issues.

    ⏰ Working hours and overtime

    Hours are the most common source of claims. Track them carefully and pay them correctly.

    💡
    Illustrative calculation. Base 10 an hour, overtime 1.5× (law varies). For 12 overtime hours: 12 × 10 × 1.5 = 180.
    • Keep signed or system-generated time records.
    • Watch for long runs of overtime, which cause fatigue and safety risk.
    • Make sure supervisors cannot approve unpaid “extra hours”.
    Example: a records win
    A worker claims 40 unpaid hours. Time-clock records show 22. You pay the 22, show the evidence, and the claim closes without a dispute.

    💵 Wage payment and records

    ControlWhy it matters
    Pay on the legal date, every timeLate pay is the number one dispute trigger
    Clear payslips in the worker’s languagePrevents “I do not understand my pay” complaints
    Lawful, documented deductions onlyUnlawful deductions create claims
    Electronic or traceable paymentGives proof of payment where the law or process uses it
    Records kept for the required periodEvidence for inspections and claims
    Example: a payslip fix
    Workers complain about “missing” pay. The payslip lists allowances under codes nobody understands. A plain-language payslip with three languages ends the complaints.

    🚪 Termination, demobilization and fairness

    • Follow the notice and process required by law and contract.
    • Use written, uniform criteria when choosing who leaves.
    • Pay final dues correctly and on time.
    • Never base choices on age, nationality, gender, union activity or similar traits.
    📝
    Dismissing someone right after they raised a complaint or joined a union can look like retaliation. Document the real, job-related reason before acting and take advice.
    Example: a fair release
    When 60 of 200 welders must leave, you score everyone on remaining scope, skills, performance and contract end date. The notes are filed, and the one challenge is answered in a day.

    🕵️ Inspections and audits

    A labor inspector or client auditor may visit. Be ready, open and cooperative.

    PrepareExample
    DocumentsContracts, permits, payslips, time records, accident log
    PeopleA named contact who knows the files
    SiteCamp, kitchens, transport and safety notices in good order
    Follow-upWritten action plan for any finding
    Example: an inspection handled well
    An inspector finds one expired medical certificate. You show the renewal booked for Monday and the corrective list. The finding closes with no penalty.

    🔔 Keeping up with change

    1. Subscribe to official updates from the labor authority and legal counsel.
    2. When a rule changes, note the effective date.
    3. Assess the effect on pay, CBA clauses and contracts.
    4. Tell HR, payroll and supervisors before the date.
    5. Update the compliance map and record the change.
    Example: a minimum-wage change
    A new minimum wage starts on 1 January. You find 80 workers below it, payroll adjusts them in the December run, and no worker is underpaid on day one.

    The Labor Union Interface

    How to build a respectful, predictable working relationship with unions and worker representatives while staying fair, lawful and neutral.

    🏢 Understanding unions

    Unions are organizations of workers formed to represent their interests. Structures vary by country, but you will usually meet these levels.

    LevelWhoWhat they do
    Site delegate / stewardElected or appointed by workers on siteRaises day-to-day issues
    Branch or regional officialFull-time union staffLeads negotiations and bigger disputes
    National federationLarge union bodySets policy and sector agreements
    Example: knowing who to call
    A crew issue is minor, so you speak to the site delegate. A wage-scale discussion needs the regional official, so you invite both and agree who leads.

    📅 Building a regular rhythm

    A predictable meeting rhythm builds trust and catches issues early.

    example standing-meeting templateFrequency:   Fortnightly, 45 minutes
Attendees:   Labor Relations Officer, HR, site delegates
Agenda:      1. Actions from last meeting  2. New issues  3. Upcoming changes  4. AOB
Ground rules: Facts first, no surprises, minutes within 24 hours
    Example: no surprises
    You tell delegates about a coming rota change two weeks early in the standing meeting. They bring concerns, you adjust one shift, and the change starts smoothly.

    🪑 Access, facilities and rights

    • Provide meeting space and notice boards where the law or agreement requires.
    • Allow union time for representatives as agreed.
    • Respect rights to meet workers outside work hours as permitted.
    • Treat union members and non-members equally.
    📝
    Facilities and time off for representatives are set by law and agreement. Check the CBA and local rules before agreeing or refusing.
    Example: a clear rule
    The CBA gives delegates 4 hours a week of union time. You agree a simple request form, so supervisors stop arguing about it.

    📞 Communication and escalation

    LevelIssueWho speaks
    DailyCrew-level questionsForeman with delegate
    WeeklyRecurring issues and actionsLabor Relations Officer and delegates
    MonthlyPolicy, rota and cost changesHR manager and union official
    UrgentStoppage risk, safety or serious complaintProject director and union official with counsel
    Example: clear voices
    During a dispute, only the project director and you speak for management. Mixed messages from three supervisors stop, and rumors drop.

    👥 Multiple unions and non-union workers

    • Know which union represents which group and what the law or agreement says about recognition.
    • Treat all unions with the same respect and process.
    • Keep non-union workers inside the same grievance procedure.
    • Avoid favoring one group in pay or promotion.
    Example: three unions, one rule
    Three unions represent three crafts. You give each the same meeting rhythm, the same notice rule and the same grievance steps, which removes claims of unfair treatment.

    🧭 Ethics and red lines

    NeverInstead
    Retaliate against workers for union activity or complaintsDocument and apply rules equally
    Interfere in union elections or decisionsStay neutral
    Spy on representatives or collect private data unlawfullyUse only lawful, relevant information
    Make promises you cannot keepCommit only to what is approved
    ⚠️
    If a manager asks you to “find something” on a union leader, decline and escalate to HR or legal. Your credibility depends on neutrality.
    Example: holding the line
    A project manager asks for a leader’s personal social media history. You say no, explain the legal risk in writing, and offer a lawful route: a fact-based conduct review if there is a real work issue.

    Vetting & Screening Workflows

    How to find, question and present an EPC Labor Relations Officer, with templates you can copy today.

    🪜 Titles and seniority by scope

    Titles vary, so judge by scope.

    LevelTypical scopeWhat they can do alone
    CoordinatorAdmin support and recordsLogs grievances, books meetings, tracks actions
    OfficerOwns labor relations for a siteRuns grievances, advises managers, works with delegates
    Senior / LeadOwns a large projectLeads negotiations, handles stoppages, advises directors
    ManagerOwns several projects or countriesSets strategy, leads CBAs, manages risk and counsel
    Example: scope over title
    A “Labor Relations Manager” whose examples are all “filed grievance forms” has coordinator scope. Another candidate titled “HR Officer” who led a CBA for 1,200 workers has officer-to-senior scope.

    📞 The intake call

    Good screening starts before sourcing. Ask the hiring manager:

    1. How big is the workforce, and which country or countries?
    2. Is there a union, a CBA or both?
    3. Is a negotiation or renewal coming up?
    4. Direct hires, subcontractors or agencies?
    5. Any recent disputes or stoppages?
    6. Language needs and onsite expectations?
    7. Budget, rotation and start date?
    JD saysBrief you write
    “Tough labor negotiator with 15 years, all unions, all countries.”Must-have: led a CBA negotiation for 500+ workers on a construction or EPC project. Nice: multi-country, mediation. Flexible: 8 years if scope is strong.
    Example: a sharper brief
    Instead of “union experience”, the brief says: “Has led management in at least one site-level CBA negotiation and resolved grievances for 1,000+ workers.” Sourcing becomes faster.

    🔎 Boolean sourcing

    Boolean search combines keywords with AND, OR, NOT and quotes.

    linkedin / job board: labor relations officer("labor relations" OR "labour relations" OR "industrial relations" OR "employee relations")
AND (EPC OR construction OR "oil and gas" OR megaproject)
AND ("collective bargaining" OR CBA OR union OR grievance)
NOT (recruiter OR "talent acquisition" OR intern)
    compliance-focused variant("labor law" OR "labour law" OR "employment law compliance")
AND (site OR project OR camp)
AND ("labor inspection" OR "labour inspection" OR audit OR "wage protection")
    💡
    Why NOT recruiter? “Employee relations” and “labor relations” sometimes appear in recruiting profiles. Excluding those words removes unrelated profiles.
    Example: narrowing a search
    labor relations AND construction → 6,000 profiles. Add collective bargaining OR CBA → 1,400. Add the client’s region → 160, a list you can review.

    🎙️ The phone screen (15 to 20 minutes)

    Advanced: KPIs, Risk & Scenarios

    What separates a good officer from a trusted advisor: measuring results, handling serious disputes, working across countries, and doing it ethically.

    📊 KPIs that matter

    KPIFormulaExample
    First-step resolutionGrievances resolved at step 1 ÷ all grievances72 ÷ 90 = 80%
    Union densityUnion members ÷ eligible workers420 ÷ 600 = 70%
    Stoppage daysDays of lost work from disputes0 this year, 3 last project
    Average days to closeClosing date − filing date, averaged6 days
    Repeat grievancesSame issue raised again within 90 days4 out of 90
    💡
    Reading the numbers. Low first-step resolution means supervisors need training. Many repeats mean you fixed symptoms, not causes. Rising complaint themes are your early warning.
    Example: one change per quarter
    First-step resolution is 55%. The review finds supervisors do not know the procedure. A 1-hour briefing lifts it to 80% next quarter.

    ⚠️ Risk and compliance

    RiskExampleControl
    Wage claimsLate pay or unpaid overtimePayroll controls and records
    Retaliation claimsDismissal after a complaintDocumented real reason; uniform rules
    Unlawful stoppageStrike during a no-strike termProcedure, talks, counsel
    Permit and contract breachesExpired permits, unclear contractsCompliance map and audits
    ReputationPoor camp conditions go publicRegular welfare checks
    📝
    Fair treatment. Use written, uniform, job-related criteria for decisions about people, and never base them on union activity, age, nationality or gender.
    Example: a retaliation risk avoided
    A manager wants to remove a delegate after a heated meeting. You ask for the documented work reason first. There is none, so no action is taken, and a claim is avoided.

    🧪 Scenario planning

    ScenarioWhat happensYour response
    Strike notice during a CBA termUnion gives notice on a matter the CBA sends to arbitrationPoint to the procedure and no-strike clause, offer urgent talks or mediation, record everything, take legal advice
    Wildcat stoppageWorkers stop without noticeSecure safety, meet representatives, find root cause, avoid collective punishment
    CBA expires with no dealTalks stall near the end dateAgree an extension or interim terms, escalate to neutral help
    Many demobilizations at onceLarge releases raise worryEarly dates, clear criteria, settlement and redeployment options
    ⚠️
    Illustrative cost. A 3-day stoppage of 300 workers is about $108,000 (300 × 3 × 8 × 15). Compare it with the claim, but also count repeat risk, precedent, legality and morale.
    Example: a strike notice handled
    The union gives 48 hours’ notice over an overtime clause that the CBA sends to arbitration. You propose urgent mediation, cite the procedure, brief counsel and keep talking. The notice is withdrawn.

    🌐 Working across countries

    • Map each country’s rules, authorities and union structures.
    • Use local counsel and local staff where possible.
    • Apply the stricter of company policy and local law, where lawful and sensible.
    • Respect languages and cultures in notices, meetings and payslips.
    Example: two countries, one policy
    A project in two countries has different overtime rules. You keep one company principle (no unpaid work) and apply each country’s rate and limit through two compliance maps.

    📖 Full case study

    Req: Labor Relations Officer for a megaproject with 1,200 workers, three unions and a CBA expiring in 4 months.

    1. Intake: Peak 1,200 workers, three unions, CBA expiry in 4 months, a stoppage of 3 days on the last project.
    2. Prepare: Compliance map, data pack and costing; the 4% claim costs 600,000 a year for 500 covered workers.
    3. Negotiate: 3% plus an attendance bonus agreed. 3% costs 450,000, saving 150,000 a year versus the claim.
    4. Run: 90 grievances in a year, 72 at step 1 (80%).
    5. Prevent: Fortnightly union meetings, early-warning log, payroll cut-off moved two days earlier.
    6. Result: 0 stoppage days (3 last project). CBA renewed 3 weeks before expiry.
    ✅
    Why it worked: early preparation, costed proposals, a predictable meeting rhythm, a fair procedure, and fixing root causes.

    The Role & Procurement Hiring Basics

    Before you screen a single expeditor or inspector, understand what an EPC Procurement & Supply Chain Recruiter owns, how a requirement travels from client to candidate, and the words every procurement hiring conversation uses.

    🧭 What does this recruiter do?

    An EPC Procurement & Supply Chain Recruiter finds, screens and presents the people who buy, chase, inspect and move equipment and materials for large construction projects: refineries, power plants, LNG terminals, solar farms, water plants and mines. You do not buy the equipment yourself. You find the people who make sure the right equipment arrives at site, on time, to the right quality.

    🔎
    Source
    Find procurement managers, buyers, expeditors, vendor inspectors and logistics staff on LinkedIn, job boards, inspection-agency networks and your own database.
    🧪
    Screen
    Check project experience, equipment types, certifications, INCOTERMS knowledge, availability, rate and travel readiness.
    📤
    Submit
    Send the client a short, evidence-based write-up with verified certificates, not just a CV.
    ✈️
    Mobilize
    Coordinate interviews, offers, visas, medicals and travel so the person arrives at the vendor shop or site ready to work.
    💡
    Example. A client says: "We need 2 field expeditors and 1 static equipment inspector for our gas plant packages in South Korea, starting in 3 weeks." Your job is to turn that sentence into a short list of verified, available, travel-ready people within the agreed turnaround time (TAT).

    🛤️ The hiring pipeline

    Every requirement ("req") moves through the same stages. In EPC procurement hiring, two stages carry extra weight: the certificate check (for inspectors) and mobilization (getting a person to a factory or site in another country).

    Read left to right, then continue on the next row.
    StageWhat happensTypical output
    IntakeMeet the procurement or QA/QC manager. Clarify role, equipment type, location, rotation, rate and start date.Written req summary
    SourcingSearch, message and re-engage candidates, including freelance inspectors and agency staff.Long list (20–50 profiles)
    ScreeningCV check, 15–20 minute call, project and equipment questions, certificate verification.Shortlist (3–5 people)
    SubmittalPresent candidates with evidence, certificates, rate and availability.Submission email or ATS note

    Example: one req, start to finish
    Req #3107 (Field Expeditor, Italy) arrives Monday 9:00. Intake call at 11:00. By evening you have 30 profiles. Tuesday: 7 calls. Wednesday noon: 3 candidates submitted. Client interviews Thursday, offer Friday. Visa-free travel inside Europe means the expeditor starts at the vendor shop the following Monday. TAT = 2 days; time to mobilize = 3 days after offer.

    📚 Key vocabulary

    You will hear these words every day. Learn them first and the rest of the course gets much easier.

    TermMeaningExample
    EPCEngineering, Procurement and Construction: one contractor designs, buys and builds the facility."It is an EPC contract for a 500 MW power plant."
    PackageA group of items bought together from one vendor."The pumps package has 14 pumps."
    Vendor / supplierThe company that makes or sells the equipment."The compressor vendor is in Germany."
    POPurchase Order: the contract to buy from a vendor."The PO for valves was placed in March."
    ExpeditingChasing vendors so documents and equipment arrive on time."Expediting found the motor was 3 weeks late."
    Vendor inspectionChecking equipment quality at the factory before shipment."The inspector witnessed the hydrotest."
    Long-lead itemEquipment that takes many months to make, so it is ordered early."Transformers are long-lead: order them first."
    INCOTERMSStandard trade terms that say who pays for transport and who carries the risk, and where."Delivery is FCA vendor's works."
    RotationA work pattern of weeks on site followed by weeks at home."It is a 28/28 rotation."
    MobilizationGetting a hired person to the work location ready to start."Mobilization needs a visa and a medical."
    Example: decoding a real message
    "Need a desk expeditor for the long-lead packages, FCA terms, hybrid, start ASAP."
    Decoded: the client wants someone who chases vendors by phone and email (desk expeditor), focused on equipment that takes many months to make (long-lead), where the vendor hands the goods to the client's carrier at an agreed place (FCA), and the person can partly work from home.

    👥 Who you work with

    🏢 Client side

    • Procurement manager: owns buying, vendors and procurement staff.
    • QA/QC or inspection manager: decides which inspectors are qualified.
    • Project manager: cares about schedule and cost.
    • HR / contracts: approves rates, contracts, visas and compliance.

    🧑‍🔧 Candidate side

    • Permanent staff: employed by an EPC contractor or owner.
    • Freelance contractors: paid by day or hour, move project to project.
    • Third-party inspection (TPI) staff: work for inspection agencies.
    • Referrals: expeditors and inspectors know each other well.
    📝
    Tip. Build a relationship with the QA/QC manager, not only procurement. Inspectors are approved or rejected by QA/QC, so knowing exactly which certificates and equipment types they accept saves you many wasted submittals.
    Example: a useful QA/QC chat
    The QA/QC manager says: "The last inspector had pressure vessel experience but had never seen a heat exchanger tube bundle test." You add "Which heat exchanger tests have you witnessed?" to every static inspector screen. Your next two submittals are both approved.

    📡 Signal, not keywords

    You do not need to know how to inspect a weld or negotiate a PO. You need to hear whether the candidate does. Signal is evidence that someone has really done the work: equipment names, values, dates, problems solved and honest limits.

    🔴 Low signal

    "Responsible for procurement and expediting activities on various oil and gas projects."

    🟢 High signal

    "Expedited 35 POs worth about $60M for an LNG project: compressors, heat exchangers and valves. Recovered a 5-week delay on a gas turbine by tracking the late sub-supplier and arranging a partial shipment."

    ✅
    Rule of thumb: ask what equipment, how much (value or quantity), which project, and what went wrong. Real procurement people answer all four without hesitating.
    Example: the four questions in action
    CV: "Vendor inspection of static equipment."
    What equipment? Pressure vessels and air coolers. How much? About 40 items over 2 years. Which project? A refinery expansion. What went wrong? A vessel failed radiography on a nozzle weld; it was repaired and re-tested before release. That is a candidate who has actually done it.

    Module 02 · Industry Literacy

    EPC Project Fundamentals

    A plain-English map of how a large industrial project is designed, bought and built, so every line on a procurement CV has a place to live in your head.

    🏗️ What is an EPC project?

    Think of building a house. Someone draws the plans, someone buys the bricks, windows and boiler, and someone builds it. In an EPC project, one contractor does all three for a very large facility.

    📐
    E = Engineering
    Design the plant and write the specifications for every item to be bought.
    🛒
    P = Procurement
    Buy, chase, inspect and ship equipment and materials. This is your area.
    👷
    C = Construction
    Install everything at site, then test and start up the plant.
    🏢
    The Owner
    The company that pays for and will operate the plant, often called the client or employer.
    💡
    Why procurement matters. On many EPC projects, equipment and materials are a large share of total project cost, and late equipment is one of the most common reasons projects finish late. That is why clients pay well for strong expeditors and inspectors.

    🗓️ Project phases

    Phases overlap. Procurement starts while engineering is still running, because long-lead equipment must be ordered early.

    Read left to right. Procurement (highlighted) is where your candidates work.
    PhaseWhat happensProcurement hiring demand
    FEEDFront-End Engineering Design: basic design and cost estimate.Low a few senior procurement planners
    Detailed engineeringFull design, data sheets, material requisitions.Rising buyers, procurement engineers
    ProcurementOrders placed, vendors manufacture, inspect, ship.Peak expeditors, inspectors, logistics
    ConstructionEquipment installed at site.High material controllers, site logistics
    CommissioningTesting and start-up.Low spare parts and warranty buyers
    Example: timing your sourcing
    A client says its major POs were placed last month. That means vendors are starting to manufacture now, so the client will soon need expeditors and vendor inspectors. Start building that pipeline today, before the req arrives.

    📄 Contract models

    The contract model tells you who your candidate will actually work for and how much risk sits on their shoulders.

    ModelHow it worksWhat it means for hiring
    EPC / LSTKLump Sum Turnkey: the contractor delivers the complete plant for a fixed price and carries most of the cost and schedule risk.Contractor hires large procurement teams; strong focus on cost control and schedule.
    EPCMEngineering, Procurement and Construction Management: the contractor manages the work, but purchase orders and contracts are usually placed in the owner's name.Candidates must follow the owner's procedures and approvals; more reporting.
    ReimbursableThe owner pays actual cost plus a fee.Less price pressure; documentation and audit trail matter more.
    PMCProject Management Consultant supervises the contractor for the owner.Hires "owner's representative" inspectors and procurement reviewers.
    Example: same job title, different employer
    A "Senior Buyer" on an LSTK project works for the contractor and is judged on savings and schedule. A "Senior Buyer" on an EPCM project places orders on behalf of the owner and must follow the owner's approval rules. Ask: "Whose name was on the purchase orders?"

    🌱 Greenfield vs brownfield

    🌾 Greenfield

    A brand-new facility on empty land. Large, new equipment packages, big logistics challenges (roads, ports, heavy lifts). Needs many expeditors and logistics staff.

    🏭 Brownfield

    Upgrading or expanding a plant that is already running. Smaller, urgent orders, matching existing equipment, shutdown deadlines. Needs buyers who understand spares and fast-track orders.

    Example: matching experience
    A refinery shutdown needs a buyer who can source 200 replacement valves in 6 weeks. A candidate from brownfield shutdown work fits better than one who only bought new equipment on a 4-year greenfield project.

    🏭 Sectors & what they buy

    SectorTypical equipment boughtUseful candidate background
    Oil & gas / refiningPressure vessels, heat exchangers, pumps, compressors, valves, pipingStatic and rotating inspection, API codes
    LNG & petrochemicalsLarge compressors, cryogenic equipment, modulesLong-lead expediting, heavy-lift logistics
    PowerGas and steam turbines, transformers, switchgear, boilersElectrical inspection, IEC standards
    RenewablesSolar panels, inverters, wind turbines, batteries, cablesHigh-volume logistics, factory audits
    WaterPumps, membranes, pipes, tanksRotating equipment, coating inspection
    MiningCrushers, conveyors, mills, structural steelMechanical and structural inspection
    Example: transferable sectors
    A transformer expeditor from a power project can usually move to a solar project (which also buys large transformers and switchgear). A pressure vessel inspector from refining is a natural fit for petrochemicals.

    ⚙️ Equipment categories

    Inspectors and buyers usually specialise by equipment category. Matching the category is the single most important screening check for an inspector.

    CategoryExamplesMeaning
    StaticPressure vessels, columns, heat exchangers, tanks, pipingEquipment with no moving parts
    RotatingPumps, compressors, turbines, fansEquipment that spins or moves
    ElectricalTransformers, switchgear, motors, cablesPower generation and distribution
    InstrumentationControl valves, analysers, transmitters, control systemsMeasure and control the process
    Bulk materialsPipe, fittings, flanges, valves, cable, structural steel, boltsHigh-quantity items, ordered by quantity not tag
    ⚠️
    A "Mechanical Inspector" title can mean static or rotating. Always ask: "Which equipment have you inspected most in the last 3 years?"
    Example: category mismatch
    The req asks for a rotating inspector to witness compressor run tests. A candidate with 10 years of tank and vessel inspection is a static specialist. Strong CV, wrong category: do not submit without asking the client.

    Module 03 · The Procurement Lifecycle

    The EPC Procurement Lifecycle

    Follow one piece of equipment from the engineer's request to its arrival at site, and learn which role owns each step.

    🔁 Lifecycle overview

    Read left to right, then continue on the next row. Steps 5 and 6 run in parallel while the vendor manufactures.
    one pump, start to finish1. Engineer writes MR for 4 cooling-water pumps   -> Procurement engineer
2. RFQ sent to 5 approved vendors                 -> Buyer
3. Engineering checks offers (TBE), buyer compares prices (CBE)
4. PO placed with the best-value vendor           -> Buyer / procurement manager
5. Vendor drawings, materials, progress chased    -> Expeditor
6. Performance test witnessed at factory          -> Vendor inspector
7. Pumps packed, shipped, cleared through customs -> Logistics coordinator
8. Pumps received and stored at site              -> Material controller

    📝 From requisition to PO

    StepPlain-English meaning
    MR (Material Requisition)Engineering's shopping list for one package: specifications, data sheets, quantities and required documents.
    AVL (Approved Vendor List)The list of vendors the owner allows for each type of equipment.
    RFQ / ITBRequest for Quotation / Invitation to Bid: the package sent to vendors asking for offers.
    TBE (Technical Bid Evaluation)Engineers check each offer meets the specification.
    CBE (Commercial Bid Evaluation)Procurement compares price, delivery, payment terms, INCOTERMS and warranty.
    PO (Purchase Order)The signed contract with the chosen vendor.
    Example: why the cheapest bid lost
    Vendor A quoted the lowest price but 60 weeks delivery and EXW terms. Vendor B was 6% more expensive but 42 weeks delivery and DAP site. The buyer recommended Vendor B because the schedule saving and included transport outweighed the price difference. A buyer who can explain a decision like this is showing real CBE experience.

    📦 After the PO is placed

    Once a PO is signed, the vendor starts work. The project now needs to track three streams at once: documents, manufacturing progress and quality.

    📑
    Vendor documents
    Drawings, calculations and test procedures listed in the VDRL (Vendor Document Requirements List). Engineering must approve them before manufacture.
    🏭
    Manufacturing progress
    Raw material ordered, fabrication, assembly, testing, painting, packing. Tracked by the expeditor against milestones.
    🔍
    Quality
    Inspection at agreed points in the ITP (Inspection and Test Plan), ending with a release for shipment.
    Example: a document delay becomes a delivery delay
    A heat exchanger vendor cannot start welding until engineering approves the drawings. Approval takes 5 weeks instead of 2. The expeditor spots this in the weekly report and pushes engineering to review faster, saving 2 weeks. Good expeditors chase both the vendor and their own team.

    ⏳ Long-lead items

    Long-lead items (LLIs) take many months, sometimes more than a year, to design, make and test. They often control the whole project schedule, so they get the most experienced expeditors and inspectors.

    Gas and steam turbinesLarge compressorsPower transformersHV switchgearLarge pressure vessels and reactorsSpecial alloy pipe and valvesLarge pumps
    📝
    Screening angle. Ask: "What was the longest-lead item you handled, and how did you track its sub-suppliers?" A big item depends on many smaller suppliers (castings, forgings, motors, bushings). Strong candidates name them.
    Example: the hidden sub-supplier
    A compressor vendor reports "on schedule" every week, but the casting for the compressor body comes from a foundry that is 6 weeks late. An experienced expeditor asks for the sub-order status, finds the problem early and escalates it while there is still time to recover.

    💼 Commercial terms you will hear

    TermMeaningExample
    Liquidated damages (LDs)Pre-agreed compensation the vendor pays if it delivers late."0.5% of PO value per week of delay, capped at 10%."
    Payment milestonesPayments released when stages are reached."10% on drawings approval, 60% on shipment, 30% on site acceptance."
    Bank guaranteeA bank promise that protects the buyer if the vendor fails."Advance payment guarantee for the 10% down payment."
    Letter of credit (LC)A bank pays the vendor once shipping documents are presented.Common with new or overseas vendors.
    Change order / variationA formal change to the PO scope, price or date."Added 2 spare impellers: change order #3."
    Example: a procurement manager answer with depth
    "The vendor was 4 weeks late, which would have triggered LDs. Instead of only claiming LDs, we agreed to waive part of them in exchange for air-freighting the critical spool pieces, which protected the site schedule." This shows commercial judgement, not just rule-following.

    🧩 Who works at each step

    StepMain roleWhat to screen for
    MR, RFQ, bid evaluationBuyer / procurement engineerPackages bought, values, number of vendors, CBE examples
    PO award & negotiationProcurement managerPortfolio value, team size, savings, approval levels

    👨‍👩‍👧 The procurement family

    🧑‍💼
    Buy
    Procurement manager, buyer, procurement engineer, subcontracts manager.
    ⏱️
    Chase

    🔍

    🚢

    Example: one team on a refinery project
    1 procurement manager, 6 buyers, 1 expediting lead with 5 expeditors, 1 inspection coordinator with 8 vendor inspectors (some via agencies), 2 logistics coordinators, and 4 material controllers at site. Each group is a different search for you.

    📋 Role-by-role guide

    "I selected vendors and negotiated prices" is buyer work. "I visited the vendor weekly and reported progress against the delivery date" is expeditor work. Many CVs mix both; ask which took most of their time.

    Module 05 · INCOTERMS Knowledge

    INCOTERMS 2020 for Recruiters

    The three-letter delivery terms on every purchase order. Learn what they mean in plain English, and how to test whether a candidate really understands them.

    🌍 What are INCOTERMS?

    INCOTERMS (International Commercial Terms) are standard rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC). The current version is INCOTERMS 2020, in force since 1 January 2020. Each term is three letters plus a named place, for example FCA Busan or DAP Jobsite.

    ✅ They answer

    • Where the seller delivers the goods
    • Where risk of loss or damage passes to the buyer
    • Who pays which costs (transport, insurance, export, import)
    • Who does export and import customs clearance

    ❌ They do not answer

    • When ownership (title) of the goods passes
    • Payment terms or price
    • What happens if the contract is broken
    • These are written elsewhere in the PO
    Example: a pizza delivery analogy
    EXW = you collect the pizza from the shop yourself. FCA = the shop hands it to your courier. DAP = the shop drives it to your door; you carry it inside. DPU = they carry it inside for you. DDP = delivered to your door with every fee and tax already paid by the shop.

    📖 The 11 terms

    INCOTERMS 2020 has 7 terms for any mode of transport and 4 for sea and inland waterway only.

    TermFull nameModeRisk passes to buyer when…Seller pays main freight?
    EXWEx WorksAnyGoods are made available at the seller's premisesNo
    FCAFree CarrierAnyGoods are handed to the buyer's carrier at the named placeNo
    CPTCarriage Paid ToAnyGoods are handed to the first carrierYes
    CIPCarriage and Insurance Paid ToAnyGoods are handed to the first carrierYes, plus insurance
    DAPDelivered at PlaceAnyGoods arrive at the named place, ready for unloadingYes
    DPUDelivered at Place UnloadedAnyGoods are unloaded at the named placeYes
    DDPDelivered Duty PaidAnyGoods arrive at the named place, import-cleared, ready for unloadingYes, plus import duties
    FASFree Alongside ShipSeaGoods are placed alongside the vessel at the loading portNo
    FOBFree On BoardSeaGoods are on board the vessel at the loading portNo
    CFRCost and FreightSeaGoods are on board the vessel at the loading portYes
    CIFCost, Insurance and FreightSeaGoods are on board the vessel at the loading portYes, plus insurance
    📝
    The "C" terms are the classic trick. Under CPT, CIP, CFR and CIF the seller pays for transport all the way to the destination, but the risk passes to the buyer much earlier, at origin. Cost point and risk point are in two different places.

    📍 Where risk moves

    Read the journey from left (seller) to right (buyer). Each dot is the point where risk passes for the terms listed under it.

    The further right a term sits, the more risk and responsibility stays with the seller. Green = buyer carries the most; orange = seller carries the most.
    Example: who carries the loss?
    A transformer is shipped CIF Jebel Ali. It is damaged by a storm at sea. The seller paid the freight and bought insurance, but risk passed to the buyer when the transformer was loaded on board at the origin port. So the buyer carries the loss and claims on the insurance policy the seller arranged.

    🏗️ INCOTERMS on EPC projects

    Common choiceWhy an EPC contractor uses it
    FCA vendor's worksThe contractor uses its own freight forwarder for all packages, so it controls schedule, consolidation and heavy-lift planning.
    FOB / CFR origin portTraditional for bulk or break-bulk sea cargo loaded directly onto a vessel.
    DAP jobsiteLocal or regional vendors deliver to site by truck; the contractor handles import clearance.
    DDPUsed less often for imports, because the seller must handle import duties and taxes in the buyer's country, which can be difficult.
    Example: a logistics coordinator with real depth
    "We bought 40 packages from 12 countries on FCA terms so our forwarder could consolidate cargo in Antwerp and ship one heavy-lift vessel to site. That saved two separate charters." The candidate explains why the term was chosen, not just its name.

    💬 Screening questions

    AskStrong answer containsWeak answer
    "Which INCOTERMS did you use most, and why?"A named term, the project, and the reason (control of freight, cost, customs)"Whatever was in the PO."
    "Under CIF, where does risk pass?"On board the vessel at the port of shipment, even though the seller pays freight to destination"At the destination port."
    "What is the difference between DAP and DDP?"Under DDP the seller also clears import and pays duties; under DAP the buyer does"They are the same."
    "Why use FCA instead of FOB for containers?"Containers are handed to the carrier at a terminal before loading, so FCA matches the real hand-over point"FOB is only for planes."
    "What changed in INCOTERMS 2020?"DAT became DPU; CIP now needs higher insurance cover; FCA can allow an on-board bill of ladingCannot name any change
    Example: memorised vs understood
    Candidate A recites all 11 full names perfectly but cannot say who pays import duty under DAP. Candidate B mixes up one full name but explains: "Under DAP, the vendor's truck arrives at our site, we pay import duty and unload." B understands how it works on a real project.

    🪤 Common traps

    ❌ Misconception

    • "Risk passes where the seller stops paying."
    • "INCOTERMS decide who owns the goods."
    • "DAT is still a current term."
    • "CIF insurance covers everything."

    ✅ Reality

    • Not under C terms: cost and risk points differ.
    • Ownership is set by the contract, not INCOTERMS.
    • DAT was replaced by DPU in 2020.
    • CIF requires only minimum cover; CIP 2020 requires higher cover.
    ⚠️
    Check the version. A CV that says "10 years' experience with INCOTERMS 2020" is impossible in 2026, since that version began in 2020. It may simply be careless wording, so ask politely which versions they have used.

    Module 06 · Sourcing Expeditors & Vendor Inspectors

    Expeditors & Vendor Inspectors

    The two most requested procurement roles on EPC projects. Learn what good looks like, which certificates matter and how to verify them.

    ⏱️ Expediting explained

    Expediting means making sure vendors deliver documents and equipment on the dates in the PO, and raising the alarm early when they will not.

    🖥️ Desk expeditor

    Works from the office or home. Uses calls, emails, vendor reports and trackers. Handles many POs at once, usually lower-risk items.

    🏭 Field / shop expeditor

    Visits vendor factories in person to see real progress. Assigned to critical and long-lead items. Travels often, sometimes based near a cluster of vendors.

    Many contractors give each PO a criticality level (for example 1–4 or A/B/C). The more critical the PO, the more often someone visits the factory.

    Progress reportsMilestone trackingSub-order statusVDRL trackingS-curveRecovery planShop visit report
    Example: desk vs field in action
    A desk expeditor sees that the vendor's report says "80% complete" for 3 weeks in a row. That is suspicious, so a field expeditor visits and finds the assembly bay empty because a motor has not arrived. The visit turns a vague report into a real problem the project can fix.

    💬 Screening expeditors

    AskStrong answer containsWeak answer
    "How many POs did you handle, and what equipment?"Numbers, equipment types, PO values, project names"Many POs for the project."
    "Tell me about a late item you recovered."Root cause, actions (visit, escalation, partial shipment, air freight), weeks recovered"I sent lots of emails."
    "How do you know a vendor's report is true?"Shop visits, photos, checking sub-supplier dates, comparing with inspection reports"I trust the vendor."
    "What goes in your weekly report?"Forecast vs PO date, critical issues, document status, actions with owners"Status of each PO."
    ✅
    Senior signal: talks about sub-suppliers, forecast dates and escalation, and can describe a time they warned the project early enough to change the outcome.

    🔍 Vendor inspection explained

    A vendor inspector (also called a source or shop inspector) visits the factory to confirm the equipment is built and tested to the specification before it ships. Fixing a problem at the factory is far cheaper than fixing it at site.

    TermMeaning
    ITP (Inspection and Test Plan)The list of every check and test, and who must attend each one.
    Hold point (H)Work must stop until the inspector or client releases it.
    Witness point (W)The inspector is notified and may attend; if they do not come after proper notice, work can continue.
    Review point (R)The inspector reviews documents (certificates, reports) rather than attending.
    PIM / pre-inspection meetingKick-off meeting with the vendor to agree the ITP and procedures.
    FAT (Factory Acceptance Test)The final functional test at the factory before shipping.
    NCR (Non-Conformance Report)A formal record that something does not meet the requirement.
    IRC / release noteThe inspector's document that releases the item for shipment.
    MDR / MRBManufacturer's Data Record (Book): the final pack of quality records proving compliance.
    Example: hold vs witness
    The hydrotest of a pressure vessel is a hold point: the vendor cannot paint the vessel until the inspector has seen it pass. The dimensional check is a witness point: the vendor gives 5 days' notice, and if the inspector cannot attend, the vendor proceeds and sends the report.

    Screening Workflows

    Repeatable steps that turn a vague procurement requirement into a short, defensible shortlist, with templates you can copy today.

    🗺️ End-to-end workflow

    Read left to right, then continue on the next row.

    Each stage has an exit rule: a clear yes or no before the candidate moves forward.

    Example: exit rules for an inspector req
    CV score below 12/18 → stop. Call confirms the right equipment category → move on. Certificate not found on the issuer's register → pause until resolved. Everything verified → write up and submit. Clear rules mean you can explain every decision to the client.

    📞 The intake call

    Ask the procurement or QA/QC manager these questions before you source anyone:

    1. Which packages or equipment will this person handle?
    2. Which certificates are mandatory, and which are nice-to-have?
    3. Where is the work: office, site, or vendor factories? Which countries?
    4. What is the rotation, duration and expected start date?
    5. What is the rate or salary range, and are travel, accommodation and per diem covered?
    6. Does the owner need to approve the person (common for inspectors)?
    7. Why did the last candidate fail?

    Example: turning a messy request into a clear brief.

    Client saysBrief you write
    "Need an experienced inspector, all equipment, ASAP, must know everything about QA/QC."Must-have: static equipment (vessels, exchangers), CSWIP 3.1 or AWS CWI, 5+ years vendor inspection. Nice: API 510. Location: resident in Korea, 6 months. Start: within 3 weeks. Owner approval: yes.

    Module 08 · Advanced Practice

    Advanced: Calibration, Verification & Metrics

    What separates a good recruiter from a trusted workforce partner: aligning with QA/QC and procurement leads, mobilizing people fast, catching fake certificates and proving results with numbers.

    🎯 Calibrating with the client

    Calibration means agreeing, with real examples, on what "good" looks like before you search at scale.

    1. Pick 3 profiles: one clear yes, one clear no, one maybe.
    2. Send them to the QA/QC or procurement lead for a 1-line verdict on each.
    3. In a 20-minute call, ask "What made the maybe a maybe?"
    4. Update your scorecard and repeat after the first 5 interviews.
    💡
    Example. The lead says the "maybe" had never worked under an owner-approved ITP. You add "Have you worked to a client or owner ITP with hold points?" to your screen, and rejections drop.

    ✈️ Rates, rotation & mobilization

    Pay depends on scarcity, location, certificates, rotation and contract type. Never quote numbers from memory; use current data from your firm, job postings and recent placements.

    FactorPushes rate upPushes rate down
    EquipmentTurbines, compressors, transformers, special alloysBulk materials, simple items
    CertificatesMultiple verified, specialist (e.g. API SIRE)None or expired
    LocationRemote sites, hardship regions, short-notice travelHome-country office work
    DurationShort, urgent assignmentsLong, stable contracts

    Mobilization checklist (start early, these often take longer than the hiring itself):

    Passport validityWork visa / business visaMedical fitnessBackground checksOwner approvalFlights & accommodationSite induction & safety trainingPPE
    Example: the visa decides the start date
    A client needs an inspector in a country where work visas take 4–6 weeks. A slightly less experienced candidate who already holds a valid visa may be the better submittal if the start date is fixed. Present both options honestly and let the client choose.

    🕵️ Certificate fraud & verification

    Inspector reqs are a target for fake or borrowed certificates. A calm, consistent verification routine protects your client and your reputation.

    Warning signHow to verify
    Certificate number not found on the issuer's registerContact the issuing body; ask the candidate to clarify; do not submit until resolved
    Name or photo on certificate does not match IDCompare with passport during a video call
    Certificate expired but CV says "current"Present it as lapsed, ask about renewal plans, tell the client
    Project names cannot be confirmedTake references from supervisors on those projects
    Answers about tests are genericAsk a specific follow-up: "What pressure and hold time did you use for the last hydrotest you witnessed?"
    📝
    Be fair. A typo or a recently renewed certificate is not fraud. Use the same checks for everyone and escalate only on evidence.
    Example: a fair verification step
    A candidate's API 510 number does not appear on the register. You ask politely. The candidate explains the certificate lapsed last year and sends proof of booking the renewal exam. You submit them clearly marked "API 510 lapsed, renewal booked", so the client decides with the full facts.

    📊 Metrics that matter

    MetricFormulaExample
    Submittal-to-interviewInterviews ÷ submittals6 ÷ 10 = 60%
    Interview-to-offerOffers ÷ interviews3 ÷ 6 = 50%
    Time to submit (TAT)First submittal date − req dateMon → Wed = 2 days
    Time to mobilizeStart date at site/vendor − offer acceptance date12 days
    Headcount fill ratePositions filled ÷ positions requested9 ÷ 10 = 90%
    Assignment completionContractors finishing assignment ÷ contractors started14 ÷ 15 = 93%
    💡
    Reading the numbers. Low submittal-to-interview means you submit the wrong people: tighten screening. Long time to mobilize means start visas, medicals and owner approvals earlier, in parallel with interviews.
    Example: diagnosing a month
    You submitted 12 inspectors and only 3 were interviewed: 25%. The client's feedback says "wrong equipment type" for 6 of them. Add an equipment-category gate before the call and re-measure next month.

    📖 Full case study

    Req: LNG project: 4 field expeditors and 6 vendor inspectors (3 static, 2 rotating, 1 electrical) across Korea, Japan and Italy. Start within 4 weeks.

    1. Intake: confirmed equipment per person, mandatory certificates, owner approval required, 6-month assignments.
    2. Source: separate Boolean searches per discipline; 210 profiles; 40 shortlisted by scorecard.
    3. Screen: 24 calls; 9 rejected for wrong equipment category; 15 remain.
    4. Verify: all certificates checked on registers; 1 lapsed certificate flagged honestly.
    5. Submit: 14 candidates with write-ups within 6 days; visa paperwork started for likely hires.
    6. Result: 10 offers, 10 acceptances, all mobilized by day 26.
    ✅
    Why it worked: one brief per discipline, consistent scoring, verified certificates in every write-up, and mobilization started in parallel with interviews.

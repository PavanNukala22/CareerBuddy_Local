    The Role & EPC Hiring Basics

    Before you hire a single welder or engineer, understand what an EPC Project Recruitment Manager owns, how a project's manpower plan becomes people on site, and the vocabulary every project meeting uses.

    🧭 What does this role do?

    An EPC Project Recruitment Manager plans and delivers the entire workforce for large engineering and construction projects: refineries, power plants, pipelines, LNG terminals, water plants and factories. One project can need hundreds or thousands of people, from design engineers in an office to welders and scaffolders on site, all arriving at the right time.

    The job has four core skills, and this course covers each one in depth:

    📈
    High-volume recruitment management
    Running campaigns that hire 50, 500 or 2,000 people without losing quality or control.
    📜
    EPC contract execution familiarity
    Knowing which contract clauses decide who you hire, when, and with whose approval.
    🗓️
    Project mobilization timelines
    Planning backwards from the date people must be on site: medicals, visas, travel, induction.
    🤝
    Client staffing interface
    Being the single point of contact for the client on CV approvals, reports and escalations.
    💡
    Example. A contractor wins a contract to build a gas processing plant. The plan says: "Peak manpower 1,200 in month 9." Your job is to turn that line into named, tested, cleared people on site, month by month, without delaying the project.

    🛤️ The EPC staffing pipeline

    Every position, from Project Director to rigger, moves through the same stages. In EPC, the stages after the offer (medicals, visas, travel) are where most delays happen, which is the biggest difference from office recruitment.

    📚 Key vocabulary

    You will hear these words in every project meeting. Learn them first and the rest of the course gets much easier.

    TermMeaningExample
    EPCEngineering, Procurement and Construction: one contractor designs, buys and builds."We won the EPC for the desalination plant."
    Manpower histogramChart of headcount needed per month over the project."Histogram peaks at 1,200 in month 9."
    Direct manpowerCraft workers who physically build: welders, fitters, electricians."800 directs at peak."
    Indirect manpowerSupervision and support: engineers, HSE, QA/QC, planners, admin."One indirect for every four directs."

    Example: decoding a real message
    "Raise MRFs for 30 directs and 6 indirects; mobilize before the month-4 peak; Construction Manager CV needs client approval."
    Decoded: submit formal hiring requests (MRF) for 30 craft workers (directs) and 6 support staff (indirects), get them on site before headcount peaks (mobilize), and send the senior manager's CV to the client (key personnel).

    👥 Who you work with

    🏗️ Contractor (your side)

    • Project Director / Manager: owns delivery, cost and schedule.
    • Construction Manager: decides craft numbers on site.
    • Planning / Controls: owns the histogram and schedule.
    • HR, Mobilization & Logistics: visas, travel, camp.

    🏢 Client & supply side

    • Client (owner): approves key CVs, audits manpower.
    • PMC: a consultant who manages the project for the client.
    • Manpower agencies: supply craft workers at volume.
    • Candidates: engineers, supervisors and craft workers.
    📝
    Tip. The planning engineer is your best friend. They update the schedule first, so they know about delays and scope changes days before the hiring requests change.
    Example: a useful planning chat
    The planner mentions: "Piping drawings are late; the piping peak moves from month 6 to month 8." You delay 80 pipefitter offers by two months and avoid paying idle workers, which could have cost the project weeks of wages.

    ⚖️ Volume with quality

    High volume tempts recruiters to "fill seats". On an EPC project, one unskilled welder can cause failed joints, rework and safety incidents. The goal is the right number, with the right skill, on the right date.

    🔴 Seat-filling

    "We mobilized 200 welders in 3 weeks." (Weld repair rate 18%, 30 sent home in month one.)

    🟢 Quality at volume

    "We mobilized 180 tested welders in 4 weeks. Repair rate 3%, early attrition 4%, every need date met."

    ✅
    Rule of thumb: measure every hire by count, skill, date and stay. Hitting the count but missing the other three is not success.
    Example: the four measures in action
    Weekly report: Count 95/100 electricians. Skill 100% trade tested. Date 92 arrived on the need date. Stay 2 left in the first 30 days. A clear, honest picture for the client.

    Module 02 · Industry Literacy

    EPC Fundamentals

    A plain-English map of how an EPC project runs, so every job title and every hiring request has a place in your head.

    🏭 What is EPC?

    Think of building a house. You need an architect to design it, someone to buy bricks and cement, and a builder to put it together. In EPC, one contractor does all three for a very large facility.

    📐
    E = Engineering
    Designing the plant: drawings, calculations, specifications. Office-based engineers and designers.
    📦
    P = Procurement
    Buying equipment and materials: pumps, pipes, cables. Buyers, expediters, inspectors.
    🏗️
    C = Construction
    Building on site: civil, steel, piping, electrical. Largest headcount by far.
    ⚡
    + Commissioning
    Testing and starting the plant so it runs as designed. Small, specialist teams.
    💡
    Why it matters to you. Each letter needs a different type of worker at a different time. Hiring 300 scaffolders during the engineering phase wastes money; hiring them late delays the whole project.

    🔁 Project phases and headcount

    Headcount follows the project phases. It is small during design, climbs steeply in construction, peaks, then drops as commissioning begins. This shape is the manpower histogram.

    An illustrative 12-month histogram. Hover a bar to see its headcount.
    PhaseWhat happensWho you hire
    FEEDFront-End Engineering Design: early concept and costSenior process and discipline engineers
    EngineeringDetailed drawings and specificationsEngineers, designers, document controllers
    ProcurementBuying, expediting, inspecting, shippingBuyers, expediters, vendor inspectors, logistics
    ConstructionCivil, steel, mechanical, piping, electrical, instrumentsSupervisors, foremen and large numbers of craft workers
    Commissioning & handoverPre-commissioning, testing, start-upCommissioning engineers and technicians
    Example: reading a histogram
    The chart above rises from 150 in month 4 to 650 in month 8. That means you must add about 500 people in four months, roughly 125 a month, and because visas and medicals take weeks, the hiring for month 8 must start around month 6.

    📑 Contract models

    The contract type changes who carries the cost risk, and that changes how tightly headcount is controlled.

    ModelHow it worksStaffing impact
    LSTK (Lump Sum Turnkey)Fixed price; contractor hands over a working plant.Contractor pays for overruns, so headcount and productivity are tightly controlled.
    EPCMContractor manages engineering, procurement and construction; the client usually holds the construction contracts.You hire mainly management and supervision; craft labour often comes through the client's subcontractors.
    Reimbursable (cost-plus)Client pays actual costs plus a fee or margin.Client audits every hire and rate; documentation must be perfect.
    Manpower supplyContractor supplies people at agreed hourly or monthly rates.Rates, timesheets and replacements are the contract.
    Example: same role, different contract
    On an LSTK job, the Project Manager asks you: "Can we do this with 45 fitters instead of 50?" because every extra person cuts profit. On a reimbursable job, the client asks: "Show us the approved MRF and rate for every one of the 50."

    🪜 Project roles & hierarchy

    LevelExample titlesHiring notes
    Project managementProject Director, Project Manager, Construction ManagerKey personnel; client approval; long notice periods
    Discipline leads & engineersPiping, Mechanical, Electrical, Civil, Instrumentation EngineersMatch sector experience (oil & gas, power)
    Support functionsHSE, QA/QC, Planning, Cost Control, Document ControlCertificates matter (NEBOSH, inspection codes)
    SupervisionGeneral Foreman, Foreman, ChargehandCrew leaders; check team size handled
    Craft (direct)Welder, Pipefitter, Electrician, Rigger, ScaffolderVolume hiring; trade tests; certificates
    Example: matching level to process
    A Construction Manager needs a formal CV to the client, reference checks and a 2-month notice buy-out. A batch of 40 scaffolders needs an agency campaign, a scaffolding trade test and a valid scaffold certificate check. Different levels, different processes.

    🌍 Project types & sectors

    🌱 Greenfield

    A new facility on empty land. Long build, steady ramp-up, large camp and logistics needs.

    🏭 Brownfield

    Work inside an operating plant. Strict permits, site-specific safety training, short shutdown windows.

    Oil & gasRefining & petrochemicalsPower & renewablesWater & desalinationMining & metalsInfrastructure
    Example: a shutdown (turnaround)
    A refinery stops for 21 days of maintenance and needs 300 extra workers. Every worker must be permit-trained and on site before day 1, because each day of shutdown costs the client lost production. Timing is everything.

    🪤 Common beginner traps

    ❌ Mistake

    • "A welder is a welder."
    • "Hire everyone at the start."
    • "The client doesn't care who we hire."

    ✅ Reality

    • Process, material and position matter (for example, a 6G pipe welder versus a structural welder).
    • Hire to the histogram; early hires sit idle on the payroll.
    • Key personnel and specified roles need client approval under the contract.
    Example: avoiding the welder trap
    The MRF says "Welder, 6G, TIG/SMAW, carbon steel pipe." A candidate with 10 years of structural welding in flat position is not a match, however experienced. Ask about process, material and test position before you submit.

    Module 03 · EPC Contract Execution Familiarity

    EPC Contract Execution

    You are not a lawyer, but the contract decides who you can hire, how fast, at what rate and with whose approval. Learn the clauses that shape every recruitment decision.

    📜 Why a recruiter reads the contract

    An EPC contract can run to thousands of pages, but a few sections directly control staffing. If you ignore them, you can make hires the client refuses to pay for, or miss approvals that block a person from entering site.

    ⚠️
    Always work from the actual contract. Clause names and rules differ by client and country. Ask the contracts team for the staffing-related sections and keep a one-page summary.
    Example: a costly miss
    A recruiter mobilizes a QA/QC Manager without client CV approval. The contract requires approval for all "specified personnel", so the client refuses site access. The person sits in a hotel for 10 days while a CV is rushed through. Reading one clause would have prevented it.

    🔑 Clauses that drive staffing

    Clause (typical name)What it usually saysWhat you do
    Key personnelNamed people for senior roles; no change without client consent and an equal or better replacement.Keep backup candidates for every key role.
    Personnel approvalClient reviews CVs for listed roles within a set number of days.Track submissions and approval deadlines.
    Manpower plan / histogramContractor commits to a staffing plan and reports against it.Hire to the plan; report gaps early.
    Rate scheduleAgreed rates per role (mainly reimbursable or manpower supply).Never offer above the rate without approval.
    Removal / replacementClient can require removal of anyone; contractor replaces at its own cost.Keep a standby pool.
    Liquidated damages (LDs)Fixed penalties for late milestones.Understand which hires sit on the critical path.
    HSE & qualificationsMinimum training, certificates and medical fitness.Build them into screening, not after the offer.
    Local contentRequired share of national hires or local spend.Track nationality mix every week.
    Example: the replacement clause in action
    The client removes a scaffolding foreman for a safety violation. Under the removal clause, the contractor must replace him at its own cost within 7 days. Because you kept two pre-approved foremen on standby, the replacement arrives in 3 days.

    📋 Reading a manpower exhibit

    Many contracts attach a staffing table (an "exhibit" or "schedule"). Read it like a shopping list with dates.

    contract exhibit (simplified)Position                 Qty  Mob. month  Approval  Rate band
Project Manager           1      M1         Key       A
Construction Manager      1      M3         Key       A
Piping Superintendent     2      M4         CV        B
QA/QC Inspector (Piping)  6      M5         CV        C
Pipe Welder 6G           80      M6         Test      D
Pipefitter               60      M6         Test      D
    • Key = named key personnel: strict approval and replacement rules.
    • CV = client reviews the CV before mobilization.
    • Test = trade test results must be recorded, often witnessed by the client.
    • Rate band = the maximum you can pay without a commercial change.
    Example: turning the exhibit into actions
    80 welders in month 6 with client-witnessed tests means you must book test bays and client inspectors for month 4–5, not month 6. The exhibit tells you what; your plan must work out when to start.

    🔄 Changes & variations

    Projects change. A variation order (or change order) adds, removes or alters scope. Each one can change the manpower plan.

    1. Hear about the change early (planning, project controls).
    2. Ask: which trades, how many, from when, for how long?
    3. Update the hiring plan, histogram and recruitment budget.
    4. Agree the changes with the client and record them in writing.
    Example: a scope addition
    The client adds a second storage tank. Planning estimates 25 extra tank welders and 10 riggers for 4 months. You raise the hiring plan change, get the commercial team to confirm cost recovery, and only then start the campaign.

    🌐 Compliance & local content

    Many countries require a share of jobs to go to nationals (for example, programmes often called nationalization, localization or in-country value). Contracts may set targets and penalties.

    AreaWhat to check
    Local contentTarget percentage, which roles count, reporting format
    ImmigrationVisa categories, quotas, profession titles on the visa
    Labour lawWorking hours, overtime, rest days, contract language
    Ethical recruitmentNo recruitment fees charged to workers; workers keep their own passports
    HSEMandatory site inductions, fitness-for-work medicals, certifications
    📝
    Rules change often. Confirm current local content and immigration rules with your legal or government-relations team before each campaign.
    Example: tracking local content
    The contract requires 30% national hires in indirect roles. Week 10 report: 120 indirects, 30 nationals = 25%. You add a graduate engineer programme and a national HSE trainee intake to close the 5-point gap before the quarterly audit.

    🔬 Worked example

    request from the project manager"Mobilize a new Piping Superintendent within 10 days.
 The current one is leaving. Pay whatever it takes."

    What the contract tells you:

    • Piping Superintendent is a CV-approval role: the client has 5 working days to review.
    • The rate schedule caps the role at band B: "whatever it takes" needs commercial approval.
    • The replacement must be equal or better in experience.
    ✅
    Your reply: "I can submit two band-B CVs to the client by day 2. Approval takes up to 5 working days, so mobilization is realistic by day 10–12. If we need to pay above band B, I need commercial sign-off today."

    Module 04 · High-Volume Recruitment Management

    High-Volume Recruitment

    How to hire hundreds of skilled people in weeks, with numbers you can plan, channels you can scale and controls that keep quality high.

    📈 What "high volume" means in EPC

    High volume is not just "many hires". It means many hires in a short window, across many trades, with fixed deadlines. Interviewing one person at a time does not work at this scale; you need a production line.

    🎯
    Targets by trade
    Break the total into trades, quantities and need dates.
    🔢
    Funnel math
    Calculate how many to screen to get the number you need.
    🏭
    Batches
    Move people in groups through tests, medicals and visas.
    📊
    Daily tracking
    One dashboard, one source of truth, updated every day.
    Example: breaking down a big number
    "Hire 800 by week 10" becomes: 220 welders, 180 pipefitters, 120 electricians, 100 scaffolders, 80 riggers, 60 instrument fitters, 40 foremen. Each line gets its own funnel, channel and owner.

    🔢 Funnel math: work backwards

    Not everyone you screen will pass, accept and turn up. Use your past conversion rates to work backwards from the number you need on site.

    StageConversionCalculationNumber needed
    On site (target)——100
    Accepted offers90% show up100 ÷ 0.9112
    Offers made80% accept112 ÷ 0.8140
    Trade tested50% pass140 ÷ 0.5280
    Screened70% go to test280 ÷ 0.7400
    💡
    Use your own data. Conversion rates vary by trade, country and agency. Welder pass rates are often lower than general helper pass rates. Track them on every campaign and reuse them.
    Example: a quick calculation
    You need 60 riggers. Show-up 75%, acceptance 80%, test pass 50%. 60 ÷ 0.75 = 80 accepted; 80 ÷ 0.8 = 100 offers; 100 ÷ 0.5 = 200 trade tests. Book 200 test slots, not 60.

    🔎 Sourcing channels

    ChannelBest forWatch out for
    Own database / ex-employeesFast re-hire of proven peopleOutdated contact details and certificates
    Manpower agenciesLarge craft volumes, overseas campaignsQuality varies; audit their fee practices
    Referrals from foremenCrews that already work well togetherFavouritism; still test everyone
    Job boards & LinkedInEngineers, supervisors, specialistsLow response for craft roles
    Trade schools & local job fairsHelpers, apprentices, local contentNeed training and supervision
    Demobilizing projectsExperienced crews finishing nearbyTiming must line up
    Example: mixing channels
    For 180 pipefitters: 40 from your database (re-hires), 30 from foreman referrals, 90 from two agencies in different countries, and 20 from a nearby project that ends in month 5. If one channel fails, the others keep the campaign alive.

    🧪 Mass screening & trade tests

    Screen in three layers so you spend expensive test time only on likely candidates.

    1. Paper check (2 minutes): trade, years, project type, certificates, passport validity.
    2. Short interview (10 minutes): real project details, tools, materials, safety awareness.
    3. Practical trade test: same task, same marking sheet, independent assessor.
    TradeTypical testCertificates to check
    Pipe welderTest coupon in the required position (for example 6G), inspectedWelder qualification record, NDT result
    PipefitterRead an isometric drawing and fit a spoolTrade certificate
    ElectricianCable termination and testing taskElectrical licence where required
    RiggerLoad calculation and sling selectionRigger certification
    ScaffolderErect and inspect a scaffold sectionScaffolding level certificate
    Example: saving test time
    500 welder CVs arrive. The paper check removes 180 (wrong process or expired passports). Interviews remove 120. Only 200 go to the test bay, saving about 300 test coupons and several days of inspector time.

    📦 Batching & pipeline control

    Move people in batches (for example, 40 a week) so that medical centres, visa processing, flights and camp beds are booked in predictable blocks.

    weekly batch trackerBatch  Trade        Offered  Medical  Visa  Ticketed  On site
B1     Welder 6G       40       38       35      35        34
B2     Welder 6G       40       39       30      --        --
B3     Pipefitter      45       41       --      --        --
B4     Electrician     30       --       --      --        --
    ⚠️
    Watch the gaps between columns. In B2, 39 passed medicals but only 30 visas are issued. That gap is where you act today, not next week.
    Example: fixing a bottleneck
    The tracker shows visas stuck at 30 of 39 for B2. You call the mobilization team and find 9 applications had passport photos rejected. New photos are submitted the same day, and the batch still makes its flight.

    🛡️ Ethical recruitment

    Large overseas campaigns carry real risks of worker exploitation. International guidance (such as the ILO's fair recruitment principles and the "Employer Pays Principle") says workers should not pay to get a job.

    🟢 Good practice

    • Employer pays recruitment costs
    • Contract in a language the worker understands
    • Workers keep their own passports
    • Agencies audited and contractually bound

    🔴 Red flags

    • Workers paid agents to get the job
    • Job or salary changed after arrival
    • Agency or employer holds passports
    • Workers in debt to the recruiter
    Example: an agency audit
    During worker interviews at arrival, 6 of 40 say they paid a fee to a sub-agent. You stop new orders with that agency, report it under company policy, and arrange reimbursement as your policy requires. Clients and lenders increasingly audit this.

    Module 05 · Project Mobilization Timelines

    Mobilization Timelines

    The offer is not the finish line. Learn to plan backwards from the need date so every person is cleared, travelled and inducted on time.

    🚚 What is mobilization?

    Mobilization is everything between an accepted offer and a worker starting productive work on site: medicals, visas, background checks, travel, accommodation, PPE and site induction. On international projects, it often takes longer than the recruitment itself.

    💡
    Key idea: the need date (sometimes called the required-on-site date) comes from the project schedule. Your recruitment start date is calculated from it, not the other way round.
    Example: a local versus an overseas hire
    A local electrician can start in 7 days after offer (medical and induction only). An overseas electrician for the same role may need 5–7 weeks (medical, visa, flights, camp). Same job, very different start times.

    ⏳ Lead-time items

    The durations below are illustrative. Real figures depend on country, visa type and season, so use your own project's data.

    ItemIllustrative durationWho controls it
    Offer and acceptance2–7 daysYou and the candidate
    Notice period (staff)0–90 daysCandidate's current employer
    Pre-employment medical3–10 daysApproved medical centre
    Work visa / permit2–8 weeksGovernment authorities (least controllable)
    Background / certificate verification3–15 daysVerification provider, issuing bodies
    Travel and accommodation2–5 daysTravel and camp teams
    Site induction and access pass1–3 daysSite HSE and client security
    Example: spotting the long pole
    For an overseas QA/QC inspector: offer 5 days, notice 30 days, medical 7 days (during notice), visa 21 days (during notice). The notice period is now the longest item, so negotiating a notice buy-out saves more time than chasing the visa.

    ⏪ Backward planning

    1. Take the need date from the schedule.
    2. List each mobilization step and its duration.
    3. Mark which steps must be in sequence and which can run in parallel.
    4. Add the durations of the longest chain, plus a buffer.
    5. Subtract from the need date: that is your latest offer date. Recruitment must finish before it.
    worked example (overseas pipefitter)Need date on site ............ 1 March
Offer + acceptance ............  5 days
Medical ........................  7 days
Visa ........................... 21 days
Travel ........................  3 days
Induction .....................  2 days
-------------------------------------------
Sequential total ............... 38 days -> latest offer: 22 January
Add 7-day buffer ............... 45 days -> target offer: 15 January
    Example: running steps in parallel
    If the visa application can start with a medical booked the same week, the medical (7 days) runs inside the visa window (21 days). The chain drops from 38 to 31 days, giving you a full extra week.

    📅 The mobilization timeline

    Each bar is one step; the visa is the longest. Hover a bar for its days.
    Example: reading the chart
    The visa bar takes almost half the timeline. If visa processing slips by 10 days, the 7-day buffer is used up and the worker arrives 3 days late. That is why you start visa paperwork the moment an offer is accepted.

    ✅ Tracking readiness

    Use a mobilization tracker with one row per person and one column per step. Colour each cell: done, in progress, blocked.

    NameTradeOfferMedicalVisaTicketNeed dateStatus
    R. KumarWelder 6G✔✔✔✔1 MarReady
    J. SantosPipefitter✔✔In progress—1 MarAt risk
    A. HassanRigger✔Failed——1 MarReplace
    Example: acting on the tracker
    A. Hassan failed the medical. You immediately offer the role to the next rigger on the standby list, whose medical is already valid. The need date is still met.

    🧯 Risks & buffers

    RiskMitigation
    Visa delays or quota limitsApply early, use more than one source country, keep local alternatives
    Medical failuresOver-offer by a known margin; pre-screen fitness
    No-shows at the airportWeekly contact before travel; confirm 48 hours before flight
    Camp beds not readyShare the arrival plan with logistics two weeks ahead
    Schedule slipsHold offers flexible; agree date-change terms with agencies
    ✅
    Rule of thumb: the less control you have over a step, the bigger its buffer. Government processes need more buffer than your own offer letters.
    Example: over-offering
    Past data shows 10% of overseas craft hires drop out before travel. For 50 needed, you offer 56 and keep 4 more on standby. You land 51 on the need date.

    Module 06 · Client Staffing Interface

    Client Staffing Interface

    You are the client's single point of contact for people. Learn how to submit, report, escalate and handle rejections so the client trusts your numbers.

    🏢 Who is the client?

    On an EPC project, the client (owner) is the company that will own and run the facility. Large clients often hire a PMC (Project Management Consultant) to supervise the contractor on their behalf.

    👔
    Client project manager
    Cares about schedule, key personnel and contract compliance.
    🧑‍🔧
    Client discipline reps
    Review CVs for technical fit in their discipline.
    🦺
    Client HSE
    Checks training, medicals and site access eligibility.
    📊
    PMC / auditors
    Audit manpower numbers, rates and local content.
    Example: one CV, several reviewers
    A Lead Electrical Engineer CV goes to the client's electrical lead (technical fit), the client PM (key-personnel rules) and client HSE (certificates). Knowing all three reviewers helps you prepare a CV that passes first time.

    📤 CV submission & approval

    Most clients require a fixed CV format and a cover sheet. Follow it exactly; non-compliant CVs are often returned without review, costing days.

    cv cover sheet templatePosition: Piping Superintendent  |  Contract ref: Exhibit C, line 14
Candidate: M. Rahman  |  Nationality: ---  |  Availability: 15 days
Experience: 18 yrs total, 9 yrs as Superintendent
Relevant projects:
- Gas processing plant, 2 x 400 MMSCFD, peak 600 piping crew
- Refinery expansion, brownfield, live-plant permits
Certificates: verified (copies attached)  |  References: 2 checked
Meets contract minimums: YES (15 yrs required, 18 offered)
Submitted: 6 Oct 2026  |  Response due (contract): 13 Oct 2026
    💡
    Always state the contract minimum and how the candidate meets it. Reviewers check that first.
    Example: a faster approval
    One recruiter sends a raw CV; the client asks three questions and approves in 9 days. Another sends the cover sheet above, answering those questions up front; the client approves in 2 days.

    📊 Manpower reporting

    The client expects a regular (often weekly) manpower report comparing planned and actual headcount.

    DisciplinePlannedActualVarianceAction
    Piping180172−8Batch B4 arrives Monday (+10)
    Electrical9091+1On plan
    Scaffolding7055−15Second agency engaged; recovery by week 14
    ⚠️
    Never hide a gap. A gap reported early with a recovery plan builds trust. A gap discovered by a client audit destroys it.
    Example: a good report line
    "Scaffolding −15 due to 12 visa delays and 3 test failures. Mitigation: 20 local scaffolders from Agency B in testing this week. Expected recovery: week 14." Cause, action and date in one line.

    🗣️ Interface meetings & escalation

    1. Weekly manpower meeting: plan vs actual, upcoming key CVs, risks.
    2. Action log: every request gets an owner and a due date.
    3. Escalation path: recruiter → recruitment manager → project manager → project director, agreed in advance.
    4. Written confirmation: every agreement in a meeting is confirmed by email the same day.
    Example: escalating the right way
    Three key CVs have waited 12 days against a 5-day contract window. You raise it in the weekly meeting with dates, then email the client PM: "These approvals affect the month-4 piping start; can we agree a decision by Friday?" Facts, impact, a clear ask.

    🔁 Handling rejections

    ❌ Weak response

    • Resubmit the same CVs reformatted
    • Argue the client is unfair
    • Flood the client with more CVs

    ✅ Strong response

    • Ask for the specific reason for each rejection
    • Update screening criteria
    • Calibrate with one approved and one rejected CV
    Example: learning from rejections
    The client rejects 4 of 10 QA/QC inspector CVs: "no experience with the required inspection code." You add that code to the first screening question. The next batch: 9 of 10 approved.

    🔬 Worked example

    Recruitment Workflows & Tools

    Repeatable steps and templates that turn a manpower plan into a controlled hiring machine.

    🗺️ End-to-end workflow

    Read left to right, then continue on the next row.

    Each step has an exit rule: a clear yes or no before the candidate moves on.

    Example: exit rules
    No approved MRF → no campaign. Scorecard below 12/18 → stop. Trade test fail → stop (re-test allowed after 30 days). Client approval → offer. Medical fit → visa. Clear rules make every decision explainable in an audit.

    📝 Intake from the MRF

    Before sourcing, confirm these points with the requesting manager:

    1. Exact trade or title, and the specific skill (process, material, code).
    2. Quantity and need date; is it one date or phased?
    3. Duration on the project and rotation pattern.
    4. Approval type: key personnel, CV approval, or trade test only?
    5. Rate band and allowances under the contract.
    6. Local content requirements for this role.
    7. Mandatory certificates and site-specific training.
    Example: turning a vague MRF into a brief
    MRF says: "Electricians x 30, ASAP." Brief you write: 30 industrial electricians (cable pulling and termination, HV not required), 15 by 1 Feb and 15 by 1 Mar, 12 months, 56/28 rotation, trade test only, rate band D, 20% local target.

    Advanced: Peaks, Metrics & Risk

    What separates a recruiter who fills orders from a recruitment manager who protects the project schedule: planning peaks, measuring results and managing risk.

    ⛰️ Peak & multi-project planning

    When several projects peak together, they compete for the same trades, agencies, visa quotas and camp beds.

    • Stagger mobilization where the schedules allow it.
    • Share a talent pool: move crews from a finishing project to a starting one.
    • Reserve agency capacity months ahead with forecast volumes.
    • Plan demobilization early so released crews can be redeployed instead of lost.
    • Shift hiring with the phase: as construction falls, commissioning specialists rise.
    Example: redeploying a crew
    Project A finishes piping in month 10; Project B needs 120 pipefitters in month 11. You plan the transfer in month 7, keep the crew together with a short break, and avoid a 6-week overseas campaign.

    📊 Metrics that matter

    MetricFormulaExample
    Fill ratePositions filled ÷ positions required470 ÷ 500 = 94%
    On-time mobilizationArrived by need date ÷ total arrived430 ÷ 470 = 91%
    CV approval rateCVs approved ÷ CVs submitted36 ÷ 45 = 80%
    Trade test pass ratePassed ÷ tested140 ÷ 280 = 50%
    No-show rateDid not travel ÷ accepted offers12 ÷ 112 = 11%
    Early attritionLeft in first 30 days ÷ arrived9 ÷ 300 = 3%
    Cost per hireTotal recruitment cost ÷ hiresTrack by channel
    💡
    Reading the numbers. A low CV approval rate means screening quality. A high no-show rate means weak pre-mobilization engagement. High early attrition from one agency means its screening or promises need checking.
    Example: diagnosing a campaign
    Fill rate is 85%, but no-shows are 25%. The problem is not sourcing; it is the gap between offer and travel. You add weekly calls, a WhatsApp group per batch, and a 48-hour pre-flight confirmation. No-shows fall to 9%.

    At arrival, the site gate compares each worker's face with the photo taken at the trade test. Two mismatches are found and sent back; the agency is put on review. Everyone went through the same check.

    📖 Full case study

    Project: Gas processing plant, LSTK. Peak 650 in month 8. 25% local content target. LDs for late mechanical completion.

    1. Plan: Break the histogram into 12 trades with monthly targets; set funnel numbers from past data.
    2. Contract: List key personnel, CV-approval roles, rate bands and local content rules.
    3. Source: Database re-hires, three agencies in two countries, local trade school intake.
    4. Screen: Scorecard, trade tests witnessed by client, certificate verification.
    5. Mobilize: Weekly batches of 40–60; backward planning with 7-day buffers.
    6. Interface: Weekly manpower report and meeting; escalation path agreed in month 1.
    7. Result: Peak reached in week 2 of month 8; 93% on-time mobilization; 27% local content.
    ✅
    Why it worked: plan from the histogram, read the contract, use data for the funnel, start mobilization early, and tell the client the truth every week.

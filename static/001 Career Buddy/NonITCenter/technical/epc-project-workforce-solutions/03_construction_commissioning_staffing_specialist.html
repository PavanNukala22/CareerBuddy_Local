    The Role & Site Hiring Basics

    Before you screen a single welder or commissioning engineer, understand what a Construction & Commissioning Staffing Specialist owns, how site roles change as a plant is built and started up, and the words used on every site.

    🧭 What does this role do?

    A Construction & Commissioning Staffing Specialist finds, screens and deploys the people who build a heavy industrial plant (welders, pipefitters, electricians, riggers, supervisors) and the people who test and start it up (pre-commissioning crews, commissioning technicians and engineers). You do not do the technical work. You must tell a safe, skilled site worker from a risky one, and know which roles the project needs at each stage.

    The role has three core skills, and this course covers each in depth:

    🦺
    Heavy industrial site candidate screening
    Checking safety record, certificates, fitness and real skill for high-risk sites.
    🧪
    Testing & pre-commissioning role identification
    Knowing which test and start-up roles exist, and when the project needs them.
    🏭
    Brownfield / greenfield project staffing
    Adapting your hiring to a new site or to work inside a running plant.
    💡
    Example. A refinery expansion needs 400 construction workers now and, eight months later, 60 pre-commissioning and commissioning specialists. Your job is to staff both waves on time, with people who are safe to work next to a live plant.

    🛤️ The site staffing pipeline

    Every site role moves through the same stages. Compared with office hiring, two extra gates matter most: safety and certificate screening and the practical test.

    📚 Key vocabulary

    These words appear in every site meeting and job request. Learn them first.

    TermMeaningExample
    Mechanical completion (MC)The plant is physically built and statically tested to the design."MC for Unit 2 is planned for month 14."
    Pre-commissioningChecks before process fluids go in: flushing, pressure tests, loop checks."Pre-comm crews start flushing next week."
    CommissioningRunning systems with real fluids to prove they work."Cooling water system is in commissioning."
    RFSUReady for Start-Up: a system is cleared to start."RFSU target: 30 June."
    Punch listList of unfinished or faulty items to fix."120 punch items still open."
    HydrotestPressure test of pipes or vessels using water."Line 6 hydrotest at 1.5 times design pressure."
    Loop checkConfirms an instrument signal reaches the control system correctly."300 loops to check in the control room."
    PTWPermit to Work: written approval for risky work."No hot work without a PTW."
    LOTOLockout/Tagout: isolating energy before work."Pump isolated under LOTO."
    Greenfield / BrownfieldNew site / work inside or next to a running plant."It's a brownfield tie-in job."
    Example: decoding a real message
    "MC slips two weeks; pre-comm needs 8 more hydrotest hands and 4 loop-check techs; brownfield area so PTW-trained only."
    Decoded: the build finish date (MC) moves out two weeks. The testing stage (pre-commissioning) still needs extra pressure-test workers and instrument technicians, and because they will work inside a running plant (brownfield), every hire must already know permit-to-work rules (PTW).

    👥 Who you work with

    🏗️ Project side

    • Construction Manager: decides craft numbers and supervision.
    • Commissioning Manager: owns test and start-up roles.
    • HSE Manager: sets safety and training rules.
    • Planning engineer: knows when each system will be ready.

    🏢 Client & supply side

    • Client operations team: takes over the plant, often joins commissioning.
    • Client HSE: approves site access and inductions.
    • Manpower agencies: supply craft workers.
    • Specialist contractors: flushing, testing, vendor start-up teams.
    📝
    Tip. The Commissioning Manager usually knows months ahead which systems start first. A short monthly chat lets you plan specialist hiring long before the formal request arrives.
    Example: a useful commissioning chat
    The Commissioning Manager says: "Power systems are energised first, then utilities, then process." You start hiring electrical commissioning technicians first, followed by mechanical and instrument specialists, matching the start-up order.

    🦺 Safety before speed

    On a heavy industrial site, one unsafe worker can cause a fire, a fall or a dropped load. A fast hire who is unsafe is not a good hire. Your screening must prove three things: safe, skilled and fit.

    🔴 Speed only

    "We mobilized 50 riggers in 10 days." (Two dropped-load incidents in month one, one rigger removed by the client.)

    🟢 Safe and fast

    "We mobilized 46 tested, certified riggers in 14 days. No lifting incidents; client approved all on first review."

    ✅
    Rule of thumb: every site hire must pass three gates: safety record, valid certificates, practical proof. Missing any gate means not ready, however urgent the request.
    Example: the three gates in action
    Scaffolder candidate: Safety: no incidents, can explain a scaffold inspection tag. Certificate: valid scaffolding card, checked with the issuer. Proof: passes a practical erection test. All three passed: ready to deploy.

    Module 02 · Industry Literacy

    Construction to Handover

    A plain-English map of how a plant goes from empty land to a running facility, so every site job title has a place in your head.

    🏭 From empty site to running plant

    Think of building and starting a new car factory. First you build the walls and machines (construction). Then you check every pipe and wire without running anything (pre-commissioning). Then you switch machines on one by one (commissioning). Finally you run the whole line and hand the keys to the owner (start-up and handover).

    🏗️
    Construction
    Civil, steel, equipment, piping, electrical, instruments. Largest headcount.
    🔍
    Pre-commissioning
    Cleaning, pressure tests, cable tests, loop checks. No process fluids yet.
    ⚡
    Commissioning
    Energising and running systems with real fluids, one system at a time.
    🔑
    Start-up & handover
    First production, performance tests, transfer to the client's operators.
    💡
    Why it matters to you. Each stage needs a different kind of worker. Builders are many and short-term; testers are fewer, more specialised and harder to find.

    🧱 Construction disciplines

    DisciplineWhat they buildTypical trades
    Civil & structuralFoundations, roads, steel framesCarpenters, steel fixers, structural fitters, ironworkers
    MechanicalPumps, compressors, vessels, tanksMillwrights, mechanical fitters, riggers
    PipingPipelines and process pipingPipe welders, pipefitters, NDT technicians
    ElectricalPower cables, motors, substationsElectricians, cable jointers, cable pullers
    InstrumentationSensors, valves, control cablesInstrument fitters, instrument technicians
    Example: mapping a request to disciplines
    A request for "30 people for the new compressor house" splits into: civil (foundations), mechanical (setting the compressor), piping (connecting lines), electrical (motor power) and instruments (sensors). Ask the Construction Manager for the split by discipline before sourcing.

    🧩 Systems completion

    Construction is organised by area ("Unit 2, east side"). Commissioning is organised by system ("cooling water system", "fire water system"). Each system is split into subsystems, tested and handed over one at a time.

    • Inspection and Test Records (ITRs): forms proving each item was checked.
    • Punch list: faults found; category A items usually block the next step, category B can follow later.
    • System handover: a signed package moving a system from construction to commissioning.
    Example: why systems matter for hiring
    The plan shows the power system handed over first in month 13, then utilities in month 14, then process systems in month 15. That order tells you to mobilize electrical commissioning technicians first, then mechanical, then process and instrument specialists.

    📅 Phase timeline

    The phases overlap. Pre-commissioning starts on finished systems while construction continues elsewhere. That overlap is when both kinds of hiring run at once.

    An illustrative 20-month project. The shaded overlap is where construction and pre-commissioning run together. Hover a bar for its months.
    Example: reading the timeline
    Pre-commissioning starts in month 10, so its supervisors and crews must be sourced around months 7–8 to allow for testing, medicals and travel. Wait until month 10 to start hiring and the first systems sit idle.

    🪜 Site hierarchy

    LevelConstruction titlesCommissioning titles
    ManagerConstruction Manager, Site ManagerCommissioning Manager, Start-up Manager
    Lead / engineerDiscipline Superintendent, Field EngineerLead Commissioning Engineer (electrical, mechanical, instrument)
    SupervisorGeneral Foreman, ForemanPre-commissioning Supervisor, Test Supervisor
    Technician / craftWelder, Fitter, Electrician, RiggerCommissioning Technician, Loop Check Technician, Flushing crew
    Example: matching level to screening
    A Foreman is screened on crew size led, safety leadership and trade skill. A Lead Commissioning Engineer is screened on systems started up, plant types and handover experience. Same site, very different questions.

    🪤 Common beginner traps

    ❌ Mistake

    • "An electrician can do commissioning."
    • "Commissioning starts after construction ends."
    • "Testing roles are all the same."

    ✅ Reality

    • Installing cables differs from testing and energising systems; check real test experience.
    • Phases overlap; pre-comm starts on finished systems while building continues.
    • Flushing, hydrotest, loop checks and start-up each need different skills.
    Example: avoiding the electrician trap
    A CV says "Electrician, 12 years, commissioning." You ask: "Which tests did you sign off, and which systems did you energise?" He answers: "I pulled cable for the commissioning team." That is construction experience, not commissioning.

    Module 03 · Heavy Industrial Site Candidate Screening

    Heavy Industrial Screening

    How to screen site workers for high-risk plants: safety record, certificates, fitness and real experience, quickly and fairly.

    🏭 What "heavy industrial" means

    Heavy industrial sites are large plants with high-energy hazards: refineries, petrochemical plants, power stations, LNG terminals, steel mills, mines and chemical plants. Typical risks include working at height, heavy lifts, hot work, confined spaces, high voltage and toxic gases.

    Working at heightHeavy liftsHot workConfined spaceHigh voltageToxic gas (e.g. H₂S)Pressure
    Example: same trade, different site
    An electrician from house wiring and one from a refinery both say "electrician". The refinery electrician knows hazardous-area equipment, permits and isolation. For a refinery job, only the second is a direct match.

    🧅 The screening layers

    1. Paper check (2–3 minutes): trade, plant types, years, certificates, expiry dates.
    2. Safety screen (5 minutes): incident history, permit experience, safety behaviour questions.
    3. Experience screen (5–10 minutes): last projects, exact tasks, crew size, tools.
    4. Practical proof: trade test or technical interview (Module 04).
    5. Fitness and verification: medical, references, certificate verification.
    💡
    Order matters. Cheap checks come first. Do not spend a test bay slot on someone whose key certificate expired last year.
    Example: layers saving time
    200 rigger CVs: paper check removes 60 (no lifting certificate). Safety screen removes 25. Experience screen removes 35. Only 80 go to the practical test, saving two days of test-yard time.

    🛡️ Safety record & attitude

    A clean record helps, but safety attitude matters more. Ask about real situations. Good workers describe what they did, not just the rules.

    QuestionStrong answerWeak answer
    "Tell me about a time you stopped work for safety."Specific hazard, who they told, how it was fixed"I've never needed to."
    "What do you check before entering a confined space?"Permit, gas test, standby person, rescue plan"I just go in carefully."
    "How do you know a scaffold is safe to use?"Inspection tag, date, inspector signature, no missing parts"It looks strong."
    "Have you had any incidents?"Honest answer, what they learnedAvoids the question
    📝
    Be fair. One past incident does not automatically disqualify a person. Look at what happened, what they learned, and what references say, then follow your company's policy.
    Example: an honest incident answer
    "Five years ago I cut my hand because I removed a grinder guard. Since then I check guards before every job and teach new helpers to do the same." Honest, specific and changed behaviour: a positive sign.

    📜 Certificates & fitness

    Certificate names differ by country and client. Use the client's list, and always check validity, issuer and expiry.

    AreaExamples of what clients often require
    WeldingWelder qualification record for the process, material and position
    LiftingRigger / slinger and crane operator certificates
    ScaffoldingScaffolder certificate at the required level
    ElectricalLocal electrical licence; hazardous-area competence (for example CompEx) where flammable gases may be present
    Safety trainingWorking at height, confined space entry, H₂S awareness, first aid, fire watch
    Supervisor safetyRecognised safety qualifications for supervisors and HSE staff (for example NEBOSH or IOSH)
    Fitness for workPre-employment medical; some roles need extra checks (respirator fit, working at height)
    ⚠️
    Verify, don't trust paper. Check certificates with the issuing body or its online register where possible. Requirements change, so confirm the current list with client HSE for each project.
    Example: an expiry trap
    A scaffolder's card expires in 3 weeks; the job lasts 9 months. Book the renewal course before mobilization, or he will be stopped at the gate on his first day after expiry.

    🎙️ Experience screening questions

    TradeAskListen for
    Pipe welder"Which processes, materials and positions did you weld last?"Specific process (TIG, stick), material (carbon steel, stainless), position (6G), repair rate
    Pipefitter"How do you read an isometric drawing?"Dimensions, elevations, weld numbers, spool fitting
    Rigger"How do you choose a sling for a 5-tonne lift?"Load weight, sling angle, working load limit, lift plan
    Electrician"What do you do before working on a motor?"Isolation, LOTO, test for dead, permit
    Foreman"How big was your crew and how did you plan their day?"Crew size, toolbox talks, task planning, productivity
    Example: a strong welder answer
    "Last job: refinery, 6-inch stainless lines, TIG root and fill, 6G position, about 2% repair rate on X-ray." Plant, material, process, position and quality: move to the trade test.

    🚩 Red flags

    • Certificates from issuers you cannot verify, or with altered dates.
    • Plant names or dates that do not match the project's real timeline.
    • Cannot describe the basic safety steps of their own trade.
    • Many very short site stays with no clear reason.
    • Says "we" for everything and cannot describe their own task.
    Example: a timeline red flag
    CV: "Commissioning technician, LNG Plant X, 2015–2017." Public information shows Plant X was still in early construction during those years. Ask politely: "What stage was the plant at when you worked there?" before deciding.

    Module 04 · Trade Tests & Competency

    Trade Tests & Competency

    Paper shows what someone claims; a test shows what they can do. Learn to organise practical tests and technical interviews that are fast, fair and trusted by the client.

    🧪 Why test?

    A trade test is a practical task done on real tools under observation. It proves current skill, catches inflated CVs and gives the client evidence. Many EPC contracts require test records for craft workers.

    📄 CV only

    "Pipe welder, 6G, 15 years." No proof the person can still weld to today's standard.

    🔥 Tested

    Test coupon welded in 6G, visually inspected and X-rayed: pass. Recorded with photo ID and date.

    Example: what testing catches
    Of 100 welders with "6G" on their CV, 55 pass the test. Without testing, 45 under-skilled welders could have reached site and caused weld repairs, delays and rework costs.

    🛠️ Tests by trade

    TradeTypical practical testPass evidence
    Pipe welderWeld a test coupon in the required process and positionVisual inspection plus X-ray or bend test
    PipefitterFabricate a small spool from an isometric drawingDimensions within tolerance
    ElectricianTerminate and test a power cableCorrect terminations, insulation test result
    Instrument technicianCalibrate a transmitter; trace a loopCorrect readings, documented results
    RiggerPlan and signal a lift; choose slingsCorrect load calculation and signals
    ScaffolderErect and tag a scaffold sectionInspection checklist passed
    Example: matching the test to the job
    The job needs stainless steel welding in 6G. A carbon steel flat-position test proves nothing useful. Always copy the process, material and position from the job requirement into the test.

    📋 Marking sheets

    Every candidate for the same role gets the same task and the same marking sheet.

    sample marking sheet: electricianCandidate ID: E-0142   Photo ID checked: YES   Date: 12 Oct 2026

    💡
    Safety is a gate, not just points. A candidate who scores 90 overall but skips isolation should fail.
    Example: a safety-gate fail
    Candidate scores 22/25 on preparation, 25/25 on termination and 24/25 on testing, but 10/25 on safety because he did not isolate. Total 81, but the safety gate (20+) is missed: fail, with re-test allowed after training.

    🗣️ Technical interviews for engineers and supervisors

    Engineers and commissioning leads are usually assessed by structured technical interview, often with a senior engineer from the project.

    RoleAskStrong answer includes
    Pre-commissioning Supervisor"How did you plan the flushing of a system?"Sequence, water quality, temporary connections, acceptance criteria, records
    Electrical Commissioning Engineer"Walk me through energising a substation for the first time."Checks, insulation tests, protection settings, permits, step-by-step energising
    Commissioning Manager"How many systems did you take to RFSU and what went wrong?"Numbers, plant type, problems solved, handover to operations
    Example: probing with "what went wrong?"
    A commissioning lead says: "Our first pump tripped on start because the suction strainer was blocked. We added a strainer check to every pump start-up sheet." Real experience shows in the specific problems people remember.

    🗂️ Competency matrix

    A competency matrix lists each person against the skills the project needs, so supervisors can assign work safely.

    NameCable terminationInsulation testLOTOHazardous areaEnergising
    A. SilvaCompetentCompetentCompetentCertifiedSupervised
    K. OseiCompetentSupervisedCompetentNot yetNot yet
    Example: using the matrix
    A task in a gas area needs hazardous-area competence. The matrix shows K. Osei is not yet certified, so the supervisor assigns A. Silva and books K. Osei on the next course.

    ⚖️ Fair testing

    • Same task, same materials, same time limit and same marking sheet for everyone.
    • Independent assessors, not the foreman who referred the candidate.
    • Photo ID check at the test, and the same ID checked again at the site gate.
    • Instructions in a language the candidate understands.
    • A written re-test policy (for example, re-test after a set period or training).
    Example: a fair re-test
    A candidate scores 68% against a 70% pass mark and has strong references. The policy says: fail, re-test after 30 days. You apply it exactly, record it, and invite the candidate to re-test. Bending rules for one person makes every result questionable.

    Module 05 · Testing & Pre-Commissioning Job Role Identification

    Testing & Pre-Commissioning Roles

    Learn what each test activity is, which job roles carry it out, and how to read a commissioning plan to know exactly who to hire and when.

    🔬 Testing activities explained

    ActivityPlain-English meaningStage
    Flushing / blowingCleaning debris out of pipes with water, air or steamPre-comm
    HydrotestFilling with water and pressurising to prove strength and no leaksPre-comm
    Pneumatic testPressure test with air or gas; stores more energy, so higher riskPre-comm
    Insulation resistance testChecking cables and motors are not leaking current (often called a "megger" test)Pre-comm
    Loop checkProving each instrument signal reaches the control system correctlyPre-comm
    Motor solo runRunning a motor uncoupled to check direction and vibrationCommissioning
    Function testsTesting alarms, trips and safety systems actually workCommissioning
    Start-up & performance testRunning the plant with real product and proving capacityStart-up
    Example: one pipe's journey
    A new cooling water line is flushed to remove welding debris, then hydrotested to 1.5 times design pressure, then drained and reconnected (reinstated). Later, its pump has a solo run, and the system is commissioned with water flowing.

    🧑‍🔧 Roles by activity

    RoleMain activitiesKey screening points
    Commissioning ManagerPlans all testing and start-up; leads the teamNumber of plants started up, RFSU experience, leadership
    Lead Commissioning Engineer (E / M / I)Leads one discipline's tests and proceduresSystems commissioned, procedures written, plant type
    Pre-commissioning SupervisorRuns flushing, pressure tests, reinstatement crewsTest packs managed, safety on pressure tests
    Hydrotest / Pressure Test SupervisorPrepares and runs pressure testsTest limits, exclusion zones, test records
    Electrical Commissioning TechnicianInsulation tests, protection checks, energisingHV experience, isolation, test equipment
    Instrument / Loop Check TechnicianCalibration, loop checks, function testsControl systems used, loop counts, records
    Mechanical Commissioning TechnicianAlignment, solo runs, rotating equipment checksPumps and compressors, vibration checks
    Completions Engineer / CoordinatorTracks ITRs, punch lists, handover packagesCompletions database, document control, accuracy
    Example: spotting the right title
    The request says: "We need someone to track 4,000 ITRs and build system handover packs." That is a Completions Coordinator, not a commissioning engineer. Sourcing the wrong title wastes weeks.

    📑 Reading a commissioning plan

    The commissioning plan lists systems, their order and dates. Turn it into a role list.

    commissioning plan extract (simplified)System                  Type        Handover   Main tests
01 Power distribution   Electrical  Month 13   Insulation, protection, energise
02 Instrument air       Utility     Month 14   Flushing, pressure test, loop checks
03 Cooling water        Utility     Month 14   Flushing, hydrotest, pump solo runs
04 Fire water           Safety      Month 14   Hydrotest, pump runs, function tests
05 Gas compression      Process     Month 15   Pneumatic test, loop checks, compressor run
    1. Group systems by discipline load (electrical, mechanical, instrument).
    2. List the activities and the role each needs.
    3. Ask the Commissioning Manager for crew sizes per activity.
    4. Set need dates a few weeks before each system handover.
    Example: turning the plan into roles
    System 01 in month 13 needs electrical commissioning technicians by month 12. Systems 02–04 in month 14 need flushing crews, hydrotest supervisors and loop check technicians by month 13. System 05 needs compressor specialists by month 14.

    ⏱️ Timing the hiring

    Specialist commissioning people are scarce and often committed to other projects. Plan backwards with extra time.

    Role typeTypical sourcing timePlan to start sourcing
    Commissioning Manager / LeadsLong (scarce, notice periods)4–6 months before need
    Commissioning techniciansMedium2–3 months before need
    Pre-comm crews (flushing, test hands)Shorter; can come from construction1–2 months before need

    Durations are illustrative; use your own project and market data.

    Example: starting early
    The Commissioning Manager is needed in month 9 to write procedures. You start sourcing in month 4; the chosen candidate has a 3-month notice period and joins in month 8, one month early, with time to plan.

    🔁 Construction-to-commissioning transfer

    Strong construction workers who know the systems are a valuable source for pre-commissioning roles. Transferring them keeps knowledge on the project and reduces new hiring.

    ✅ Good transfer candidates

    • Pipefitters to flushing and reinstatement crews
    • Electricians to insulation testing (with supervision)
    • Instrument fitters to loop check support

    ⚠️ Check carefully

    • Test experience, not just installation
    • Training on test safety (pressure, energising)
    • Supervisor sign-off on the competency matrix
    Example: a planned transfer
    20 pipefitters finish piping in Unit 2. After a two-day pressure test safety course, 12 join the hydrotest and reinstatement crews. You only need to hire 6 new test hands instead of 18.

    🔬 Worked example

    request from the commissioning manager"Send me 10 commissioning engineers by next month."

    What you ask: Which systems? Which activities? Engineers or technicians? Which disciplines?

    ✅
    What you learn: the real need is 2 lead engineers (1 electrical, 1 instrument), 5 loop check technicians and 3 pre-commissioning supervisors. Correct titles mean faster sourcing, lower cost and the right skills on site.

    Module 06 · Brownfield / Greenfield Project Staffing

    Brownfield vs Greenfield

    A new plant on empty land and work inside a running plant need different people, different checks and different plans. Learn to staff both.

    🌍 Greenfield vs brownfield

    Factor🌱 Greenfield🏭 Brownfield
    SiteEmpty land, often remoteInside or next to an operating plant
    VolumeLarge, long ramp-upSmaller, but in tight time windows
    Main risksLogistics, camp, heat, remotenessLive hydrocarbons, gas, energised equipment
    PermitsStandard construction permitsStrict PTW, LOTO, gas testing for most jobs
    Key experienceNew build, large crews, remote sitesLive-plant work, tie-ins, shutdowns
    InductionProject inductionProject plus client plant-specific induction
    Example: same trade, different needs
    A greenfield pipefitter must handle long rotations in a remote camp. A brownfield pipefitter must work beside live process lines, wait for gas tests and stop instantly when an alarm sounds.

    🌱 Greenfield staffing

    • Volume planning: hire to the histogram over many months.
    • Remote logistics: camps, rotations, travel and welfare.
    • Local content: local hires and training programmes are often required.
    • Supervision ratio: enough experienced foremen for large new crews.
    💡
    Rotation matters. Ask candidates about long rotations in remote camps before offering; early leavers on remote sites are expensive to replace.
    Example: local skills gap
    A remote greenfield site has a 40% local hiring target, but few local welders. You pair 30 local trainees with experienced welders in a structured programme, test them every 3 months, and promote those who pass.

    🏭 Brownfield staffing

    • Live-plant discipline: permits, isolation, gas testing and alarms.
    • SIMOPS: simultaneous operations; construction happens while the plant runs nearby.
    • Tie-ins: connecting new piping to existing lines, often during short stops.
    • Client inductions: plant-specific rules, muster points, emergency response.
    ⚠️
    Brownfield screening must include permit experience. A great greenfield worker with no live-plant experience needs extra training and supervision before working in operating areas.
    Example: a brownfield screening question
    "Your gas detector alarms during hot work next to a live unit. What do you do?" Strong answer: "Stop work, make the area safe, leave by the agreed route to the muster point, and report. Restart only with a new gas test and permit."

    ⏳ Shutdowns & turnarounds

    A shutdown (or turnaround) is a planned stop of a running plant for maintenance and tie-ins. Each day of shutdown means lost production for the client, so the window is short and fixed.

    FeatureStaffing impact
    Short fixed window (often days to a few weeks)Everyone must be cleared and inducted before day 1
    24-hour workingDay and night shifts, often doubling headcount
    Peak demand across the regionBook crews and agencies months ahead
    No slippage allowedKeep pre-cleared standby workers
    Example: shutdown standby
    A 21-day shutdown needs 300 workers. You clear 330, keeping 30 on standby with valid medicals and inductions. When 18 workers drop out in the final week, the standby list fills every gap.

    🎯 Screening by project type

    🌱 Greenfield questions

    • "What is the longest rotation you have completed?"
    • "How large were the crews on your last new-build?"
    • "How do you cope with remote camp life?"

    🏭 Brownfield questions

    • "Describe the permit process at your last operating plant."
    • "Have you done a tie-in to a live line?"
    • "How many shutdowns have you worked, and in which roles?"
    Example: hybrid expansion
    A new unit is built next to a running refinery. Crews work on new build, but final tie-ins cross into the live plant. You screen for both: new-build productivity and live-plant permit discipline.

    🔬 Worked example

    request"Brownfield revamp: 60 craft for 10 weeks, 5-day tie-in shutdown in week 8.
 Client requires plant-specific induction (2 days) for all."
    1. Screen for live-plant and permit experience first.
    2. Book client inductions in batches before mobilization.
    3. Plan extra shift crews for the 5-day shutdown in week 8.
    4. Keep 10% pre-inducted standby workers.
    ✅
    Result: 60 inducted workers on site in week 1, a night-shift crew of 25 ready for week 8, and 6 standby workers who covered last-minute medical failures.

    Module 07 · Workflows & Tools

    Site Staffing Workflows & Tools

    Repeatable steps and templates that turn a site request into safe, tested people at the gate on time.

    🗺️ End-to-end workflow

    Read left to right, then continue on the next row.
    Example: exit rules
    Expired critical certificate → stop (or renew before offer). Safety screen fail → stop. Scorecard below 12/18 → stop. Trade test fail → re-test per policy. 30-day review flags poor safety behaviour → retrain or release. Every decision is recorded.

    📝 Intake with site managers

    1. Which activity exactly: construction, pre-commissioning, commissioning or start-up?
    2. Which systems or areas, and in what order?

    Advanced: Crossover, Retention & Metrics

    What separates a site recruiter from a trusted staffing specialist: managing the construction-to-commissioning crossover, keeping key people to the end, and proving quality with numbers.

    🔀 Managing the crossover

    Around mechanical completion, construction headcount falls while pre-commissioning and commissioning rise. Poor planning loses the people who know the plant.

    • Map construction workers to future test roles 3–4 months before MC.
    • Keep enough construction crews to close punch items.
    • Release workers by system completion, not all at once.
    • Offer redeployment to other projects for those released.
    Example: a smooth crossover
    Construction peaks at 900 in month 12. You plan: 120 move to pre-commissioning, 60 stay for punch-list work, 300 redeploy to a sister project, and the rest are released in stages by system. No sudden loss of knowledge.

    🔒 Retention to completion

    As the end approaches, good people leave for their next job. Losing a commissioning lead before RFSU can delay start-up.

    ToolHow it helps
    Completion bonusPaid when the person stays to a milestone such as RFSU
    Redeployment promiseA clear next project removes the need to job hunt
    Transparent end datesPeople plan around facts, not rumours
    RecognitionCredit for systems handed over builds commitment
    Example: a retention bonus
    Three commissioning engineers receive offers elsewhere two months before RFSU. A completion bonus tied to RFSU plus a confirmed role on the next project keeps all three. Start-up happens on schedule.

    MetricFormulaExample

    Example: diagnosing by source
    Welders from Agency A have a 1.5% repair rate; Agency B has 7%. You audit Agency B's test centre and find tests were not X-rayed. After fixing it, B's next batch drops to 2.5%.

    🕵️ Fraud & verification

    Warning signHow to verify
    Different person arrives from the one testedPhoto at test, medical and gate; compare every time
    Certificate cannot be found by the issuerCheck online registers or contact the issuer directly
    Commissioning CV listing only construction tasksAsk which tests they signed and which systems they started

    📝

    Example: a gate check
    At the gate, two workers' faces do not match their test photos. They are sent back, the agency is put on review, and photo checks are added at every stage. The client is informed the same day.

    📖 Full case study

    Project: Gas plant expansion next to a running facility (hybrid brownfield). Construction peak 700. Pre-commissioning from month 11. RFSU in month 17.

    1. Roles: Converted the commissioning plan into 14 role types with need dates.
    2. Screening: Safety and permit screening first; certificates verified with issuers.
    3. Testing: Client-witnessed trade tests; technical interviews for leads.
    4. Brownfield: Plant inductions booked in batches; 10% standby for tie-in shutdowns.
    5. Crossover: 110 construction workers trained and moved into pre-commissioning.
    6. Retention: Completion bonus for 15 key commissioning roles.
    7. Result: RFSU on schedule; 95% on-time deployment; no serious safety incidents linked to new hires.
    ✅
    Why it worked: correct role identification, safety-first screening, fair testing, and planning the crossover early.

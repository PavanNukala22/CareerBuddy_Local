    The Role & Contracts Hiring Basics

    Before you screen a single claims consultant or commercial manager, understand what an EPC Contracts & Claims Staffing Lead owns, how a requirement travels from client to candidate, and the words every contracts conversation uses.

    🧭 What does this lead do?

    An EPC Contracts & Claims Staffing Lead finds, screens and deploys the people who protect a project's money and legal position: contracts managers, commercial managers, quantity surveyors, claims specialists, forensic delay experts and contract lawyers. You do not write claims or give legal advice. You make sure the client gets people who can, without conflicts of interest.

    🔎
    Source
    Find contracts, commercial, claims and legal professionals through LinkedIn, professional bodies, consultancies, law networks and referrals.
    🧪
    Screen
    Check contract forms used (FIDIC, NEC), claim outcomes, contract values, qualifications and communication.
    ⚖️
    Check conflicts
    Confirm the person has not worked for the other side on the same project or dispute.
    ✈️
    Deploy
    Coordinate interviews, offers, confidentiality agreements and mobilization to site, office or hearing.
    💡
    Example. A client says: "We need a Claims Manager with FIDIC experience and a Senior Quantity Surveyor for our power plant. We have three major delay events and our final account is due in 6 months." Your job is to turn that into a short list of verified, conflict-free, available people within the agreed turnaround time (TAT).

    📚 Key vocabulary

    TermMeaningExample
    FIDICInternational Federation of Consulting Engineers; publishes widely used standard construction contracts."The project uses the FIDIC Silver Book."
    EmployerThe owner who commissions and pays for the project (FIDIC term)."The Employer is a national power company."
    ClaimA request by one party for extra time and/or money (or other relief) under the contract."We submitted a claim for 60 days and $4M."
    EOTExtension of Time: moving the completion date because of an event the contractor is not responsible for."We were granted a 45-day EOT."
    VariationA formal change to the works or scope."Variation 12 added a second pipe rack."
    NoticeA formal written communication the contract requires, often with a deadline."The notice of claim must go within 28 days."

    Example: decoding a real message
    "Need a QS to prepare the EOT and prolongation claim under FIDIC Yellow 2017, plus support for the DAAB referral."
    Decoded: the client wants a quantity surveyor to prepare a request for more time and the extra costs of the longer project (prolongation), under the FIDIC design-build contract, and to help bring the dispute to the Dispute Avoidance/Adjudication Board (DAAB).

    👥 Who you work with

    🏢 Client side

    • Commercial director: owns contract value, margin and claims.
    • Contracts manager / head of contracts: judges contract knowledge.
    • In-house legal counsel: leads disputes, hires lawyers and experts.
    • Project director: needs the commercial team to protect the project.

    🧑‍💼 Candidate side

    • Contractor / owner staff: contracts and commercial teams.
    • Claims consultancies: claims, delay and quantum specialists.
    • Law firms: construction lawyers, paralegals, document reviewers.
    • Independent experts: delay and quantum expert witnesses.
    📝
    Tip. Always confirm which side your client is on: contractor or employer. In claims work, the same person usually cannot act for both sides on the same project.
    Example: a useful commercial-director chat
    The commercial director says: "The last claims manager wrote long reports but never linked events to the critical path." You add "How did you prove cause and effect between the event and the delay?" to every claims screen. Your next submittal is hired.

    📡 Signal, not legal jargon

    Contracts CVs are full of clause numbers and legal words. Signal is evidence of what the person achieved: contract values, claims prepared or defended, outcomes, and their personal role.

    🔴 Low signal

    "Extensive knowledge of FIDIC, NEC and JCT. Responsible for contract administration, claims and dispute resolution on major projects."

    🟢 High signal

    "Prepared the contractor's EOT claim under FIDIC Yellow 1999 on a $450M power plant: 120 days claimed, 95 days awarded by the Engineer, plus $6.2M prolongation cost agreed without arbitration."

    ✅
    Rule of thumb: ask which contract, how much, which side, and what was the outcome. Real claims and commercial people answer all four.
    Example: the four questions in action
    CV: "Claims management on EPC project."
    Which contract? FIDIC Silver 2017. How much? $18M variation and disruption claim. Which side? Contractor. Outcome? $11M agreed at amicable settlement after the DAAB decision. That is someone who has actually done it.

    Module 02 · Industry Literacy

    Construction Contract Fundamentals

    A plain-English map of how construction contracts share risk, price work and handle change, so every line on a contracts CV has a place to live in your head.

    📜 What a contract does

    Think of renovating a house with a builder. You agree what will be built, for how much, by when, and what happens if something changes or goes wrong. A construction contract does the same for a project worth hundreds of millions of dollars, and most importantly it decides who carries which risk.

    🏗️
    Scope
    What must be designed, supplied and built.
    💰
    Price & payment
    How much, and when money is paid.
    🗓️
    Time
    Completion date and what happens if it is late.
    ⚖️
    Risk & disputes
    Who pays for surprises, and how disagreements are settled.
    Example: risk allocation
    Unexpected rock is found during excavation. Under one contract the employer pays for the extra work; under another the contractor must absorb it. The words in the contract decide, and contracts professionals are the people who know where to look.

    🤝 The parties

    PartyRoleExample
    Employer / OwnerCommissions and pays for the projectA national oil company
    ContractorDesigns and/or builds under the contractAn EPC contractor
    Engineer / Employer's RepresentativeAdministers the contract: certifies payments, agrees or determines claimsA consulting engineering firm
    Subcontractors & suppliersDeliver parts of the work under their own contractsPiping subcontractor, compressor vendor
    LendersFinance the project and may require reports and approvalsDevelopment banks, export credit agencies
    Example: why the side matters
    A contractor needs a claims specialist to prepare claims. The employer needs one to assess and defend against them. Both need the same skills, but a person cannot work for both sides on the same project.

    💵 Pricing models

    ModelHow it worksCommercial focus for your candidates
    Lump sumOne fixed price for defined scopeVariations and claims are the main way to recover extra costs
    Re-measurement / unit ratePaid for measured quantities at agreed ratesAccurate measurement; quantity surveyors are central
    Cost reimbursableActual cost plus a feeCost audit, allowable vs disallowed costs
    Target costSavings or overruns against a target are sharedPain/gain calculations, cost tracking
    Example: matching pricing experience
    A client on a lump sum EPC contract needs someone who has won variations and EOT claims. A candidate whose whole career was cost reimbursable audits may lack that claims experience. Ask: "What pricing model were your last three contracts?"

    📚 Standard contract forms

    Most large projects use a published standard form, often with amendments ("particular conditions"). Knowing the names helps you match candidates.

    FormWhere it is commonWords a candidate will use
    FIDICInternational projects: Middle East, Africa, Asia, development-bank funded workEngineer, Notice of Claim, DAAB, Sub-Clause 20
    NEC (NEC4)UK and some international infrastructureCompensation events, early warnings, Project Manager
    JCTUK building projectsRelevant events, loss and expense
    LOGICUK oil and gas industryVariations, company/contractor
    Bespoke owner formsLarge national oil companies and ownersOwner-specific procedures
    ⚠️
    Concepts transfer between forms, but procedures and deadlines differ. Ask the client: "Would you accept a strong NEC4 commercial manager for a FIDIC project?" before submitting.

    🔁 The contract lifecycle

    StageCommercial activityTypical hires
    TenderReview contract terms, price risk, negotiateContracts managers, commercial bid managers
    Award & mobilizationSet up procedures, subcontracts, notice registersContracts administrators, QSs
    ExecutionMonthly payments, variations, notices, claimsCommercial managers, QSs, claims specialists
    CompletionFinal account, outstanding claimsSenior QSs, claims managers
    DisputesDAAB, arbitration, expert evidenceLawyers, delay and quantum experts, document reviewers
    Example: timing your sourcing
    A client says the project reaches mechanical completion in 4 months and "several issues are still open". That signals upcoming final account and claims work. Start building a pipeline of senior QSs and claims managers now.

    🪤 Common beginner traps

    ❌ Mistake

    • "A claim means the contractor is being difficult."
    • "Contracts manager and lawyer are the same job."
    • "FIDIC experience is all the same."
    • "Any 'claims' CV fits."

    ✅ Reality

    • Claims are a normal contractual process for time and money.
    • Contracts managers administer contracts; lawyers give legal advice and represent parties.
    • Different books and editions have different procedures and deadlines.
    • Insurance and healthcare "claims" roles are a different field entirely.

    Module 03 · FIDIC Contract Knowledge

    FIDIC Contracts for Recruiters

    The most widely used international construction contracts. Learn the books, the claims timeline and the questions that reveal real FIDIC experience.

    🌍 What is FIDIC?

    FIDIC (Fédération Internationale des Ingénieurs-Conseils, the International Federation of Consulting Engineers) publishes standard contract forms used on construction projects around the world. The two main editions in use are 1999 and 2017 (the 2017 books were reprinted with amendments in 2022).

    💡
    Why the edition matters. The 2017 edition made the claims procedure more detailed and stricter, and introduced the DAAB. A candidate who only knows 1999 may miss 2017 deadlines. Always ask which book and which edition.

    📚 The FIDIC books

    FIDIC contracts are known by the colour of their covers.

    BookFull title (short)Who designsTypical use
    RedConditions of Contract for ConstructionEmployerBuildings, civil works designed by the employer's engineer
    YellowPlant and Design-BuildContractorPower, process plant, mechanical and electrical works
    SilverEPC / Turnkey ProjectsContractorEPC projects where the contractor carries most risk, often privately financed
    GreenShort Form of ContractEitherSmaller, simpler projects
    GoldDesign, Build and OperateContractorProjects where the contractor also operates the facility
    PinkHarmonised Red Book for development banksEmployerProjects funded by multilateral development banks
    Example: book tells you the risk
    A client building a gas plant on the Silver Book carries less risk than its contractor, who bears design and most unforeseen risks for a fixed price. The contractor therefore needs very strong claims and commercial people, because there are fewer grounds for extra time and money.

    🧑‍⚖️ Engineer vs Employer's Representative

    🛠️ The Engineer (Red & Yellow)

    Appointed by the employer to administer the contract. Certifies payments and must act neutrally when agreeing or determining claims (Sub-Clause 3.7 in 2017).

    🏢 Employer's Representative (Silver)

    There is no Engineer. The employer administers the contract directly, usually through its own representative.

    Example: a hiring implication
    A consulting firm hires a "Contracts Engineer" to sit in the Engineer's team on a Red Book project. The right candidate must be comfortable making neutral determinations, not just arguing one side. Ask: "Describe a determination you made that neither party liked."

    ⏱️ The claims procedure (FIDIC 2017)

    Under the 2017 Red, Yellow and Silver books, Clause 20 sets a strict path for claims. Missing the first notice can mean losing the right to claim (a time bar).

    Read left to right, then continue on the next row. Red boxes are hard deadlines; day counts run from when the party became (or should have become) aware.
    StepWhat it means
    Notice of Claim (≤ 28 days)A short written notice that a claim exists, as soon as practicable and no later than 28 days after becoming aware of the event. Late notice risks losing the claim.
    Fully detailed claim (≤ 84 days)The full claim with its contractual or legal basis, facts and calculations, normally within 84 days of becoming aware (or another period agreed).
    Agree or determineThe Engineer first tries to reach agreement, then makes a fair determination if there is none.
    DAABDispute Avoidance/Adjudication Board: helps avoid disputes and makes decisions on referred disputes.
    Notice of Dissatisfaction (≤ 28 days)A party unhappy with a DAAB decision must give this notice within 28 days, or the decision becomes final and binding.
    ArbitrationAfter an attempt at amicable settlement, the dispute is finally decided by arbitration.
    Example: counting the deadlines
    The contractor becomes aware of a late design approval on 1 March. Notice of Claim is due no later than 29 March (28 days). The fully detailed claim is due by 24 May (84 days). A strong claims specialist sets these dates in a notice register on day one.
    ⚠️
    Particular conditions often change these periods, and the 1999 edition uses a different structure (for example, a Dispute Adjudication Board, DAB). Always confirm the project's actual contract with the client; never assume.

    🗝️ Key concepts to listen for

    ConceptPlain-English meaning
    Particular ConditionsProject-specific changes to the standard FIDIC wording
    VariationAn instructed change to the works, valued under the contract
    Extension of Time (EOT)More time to complete because of a qualifying event
    Delay damagesPre-agreed amounts the contractor pays if late without entitlement to an EOT
    Interim Payment CertificateThe Engineer's monthly certificate of the amount due
    Taking-Over CertificateConfirms the employer has taken over the works
    Contemporary recordsRecords made at the time: diaries, photos, letters, schedule updates

    Module 04 · Claims & Disputes

    Claims & Disputes Explained

    What claims are made of, how delay and money are proven, and how disputes climb from negotiation to arbitration.

    📂 Types of claims

    Claim typeWhat is asked forExample
    Extension of Time (EOT)More time, so delay damages do not applyLate site access from the employer
    Prolongation costExtra time-related costs (site staff, equipment, offices)45 extra days of site overheads
    DisruptionLost productivity even without overall delayCrews working around late design changes
    Variation claimPayment for changed or added workA second pipe rack instructed by the employer
    AccelerationCost of working faster to recover timeNight shifts to meet the original date
    Example: one event, three claims
    The employer approves foundation drawings 6 weeks late. The contractor claims an EOT of 6 weeks, prolongation costs for those weeks, and disruption because crews had to work out of sequence.

    🔗 Cause and effect

    A strong claim links each event (cause) to its specific delay or cost (effect), supported by records. Claims that lump everything together without these links are called global claims, and tribunals often reject or reduce them.

    🔴 Global claim

    "The project overran by $30M and 9 months due to the employer's many changes and delays."

    🟢 Linked claim

    "Event 7 (late approval of cable routing, letter ref. 112) delayed the critical cable-pulling path by 34 days, shown in the March schedule update. Cost: $1.8M, from site records."

    📝
    Concurrent delay happens when an employer-risk event and a contractor-risk event delay completion at the same time. How it is treated depends on the contract and the governing law; strong claims specialists can explain the approach they used.

    🗓️ Delay analysis methods

    Forensic planners and delay experts use recognised methods, described in guidance such as the SCL Delay and Disruption Protocol (Society of Construction Law) and AACE Recommended Practice 29R-03 (Forensic Schedule Analysis).

    MethodPlain-English idea
    As-planned vs as-builtCompare the original plan with what actually happened
    Impacted as-plannedAdd delay events into the original plan and see how the finish moves
    Time Impact Analysis (TIA)Insert a delay event into the schedule current at that time and measure its effect on completion
    Windows analysisSplit the project into periods and analyse the critical delay in each window
    Collapsed as-builtRemove delay events from the as-built schedule to show "but for" completion
    Example: screening a delay expert
    Ask: "Which method did you use on your last delay analysis and why that one?" Strong answer: "Windows analysis, because we had reliable monthly schedule updates for the whole project." The choice depends on what records exist; a real expert explains the reason.

    💰 Quantum: valuing a claim

    Quantum means the money part of a claim. Quantity surveyors and quantum experts calculate it from records: payroll, equipment logs, invoices, site overhead costs.

    Delay specialistQuantum specialist
    Answers "how much time?"Answers "how much money?"
    Background: planning, forensic scheduling (often AACE FSP)Background: quantity surveying, cost (often RICS)
    Tools: P6, delay analysis methodsTools: cost records, valuation methods
    Example: two experts, one claim
    The delay expert concludes the employer caused 40 days of critical delay. The quantum expert then values those 40 days of site overheads at $2.4M using actual cost records. Clients often need both, so they are two separate searches for you.

    🪜 The dispute resolution ladder

    StepWhat happensPeople you may staff
    NegotiationCommercial teams try to agreeCommercial managers, claims managers
    Engineer's determinationThe Engineer decides under the contractClaims specialists, QSs
    DAAB / adjudicationA board or adjudicator gives a decisionClaims consultants, delay and quantum specialists
    Mediation / amicable settlementA neutral helps the parties agreeSenior commercial and legal staff
    ArbitrationA tribunal makes a binding awardLawyers, expert witnesses, document review teams
    Example: staffing grows as disputes climb
    At negotiation, a client needs 1 claims manager. If the dispute reaches arbitration, it may need external counsel, 2 expert witnesses, 4 claims analysts and a 10-person document review team for 3 months.

    🧑‍⚖️ Experts & their duties

    An expert witness gives an independent opinion to the tribunal. Their main duty is to the tribunal, not to the party that pays them.

    ⚠️
    Red flag: a candidate for an expert role who says they will "make the numbers work for the client". That shows they misunderstand the duty of independence and could damage the client's case.
    Example: screening for independence
    Ask: "Tell me about a time your expert opinion did not fully support the side that appointed you." A strong expert has an honest answer and explains why independence protects credibility.

    Module 06 · Commercial Manager Screening

    Screening Commercial Managers

    The person who protects a project's money every month. Learn what they own, how to test it and what strong evidence sounds like.

    💼 What a commercial manager owns

    A commercial manager makes sure the project gets paid for all the work it does and keeps its margin. On a large EPC project, they usually lead a team of quantity surveyors and contracts administrators.

    Monthly payment applicationsVariationsClaimsCash flowMargin / profit forecastSubcontract accountsFinal accountCommercial risk register
    Example: a commercial manager's month
    Week 1: submit the monthly payment statement. Week 2: chase three variations awaiting the Engineer's valuation. Week 3: review subcontractor payment applications. Week 4: report cash flow, margin forecast and commercial risks to the project director.

    🔁 The payment cycle

    StepWhoWhat happens
    1. Statement / applicationContractor's commercial teamValue of work done this period, plus variations and adjustments
    2. CertificationEngineer (or employer)Checks and certifies the amount due
    3. PaymentEmployerPays the certified amount within the contract period
    4. ReconciliationCommercial teamTracks differences between applied, certified and paid
    Example: why the gap matters
    The contractor applied for $12M, the Engineer certified $10.5M, and $1.5M is disputed. A strong commercial manager knows exactly what makes up that $1.5M and has a plan to recover each part.

    🔄 Variations & final account

    On lump-sum EPC contracts, variations are the main way to be paid for extra work. At the end, everything is settled in the final account.

    StageWhat a strong commercial manager does
    Change identifiedGives notice on time, records costs from day one
    Variation pricedUses contract rates where possible, documents the basis
    Variation agreedGets written agreement; updates the forecast
    Final accountReconciles all variations, claims and adjustments; negotiates closure
    Example: final account evidence
    "Closed the final account on a $380M power project at $431M. Of $64M in variations and claims submitted, $51M was agreed, about 80%, without arbitration." Values, recovery rate and outcome: owned commercial experience.

    🧩 Subcontract management

    Commercial managers also manage the contractor's own subcontracts, where the contractor is now the "employer". Good candidates protect the project in both directions.

    Example: back-to-back protection
    The employer instructs a change to the piping. The commercial manager claims it upstream from the employer and instructs and values it downstream with the piping subcontractor on matching terms, so the contractor does not pay more than it recovers.

    💬 Screening questions

    AskStrong answer containsWeak answer
    "What contract value and team did you manage?"Value, contract form, team size, phase"A large project."
    "Tell me about a final account you settled."Values claimed vs agreed, issues, negotiation approach, timeline"Finance handled it."
    "How do you protect margin on a lump-sum contract?"Early notices, variation tracking, cost forecasting, subcontract alignment"Keep costs low."
    "What was in your last monthly commercial report?"Applied vs certified vs paid, variations, cash flow, margin, risks"Project costs."

    🚩 Red flags & worked example

    • Cannot give recovery percentages or final account values.
    • Only "processed payments" with no variation or claim ownership.
    • Treats commercial and cost control as the same job (cost control tracks spend; commercial secures income and entitlement).

    🟢 Candidate A

    "Commercial manager on a $520M FIDIC Yellow 2017 project, team of 6 QSs. Recovered $28M of $35M variations; set up the notice register that avoided any time bars; closed 22 subcontract accounts."

    Verdict: values, team, outcomes, contract. Advance.

    🔴 Candidate B

    "Handled commercial matters and contractual issues on several major projects with significant value."

    Verdict: no numbers, no contract, no outcome. Probe deeper before submitting.

    Module 07 · Legal Workforce Deployment

    Legal Workforce Deployment

    How to place lawyers, paralegals and document review teams on projects and disputes safely: qualifications, conflicts, confidentiality and supervision.

    ⚖️ What is legal workforce deployment?

    Large projects and disputes need legal people quickly and often in other countries: a contract lawyer at site, a team reviewing 500,000 documents for an arbitration, or in-house counsel seconded to a project. Legal workforce deployment means finding, checking and placing these people correctly.

    ⚠️
    Your boundary. As a recruiter you do not give legal advice. Questions about whether someone may practise law in a country, or whether a conflict is acceptable, are decided by the client's legal team and the relevant regulators.

    👩‍⚖️ Legal roles on EPC projects

    RoleMain job
    Project / construction lawyerAdvises on contracts, notices, claims and disputes
    In-house counsel (seconded)Works inside the project team as its legal adviser
    ParalegalSupports lawyers with documents, research and bundles
    Document reviewerReviews large volumes of documents for relevance and privilege, under lawyer supervision
    Contracts administratorManages correspondence and contract records (not a legal adviser)

    🌐 Qualification & jurisdiction

    A lawyer is admitted in a specific jurisdiction (country or state). Being qualified in one place does not automatically allow giving legal advice everywhere.

    CheckWhy
    Where is the candidate admitted, and is the practising status current?Confirms they are a qualified lawyer in good standing
    Which law governs the contract?Clients often want lawyers qualified in the governing law
    What are the local rules for foreign lawyers in the project country?Some countries restrict what foreign lawyers may do
    What professional indemnity cover applies?Protects client and lawyer if advice is wrong
    Example: the right question to the client
    A strong English-qualified construction lawyer is proposed for a site role in another country. You ask the client's legal team: "Is this qualification acceptable for the scope of advice you need there?" and record their answer before submitting.

    🔒 Conflicts, confidentiality & privilege

    ⚔️
    Conflicts of interest
    Has the person (or their firm) worked for the other party on this project or dispute? Ask every candidate, record the answer, escalate to the client's legal team.
    🤐
    Confidentiality
    Candidates sign confidentiality agreements before seeing case material. Never share dispute details in job adverts.
    🛡️
    Legal privilege
    Some communications with lawyers are protected from disclosure. Reviewers must work under lawyer supervision so privilege is handled correctly.
    Example: a discreet job advert
    Wrong: "Document reviewers needed for the $300M dispute between Company X and Contractor Y." Right: "Document reviewers needed for a construction arbitration, 3 months, confidentiality agreement required."

    📑 Deploying a document review team

    1. Scope: number of documents, languages, deadline, platform (e-discovery tool).
    2. Team shape: supervising lawyers, experienced reviewers, quality checkers.
    3. Checks: conflicts check and confidentiality agreement for every reviewer.
    4. Training: case background, coding guide, privilege rules from the supervising lawyer.
    5. Security: approved devices, secure access, no downloading.
    6. Quality: sample checks on coding decisions, daily progress reporting.
    Example: sizing a team
    400,000 documents must be reviewed in 8 weeks. If an experienced reviewer completes about 50 documents an hour (an assumption to agree with the client), that is 8,000 reviewer-hours. At 35 productive hours a week for 8 weeks (280 hours each), you need about 29 reviewers, plus supervisors and quality checkers.

    📖 Full case study

    Req: Contractor heading to arbitration on a FIDIC Silver 2017 LNG project: 1 claims manager, 1 forensic planner, 1 quantum QS and a 20-person document review team, starting within 5 weeks.

    1. Intake: confirmed side, counterparty names for conflict checks, governing law and confidentiality terms.
    2. Source: claims consultancies, contractor commercial teams and a legal staffing network; 70 profiles.
    3. Conflicts: 12 removed for links to the employer; all others confirmed in writing.
    4. Screen: 18 specialist calls; reviewers tested on a short coding exercise set by the supervising lawyer.
    5. Submit: specialists in 6 days; reviewers in 2 batches.
    6. Result: all 23 deployed by week 5, every one with a signed confidentiality agreement.
    ✅
    Why it worked: conflict checks first, one brief per role, discreet adverts, and close coordination with the client's legal team.

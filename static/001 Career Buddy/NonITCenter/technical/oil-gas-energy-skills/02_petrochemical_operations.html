    Petrochemical Fundamentals: Feedstocks, Building Blocks & Safety

    Start from zero: what a petrochemical plant makes, where it sits next to the refinery, which feedstocks it uses, the olefins and aromatics it produces, and the safety basics every operations person needs on day one.

    🏭 What is a Petrochemical Plant?

    Petrochemical Plant

    process operationsengineering
    Definition
    A petrochemical plant converts hydrocarbon feedstocks (naphtha, ethane, propane, LPG, reformate) into chemical building blocks — ethylene, propylene, butadiene, benzene, toluene, xylenes — and their derivatives such as polyethylene, polypropylene, ethylene glycol and styrene.
    Why it matters
    Petrochemicals are in plastics, packaging, fibres, tyres, pipes, medical devices and detergents. A plant earns its margin by producing on-specification chemicals at high purity, continuously and safely.
    Explanation
    A refinery mainly makes fuels; a petrochemical plant makes molecules for the chemical industry. Purity requirements are much tighter (e.g. polymer-grade ethylene ≥ 99.9%), reactions are often more energetic (polymerization, oxidation), and some units run at extremes — cracking furnaces above 800 °C, cold sections near −100 °C, LDPE reactors above 1,000 bar.
    Types
    • Olefins complex — steam cracker making ethylene, propylene, C4s and pyrolysis gasoline.
    • Aromatics complex — benzene, toluene, xylenes, paraxylene.
    • Polymer plants — PE, PP, PVC, PS.
    • Intermediates — ethylene oxide/glycol, styrene, methanol, phenol.
    • Integrated refinery-petrochemical complex — shares feeds, hydrogen, fuel gas and utilities with a refinery.
    Components
    Feed storage, cracking furnaces, compression, cryogenic separation, reactors, fractionation, polymer finishing (extruders, silos, bagging), product storage, utilities, flare, control room (DCS), quality-control laboratory.
    Process / workflow
    Receive feed → crack or react → separate and purify → convert to derivatives → finish (pelletise, store) → test and certify → dispatch.
    Petrochemical example
    A 1 million tonne/year ethylene cracker on naphtha feed also produces roughly 0.5 Mt/y propylene plus butadiene and pygas. Downstream PE and PP plants turn the ethylene and propylene into pellets for film, pipes and moulded parts.
    Common mistakes
    Treating it like a refinery with “tighter specs”; forgetting reactive-chemical hazards; ignoring that a cracker trip stops every downstream polymer plant.
    Best practices
    Learn the complex's block flow first, then your unit's P&ID; always ask “who uses my product and what purity do they need?”
    🧪 Practice activity: Fuel or chemical?
    Classify: diesel, polymer-grade propylene, jet fuel, paraxylene, LPG to customers, HDPE pellets.
    Answer: Fuels (refinery): diesel, jet fuel, LPG to customers. Petrochemicals: polymer-grade propylene, paraxylene, HDPE pellets.
    EXAMPLE · One molecule, many hands

    An ethylene molecule is made in the cracker, purified in the C2 splitter, polymerised in the PE reactor, pelletised in finishing, tested by the QC lab and shipped by logistics. Each step has its own operations team and its own Petrochemical Operations roles.

    🛢️ Feedstock Basics

    Petrochemical Feedstocks

    process operationscalculations
    Definition
    Feedstock is the hydrocarbon stream a petrochemical unit converts. The main cracker feeds are ethane, propane, butane (LPG), naphtha and sometimes gas oil.
    Why it matters
    Feed decides product slate, energy use, coking rate and cost. Light feeds (ethane) give mostly ethylene; heavy feeds (naphtha) give ethylene plus a lot of propylene, C4s and pyrolysis gasoline.
    Explanation
    • Typical ethylene yield: ethane ~75–80% (per pass with recycle), propane ~40–45%, naphtha ~28–34% by weight.
    • PONA analysis of naphtha (Paraffins, Olefins, Naphthenes, Aromatics): high paraffin naphtha cracks best; aromatics do not crack to olefins and increase fuel oil and coke.
    • Contaminants: sulfur (needed in small amounts — DMDS is injected to passivate coils), mercury (attacks aluminium cold-box exchangers), arsine and CO (poison hydrogenation catalysts), oxygenates, water.
    Types
    Gas feeds (ethane, propane), liquid feeds (LPG, light naphtha, full-range naphtha, gas condensate, hydrotreated gas oil), recycle streams (ethane and propane recycled to dedicated furnaces).
    Components
    Feed tanks and spheres, feed analysis (PONA, density, distillation, contaminants), feed preheat and vaporisation, sulfur injection (DMDS), feed filters/guard beds.
    Process / workflow
    Planning selects feed slate → feed certified by lab → operations sets furnace severity per feed → yields checked by on-line analysers → feed changes managed with a feed-switch procedure.
    Petrochemical example
    Switching a furnace from light naphtha to heavier naphtha: ethylene yield drops, pygas and fuel oil rise, coking rate increases — so run length between decokes shortens and the coil outlet temperature target is adjusted.
    Common mistakes
    Switching feed without changing steam-to-hydrocarbon ratio; ignoring mercury in condensate feeds; not checking aromatics content.
    Best practices
    Use a feed-switch checklist with new severity targets; increase sampling during switches; track contaminants against guard-bed capacity.
    🧪 Practice activity: Pick the feed
    The market needs more propylene and butadiene. Should the cracker shift toward ethane or naphtha?
    Answer: Naphtha (or LPG). Ethane cracking gives very little propylene and almost no butadiene; heavier feeds give much more co-product.

    Basic Petrochemical Process Flow

    P&IDprocess operations
    Definition
    The process flow is the route of feed and products between units, shown on the Block Flow Diagram (BFD), Process Flow Diagram (PFD) and in full detail on the Piping & Instrumentation Diagram (P&ID).
    Why it matters
    A cracker hot section, cold section, polymer plants and utilities are tightly linked. Knowing the flow lets operators predict the effect of any change before making it.
    Explanation
    Feed + dilution steam → cracking furnaces → transfer-line exchangers (TLE) and quench → cracked gas compressor → caustic wash and drying → chilling train / cold box → demethanizer, deethanizer, acetylene hydrogenation, C2 splitter → polymer-grade ethylene. C3+ goes to the depropanizer and C3 splitter (propylene), C4s to butadiene extraction, pygas to hydrotreating and aromatics.
    Types
    BFD (one box per unit), PFD (main equipment and heat/material balance), P&ID (every line, valve, instrument and interlock).
    Components
    Feed lines, recycle streams (ethane, propane), refrigeration loops, flare connections, utility headers, product rundown lines.
    Process / workflow
    Trace a stream: source → line numbers → control and block valves → instruments → receiver. Confirm in the field.
    Petrochemical example
    Ethane leaving the bottom of the C2 splitter is not a product — it is recycled to an ethane cracking furnace. If that furnace is down for decoking, ethane must go to fuel gas, raising fuel-gas pressure across the complex.
    Common mistakes
    Forgetting recycle loops; using an out-of-date P&ID; not knowing which flare header a relief valve discharges to.
    Best practices
    Walk lines in the field with the P&ID; keep line-up checklists; review recycle and refrigeration loops at handover.
    🧪 Practice activity: Trace ethylene
    List the main steps from naphtha feed to polymer-grade ethylene.
    Answer: Furnace (cracking) → TLE/quench → cracked gas compression → caustic wash (H2S/CO2) → drying → chilling/demethanizer (remove H2, CH4) → deethanizer → acetylene hydrogenation → C2 splitter → ethylene product.

    🦺 Basic Safety Concepts

    Petrochemical Hazards & Basic Controls

    process safetysafety
    Definition
    The specific hazards of petrochemical plants and the basic controls that keep people and plant safe.
    Why it matters
    Petrochemical plants combine flammable light hydrocarbons (ethylene, propylene, hydrogen) with reactive chemistry, cryogenic temperatures and toxic aromatics.
    Explanation
    • Light hydrocarbons: ethylene and hydrogen have wide flammable ranges and low ignition energy; releases form vapour clouds quickly.
    • Cryogenic: cold liquids cause cold burns and can make carbon steel brittle.
    • Toxic: benzene (carcinogen), butadiene (carcinogen), H2S in cracked gas, caustic.
    • Reactive: runaway polymerization, ethylene decomposition at high pressure, butadiene peroxides/“popcorn” polymer.
    • Pyrophoric: aluminium alkyl co-catalysts (e.g. TEAL) ignite on contact with air and react violently with water.
    Types
    Occupational safety (falls, burns, noise) and process safety (loss of containment, fire, explosion, runaway).
    Components
    FR clothing, gas monitors, cryogenic gloves/face shields, PTW, LOTO, gas testing, fire & gas detection, emergency shutdown, reactor kill systems, Stop Work Authority.
    Process / workflow
    JSA → permit → isolate and gas test → toolbox talk → work → hand back → remove isolations.
    Petrochemical example
    A technician draining a cold-section level glass wears cryogenic gloves and face shield, drains slowly to the closed flare drain, and never drains cold liquid into a carbon-steel line not rated for low temperature.
    Common mistakes
    Draining cold liquid to an open drain; handling catalyst alkyls without the right PPE; assuming a line is empty because a gauge reads zero.
    Best practices
    Know the material safety data for every chemical on your unit; use closed drains and sampling; use Stop Work Authority when unsure.
    🧪 Practice activity: Spot the hazard
    A drum of co-catalyst (aluminium alkyl) has a small leak at a flange. Why must water not be used to clean it up?
    Answer: Aluminium alkyls react violently with water and ignite in air. Use the site's dedicated procedure (e.g. dry inert absorbent, nitrogen blanketing) and trained responders.

    🚢 Feed Receipt & Handling

    Feed Receipt, Storage & Conditioning

    process operationsmeasurementsequipment/process operation
    Definition
    The controlled receipt, storage, analysis and conditioning of feedstocks (naphtha, LPG, ethane, propane) before they enter cracking furnaces or reactors.
    Why it matters
    Off-spec or contaminated feed causes coking, catalyst poisoning, cold-box damage (mercury) and product contamination that may only show up hours later.
    Explanation
    Liquid feeds are stored in floating-roof tanks; LPG in pressurised spheres or bullets; ethane often arrives by pipeline. Each lot is sampled for density, distillation, PONA, sulfur, mercury and other contaminants. Liquid feeds are preheated and vaporised in the furnace convection section; DMDS (sulfur) is dosed to passivate coil surfaces and reduce CO formation.
    Types
    Pipeline receipt, marine receipt, truck/rail LPG receipt; dedicated vs blended feed tanks.
    Components
    Tanks, spheres, feed pumps, coalescers, mercury and arsine guard beds, analysers, feed flow controllers per furnace pass.
    Process / workflow
    Line-up check → receipt → settle and drain water → sample and certify → line up to furnaces → set feed rate per pass → monitor contaminants.
    Petrochemical example
    A new condensate cargo contains mercury. Without a mercury guard bed, mercury can concentrate in the cold section and cause liquid-metal embrittlement of aluminium plate-fin exchangers — a potential catastrophic failure.
    Common mistakes
    Feeding before lab release; not draining water from feed tanks; ignoring guard-bed breakthrough data.
    Best practices
    Feed acceptance limits in the operating envelope; guard-bed outlet monitoring; feed-switch procedure with new severity targets.
    🧪 Practice activity: Accept or reject?
    Naphtha lab result: aromatics 22% (limit 15%). What happens if you feed it anyway?
    Answer: Aromatics hardly crack to olefins; ethylene yield falls, fuel oil and coke rise, run length shortens. Hold the lot and agree a blend ratio with planning.

    🔥 Steam Cracking

    Figure 2.1 · Cracking furnace and quench (typical values, not design figures)

    Steam Cracking

    process operationsP&IDcalculationstroubleshooting
    Definition
    Steam cracking (pyrolysis) breaks saturated hydrocarbons into smaller, unsaturated molecules (olefins) by heating them very quickly to high temperature in the presence of steam, without catalyst.
    Why it matters
    It is the heart of the olefins complex — almost all the world's ethylene and much of its propylene and butadiene come from steam crackers.
    Explanation
    Feed and dilution steam are preheated in the convection section, then cracked in the radiant coils at ~800–870 °C for a fraction of a second. Steam lowers hydrocarbon partial pressure (favouring olefins) and slows coking. Products must be cooled within milliseconds in the TLE to stop secondary reactions. Key handles: COT (severity), steam-to-hydrocarbon ratio, feed rate per pass, coil outlet pressure.
    Types
    Gas crackers (ethane/propane) and liquid crackers (naphtha/gas oil); dedicated recycle furnaces for ethane/propane.
    Components
    Firebox, burners, convection banks, radiant coils, crossover, TLEs, steam drum, decoking lines, TMT thermocouples, BMS.
    Process / workflow
    Run on feed → monitor TMT, coil ΔP and TLE outlet temperature (all rise as coke builds) → when limit reached take furnace off feed → steam-air decoke (typically every 20–60 days for liquid feeds) → return to feed.
    Petrochemical example
    Raising COT by 10 °C increases ethylene yield and reduces propylene (lower P/E ratio), but speeds coking and shortens run length. The engineer weighs extra ethylene against more frequent decokes.
    Common mistakes
    Running unbalanced pass flows; cutting dilution steam to save energy; letting TMT exceed IOW limits (carburisation, creep).
    Best practices
    Balance passes; trend TMT, coil ΔP and TLE outlet daily; plan decokes with the Planner to keep ethylene production smooth.
    🧪 Practice activity: End of run
    Name three signs that a cracking furnace is approaching end of run.
    Answer: Rising tube metal temperatures, rising coil pressure drop, and rising TLE outlet temperature (TLE fouling).

    🌀 Quench, Compression & Treatment

    Quench, Cracked Gas Compression & Acid Gas Removal

    equipment/process operationprocess safetytroubleshooting
    Definition
    The section that cools cracked gas, removes heavy oil and water, compresses the gas in stages, and removes acid gases and water before cryogenic separation.
    Why it matters
    The cracked gas compressor (CGC) is often the largest machine on site; if it trips, the entire cracker must drop feed and flare.
    Explanation
    Liquid crackers use a quench oil tower (recovering heat, removing fuel oil) then a quench water tower (condensing dilution steam and pygas). Gas is compressed in 4–5 stages with interstage cooling to keep discharge temperature low (limit ~90–100 °C to slow fouling by polymer). A caustic tower removes H2S and CO2; molecular sieve dryers remove water to ppm level so it cannot form ice or hydrates in the cold section.
    Types
    Oil quench / water quench; multi-stage centrifugal compressors; caustic or amine + caustic washing; regenerable molecular-sieve dryers.
    Components
    Quench towers, dilution steam generation, CGC with steam turbine, interstage drums and coolers, wash oil injection, caustic tower, spent-caustic handling, dryers.
    Process / workflow
    Monitor quench tower bottoms temperature/viscosity → CGC suction pressure, discharge temperatures, wash oil → caustic strength and outlet CO2 → dryer outlet moisture → regenerate dryers on schedule.
    Petrochemical example
    CGC 3rd-stage discharge temperature creeps up and efficiency falls. Cause: polymer fouling on the impellers. Response: optimise wash-oil injection and interstage cooling; plan an online water/wash-oil wash per procedure.
    Common mistakes
    Neglecting quench water emulsion (pygas carry-over); letting caustic run weak (CO2 breaks through and freezes in the cold section); regenerating dryers late.
    Best practices
    Trend CGC polytropic efficiency; keep caustic strength in its band; alarm on dryer outlet moisture.
    🧪 Practice activity: Why dry so hard?
    Why must cracked gas be dried to ppm water before the cold section?
    Answer: At cryogenic temperatures water forms ice and hydrocarbon hydrates that block exchangers, valves and columns, forcing a shutdown to derime (warm up).

    ❄️ Cold Separation & Fractionation

    Cryogenic Separation, Hydrogenation & Splitters

    P&IDcalculationsquality
    Definition
    The cold section chills dry cracked gas with propylene and ethylene refrigeration to separate hydrogen, methane, C2 and C3 streams; hydrogenation reactors and splitter columns then make polymer-grade ethylene and propylene.
    Why it matters
    Product purity is set here. Ethylene customers (PE plants) can tolerate only ppm levels of acetylene, CO, water and sulfur.
    Explanation
    • Chilling train / cold box → demethanizer removes H2 and CH4 (to fuel gas/hydrogen recovery).
    • Deethanizer separates C2 from C3+.
    • Acetylene converter hydrogenates acetylene to ethylene over Pd catalyst — selectivity matters; too much H2/temperature over-hydrogenates ethylene to ethane and can cause a temperature runaway.
    • C2 splitter (very high reflux, often ~100+ trays) separates ethylene from ethane; ethane is recycled.
    • Depropanizer, MAPD converter, C3 splitter make polymer-grade propylene.
    Types
    Front-end demethanizer vs front-end deethanizer/depropanizer flowsheets; front-end vs tail-end acetylene hydrogenation.
    Components
    Plate-fin exchangers, expanders, refrigeration compressors, hydrogenation reactors, splitters, on-line GC analysers.
    Process / workflow
    Control cold-box temperatures → demethanizer bottoms methane → converter inlet temperature and H2/acetylene ratio → splitter reflux and product purity by GC → lab confirmation.
    Petrochemical example
    Acetylene at the converter outlet rises from <1 ppm to 4 ppm (“breakthrough”). The operator diverts ethylene to off-spec/flare per procedure, raises converter inlet temperature in small steps, and checks for CO (which moderates/poisons the Pd catalyst).
    Common mistakes
    Large converter temperature steps (runaway risk); ignoring green-oil build-up; sending off-spec ethylene to the PE plant.
    Best practices
    Small, slow converter moves; continuous acetylene analyser with alarm; clear off-spec routing procedure agreed with downstream plants.
    🧪 Practice activity: Why recycle ethane?
    Why is ethane from the C2 splitter bottoms sent to a furnace instead of being sold?
    Answer: It can be cracked again to make more ethylene — recycle cracking raises overall ethylene yield from the feed.

    🧬 Aromatics Processing

    Aromatics: Pygas Hydrotreating, Extraction & Xylenes

    process operationsprocess safetyquality
    Definition
    Processes that recover and purify benzene, toluene and xylenes (BTX) from pyrolysis gasoline or reformate, and convert them into higher-value aromatics such as paraxylene.
    Why it matters
    Aromatics are feed for styrene, phenol, nylon and polyester. Benzene is also a carcinogen, so containment and exposure control are critical.
    Explanation
    Pygas hydrotreating: first stage (Pd/Ni, mild) saturates dienes and styrene that would form gum; second stage (Co-Mo/Ni-Mo) removes sulfur and saturates olefins. Solvent extraction (e.g. sulfolane) separates aromatics from non-aromatics; fractionation then splits B, T and X. Paraxylene is recovered by adsorption (simulated moving bed) or crystallisation; xylene isomerisation converts other xylenes to paraxylene.
    Types
    Extractive distillation, liquid–liquid extraction, toluene disproportionation, transalkylation, isomerisation.
    Components
    Hydrogenation reactors, extractor, stripper, solvent recovery, BTX columns, adsorbers, clay treaters (remove trace olefins).
    Process / workflow
    Stabilise pygas → hydrotreat → extract → fractionate → clay treat → test (benzene purity, colour, acid wash colour, bromine index).
    Petrochemical example
    Benzene product fails bromine index (trace olefins). The clay treater is near end of life; operations switches to the fresh clay bed and schedules clay change-out.
    Common mistakes
    Open sampling of benzene; first-stage reactor temperature excursions from diene polymerization; solvent degradation from oxygen ingress.
    Best practices
    Closed-loop sampling; nitrogen blanketing; monitor solvent quality (acidity, degradation products).
    🧪 Practice activity: Two stages
    Why is pygas hydrotreated in two stages instead of one hot stage?
    Answer: Dienes and styrene polymerise to gum at high temperature, fouling the reactor. A mild first stage saturates them; the hotter second stage then removes sulfur safely.

    Polymerization Operations

    process safetyqualityproblem-solving
    Definition
    Polymerization joins monomer molecules (ethylene, propylene) into long chains using a catalyst or initiator, producing polymer powder or melt that is then pelletised.
    Why it matters
    Polymerization is strongly exothermic. Heat removal must always exceed heat generation, or the reaction runs away. Grade quality (MFI, density) is set by reactor conditions.
    Explanation
    Hydrogen acts as a chain-transfer agent — more H2 gives shorter chains and higher MFI. Comonomer lowers density. Temperature and catalyst feed set production rate. Impurities (water, oxygen, CO, sulfur, acetylene) poison catalysts. A kill system injects a poison (e.g. CO) to stop the reaction in an emergency.
    Types
    Slurry, gas-phase, solution, high-pressure free-radical; Ziegler-Natta, chromium, metallocene catalysts.
    Components
    Catalyst and co-catalyst feed systems, reactor, recycle gas compressor and cooler, product discharge, degassing/purge bin, extruder, pelletiser, blending silos.
    Process / workflow
    Purify monomer → feed catalyst → control temperature, pressure, H2/C2 and comonomer ratios → discharge powder → degas → extrude with additives → pelletise → test MFI/density → release to silo.
    Petrochemical example
    In a gas-phase reactor, loss of cycle-gas cooling causes temperatures to rise; polymer softens and forms sheets/chunks that block the distributor plate. Operators reduce catalyst feed and, if temperature keeps rising, activate the kill system.
    Common mistakes
    Adjusting catalyst rate to chase MFI (instead of H2); ignoring static build-up in gas-phase reactors; poor purge-bin operation leaving hydrocarbons in powder (fire risk in silos).
    Best practices
    Know the kill procedure by heart; trend reactor skin temperatures and static; follow grade transition recipes exactly.
    🧪 Practice activity: MFI control
    Pellet MFI is below the grade target (polymer too viscous). Which reactor variable is the normal handle?
    Answer: Increase the hydrogen-to-monomer ratio slightly — more chain transfer gives shorter chains and higher MFI.

    🔥 Cracking Furnaces & TLEs

    Cracking Furnace Operation & Decoking

    equipment/process operationprocess safetypreventive maintenance
    Definition
    Cracking furnaces are fired heaters with radiant coils where feed cracks; transfer-line exchangers (TLEs) immediately quench the effluent and generate super-high-pressure steam.
    Why it matters
    Furnaces set the complex's yield and capacity, use most of its fuel, and carry fire and tube-rupture hazards.
    Explanation
    Coke builds inside coils and TLEs. As it grows, TMT and coil ΔP rise. Tubes suffer carburisation and creep at high temperature — exceeding TMT limits shortens tube life sharply. Decoking: furnace off feed → steam only → controlled air addition to burn coke, monitoring CO2/CO in effluent and TMT → back to feed. Effluent during decoking goes to a decoke drum/firebox, not to the cracked gas system.
    Types
    Steam-air decoking, steam-only spalling; online (one furnace decoking while others run) vs offline mechanical cleaning of TLEs.
    Components
    Burners and BMS, radiant coils, TMT thermocouples and pyrometers, TLEs and steam drum, decoke valves (feed/decoke switching), dampers, O2 analysers.
    Process / workflow
    Daily: check flames, TMT, pass balance, O2, draft, steam drum level → plan decoke when end-of-run criteria hit → switch valves per checklist (never connect decoke air to the cracked-gas header) → decoke → back on feed.
    Petrochemical example
    A decoke valve sequence is done out of order and air briefly enters the cracked-gas line — a flammable mixture risk. This is why decoke switching uses interlocked valves and a sign-off checklist.
    Common mistakes
    Adding decoke air too fast (TMT spikes); losing TLE steam drum level (tube damage); ignoring flame impingement.
    Best practices
    Daily pyrometer surveys; follow the valve-switching sequence with two-person verification; record each run length for trend analysis.
    🧪 Practice activity: Steam drum level
    The TLE steam drum level falls fast. Why is this urgent?
    Answer: TLE tubes are cooled by boiling water. Without water, tubes overheat in seconds and can fail, releasing cracked gas and high-pressure steam. Restore feedwater or trip the furnace per procedure.

    🌀 Compressors & Refrigeration

    Large Compressors & Refrigeration Systems

    equipment/process operationtroubleshootingprocess safety
    Definition
    The cracked gas, propylene refrigeration and ethylene refrigeration compressors are large centrifugal machines, usually steam-turbine driven, that provide pressure and cold for separation.
    Why it matters
    Losing any of them stops the cracker. Refrigeration loss warms the cold section and can force full shutdown and flaring.
    Explanation
    Centrifugal compressors must stay away from surge (anti-surge recycle valves). Refrigeration systems are cascaded: propylene refrigeration (down to about −40 °C) condenses ethylene refrigerant, which provides colder levels (down to about −100 °C). Suction KO drums protect against liquid; dry gas seals and lube oil systems protect bearings and shafts.
    Types
    Multi-stage centrifugal, axial (air), reciprocating (hydrogen, some LDPE hyper-compressors at very high pressure).
    Components
    Compressor, steam turbine, governor, anti-surge controls, KO drums, interstage coolers, lube and seal systems, vibration and axial displacement monitoring, trip system.
    Process / workflow
    Pre-start permissives → slow roll and warm turbine → run up through critical speeds → load on recycle → monitor vibration, bearing temperatures, surge margin.
    Petrochemical example
    Propylene refrigeration compressor trips on high vibration. Ethylene refrigerant cannot be condensed; cold section temperatures rise. Operations cuts furnace feed quickly and routes off-spec ethylene to the flare while the machine is investigated.
    Common mistakes
    Anti-surge in manual; restarting after a trip without finding the cause; overlooking turbine steam quality.
    Best practices
    Trend vibration and efficiency; keep anti-surge in auto; investigate every trip.
    🧪 Practice activity: Cascade
    Why does losing the propylene refrigeration compressor also affect the ethylene refrigeration system?
    Answer: Propylene refrigerant condenses the ethylene refrigerant. Without it, ethylene refrigerant cannot be condensed and the colder refrigeration levels collapse.

    🧊 Cryogenic Equipment

    Cold Box & Cryogenic Equipment

    process safetyinspectioncalculations
    Definition
    Equipment that operates far below ambient temperature: brazed aluminium plate-fin heat exchangers (cold box), turbo-expanders, cold separators and cryogenic piping.
    Why it matters
    Cryogenic equipment is efficient but sensitive: thermal stress, mercury, and low-temperature brittleness can cause sudden failures.
    Explanation
    Plate-fin exchangers have limits on temperature difference between streams and on cool-down/warm-up rate (thermal fatigue). Minimum design metal temperature (MDMT) applies to every line: if cold liquid flashes into a carbon-steel line (for example during depressuring — auto-refrigeration), the steel can fracture in a brittle way.
    Types
    Plate-fin, coil-wound, kettle chillers; expanders; cold separators; stainless or low-temperature carbon steel piping.
    Components
    Cold box enclosure (purged with N2, insulated), temperature differential monitoring, mercury guard upstream, deriming connections.
    Process / workflow
    Controlled cool-down with temperature-rate limits → steady operation inside ΔT limits → periodic deriming (warm-up with dry gas) when ice/hydrates build up.
    Petrochemical example
    During a rapid depressure, a section of carbon-steel line cools below its MDMT. An engineering review afterwards finds the depressuring rate must be limited — added to the procedure through MOC.
    Common mistakes
    Cooling down too fast; ignoring cold-box purge; draining cold liquid to unrated lines.
    Best practices
    Respect rate limits; trend stream ΔT; check MDMT on every line in depressuring and drain paths.
    🧪 Practice activity: Why the rate limit?
    Why do procedures limit cold-box cool-down to a set number of °C per hour?
    Answer: Fast temperature changes create large thermal stresses between thin aluminium parts, which can crack brazed joints and cause leaks.

    ⚗️ Reactors

    Petrochemical Reactors

    process safetytechnical designtroubleshooting
    Definition
    Vessels where chemical conversion happens: fixed-bed catalytic reactors (hydrogenation), slurry loop reactors, gas-phase fluidised beds, and high-pressure autoclave/tubular reactors (LDPE).
    Why it matters
    Most petrochemical reactions are exothermic. Reactors need reliable heat removal, temperature monitoring and emergency stopping.
    Explanation
    Heat removal: cooling jackets (loop), cycle-gas coolers (gas-phase), inter-bed quench (fixed bed). Detection: many thermocouples, skin temperatures, pressure. Protection: high-temperature trips, kill injection, emergency depressuring, relief and, for LDPE, decomposition protection (ethylene can decompose explosively at very high pressure and temperature).
    Types
    Fixed bed, slurry loop, stirred tank, fluidised bed, tubular high pressure, autoclave.
    Components
    Reactor vessel, circulation pump (loop), distributor plate (gas phase), catalyst injection, product discharge valves/settling legs, kill system, relief devices.
    Process / workflow
    Purify feeds → start catalyst feed at low rate → raise production while watching temperatures → control grade with H2/comonomer → discharge product → monitor fouling and static.
    Petrochemical example
    A loop reactor circulation pump trips. Without circulation, polymer settles and heat removal stops — the procedure activates the kill system immediately to prevent a plugged, overheating loop.
    Common mistakes
    Increasing catalyst feed to recover rate quickly; ignoring rising static in gas-phase reactors; bypassing a kill-system interlock.
    Best practices
    Treat kill systems as safety-critical elements (proof tested); practise kill drills; trend skin temperatures.
    🧪 Practice activity: First action
    A gas-phase PE reactor shows rising skin temperatures in one zone. Name two likely causes and the first action.
    Answer: Causes: sheeting (polymer stuck to the wall due to static or poor heat removal), poor fluidisation/distribution. First action: reduce catalyst feed / production rate, check static and cycle-gas flow; prepare kill if temperatures keep rising.

    🎛️ Instrumentation, Control & P&ID Reading

    Control, Analysers & Safety Instrumented Systems

    P&IDtechnical documentationtroubleshooting
    Definition
    The measurements, control loops, on-line analysers and Safety Instrumented Systems (SIS) that keep a petrochemical unit on target and safe.
    Why it matters
    Product purity is measured in ppm and controlled continuously; dangerous conditions are stopped automatically by the SIS.
    Explanation
    • Tag letters: TIC = temperature indicating controller; AI/AT = analyser; FIC = flow controller; e.g. AI-301 could be the acetylene analyser on the converter outlet.
    • On-line GCs measure ethylene purity, acetylene, CO, methane, and furnace effluent yields (P/E ratio).
    • Ratio and cascade control: steam-to-hydrocarbon ratio per pass; H2/C2 ratio in PE reactors.
    • SIS trips furnaces (low pass flow, high TMT, low drum level), compressors, and initiates reactor kill — independent of the DCS.
    • P&ID reading: line class (incl. low-temperature rating), fail positions, interlocks, relief destinations.
    Types
    PID, cascade, ratio, split range, feed-forward; APC/MPC; SIS with SIL-rated functions.
    Components
    Transmitters, analysers with sample systems, DCS, SIS logic, shutdown valves, cause-and-effect charts.
    Process / workflow
    Read P&ID and cause-and-effect → know which trips act on which valves → check analyser health (validation, sample flow) before trusting a result.
    Petrochemical example
    The ethylene purity analyser suddenly reads 99.2%. Before diverting product, the board operator checks analyser status, sample flow and the second analyser/lab spot sample — it was a sample-system blockage.
    Common mistakes
    Trusting a stale analyser; bypassing SIS trips without a formal override; ignoring line low-temperature class on P&IDs.
    Best practices
    Daily analyser validation checks; formal override register; train on cause-and-effect charts.
    🧪 Practice activity: Decode the tags
    Decode: TAHH-112, FFIC-205, XV-310, AI-301.
    Answer: Temperature alarm high-high 112 (usually a trip); flow-ratio indicating controller 205 (e.g. steam/hydrocarbon ratio); on/off shutdown valve 310; analyser indicator 301.

    📋 Operating Procedures & MOC

    Operating Procedures & Management of Change

    documentationtechnical documentationprocess safety
    Definition
    Operating procedures are approved step-by-step instructions for every phase of operation. Management of Change (MOC) is the formal hazard review of any change to equipment, chemicals, catalysts, procedures or limits.
    Why it matters
    The Flixborough disaster (UK, 1974, 28 killed) followed a temporary bypass pipe installed on a cyclohexane oxidation plant without proper engineering review — the classic lesson behind MOC.
    Explanation
    Procedures state who, what, limits and consequences of deviation; critical steps have sign-offs. In petrochemicals, catalyst or additive changes, new polymer grades, new feeds and temporary hoses all need MOC.
    Types
    Normal, startup, shutdown, emergency, temporary operations; grade transition recipes; decoke procedures; catalyst handling procedures.
    Components
    Scope, hazards and precautions, operating limits, steps with sign-offs, references, revision control, approval.
    Process / workflow
    Draft (operators + engineers) → technical/safety review → approve → train → use in the field → periodic review and after incidents/MOCs.
    Petrochemical example
    R&D wants a trial of a new comonomer. MOC: check reactor heat removal, purge-bin degassing, flare loads, storage, SDS and lab methods; update procedures; train crews before the trial.
    Common mistakes
    Running from memory; temporary changes that become permanent; procedures that don't match the field.
    Best practices
    Procedure in hand for non-routine tasks; report mismatches; close MOCs only after training and documents are complete.
    🧪 Practice activity: MOC or not?
    (a) Replace a PE reactor thermocouple with an identical one. (b) Switch to a different supplier's catalyst. (c) Raise the C2 splitter pressure set point above its documented range.
    Answer: (a) No — replacement in kind. (b) Yes. (c) Yes.

    🔐 Permit to Work & Energy Isolation

    Permit to Work, Isolation & Equipment Preparation

    safetywork allocationprocess safety
    Definition
    The written system that controls hazardous non-routine work, and the isolation of equipment from every energy source before it is opened.
    Why it matters
    The Phillips Pasadena polyethylene plant explosion (USA, 1989, 23 killed) occurred during maintenance on a reactor settling leg when a valve that should have been isolated was opened — air hoses to the actuator had been connected the wrong way round, and there was no positive isolation.
    Explanation
    Isolation hierarchy: positive isolation (blinds, removed spool) → double block and bleed → single valve (low risk only). Lock out actuators (air and power) on valves that form isolations. Prepare equipment: depressure, drain (cold liquids carefully), purge with N2, gas test. Catalyst systems need special preparation (alkyls neutralised, pyrophoric residues kept wet or inert).
    Types
    Hot work, cold work, confined space, line-break, electrical, radiography, catalyst handling permits.
    Components
    Permit form, JSA, isolation certificate and blind list, gas test record, LOTO devices (including on valve actuators), signatures, hand-back section.
    Process / workflow
    Plan → JSA → isolate & prepare → gas test → issue at job site → toolbox talk → work → re-test → hand back → de-isolate in order.
    Petrochemical example
    Clearing a plugged reactor discharge leg: the reactor block valve is disabled from operating (actuator air disconnected and locked), a second isolation is confirmed, the leg is depressured and purged, and only then opened.
    Common mistakes
    Relying on a single air-operated valve; not locking out actuators; issuing permits from the control room without visiting the site.
    Best practices
    Verify isolations physically; lock actuators; numbered blind lists; cross-reference all permits on the same system.
    🧪 Practice activity: What failed?
    From the Pasadena example, name two isolation lessons.
    Answer: Valves forming an isolation must be disabled and locked (actuator energy removed); hazardous systems need positive/double isolation, not a single valve. Also: design hose connections so they cannot be reversed.

    🔁 Grade Transitions & Product Quality

    Grade Transitions

    qualityplanningquality plans
    Definition
    A grade transition is the planned change of a polymer plant from one product grade (MFI, density, additives) to another.
    Why it matters
    Material made during the transition is often off-grade (sold at a discount). Fast, well-run transitions save money and silo space.
    Explanation
    Transitions change H2/monomer ratio, comonomer, temperature and additive package. The Planner sequences grades in a campaign wheel (e.g. low MFI → high MFI) to minimise off-grade. The Quality Engineer defines sampling frequency and release criteria; off-grade goes to a dedicated silo.
    Types
    Simple (MFI change only), comonomer change, catalyst change (most difficult), additive package change.
    Components
    Transition recipe, grade campaign plan, QC sampling plan, off-grade silo, product release procedure.
    Process / workflow
    Plan sequence → pre-transition checks (silos, additives) → execute recipe → frequent QC sampling → switch product to prime silo when in spec → record transition time and off-grade tonnes.
    Petrochemical example
    A transition from 0.3 to 2.0 MFI took 6 hours and made 120 t off-grade. Analysis shows H2 was ramped too slowly. A revised recipe with a planned H2 “overshoot” cuts it to 3.5 hours.
    Common mistakes
    Diverting to prime silo before lab confirmation; transitions planned against the campaign wheel; additive package switched late.
    Best practices
    Standard recipes; track transition time and off-grade as KPIs; review every abnormal transition.
    🧪 Practice activity: Sequence the wheel
    Grades: MFI 8, 0.5, 2, 20. Suggest an order that reduces off-grade.
    Answer: Move steadily in one direction: 0.5 → 2 → 8 → 20 (then back down in the next cycle) — small steps between neighbouring grades instead of big jumps.

    🎯 Process Optimization

    Cracker & Polymer Optimization

    advanced technical analysiscalculationsdata analysis
    Definition
    Adjusting feed, severity, rates and recipes to maximise margin while staying inside every safety, equipment, environmental and quality constraint.
    Why it matters
    Small yield gains on million-tonne plants are worth millions per year; but the cracker is usually limited by one real bottleneck.
    Explanation
    Key levers: feed slate (ethane vs naphtha), severity (COT, P/E ratio — high severity favours ethylene, low severity favours propylene), steam ratio, furnace run length vs decoke frequency, ethane/propane recycle cracking, polymer catalyst productivity and transition time. Typical active constraints: CGC capacity, refrigeration duty, C2 splitter flooding, furnace TMT, flare capacity.
    Types
    Throughput maximisation, yield/severity optimisation, energy optimisation, catalyst cost optimisation, off-grade minimisation.
    Components
    LP/planning model, yield model (furnace simulation), APC, analyser data, economics (ethylene vs propylene vs fuel values).
    Process / workflow
    Objective → constraints → find the active constraint → small test move → measure → standardise or roll back → feed back to planning.
    Petrochemical example
    Propylene prices rise above ethylene. Planning asks for lower severity. Operations lowers COT stepwise; P/E rises from 0.50 to 0.58; run length lengthens; the CGC load changes — checked against its limit at each step.
    Common mistakes
    Optimising one furnace while the CGC is at its limit; eroding TMT margins; ignoring downstream polymer demand.
    Best practices
    Think complex-wide; never trade away IOW margins; document results.
    🧪 Practice activity: Severity direction
    Ethylene is worth much more than propylene this month. Should severity go up or down, and what is the trade-off?
    Answer: Up (higher COT, lower P/E) for more ethylene — at the cost of faster coking, shorter run length and more fuel.

    🛡️ Process Safety & Reactive Hazards

    Process Safety in Petrochemicals

    process safetyleadershipreporting
    Definition
    Management of hazards from processes and equipment to prevent loss of primary containment, fires, explosions, toxic releases and runaway reactions.
    Why it matters
    Petrochemical plants add reactive chemistry to the usual flammable hazards — an exothermic reaction out of control can overpressure a vessel faster than operators can respond.
    Explanation
    • Runaway reaction: heat generation grows exponentially with temperature while cooling grows only linearly — once past the point of no return, only the kill system or relief can save the reactor.
    • Decomposition: ethylene under very high pressure (LDPE) and ethylene oxide can decompose explosively.
    • Peroxides / popcorn polymer in butadiene service can block lines and rupture equipment.
    • Pyrophorics: alkyls, iron sulfide, some spent catalysts.
    • Measure with lagging (Tier 1/2 LOPC, API RP 754) and leading indicators (overdue SIS proof tests, open MOCs, bypassed safeguards, IOW exceedances).
    Types
    Tier 1–4 process safety events; thermal, reactive, toxic, physical hazards.
    Components
    PSI (incl. reactive chemistry data), PHA/HAZOP, procedures, training, mechanical integrity, MOC, PSSR, incident investigation, emergency planning, culture.
    Process / workflow
    Know the hazards → design and maintain barriers → operate inside limits → monitor barrier health → learn from weak signals.
    Petrochemical example
    Flixborough (1974): a temporary bypass on cyclohexane reactors failed; ~40 t of cyclohexane formed a vapour cloud that exploded, killing 28. Pasadena (1989): release from a PE loop reactor during maintenance killed 23. Both show the cost of weak MOC and isolation.
    Common mistakes
    Measuring only injury rates; deferring kill-system proof tests; normalising small leaks.
    Best practices
    Track leading indicators; treat every SIS demand and relief lift as a learning event; time-limit overrides.
    🧪 Practice activity: Leading or lagging?
    (a) Kill system proof test 3 months overdue; (b) ethylene release with fire; (c) a PSV lift on a splitter; (d) 15 open MOCs past due.
    Answer: (a) leading, (b) lagging Tier 1, (c) Tier 3 challenge to a safeguard, (d) leading.

    🔎 HAZOP Concepts

    HAZOP (Hazard and Operability Study)

    P&IDprocess safetydesign review
    Definition
    A structured team review of P&IDs, node by node, applying guidewords to parameters to find deviations, causes, consequences and safeguards.
    Why it matters
    It is the main PHA method for new designs, major changes and periodic revalidation (often every 5 years).
    Explanation

    Scope & nodes → guidewords → worksheet → risk rank → recommendations → action tracking.

    Common mistakes
    No operator in the team; credit for unmaintained safeguards; actions never closed.

    ⏹️ Shutdown Coordination

    Cracker & Polymer Plant Shutdown

    planningcoordinationcross-team coordinationprocess safety
    Definition
    Bringing units to a safe, depressured, hydrocarbon-free state (planned), or to a safe state quickly (emergency).
    Why it matters
    A cracker shutdown affects every downstream plant, the utility balance and flaring; cold sections need careful warm-up.
    Explanation
    Typical cracker sequence: coordinate downstream plants (ethylene/propylene storage or rate cuts) → reduce feed furnace by furnace → stop feed, furnaces to steam then cool → cold section: drain liquids to flare/storage, warm up at controlled rate (deriming) → depressure → N2 purge → positive isolation → gas test → hand over.     Planned, emergency (ESD), partial (one furnace, one polymer line), hot standby.

    Shutdown procedure and checklist, blind list, flare load plan, storage plan for monomers, N2 availability, communication plan.
    Process / workflow
    Pre-shutdown meeting → confirm storage/flare/N2 → execute with sign-offs → milestone communication → isolation and hand-over certificate.
    Petrochemical example
    The Coordinator confirms ethylene storage can supply the PE plants for 5 days while the cracker is down, that flare loads stay within permit during cold-section draining, and that extra N2 is booked.
    Common mistakes
    Warming cold equipment too fast; draining cold liquid to unrated lines; leaving polymer to freeze in the extruder.
    Best practices
    Checklists with sign-offs; milestone briefings; verify every blind against a numbered list.

    Order: N2 purge, reduce furnace feed, depressure, warm up cold section, gas test, positive isolation, stop feed.

    Startup Readiness & PSSR

    checklistsinspectionprocess safety
    Definition
    Confirming a unit is mechanically complete, safe and ready for hydrocarbons; the Pre-Startup Safety Review (PSSR) is the formal check after construction, modification or turnaround.

    🧭 Operational Decision Making

    Decision Making Under Pressure

    leadershipproblem-solvingprocess safety
    Definition
    Choosing to continue, adjust, reduce, kill or shut down when information is incomplete and time is short.
    Why it matters
    In reactive systems, hesitation is dangerous: the kill decision often has to be made within minutes.
    Explanation
    Fixed priority: people → environment → asset → production. Use pre-agreed trigger points (e.g. reactor temperature X → reduce catalyst; Y → kill) so the decision does not depend on emotion or production pressure. Killing a reactor costs off-grade and restart time; a runaway can cost lives.
    Types
    Routine, tactical (rate/severity), strategic (shutdown, feed change), emergency (kill, ESD).
    Components
    Facts, options, risks, authority level, communication.
    Process / workflow
    Assess → list options including “stop” → evaluate → decide at the right level → communicate → monitor triggers.
    Petrochemical example
    Gas-phase reactor bed temperature rises despite reduced catalyst feed; a customer shipment depends on this grade. Expert decision: kill the reactor at the agreed trigger — the shipment delay is a commercial loss, not a safety one.
    Common mistakes
    “One more minute” thinking; wrong authority level; no defined triggers.
    Best practices

    🏭 Real-World Petrochemical Scenarios

    Scenario A · Mercury found in a new condensate feed
    Situation: Lab finds mercury in a newly received condensate cargo.
    Expert response: Hold the tank; check mercury guard-bed capacity; blend down only within agreed limits; inform the integrity team (aluminium cold-box risk); add mercury to feed acceptance criteria via MOC; consider rejecting the cargo.
    Scenario B · Acetylene breakthrough at night
    Situation: Acetylene in ethylene rises to 3 ppm (spec <1 ppm) at 02:00.
    Expert response: Divert ethylene away from PE plants per procedure; inform PE boards; raise converter inlet temperature in small steps while watching for runaway; check CO and H2 ratio; if catalyst end-of-life, plan regeneration/swing reactor; RCA in the morning.
    Scenario C · CGC trip and steam balance collapse
    Situation: CGC trips; furnaces still on feed; flare load climbing.
    Expert response: Cut furnace feed immediately; furnaces to steam standby; manage flare (steam to tip, KO drum level); protect cold section; boilers to make up turbine steam; PE plants switch to storage ethylene; restart only after the trip cause is known.
    Scenario D · Sheeting in a gas-phase PE reactor
    Situation: Skin thermocouples show cold/hot spots, static rising, product chunks in discharge.
    Expert response: Reduce catalyst feed, check static control additive and cycle gas, prepare kill; if temperatures keep rising, kill and shut down; plan reactor inspection and cleaning; RCA on static causes (grade, impurities, additive).

    🧭 Fundamentals

    What is the difference between a refinery and a petrochemical plant?
    A refinery mainly produces fuels from crude oil. A petrochemical plant converts feedstocks such as naphtha, ethane and LPG into chemical building blocks (ethylene, propylene, aromatics) and derivatives such as polymers, with much tighter purity requirements.
    Why do ethane crackers produce less propylene than naphtha crackers?
    Ethane cracks mainly to ethylene and hydrogen. Heavier feeds like naphtha break into a wider range of molecules, giving significant propylene, C4s and pyrolysis gasoline.
    What is dilution steam and why is it used?
    Steam mixed with the feed before cracking. It lowers hydrocarbon partial pressure, which improves olefin selectivity and slows coke formation.
    What does “polymer grade” mean?
    Monomer pure enough for polymerization — typically ethylene ≥ 99.9% and propylene ≥ 99.5%, with ppm-level limits on acetylene, CO, CO2, water, oxygen and sulfur, which poison catalysts.
    What is the role of P&IDs in your daily work?
    They show every line, valve, instrument, interlock and relief path. I use them for line-ups, isolations, troubleshooting, permits and to check that the field matches the design.

    ⚙️ Process & Equipment

    How do you know a cracking furnace needs decoking?
    Rising tube metal temperatures toward the IOW limit, rising coil pressure drop and rising TLE outlet temperature. When any reaches its end-of-run criterion, the furnace is taken off feed and decoked with steam and air.
    Why must cracked gas be dried before the cold section?
    Water would freeze and form hydrates at cryogenic temperatures, blocking exchangers and columns and forcing a warm-up (derime).
    What happens in the acetylene converter and what is its main hazard?
    Acetylene is selectively hydrogenated to ethylene over a palladium catalyst. Too much hydrogen or temperature hydrogenates ethylene too, which is strongly exothermic and can cause a temperature runaway.
    How is polymer MFI controlled?
    Mainly by the hydrogen-to-monomer ratio (hydrogen acts as a chain-transfer agent); reactor temperature and catalyst type also affect it. Density is mainly controlled by comonomer content.
    What is compressor surge and how is it prevented?
    Flow reversal in a centrifugal compressor at low flow against high pressure, causing violent vibration. Anti-surge control opens a recycle valve to keep flow above the surge line.

    🛡️ Safety, Quality & Interfaces

    What is a reactor kill system?
    A system that injects a catalyst poison (e.g. CO) into a polymerization reactor to stop the reaction quickly in an emergency such as loss of cooling or circulation. It is a safety-critical element and must be proof-tested.
    What lessons do Flixborough and Pasadena teach?
    Flixborough (1974): a temporary bypass installed without proper engineering review failed — the basis of MOC. Pasadena (1989): a release during maintenance on a PE reactor leg — isolations must be positive, valve actuators locked out, and hose connections designed so they cannot be reversed.
    How do you handle off-spec ethylene going to the PE plant?
    Inform the PE plant immediately, divert or limit ethylene per the agreed procedure, find and fix the cause upstream, confirm with analyser and lab, then record and investigate.
    What is a grade transition and how do you reduce off-grade?
    Changing a polymer line to a new grade. Reduce off-grade with a campaign wheel of neighbouring grades, standard recipes, soft-sensor control and fast QC feedback.
    How do you prepare a cryogenic system for maintenance?
    Drain liquids to a rated closed system, warm up at controlled rates, depressure, purge with nitrogen, positively isolate, gas test, and only then issue the permit — always checking minimum design metal temperatures on drain and vent paths.

    🏆 Advanced & Expert

    How would you choose cracking severity?
    Using product values (ethylene vs propylene), feed, furnace TMT and run-length limits, CGC and refrigeration constraints, and downstream demand. Higher severity gives more ethylene but faster coking and more fuel.
    Catalyst productivity has fallen slowly for weeks. How do you investigate?
    Compare trends with feed source, catalyst lot and process changes; check trace impurities (water, CO, arsine, COS, oxygen) at ppb level and guard-bed performance; involve the lab and catalyst vendor; test hypotheses one at a time.
    When would you kill a polymerization reactor?
    When pre-agreed triggers are reached — loss of circulation or cooling, rapid temperature rise that does not respond to reduced catalyst, or other procedure criteria. Safety outranks production; I don't wait for “one more minute”.
    Which KPIs would you track for an olefins and polymer complex?
    Ethylene and propylene production, yields and P/E ratio, furnace run length, specific energy consumption, availability and trips, off-grade %, transition time, catalyst productivity, flaring, IOW exceedances and process safety events.

    • C Reduce hydrogen/inlet temperature per procedure, prepare to isolate the reactor if it keeps rising, inform the supervisor and PE plants
    • D Raise the cracker feed rate
    ✍️ Explain why the temperature is rising even though acetylene is already very low.
    Model answer: With acetylene already removed, excess hydrogen reacts with ethylene (ethylene → ethane), which is strongly exothermic. More heat raises the reaction rate further — the start of a runaway. The likely trigger is the hydrogen control valve after maintenance (wrong calibration/positioner). Reduce H2, check the valve, and follow the converter runaway procedure.

    2️⃣ Scenario 2 · Equipment Failure

    Situation: The propylene refrigeration compressor trips on high axial displacement. Cold-section temperatures begin rising; ethylene purity starts falling.
    Q. What should the board team do first?
    • A Cut furnace feed quickly per procedure, route off-spec ethylene to flare, protect the cold section and inform downstream plants
    • B Restart the compressor immediately without investigation
    • C Keep full feed and wait for purity to recover
    • D Increase C2 splitter reflux only

    Model answer: Review trip data (axial displacement, vibration, thrust bearing temperatures); inspect thrust bearing and lube oil (metal particles, pressure); check seal gas and KO drum levels; verify instruments; engineering/rotating equipment approval; restart per procedure on recycle with close monitoring.

    3️⃣ Scenario 3 · Process Deviation

    Situation: Furnace F-105 pass 2 TMT is 1,060 °C (IOW standard limit 1,050 °C; critical 1,080 °C). Pass 2 feed flow is 6% lower than the other passes. Run day 38 of an expected 45.
    Q. What is the most appropriate response?
    • A Ignore — below the critical limit
    • B Raise COT to finish the run faster
    • C Disable the TMT alarm
    • D Record the IOW exceedance, rebalance pass flow, lower firing/COT on that furnace, inform engineering and the Planner to bring the decoke forward if needed
    ✍️ Why does exceeding TMT limits matter even if the tube doesn't fail today?
    Model answer: Creep and carburisation damage accumulate with time at temperature; each excursion consumes tube life, leading to earlier bulging, cracking or rupture and costly coil replacement. IOWs exist to keep damage rates predictable.

    4️⃣ Scenario 4 · Product Quality Issue

    Situation: PE line 1 is making a film grade (MFI target 1.0 ± 0.1). Lab MFI results: 1.08, 1.14, 1.19 over 6 hours. Pellets are going to prime silo S-04.
    Q. What is the first action?
    • A Keep filling S-04; blending will fix it
    • B Divert pellets to the off-grade silo, verify the H₂ analyser and reduce H₂/C2 ratio, then resample
    • C Increase catalyst feed
    • D Shut down the line
    ✍️ As Quality Engineer, outline the RCA and CAPA.
    Model answer: Problem: MFI drift above spec for 6 h, X tonnes affected. Data: H2 analyser trend vs lab, H2 flow, catalyst lot, reactor temperature. Root cause (example): H2 GC drifting low, so the controller added more hydrogen. Correction: re-blend/downgrade affected lots. Corrective: recalibrate GC, add daily validation. Preventive: alarm on soft-sensor vs lab deviation. Verify: no MFI excursions for 3 months.

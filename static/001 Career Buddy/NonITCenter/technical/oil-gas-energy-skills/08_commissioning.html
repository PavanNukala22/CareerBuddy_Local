    Commissioning Fundamentals & the 10 Roles

    Start from zero: where Commissioning sits in Oil & Gas / Energy Skills, what its ten roles do, which skills each role needs, and the words every commissioning conversation uses.

    🧭 What is the Commissioning department?

    Oil & Gas / Energy Skills is a skill group made up of 9 departments. Commissioning is one of them. A new or modified plant is not ready just because construction is finished. Every pipe, instrument, motor, valve and control loop must be checked, tested, run and proven before the plant is handed to operations and hydrocarbons or steam are introduced. The department has 10 roles, from Engineer to Analyst. This course teaches what each role does, which skills it needs and how the roles work together safely.

    📐
    Define the tests
    Engineers and planners decide what is tested, in what order and to what limits.
    🔌
    Test and run
    Technicians energise, flush, test and run systems step by step.
    🔍
    Check and certify
    Inspectors and quality engineers confirm records and sign completion.
    📅
    Plan and control
    Supervisors, coordinators, planners and analysts keep time, safety and progress on track.
    EXAMPLE · One pump, four people

    An Engineer sets the test limits for a new export pump. A Technician checks rotation, aligns it and runs it. An Inspector checks the installation against the drawing and signs the record. An Analyst later reports how many pumps passed first time. One pump, four roles.

    🧰 The Oil & Gas / Energy Skills group

    Commissioning sits inside a group of 9 departments that serve plants that process or generate energy. It is the bridge between construction and operations. These terms are often mixed up, so learn them first.

    TermMeaningTypical timing
    ConstructionBuilding and installing the plant to drawingsBefore commissioning
    Pre-commissioningStatic checks with no process fluid: cleaning, flushing, pressure and leak tests, loop checks, electrical testsAfter mechanical completion of a system
    CommissioningDynamic checks: energising, running equipment, functional tests, often with a safe test fluidAfter pre-commissioning
    Start-upIntroducing process fluid and bringing the plant to normal operationAfter commissioning
    Performance testProof that the plant meets guaranteed capacity and qualityAfter stable operation
    HandoverTransfer of the system and its records to operationsLast step
    💡
    Commissioning is done system by system, not area by area. A system is a group of equipment that does one job, such as the instrument-air system or the gas compression system.
    EXAMPLE · Which word fits?

    Flushing a new pipe with water is pre-commissioning. Running a new motor on its own for the first time is commissioning. Opening the gas valve and bringing the unit to rate is start-up.

    Commissioning Lifecycle & Completions

    How a plant is split into systems, planned, certified, tested and handed over, with the numbers and documents used at each step.

    🏭 Why commissioning matters

    Most serious incidents at start-up come from something that was not checked, not finished or not understood. Commissioning finds these problems while the plant is still safe to fix.

    ReasonWhat it preventsExample
    Proves installationPlant built differently from the drawingA valve fitted backwards is found at the check
    Proves safety systemsSafety functions that fail on demandEmergency shutdown valve tested to close in time
    Proves controlSignals that do not reach the control roomLoop check finds a swapped cable
    Proves machineryDamage on first runMisaligned pump shows high vibration on a test run
    Proves performancePlant that cannot meet contract outputPerformance test shows capacity below guarantee
    Builds operator readinessOperators meeting the plant for the first time at start-upTraining and procedures checked before handover
    EXAMPLE · Cheap now, costly later

    A mislabelled instrument found at a loop check takes 20 minutes to fix. The same mistake found during start-up could trip the plant or hide a real alarm. Commissioning is the cheapest place to find problems.

    🧩 Systemisation: splitting the plant into systems

    A plant is divided into systems (and subsystems) with clear boundaries marked on P&IDs. Each system is completed, tested and handed over as one unit.

    TermMeaningExample
    SystemEquipment doing one function, with clear boundariesInstrument air, cooling water, gas compression
    SubsystemSmaller part for testingOne compressor and its lube-oil package
    BoundaryWhere one system ends and the next begins; shown by a red line on P&IDsA closed valve or blind between systems
    Utilities firstPower, air, water and nitrogen are commissioned before the process systems that need themInstrument air must work before valves can be stroked
    📌
    Systems that supply others (utilities) are done first. A late utility holds up everything that depends on it, so it is almost always on the critical path.
    EXAMPLE · Boundary question

    Is a pressure relief valve part of the compressor system or the flare system? The red-line P&ID decides. Clear boundaries stop items falling between two systems and being missed.

    🚦 Completion gates and certificates

    Commissioning is controlled by gates. A system may not move to the next stage until the gate certificate is signed.

    Certificates are signed in order; no gate may be skipped.
    GateWhat it confirmsUsually signed by
    Mechanical completionInstallation finished, inspected and records complete, except agreed punch itemsConstruction, Inspector, commissioning lead
    Pre-commissioning completeCleaning, flushing, pressure tests and loop checks doneCommissioning lead, Inspector
    Ready for commissioningSystem safe to energise or runCommissioning lead and operations
    Ready for start-upProcess fluid may be introduced; PSSR doneOperations manager and safety lead
    HandoverSystem and records formally transferred to operationsCommissioning lead and operations
    🚩
    A gate is a safety control. Skipping a certificate to save time puts people at risk and often costs more time later when problems appear during start-up.
    EXAMPLE · A gate blocks a start

    The gas system is ready except for two safety-valve test records. The Coordinator marks the gate not signed. The Inspector completes the records the same day, and the gate is signed that evening.

    📋 ITRs and punch lists

    An ITR (inspection and test record) is a signed sheet that proves one check was done. A system may have hundreds. A punch list records items still to fix.

    Punch categoryMeaningRule
    ASafety or operability items; must be fixed before commissioning or start-upBlocks the gate
    BMust be fixed before handover, but does not block start-upClosed within an agreed time
    CMinor or cosmetic itemsClosed after handover if agreed
    EXAMPLE · Counting progress

    System 24 has 120 ITRs. 90 are signed, so it is 90 ÷ 120 = 75% complete by count. Loops are tracked the same way: 1,840 of 2,300 checked is 1,840 ÷ 2,300 = 80%.

    💡
    Weight progress by effort, not just by count. A flush that takes two days is worth more than a tag check that takes five minutes.

    📈 The commissioning plan and critical path

    The plan sequences systems so that each has what it needs. It is usually built in scheduling software such as Primavera P6, with durations, links and resources.

    IdeaPlain meaningExample
    ActivityOne test or step with a durationHydrotest line L-101: 2 days
    Logic linkWhich step must finish before another can startCannot run a pump before flushing its suction line
    Critical pathLongest chain of linked steps; sets the end datePower → instrument air → control system → process systems
    FloatSpare time before the end date is affectedA cooling-water test with 5 days of float
    BaselineThe approved plan used to measure progressPlan frozen at construction handover
    EXAMPLE · Finding the critical path

    Chain A: power energised (5 d) → instrument air (4 d) → control system checks (6 d) → compressor run (5 d) = 20 days. Chain B: flush (3 d) → hydrotest (2 d) → dry (3 d) = 8 days. Chain A is critical. Chain B has 12 days of float.

    👷 People, vendors, spares and procedures

    Commissioning needs special people and materials as well as time.

    ResourceWhat must be plannedCommon problem

    Vendor representativesExperts for compressors, turbines, control systemsVendor not booked when the system is ready
    Commissioning sparesGaskets, filters, seals, chemicalsMissing spare stops a test
    ProceduresApproved step-by-step test and start-up proceduresProcedure not approved in time

    EXAMPLE · Temporary items

    Temporary start-up strainers are fitted to protect a compressor. A register lists each one, who fitted it and when it must be removed. At start-up nothing is left behind by mistake.

    💰 Progress, cost and change

    Progress and cost are tracked daily against the baseline.

    MeasureFormulaMeaning
    Schedule performance index (SPI)Earned value ÷ planned valueBelow 1.0 means behind schedule
    Cost performance index (CPI)Earned value ÷ actual costBelow 1.0 means over budget
    Percent completeWeighted work done ÷ total workWeighted by effort
    Loop-check rateLoops checked ÷ loops plannedShows instrument progress
    EXAMPLE · Reading the numbers

    By day 30 the plan says 50% of ITRs should be signed; 40% are signed. SPI = 40 ÷ 50 = 0.8. Earned value is 800,000 and actual cost is 1,000,000, so CPI = 0.8. Behind and over budget; the team checks the critical path and the largest cost drivers.

    💡
    Every change to procedure, design or boundary goes through management of change: request, risk check, approval and update of documents.

    Pre-commissioning, Commissioning & Safety

    The tests, safety controls and sequences used to bring a new system safely from construction to start-up.

    🛡️ Safety during commissioning

    Commissioning is one of the most hazardous phases of a project. Construction and commissioning crews share the site, equipment is energised for the first time, and process fluid may be introduced.

    HazardExampleControl
    EnergisationFirst power to a switchboard or motorEnergisation permit, tested dead then live-checked, barriers, authorised persons only
    Stored energyPressure testing, compressed gasExclusion zone, calibrated gauges, trained test crew
    HydrocarbonsFirst gas into the systemPurge, leak test, gas detection, PSSR, controlled steps
    Construction overlapConstruction crews working next to a commissioned systemClear boundary, handover of the area, permits for both
    Rotating equipmentFirst run of a pump or compressorGuards, procedure, standby person, emergency stop tested
    Confined spaces and heightEntering vessels, using scaffoldPermit, gas test, rescue plan, tagged scaffold
    ⚠️
    Once a system is energised or has fluid in it, treat it as live. Hold the line between construction and commissioning with signs, barriers, permits and a clear handover of each area.
    EXAMPLE · Two crews, one area

    A construction crew is still welding near a system that has been handed to commissioning. A permit-to-work check shows hot work is not allowed near the nitrogen-purged line. The Coordinator moves the welding to another area.

    🔧 Pre-commissioning activities

    Pre-commissioning prepares equipment for first use. No process fluid is used.

    ActivityWhat it doesExample
    Cleaning / flushingRemoves dirt, weld spatter and debris with liquidWater flush until the outlet is clean
    Air blowingClears lines with compressed air or steamInstrument-air line blown clear
    HydrotestProves strength and tightness with waterLine tested at 1.5 × design pressure
    DryingRemoves water so it cannot cause corrosion or iceDried to a set dew point such as −20 °C
    Lube-oil flushingCleans oil systems before machinery runsCompressor lube-oil circuit flushed until filters stay clean
    CalibrationSets instruments to known accuracyPressure transmitter checked at several points
    Electrical checksInsulation, continuity and protection settingsMegger test on a motor cable
    EXAMPLE · Why flush first?

    Weld spatter and debris from construction are left in new pipe. If flushing is skipped, debris can damage a pump seal in the first hour. Flushing is cheap protection.

    📏 Pressure testing and leak testing

    Pressure tests prove a system holds pressure. The test type depends on the code and the system.

    TestWhat it isTypical value
    HydrotestWater-filled pressure test to prove strengthProcess piping code often requires 1.5 × design pressure (before temperature correction)
    Pneumatic testGas-pressurised test where water cannot be usedOften 1.1 × design pressure under piping code; higher stored-energy risk
    Tightness (leak) testLow-pressure test of joints, often with nitrogenHeld for a set time with a soap or gas check
    Service testCheck at operating pressure using process fluidDone in controlled steps at start-up
    EXAMPLE · Test pressure arithmetic

    A line has a design pressure of 20 bar. Hydrotest at 1.5 × is 30 bar. A pneumatic test at 1.1 × is 22 bar. Both use more care than normal operation, and the area is cleared before pressure is raised.

    🚩
    Pneumatic tests store far more energy than water tests. A failure can throw debris. Use hydrotest where possible and follow strict exclusion zones for any gas test.

    ⚡ Electrical and instrument checks

    Electrical and instrument systems carry power and signals. Tests prove they are connected, safe and correct.

    CheckWhat it provesExample
    Insulation resistanceCable and motor insulation is soundReading must exceed the value in the specification
    Motor rotationMotor turns the right wayChecked uncoupled, before connecting the pump
    Protection relay testTrip settings workRelay trips breaker at set current
    Loop checkSignal path is correct end to endSimulate 4 mA, 12 mA and 20 mA and read 0%, 50%, 100%
    Valve stroke testValve opens and closes fully and in timeESD valve closes within its required time
    Cause-and-effect testSafety logic does what the matrix saysHigh-high level closes the inlet valve
    Fire and gas testDetectors alarm and trigger actionsGas detector test alarms and starts the response
    EXAMPLE · Loop-check maths

    A level transmitter is ranged 0–10 m on a 4–20 mA signal. At 12 mA the control system should read 50% (5 m), because (12 − 4) ÷ 16 = 0.5. If it reads 56%, there is a scaling or wiring error.

    💡
    Safety functions are tested to prove they work on demand, not just that they are wired. Each safety test has a signed record.

    ⚙️ Rotating equipment commissioning

    Pumps, compressors and turbines are expensive and easily damaged on first run. A careful sequence protects them.

    StepWhat is doneWhy
    Installation checkLevel, grouting, alignment, piping strainPrevents vibration and damage
    Lube and seal systemsFlush and run oil and seal-gas systemsMachinery needs clean lubrication
    Uncoupled motor runRun motor alone, check rotation and currentAvoids damage from wrong rotation
    Alignment checkLaser or dial check after piping is connectedPiping can pull the machine out of line
    Coupled run-inRun in stages while watching vibration and temperatureFinds problems while still at low load
    Trip testsCheck protective trips such as low oil or high vibrationProves protection works
    EXAMPLE · Rotation first

    A new pump motor is run uncoupled. It turns the wrong way. Two phases are swapped and rotation is corrected. If the pump had been coupled first, the impeller could have been damaged.

    🌫️ Purging and introducing hydrocarbons

    Before flammable gas enters a system, the air must be removed so a flammable mixture cannot form. This is a controlled, step-by-step process.

    Air is removed first; gas is introduced in controlled steps.
    StepWhat is checkedWhy
    Nitrogen purgeOxygen reading at the vent pointsRemove oxygen so no flammable mix forms
    Oxygen limitOxygen below the procedure limit, often a few percent or lessSafe to introduce gas
    Controlled admissionSlow introduction through a defined routeAvoids pressure surges
    Stepwise pressure-upPause at set pressures to check for leaksFinds leaks while energy is low
    Gas detectionPortable and fixed detectors activeEarly warning of any leak
    🚨
    Nitrogen is an asphyxiation hazard. Do not enter vents, vessels or low points where nitrogen may collect. Gas-test and use permits before any entry.
    EXAMPLE · Stop at each step

    At 5 bar a flange weeps gas. The crew stops, depressures, re-makes the joint and restarts the pressure-up from the last safe step. Because the leak was found at low pressure, the risk was small.

    🎯 Performance and reliability tests

    Once the plant is running, tests prove it meets its contract.

    TestWhat it provesExample
    Performance guarantee testCapacity, quality and efficiency meet contract valuesTrain produces its guaranteed gas rate for a set period
    Reliability runPlant runs stably without trips72-hour continuous run at rate
    Operability checksOperators can start, stop and respond to upsetsManual and automatic start-up sequences
    Emissions and environmental checksLegal limits are metFlare and stack readings recorded
    EXAMPLE · Reading a guarantee

    The contract says 100% rate for 72 hours with no trip. At hour 50 the plant trips on low suction pressure. The clock restarts after the cause is found and fixed. Records show why, and the Senior Engineer approves the fix.

    🧭 Fundamentals

    What is the Commissioning department, and where does it sit?
    It is one of the nine departments in Oil & Gas / Energy Skills. It has ten roles (Engineer, Senior Engineer, Project Engineer, Supervisor, Coordinator, Planner, Quality Engineer, Inspector, Technician, Analyst) and all share three skills: technical knowledge, problem-solving and documentation.
    What is the difference between pre-commissioning, commissioning and start-up?
    Pre-commissioning is static checking with no process fluid: flushing, pressure tests, loop checks. Commissioning is dynamic testing such as running equipment. Start-up introduces process fluid and brings the plant to rate.
    Why is commissioning done system by system?
    A system does one job and can be tested, certified and handed over as a unit. It gives clear boundaries, clear responsibility and safe progress.
    What is mechanical completion?
    The stage where construction of a system is finished and checked, with records signed, except for agreed punch items.
    What is an ITR?
    An inspection and test record: a signed sheet that proves one check or test was done and what the result was.
    What are punch list categories A, B and C?
    A: safety or operability items that must be fixed before commissioning or start-up. B: must be fixed before handover. C: minor items that can be closed later.
    What is the critical path?
    The longest chain of linked steps. Any delay on it delays the end date. In commissioning it often runs through utilities.
    Name the three skills in all ten roles, and why.
    Technical knowledge, problem-solving and documentation. Knowledge keeps tests correct, problem-solving fixes failures and documentation proves the work.

    📐 Engineering roles

    What does an Engineer do in commissioning?
    Writes test procedures, sets limits, defines boundaries, calculates expected results and updates red-line drawings.
    How do you check a 4–20 mA loop reading?
    12 mA is the midpoint: (12 − 4) ÷ 16 = 50%. On a 0–10 m range it should read 5 m. A different reading points to scaling or wiring errors.
    What does a Senior Engineer add?
    Advanced analysis of machinery and control behaviour, design review, troubleshooting of repeat failures and leadership.
    What is the purpose of an uncoupled motor run?
    To check rotation and motor health without risking the driven pump or compressor.
    What does a Project Engineer own?
    A package such as a compressor train: vendors, spares, drawings, dates, cost and close-out.
    How do you handle a procedure change during testing?
    Use management of change: assess risk, get approval, update the documents and brief the crew.

    👷 Site, planning and coordination roles

    What does a Supervisor check before a test?
    Permit valid, boundaries and isolations in place, previous gate signed, crew briefed and qualified, test instruments calibrated.
    What does a Coordinator do all day?
    Runs daily meetings, keeps certificate and punch registers, manages area handovers and resolves clashes between teams.
    What is a completion gate?
    A certificate that must be signed before a system moves to the next stage, such as ready for commissioning or ready for start-up.
    What does a Planner produce?
    The system-based plan, the baseline, resource plan, critical path and daily progress updates.
    Why are utilities commissioned first?
    Other systems depend on power, instrument air, cooling water and nitrogen. If a utility is late, everything after it is late.
    How do you recover a late critical system?
    Check logic for overlaps, add vendor support or shifts, remove waiting time, and agree changes with the team.

    ✅ Quality and inspection roles

    What is an inspection and test plan (ITP)?
    A document that lists checks, methods, acceptance limits and hold and witness points for a system.
    What is a hold point?
    A step where work must stop until the inspector or authority signs it off.
    What does a Quality Engineer do after repeated failures?
    Records the non-conformance, finds the root cause, defines corrective and preventive action, checks it works and closes with evidence.
    How does an Inspector sign an ITR?
    After seeing the work or test, comparing against the specification, recording readings and signing and dating the record.
    Why must test instruments be calibrated?
    An uncalibrated gauge can show a false pass or fail. Calibration tags are checked before every use.
    What would you do if a record was signed without the test being done?
    Raise it at once, stop reliance on the record, repeat the test, record an NCR and find the cause.

    🔧 Technician and analyst roles

    What is the sequence for hydrocarbon introduction?
    Leak-test with nitrogen, purge air, confirm oxygen below the limit, introduce gas in controlled steps, raise pressure in stages and check for leaks at each step.
    Why is nitrogen dangerous?
    It displaces oxygen and can cause rapid collapse without warning. Entry needs air restoration, gas tests and a permit.
    How do you calculate hydrotest pressure?
    A common rule for process piping is 1.5 × design pressure (before temperature correction). For 20 bar design: 30 bar. Always follow the governing code.
    What KPIs does an Analyst track?
    SPI, CPI, ITRs signed, loops checked, punch burn-down, first-time pass rate and productivity.
    What is SPI and CPI?
    SPI = earned value ÷ planned value; CPI = earned value ÷ actual cost. Below 1.0 means behind schedule or over budget.
    What is a PSSR?
    A pre-start-up safety review: the last safety gate before hazardous fluid is introduced.

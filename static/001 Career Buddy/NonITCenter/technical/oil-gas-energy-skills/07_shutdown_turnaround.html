    Shutdown & Turnaround Fundamentals & the 10 Roles

    Start from zero: where Shutdown & Turnaround sits in Oil & Gas / Energy Skills, what its ten roles do, which skills each role needs, and the words every turnaround conversation uses.

    🧭 What is the Shutdown & Turnaround department?

    Oil & Gas / Energy Skills is a skill group made up of 9 departments. Shutdown & Turnaround is one of them. Refineries, gas plants, petrochemical units and power stations cannot be fully inspected or repaired while they run. So the plant is stopped on purpose, made safe, opened up, inspected, repaired and restarted. The department has 10 roles, from Engineer to Analyst. This course teaches what each role does, which skills it needs, and how the roles work together safely.

    📐
    Define the work
    Engineers and planners decide what must be done and in what order.
    🔧
    Open, repair, restore
    Technicians and crews isolate, open, repair and close equipment.
    🔍
    Inspect and prove
    Inspectors and quality engineers check that work meets the specification.
    📅
    Plan and control
    Supervisors, coordinators, planners and analysts keep time, cost and safety on track.
    EXAMPLE · One heat exchanger, four people

    An Engineer decides a leaking exchanger needs a new tube bundle. A Technician isolates it, pulls the old bundle and fits the new one. An Inspector checks the tube sheet and witnesses the pressure test. An Analyst later reports how many exchangers needed unplanned work. One exchanger, four roles.

    🧰 The Oil & Gas / Energy Skills group

    Shutdown & Turnaround sits inside a group of 9 departments that all serve plants that process or generate energy. Its work touches every other department, because a turnaround is the one time most equipment is opened at once. People often mix up the words below, so learn them first.

    TermMeaningTypical length and frequency
    Turnaround (TAR)Large planned stop of a whole unit or plant for major inspection, maintenance and upgradesWeeks; often every 3 to 6 years
    ShutdownAny planned stop of equipment or a unit. Used as a general word, and also for smaller stopsDays to weeks
    OutageThe word power plants use for a planned stop of a generating unitDays to months
    Mini-shutdownShort planned stop for one defined job such as a valve or heat-exchanger repair1 to 7 days
    Emergency shutdown (ESD)Unplanned fast stop caused by a fault or hazard, usually by the safety systemUnplanned
    Routine maintenanceWork done while the plant is runningDaily and weekly
    💡
    In this course turnaround and shutdown are used together. The roles and skills are the same; only the size and the local word change.
    EXAMPLE · Which word fits?

    A refinery stops its crude unit for 35 days to clean furnaces, inspect vessels and replace exchanger bundles. That is a turnaround. A gas plant stops for 3 days to replace one compressor valve. That is a mini-shutdown. A power unit stopped for two months for boiler and turbine work is an outage.

    Turnaround Lifecycle, Scope & Planning

    How a shutdown is scoped, planned, scheduled, resourced and closed out, with the numbers and documents used at each step.

    🏭 Why plants shut down

    A plant runs for years between turnarounds. Some parts wear out slowly and cannot be reached while the unit is running. A shutdown is expensive because the plant stops earning, so every day must count.

    ReasonWhat it meansExample
    Statutory or code inspectionLaw or the inspection code sets a date for internal inspectionOpening a pressure vessel for its internal inspection
    Wear and foulingDeposits and corrosion reduce performanceCleaning a heat exchanger bundle that has lost heat transfer
    Catalyst lifeCatalyst loses activity and must be replacedReactor catalyst change-out
    Reliability workRisk-based inspection or failure history says a part is dueReplacing a thinning elbow before it leaks
    ModificationsNew equipment or tie-ins that need the unit emptyInstalling a new heater and tying it into live lines
    Regulation and safety upgradesNew rules or safety studies require changesUpgrading relief valves after a hazard study
    EXAMPLE · Not everything can wait

    A furnace tube shows thinning at the last inspection. The unit cannot be stopped now, so the repair is added to the next turnaround scope. If the tube thins faster, the plan changes. Turnarounds are planned months ahead but stay flexible.

    🎯 Building and freezing the scope

    Scope is the list of jobs. Getting it right early is the biggest single factor in a good turnaround. Late scope is the biggest cause of delay and overspend.

    Scope typeWhat goes in itCan it be dropped?
    MandatoryStatutory inspections, safety and regulatory jobsNo
    ReliabilityJobs from inspection findings and risk-based inspectionOnly after a risk review
    Capital projectsNew equipment and modificationsSometimes moved to another stop
    DiscretionaryNice-to-have improvementsYes, first to go if time or money is short
    EmergentNew work found after opening equipmentMust be judged case by case
    Scope is collected, challenged, frozen and then controlled.
    🚩
    Scope freeze matters. After the freeze date, a new job needs a formal change request, cost and schedule impact, and approval. Without this, scope grows quietly and the end date moves.
    EXAMPLE · Scope growth in numbers

    The frozen scope has 5,000 jobs. By the end, 6,000 jobs were done. Scope growth is (6,000 − 5,000) ÷ 5,000 = 20%. Emergent work this high usually means poor pre-shutdown inspection.

    📦 Work packs and readiness

    A work pack gives a crew everything needed to do one job without waiting. If the pack is incomplete, crews stand idle on a shutdown day that costs thousands.

    Work pack itemWhat it containsWhy it matters
    Scope of workClear description of what to do and what not to doPrevents misunderstanding
    Drawings and datasheetsP&IDs, isometrics, equipment drawingsShows what the equipment is
    ProcedureStep-by-step method, including bolting and test stepsMakes work repeatable
    Materials listSpares, gaskets, bolts, consumables with stock numbersParts must be on site before the stop
    Isolation planWhich valves, blinds and locks are neededKeeps the crew safe
    Manpower and toolsTrades, hours, special tools, cranes, scaffoldLets the planner load resources
    Inspection requirementsHold and witness points, test method, acceptance limitsDefines when work is accepted
    EXAMPLE · Ready or not?

    Pack for exchanger E-101 lacks a gasket stock number. The Planner marks it not ready and chases the store. A common target is that most packs are ready weeks before the shutdown starts, with a readiness check at a set date.

    📈 The schedule and the critical path

    The schedule shows which job happens when. It is usually built in scheduling software such as Primavera P6, with job durations, links between jobs and resources.

    IdeaPlain meaningExample
    ActivityOne job with a durationOpen column manway: 1 day
    Logic linkWhich job must finish before another can startCannot enter a vessel before gas testing
    Critical pathThe longest chain of linked jobs. Any delay on it delays the whole shutdownColumn opening → inspection → repair → box-up
    FloatSpare time before a job delays the end dateA pump overhaul with 6 days of float
    BaselineThe approved plan used to measure progressFrozen schedule on freeze date
    Look-aheadDetailed plan for the next 24 to 72 hoursTomorrow’s jobs by crew
    EXAMPLE · Finding the critical path

    Chain A: isolate (2 d) → open vessel (1 d) → clean (4 d) → inspect (2 d) → repair (5 d) → box-up (2 d) = 16 days. Chain B: pull bundle (1 d) → clean (2 d) → retube (4 d) → refit (2 d) = 9 days. Chain A is critical: the shutdown cannot finish before day 16. Chain B has 7 days of float.

    📌
    The critical path can change. If chain B’s retubing grows to 12 days, chain B becomes the new critical path. Planners recheck it every day.

    👷 Resources, materials and contractors

    A turnaround may bring hundreds or thousands of extra workers to a site that normally has far fewer. This needs planning for people, materials and space.

    ResourceWhat must be plannedCommon problem
    ManpowerTrades, numbers per shift, peak load, induction and permitsToo many workers at the same place at once
    Long-lead materialsItems that take months to arrive: exchanger bundles, special valves, alloy pipeLate delivery stops the job
    ContractorsScope split, rates, safety standards, supervisionUnclear scope leads to claims
    Scaffold and accessScaffold built before the stop so work starts on day oneLate scaffold delays opening
    Cranes and liftsLift plans, crane slots, laydown spaceTwo lifts need the same area
    Site logisticsLaydown, parking, welfare, waste, transportCongestion and queues
    EXAMPLE · Manpower histogram

    The plan shows 400 workers on day 1, 1,200 at the peak around day 12, and 300 by day 30. The Planner checks that the site can induct, feed and supervise 1,200 people. If it cannot, the plan is re-levelled.

    💰 Cost control and change

    A turnaround budget has jobs, contractor costs, materials, contingency and a cost for each day the plant is down. Costs are tracked against the budget every day.

    MeasureFormulaMeaning
    Schedule performance index (SPI)Earned value ÷ planned valueBelow 1.0 means behind schedule
    Cost performance index (CPI)Earned value ÷ actual costBelow 1.0 means over budget
    Percent completeWeighted work done ÷ total workWeighted by hours, not job count
    Cost varianceEarned value − actual costNegative means overspend
    EXAMPLE · Reading the numbers

    By day 10 the plan says 40% should be done. Only 36% is done, so SPI = 36 ÷ 40 = 0.9. Earned value is 900,000 and actual cost is 1,000,000, so CPI = 0.9. Behind schedule and over budget: the team looks at the critical path and the biggest cost drivers.

    💡
    Every change goes through management of change: a request, a risk check, an approval and an update of the plan. This is true for scope, design and method.

    🏁 Close-out and lessons learned

    The shutdown is not over when the unit restarts. Records are completed, punch items closed and lessons recorded for the next one.

    Close-out itemWhat it isOwner

    Last turnaround, the column cleaning ran 3 days late because the hydro-jetting contractor arrived late. The lesson: book that contractor early and confirm equipment before the stop. This is written into the next plan as a milestone.

    Safety, Isolation & Inspection

    The safety systems and inspection methods that make a turnaround possible: isolation, gas testing, permits, test methods, corrosion rates and start-up checks.

    🔒 Permit to work and isolation

    A plant holds flammable, toxic and high-pressure material. Before anyone works on equipment, it must be separated from every source of energy and fluid and the job must be approved on a permit to work (PTW).

    Isolation moves from process stop to a gas-tested, permitted job.
    Isolation methodHow it worksStrength
    Blind / spadeSolid plate fitted between flangesStrongest: physical break in the line
    Double block and bleedTwo closed valves with a vented space betweenStrong: leak between valves shows at the vent
    Single valveOne closed valveWeakest: not accepted alone for entry or high-risk jobs
    Lock-out / tag-out (LOTO)Personal lock and tag on valves and electrical switchesPrevents accidental re-energising
    ⚠️
    A closed valve is not an isolation by itself. Valves can leak or be opened by mistake. Entry work uses blinds or double block and bleed, with proof tests, and every blind goes on the blind list.
    EXAMPLE · Reading a permit

    “Vessel V-201 manway entry. Isolated by blinds B-31 to B-34. Gas test: oxygen 20.9%, LEL 0%, H2S 0 ppm. Standby person at manway. Valid for one shift.” The permit lists what work, where, which controls, who checked and how long it lasts.

    🌫️ Gas testing and confined-space entry

    A confined space is a space not designed for people that has limited entry and exit, such as a vessel, tank, column or furnace box. Hazards can be invisible.

    CheckWhy it mattersTypical limit or action
    OxygenToo little causes collapse; too much increases fire riskAbout 19.5% to 23.5% for entry (site rules apply)
    Flammable gas (LEL)Gas or vapour can igniteReading must be at or near 0% for entry and hot work
    Toxic gas (H2S, CO, benzene)Poisoning, often without warningBelow the exposure limit; use detectors and breathing apparatus where needed
    Nitrogen purgeInert gas removes oxygen and can kill quicklyNever enter until air is restored and tested
    Pyrophoric iron sulfideDeposits can self-heat and ignite in airKeep wet until removed
    Rescue planRescue must be ready before entryStandby person outside, rescue team and equipment on hand
    🚨
    Never enter to rescue someone first. Many confined-space deaths are rescuers. Raise the alarm and let the trained rescue team act.
    EXAMPLE · Retesting

    A column was gas-free at 6:00. At 10:00, steam-out of a nearby line is under way. Before the crew enters, the gas is tested again. The reading shows 8% LEL. Entry is stopped, the cause is found, and a new test is needed before work restarts.

    🪜 Hot work, lifting and work at height

    Three high-risk activities appear on almost every turnaround. Each has its own permit or plan.

    ActivityMain hazardsTypical controls
    Hot workFire and explosion from sparks and heatHot-work permit, gas test, clear area, fire watch, extinguisher, cover drains
    LiftingDropped loads, crane failure, crushingLift plan, certified crane and slings, trained rigger, barriers under the load
    Work at heightFalls and falling objectsTagged scaffold, harness, edge protection, tool tethers
    ElectricalShock and arc flashLOTO, tested dead, approved tools
    High-pressure water jettingCuts and injection injuriesTrained operators, barriers, proper PPE, no hands near the jet
    ⚠️
    Fire watch continues after hot work stops. The site procedure sets the time, often 30 minutes or more. Sparks can smoulder in hidden places.
    EXAMPLE · Scaffold tags

    A green tag on a scaffold means inspected and safe to use. A red tag means do not use. A worker finds a missing handrail on a green-tagged platform, stops work and reports it. Stop-work authority belongs to everyone.

    🚧 SIMOPS and exclusion zones

    SIMOPS means simultaneous operations: different jobs happening near each other. During a turnaround, many crews share the same space. Some jobs are dangerous to people nearby.

    JobRisk to othersControl
    Radiography (RT)RadiationBarrier, warning signs and timed exclusion zone, often at night
    Heavy lift overheadDropped loadClosed area below, lift supervisor
    Hydrotest or pressure testStored energyExclusion zone, trained test crew, pressure gauge checked
    Hot work near open drainsFireDrains covered, area checked, fire watch
    High-pressure jettingInjury from sprayBarriers and signs
    EXAMPLE · The SIMOPS matrix

    A matrix shows which jobs may happen together. “Radiography” and “scaffold erection” in the same area are marked not allowed. The Coordinator uses the matrix to move radiography to night shift.

    🔍 Inspection methods used in turnarounds

    When equipment is opened, inspectors look for damage. Different damage needs different methods.

    MethodWhat it findsTypical use
    VT (visual)Surface damage, fouling, deformationFirst check on everything
    UT thicknessWall thickness loss from corrosion or erosionVessels, pipes, tanks
    UT flaw / TOFD / phased arrayInternal cracks and weld flawsWelds in pressure equipment
    RT (radiography)Internal flaws; film or digital imageWelds and corrosion under insulation checks
    MT (magnetic particle)Surface and near-surface cracksCarbon steel welds
    PT (dye penetrant)Surface cracksNon-magnetic metals and welds
    Eddy current / IRISTube wall loss and cracksHeat exchanger and boiler tubes
    PMI (positive material identification)Alloy gradeChecking new and existing alloy parts
    Hardness testingHeat-treatment problemsWelds and repaired areas
    Floor scanning (MFL)Corrosion on tank floorsStorage tank inspection
    💡
    Method choice depends on the damage type and the metal. Visual inspection comes first; it is cheap and often finds the problem.
    EXAMPLE · Choosing a method

    Question: “How many tubes in this bundle are thinning?” Answer: eddy current or IRIS, because tube walls are inside the shell and thin. A visual check of the tube ends alone is not enough.

    📐 Inspection planning, corrosion rate and remaining life

    Inspection tells the plant how fast metal is wearing, so it can decide how long equipment can safely run. Two formulas are used everywhere.

    QuantityFormulaMeaning
    Corrosion rate(previous thickness − current thickness) ÷ time between readingsHow fast the wall is thinning
    Remaining life(current thickness − minimum required thickness) ÷ corrosion rateHow long before the wall is too thin
    Next inspectionNot more than about half the remaining life, within code limitsKeeps a safety margin
    EXAMPLE · Worked example

    A vessel wall was 12.0 mm in 2018 and is 10.4 mm in 2026, 8 years later. Corrosion rate = (12.0 − 10.4) ÷ 8 = 0.2 mm/year. The minimum required thickness is 8.0 mm. Remaining life = (10.4 − 8.0) ÷ 0.2 = 12 years. Half of that is 6 years, so the next inspection is planned within about 6 years, or sooner if the code requires.

    🧰 Pressure testing and bolted joints

    Leaks at start-up cost days. Two practices prevent most of them: a proper pressure test and a controlled bolted-joint method.

    EXAMPLE · Design pressure arithmetic

    A line has a design pressure of 20 bar. At a test factor of 1.5, the hydrotest pressure is 20 × 1.5 = 30 bar (before any correction the code requires). Test gauges must be calibrated and the area cleared before pressure is raised.

    🚩

    🧭 Fundamentals

    What is the Shutdown & Turnaround department, and where does it sit?
    It is one of the nine departments in Oil & Gas / Energy Skills. It has ten roles (Engineer, Senior Engineer, Project Engineer, Supervisor, Coordinator, Planner, Quality Engineer, Inspector, Technician, Analyst) and all share three skills: technical knowledge, problem-solving and documentation.
    What is the difference between a shutdown and a turnaround?
    A shutdown is any planned stop. A turnaround is a large planned stop of a whole unit or plant, usually every few years, for major inspection and maintenance. Power plants call a planned stop an outage.
    Why are turnarounds planned so far ahead?
    Long-lead materials, contractors, inspection data and scope reviews all take months. A stop costs production every day, so poor preparation is expensive.
    What is a scope freeze and why does it matter?
    It is the date after which new jobs need formal approval. It stops scope growth, which is the biggest cause of delay and overspend.
    What is emergent work?
    New work found only after equipment is opened. It is handled by a defined process: assess, approve, plan and record.
    What is the critical path?
    The longest chain of linked jobs. Any delay on it delays the whole shutdown, so it gets the most attention each day.
    What is float?
    The time a job can slip without delaying the end date. Critical-path jobs have zero float.
    Name the three skills in all ten roles, and why.
    Technical knowledge, problem-solving and documentation. Knowledge keeps work safe and correct, problem-solving fixes what goes wrong, and documentation proves what was done.

    📐 Engineering roles

    What does an Engineer do in a turnaround?
    Designs repairs, replacements and tie-ins, calculates thickness and remaining life, and issues the technical documents that crews use.
    How do you calculate corrosion rate and remaining life?
    Corrosion rate = (previous − current thickness) ÷ time. Remaining life = (current − minimum required thickness) ÷ corrosion rate. Example: 12.0 to 10.4 mm in 8 years is 0.2 mm/yr; with a 8.0 mm minimum the remaining life is 12 years.
    What does a Senior Engineer add?
    Advanced analysis such as fitness-for-service, design review, troubleshooting of repeat failures and leadership of the team.
    What is fitness-for-service?
    A method for judging whether damaged equipment can safely stay in service, and for how long, instead of replacing it at once.
    What does a Project Engineer own?
    A package of work such as a new heater: drawings, materials, contractor, cost, progress and close-out.
    How would you handle a design change after scope freeze?
    Raise a management-of-change request, assess safety, cost and schedule impact, get approval, and update the plan and drawings.

    👷 Site, planning and coordination roles

    What does a Supervisor check before work starts?
    That the permit is valid, isolations and blinds are in place and listed, the crew is briefed and qualified, and tools and materials are ready.
    What does a Coordinator do all day?
    Runs daily meetings, keeps permit, blind and hot-spot registers, passes information between teams and resolves clashes.
    What is a hot-spot list?
    A short list of jobs that threaten the end date, each with an owner and a closing time.
    What does a Planner produce?
    Work packs, the baseline schedule, the resource plan, the critical path and daily progress updates.
    What is a baseline and when do you change it?
    The approved plan used to measure progress. It is changed only through the approved change process, not to hide a delay.
    How do you recover a late critical-path job?
    Check logic for overlaps, add resources or shifts, remove waiting time, move float jobs aside, and agree changes with the team.

    ✅ Quality and inspection roles

    What is an inspection and test plan (ITP)?
    A document listing checks, methods, acceptance limits and hold and witness points for a job.
    What is the difference between a hold point and a witness point?
    Work must stop at a hold point until the inspector releases it. At a witness point the inspector may attend, but work can continue if they do not after notice.
    Which method for tube wall loss, and which for surface cracks?
    Eddy current or IRIS for tubes; MT on carbon steel or PT on other metals for surface cracks. Visual inspection comes first.
    What does a Quality Engineer do after a non-conformance?
    Records it, finds the root cause, defines corrective and preventive action, checks it worked and closes the NCR with evidence.
    How do you record an inspection finding?
    By equipment tag, with location, readings, photos, the limit, and the inspector’s name and date.
    What would you do if a gauge was out of calibration?
    Stop using it, tag it, check recent readings it was used for, and recheck where needed.

    🔧 Technician and analyst roles

    What are the steps of isolating a line for entry?
    Stop and depressure, drain and flush, purge or steam, fit blinds or double block and bleed, lock-out and tag-out, gas test, then issue the permit.
    Why is a single valve not enough for isolation?
    Valves can leak or be opened by mistake. A blind or double block and bleed gives a reliable separation.
    What is a safe way to tighten a flange?
    Use a new gasket, clean faces, a calibrated tool and a cross-pattern in stages, then record torque.
    What KPIs does an Analyst track?
    Schedule performance index, cost performance index, percent complete, emergent work rate, safety rates and productivity.
    What is SPI and CPI?
    SPI = earned value ÷ planned value; CPI = earned value ÷ actual cost. Below 1.0 means behind schedule or over budget.
    How would you calculate the total recordable incident rate?
    TRIR = recordable injuries × 200,000 ÷ hours worked. For example 2 injuries in 400,000 hours gives 1.0.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Scope and design

    ✍️ A pipe elbow was 12.0 mm thick 8 years ago and is now 10.4 mm. Minimum required thickness is 8.0 mm. What do you decide, as Engineer?

The elbow can run until the next turnaround, so it can stay in the scope as a monitored item, but check for local thinning at the outer bend and confirm with more readings. If readings show faster loss, add replacement to the scope through the change process.

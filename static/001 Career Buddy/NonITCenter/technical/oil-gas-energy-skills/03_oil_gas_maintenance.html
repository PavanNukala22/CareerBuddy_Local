    Oil & Gas Maintenance Fundamentals

    Start from zero: what maintenance means in oil and gas facilities, the main types of maintenance, how a job flows from a problem to a closed work order, which equipment you will look after, and the safety rules that come first.

    🛠️ What is Oil & Gas Maintenance?

    Oil & Gas Maintenance

    process operationsengineering
    Definition
    Oil & gas maintenance is all the work done to keep production, processing and transport assets — wellheads, separators, compressors, pumps, pipelines, tanks, refinery and gas-plant units — safe, available and performing as designed.
    Why it matters
    Equipment that fails can release flammable or toxic hydrocarbons, stop production worth large sums per day, and breach environmental permits. Good maintenance protects people first, then the asset and production.
    Explanation
    Maintenance is a managed process, not just repair: equipment is ranked by criticality, a strategy is chosen for each item (preventive, predictive, run-to-failure), work is planned and scheduled in a CMMS, executed safely under permit, tested, handed back to operations and analysed to improve reliability.
    Types
    Corrective, preventive, predictive/condition-based, reliability-centred, shutdown/turnaround maintenance (detailed in s1-3).
    Components
    Maintenance strategy, CMMS (e.g. SAP PM, Maximo), work orders, spares, skilled crafts (mechanical, electrical, instrument), permits and isolations, inspection, records.
    Process / workflow
    Identify need → notify → prioritise → plan → schedule → isolate & permit → execute → test → hand back → record & analyse.
    O&G example
    An offshore gas compressor runs 24/7 and exports all the platform's gas. Its maintenance plan combines daily operator checks, continuous vibration monitoring, 4,000-hour inspections and a major overhaul every few years — planned so the platform loses as little production as possible.
    Common mistakes
    Treating maintenance as “fix it when it breaks”; skipping records; working without isolation because “it's a quick job”.
    Best practices
    Plan before you touch; isolate every time; record what you found; ask “why did it fail?” not only “how do I fix it?”
    🧪 Practice activity: Repair or maintenance?
    Is changing a pump's lube oil every 3 months “repair”? Why does the difference matter?
    Answer: No — it is preventive maintenance. It is planned before failure to keep the pump healthy. Planned work is cheaper, safer and causes less production loss than breakdown repair.
    EXAMPLE · One pump, many hands

    An Analyst notices rising vibration trends. The Engineer diagnoses bearing wear. The Planner prepares the job and spares. The Supervisor allocates the crew. The Technician replaces the bearing under permit. The Inspector checks alignment records. One pump, six Oil & Gas Maintenance roles.

    🗂️ Types of Maintenance

    Maintenance Types & Strategies

    preventive maintenanceengineeringplanning
    Definition
    A maintenance strategy is the chosen way of looking after an item — when and how it is maintained.
    Why it matters
    Using the wrong strategy wastes money (over-maintaining low-risk items) or causes failures (under-maintaining critical items).
    Explanation
    • Corrective (breakdown): repair after failure. Acceptable only for low-criticality items (run-to-failure decision).
    • Preventive (time-based): fixed intervals — e.g. lube oil change every 3 months.
    • Predictive / condition-based: act when monitoring shows deterioration — vibration, oil analysis, thermography.
    • Reliability-centred maintenance (RCM): a structured method choosing the best strategy per failure mode.
    • Statutory / regulatory: legally required inspections and tests (pressure vessels, lifting equipment, PSVs).
    • Shutdown / turnaround: work that can only be done with the unit stopped.
    Types
    Reactive vs proactive; planned vs unplanned; time-based vs condition-based.
    Components
    Criticality ranking, failure modes, task lists, intervals, monitoring techniques, CMMS maintenance plans.
    Process / workflow
    Rank criticality → identify failure modes → choose strategy per mode → load maintenance plans into CMMS → review using failure data.
    O&G example
    Firewater pumps get weekly run tests and annual performance tests (statutory, safety-critical); a critical gas compressor gets continuous vibration monitoring (predictive); a workshop extractor fan runs to failure.
    Common mistakes
    Same interval for every pump; no strategy for safety-critical equipment; ignoring condition data.
    Best practices
    Match strategy to criticality and failure mode; aim for a mostly planned workload (e.g. <20% reactive).
    🧪 Practice activity: Choose a strategy
    Pick a strategy: (a) gas turbine bearings, (b) office light fittings, (c) pressure safety valves, (d) emergency diesel generator.
    Answer: (a) Predictive — vibration/oil analysis; (b) run-to-failure; (c) statutory time-based testing/overhaul; (d) preventive with periodic run tests (safety-critical).

    🔄 The Maintenance Lifecycle

    Figure 1.2 · The work management cycle — every job follows it

    Work Management Process

    planningschedulingdocumentation
    Definition
    The standard steps a maintenance job follows from the moment a need is found until the work order is closed and analysed.
    Why it matters
    A disciplined process cuts waiting time (crews without parts, permits or access), improves safety and builds the history needed for reliability improvement.
    Explanation
    A notification records the problem; it becomes a work order once approved. Planning adds steps, labour, materials, tools, permits and isolations. Scheduling fixes the date with operations. After execution, the technician records findings and failure codes; the work order is technically completed and closed.
    Types
    Emergency work (bypasses planning, still needs permit), urgent, routine, PM, shutdown work.
    Components
    CMMS, priority matrix, job plans, work packs, weekly schedule, backlog list, KPIs.
    Process / workflow
    See Figure 1.2.
    O&G example
    A leaking valve gland is notified on Monday, planned Tuesday (packing set, scaffold, permit), scheduled Thursday with operations, repaired and leak-tested Thursday, and closed with failure code “packing worn” — which later shows this valve type needs a shorter repacking interval.
    Common mistakes
    Starting work before planning; vague notifications; closing work orders without findings.
    Best practices
    “Plan the work, work the plan”; record what you found and did; review backlog weekly.
    🧪 Practice activity: Missing step
    A crew arrives for a planned job but the spare part is the wrong size. Which step failed?
    Answer: Planning — materials were not verified against the equipment data/bill of materials before scheduling (“kitting” check).

    🦺 Basic Safety Concepts

    Maintenance Hazards & Basic Controls

    process safetysafety
    Definition
    The hazards maintenance workers face in oil and gas facilities and the controls that protect them.
    Why it matters
    Many oil and gas fatalities and major accidents happened during maintenance — opening equipment that was not properly isolated, hot work near hydrocarbons, entering confined spaces, lifting.
    Explanation
    • Stored energy: pressure, hydrocarbons, electricity, springs, gravity, heat.
    • Toxic gas: H2S — never rely on smell; carry a personal monitor.
    • Fire/explosion: hot work and sparks near flammable gas.
    • Confined space: oxygen deficiency, toxic or flammable atmosphere.
    • Dropped objects and lifting; working at height; pyrophoric iron sulfide when opening sour equipment.
    Types
    Occupational (slips, falls, cuts, manual handling) and process safety (loss of containment, fire, explosion).
    Components
    Permit to Work, isolation and LOTO, gas testing, JSA/toolbox talk, PPE, fire watch, rescue plans, Stop Work Authority.
    Process / workflow
    JSA → permit → isolate & verify → gas test → toolbox talk → work → re-test during work → hand back.
    O&G example
    Before replacing a gasket on a sour gas line, the technician confirms the double block and bleed isolation, the line is depressured and purged, a gas test shows 0% LEL and 0 ppm H2S, and a breathing apparatus is used for the first break as the permit requires.
    Common mistakes
    “Quick job, no permit”; trusting a closed valve without verifying; breaking a flange facing yourself.
    Best practices
    Verify zero energy (“try” step); break flanges away from your body, bottom bolts last; use Stop Work Authority.
    🧪 Practice activity: Spot the hazard
    A technician plans to grind a bracket 5 m from a gas compressor with no gas test, because “the compressor isn't leaking”. What is missing?
    Answer: A hot work permit, continuous gas testing, fire watch and extinguisher, covering drains, and confirmation from operations — grinding sparks can ignite gas from an unnoticed leak.

    How the main oil and gas equipment classes are maintained: rotating machines, static equipment, valves and PSVs, electrical and Ex equipment, instruments and safety systems, pipelines and wellheads, and the tools and measurements that prove the job is right.

    🌀 Rotating Equipment Maintenance

    Pumps, Compressors & Turbines

    equipment/process operationtroubleshootingpreventive maintenancetools
    Definition
    Maintenance of machines that rotate — centrifugal and positive-displacement pumps, centrifugal/reciprocating/screw compressors, gas and steam turbines, gearboxes, fans and motors.
    Why it matters
    Rotating equipment causes most unplanned production losses and many hydrocarbon leaks (seal failures).
    Explanation
    Main failure modes: bearing wear, mechanical seal leakage, misalignment, unbalance, lubrication failure, cavitation, valve/ring wear (reciprocating compressors), hot-section degradation (gas turbines). Core tasks: lubrication, alignment (laser alignment within OEM tolerance), seal replacement, bearing replacement, coupling inspection, balancing, overhauls at OEM-recommended running hours.
    Types
    Running checks, minor PM (lube, filters), major overhaul, condition-based intervention.
    Components
    Casing, impeller/rotor, shaft, bearings, seals and seal support system (API 682 plans), coupling, baseplate, lube oil system, driver.
    Process / workflow
    Isolate (mechanical and electrical), drain, depressure, purge → dismantle with match-marks → inspect and measure clearances → replace parts → reassemble to OEM torques/clearances → align → hand back → run test with vibration readings.
    O&G example
    A crude export pump seal fails every 4 months. Investigation finds soft-foot and 0.3 mm misalignment after base grouting cracked. Re-grouting and laser alignment to 0.05 mm extends seal life past 2 years.
    Common mistakes
    Aligning cold machines without allowing for thermal growth; over-greasing bearings; reusing gaskets/O-rings; skipping post-repair vibration baseline.
    Best practices
    Follow OEM procedures; record as-found/as-left clearances; laser alignment; baseline vibration after every repair.
    🧪 Practice activity: Read the symptom
    A pump shows high vibration at 1× running speed, mostly radial, after an impeller was replaced. Likely cause?
    Answer: Unbalance (1× radial). Check impeller balance; misalignment usually shows strong axial vibration and 2× components.

    🛢️ Static Equipment & Piping

    Vessels, Piping, Tanks & Heat Exchangers

    inspectionmeasurementsprocess safety
    Definition
    Maintenance and inspection of equipment that contains pressure but does not move: pressure vessels, columns, piping, storage tanks, heat exchangers and fired heaters.
    Why it matters
    Static equipment holds the hydrocarbon inventory. Corrosion or cracking that goes unnoticed leads directly to loss of containment.
    Explanation
    Damage mechanisms: internal/external corrosion, corrosion under insulation (CUI), erosion, sulfide stress cracking (sour service), fatigue, fouling. Inspection codes: API 510 (vessels), API 570 (piping), API 653 (tanks). Non-destructive testing (NDT): ultrasonic thickness (UT), radiography, magnetic particle, dye penetrant. Maintenance: exchanger bundle cleaning, tube plugging, gasket replacement, coating repairs, clamps (temporary, with engineering approval and MOC).
    Types
    On-stream inspection, shutdown internal inspection, risk-based inspection (RBI), fitness-for-service (API 579).
    Components
    Shell, nozzles, internals, insulation, coatings, supports, thickness monitoring locations (TMLs).
    Process / workflow
    Inspection plan → measure (UT, visual) → calculate corrosion rate and remaining life → plan repair/replacement before retirement thickness.
    O&G example
    UT shows a produced-water line thinned from 7.1 to 5.3 mm in 3 years: corrosion rate 0.6 mm/y. With retirement thickness 3.5 mm, remaining life ≈ (5.3 − 3.5) ÷ 0.6 = 3 years — replacement is planned for the next shutdown.
    Common mistakes
    Ignoring damaged insulation (CUI); permanent use of temporary clamps; welding on live lines without hot-tap procedures.
    Best practices
    Manage clamps through MOC with expiry; keep inspection data current; repair coatings early.
    🧪 Practice activity: Remaining life
    A vessel wall is 12.0 mm, retirement thickness 9.0 mm, corrosion rate 0.25 mm/year. Remaining life?
    Answer: (12.0 − 9.0) ÷ 0.25 = 12 years (next inspection typically at half the remaining life or code maximum).

    🔩 Valves & Pressure Safety Valves

    Valve & PSV Maintenance

    process safetypreventive maintenancedocumentation
    Definition
    Maintenance of isolation valves (gate, ball, plug, butterfly), control valves, emergency shutdown (ESD) valves and pressure safety valves (PSVs), which protect equipment from overpressure.
    Why it matters
    PSVs and ESD valves are safety-critical elements. A PSV that fails to open, or is removed without control, can lead to catastrophic overpressure — Piper Alpha is the classic example.
    Explanation
    PSVs are removed on a statutory/RBI interval, pop-tested (as-found set pressure recorded), overhauled, re-set and sealed, and reinstalled with the correct inlet/outlet isolation valves locked open. ESD valves get partial/full stroke tests and leak tests. Control valves: positioner calibration, packing, trim.
    Types
    Spring-loaded and pilot-operated PSVs; rupture discs; isolation, control and ESD valves.
    Components
    Body, trim, seat, spring, bellows, cap seal, test certificate, PSV register, car-seal (lock) register.
    Process / workflow
    Check the PSV register and spare/standby PSV → permit and isolation → remove → bench test as-found → overhaul → set and seal → certificate → reinstall → car-seal isolation valves open → update register.
    O&G example
    A PSV's as-found test opens 15% above set pressure — a failed test. The Quality Engineer opens an NCR; the RCA shows product fouling; the interval is shortened and a rupture disc upstream is evaluated through MOC.
    Common mistakes
    Running equipment with its only PSV removed; leaving PSV isolation valves closed after reinstallation; not recording as-found results.
    Best practices
    Never leave equipment without relief protection unless formally risk-assessed; car-seal registers; record as-found and as-left.
    🧪 Practice activity: Why as-found?
    Why is the PSV “as-found” pop test important even if the valve is overhauled anyway?
    Answer: It shows whether the valve would have protected the equipment during the last interval. Failed as-found tests drive interval and design changes.

    ⚡ Electrical & Hazardous-Area Equipment

    Electrical & Ex Equipment

    safetyinspectiontools
    Definition
    Maintenance of motors, switchgear, transformers, UPS, generators and the explosion-protected (Ex) equipment installed in hazardous areas where flammable gas may be present.
    Why it matters
    An electrical spark or hot surface in a gas zone can ignite a release. Ex protection only works if it is maintained exactly as certified.
    Explanation
    Hazardous areas are classified into zones (Zone 0, 1, 2 for gas). Ex equipment uses protection concepts such as flameproof Ex d, increased safety Ex e, intrinsic safety Ex i. Inspection and maintenance follow IEC 60079-17 by competent persons: correct glands, no missing bolts, flamepath gaps within limits, no unauthorised modifications. Motors: insulation resistance tests, bearing greasing, thermography of terminations.
    Types
    Visual, close and detailed Ex inspections; electrical testing; thermography.
    Components
    Ex enclosures, cable glands, certification labels, hazardous area drawings, Ex register.
    Process / workflow
    Electrical isolation (LOTO at switchgear, prove dead) → Ex inspection/maintenance → restore integrity → record in Ex register.
    O&G example
    An inspection finds a flameproof junction box with two missing cover bolts. The box is no longer certified flameproof — it is repaired with the correct bolts and recorded; the RCA asks why the previous job left bolts out.
    Common mistakes
    Using non-certified glands; painting over flamepaths; replacing parts with non-Ex equivalents.
    Best practices
    Only Ex-competent people work on Ex equipment; keep the Ex register current; replace like-for-like certified parts.
    🧪 Practice activity: Zone check
    Which zone has flammable gas likely during normal operation: Zone 0, 1 or 2?
    Answer: Zone 1 (likely in normal operation). Zone 0 — present continuously or for long periods; Zone 2 — not likely, and only briefly if it occurs.

    🎛️ Instrumentation, SIS & Fire and Gas

    Instrument Maintenance & Proof Testing

    P&IDmeasurementsdocumentation
    Definition
    Calibration and repair of transmitters, analysers and control valves, and proof testing of Safety Instrumented Systems (SIS) and fire & gas (F&G) detection.
    Why it matters
    SIS functions only reduce risk if they work on demand. Undetected (“dangerous undetected”) failures are only found by proof tests at the interval set in the SIL calculation (IEC 61511).
    Explanation
    Calibration checks a transmitter against a reference at several points. A proof test exercises the whole safety function: sensor → logic → final element (e.g. ESD valve closes in the required time). Gas detectors are bump-tested/calibrated with test gas. Any trip bypassed for testing is controlled by an override procedure.
    Types
    Calibration, loop check, partial stroke test, full proof test, detector bump test.
    Components
    Calibrators, test gas, cause-and-effect charts, P&IDs, SIS proof test procedures, override register.
    Process / workflow
    Permit and override authorisation → test per procedure → record as-found/as-left → restore and remove overrides → report failures as SIS demand/failure data.
    O&G example
    A high-pressure trip proof test finds the shutdown valve closes in 28 s against a 15 s requirement. The test is failed, the actuator is repaired, and the operations team applies compensating measures until it passes.
    Common mistakes
    Leaving bypasses on after testing; testing only the transmitter, not the final element; deferring proof tests without risk assessment.
    Best practices
    Test the full loop; track overdue proof tests as a leading indicator; independent check that overrides are removed.
    🧪 Practice activity: What's missing?
    A technician calibrated a pressure transmitter used in a trip and signed off the proof test. What's missing?
    Answer: The logic and final element — the proof test must show the shutdown valve or trip action works within its required response time.

    📏 Tools, Measurements & Bolted Joints

    Tools, Measurements & Bolted Joint Integrity

    toolsmeasurementsquality
    Definition
    The correct use of calibrated tools and measuring instruments, and the controlled assembly of flanged joints so they do not leak.
    Why it matters
    Leaking flanges after maintenance are one of the most common causes of hydrocarbon releases at restart.
    Explanation
    Measurements: micrometers, dial indicators, feeler gauges, laser alignment, vibration pens, UT gauges, insulation testers — all calibrated. Bolted joints follow ASME PCC-1 principles: clean faces, correct gasket, lubricated bolts, controlled torque or tensioning in a cross pattern over several passes, by a trained/certified technician, with a joint record and tag.
    Types
    Hand torque, hydraulic torque wrench, hydraulic bolt tensioning.
    Components
    Joint register, torque tables, calibrated tools, gasket specs, joint tags, leak test procedure.
    Process / workflow
    Identify joint → check gasket/bolt spec → clean and inspect faces → assemble → tighten in passes → record → leak test before hand-back.
    O&G example
    After a turnaround, three flanges leak at restart. The joint register shows all three were tightened with an out-of-calibration torque wrench — re-torque and tool control are added as CAPA.
    Common mistakes
    Reusing spiral-wound gaskets; tightening in a circle instead of a cross pattern; no record of who made the joint.
    Best practices
    Joint integrity programme with trained technicians, tags and records; leak test every broken joint.
    🧪 Practice activity: Torque pattern
    Why are flange bolts tightened in a cross (star) pattern in several passes?
    Answer: To compress the gasket evenly. Tightening one side fully first tilts the flange, crushes part of the gasket and leaves gaps elsewhere — a leak path.

    Planning, Scheduling & CMMS

    How maintenance work is ranked, planned, scheduled and recorded: criticality and priority, job plans, weekly schedules and backlog, CMMS/ERP, spares, and the documents behind every job.

    ⚖️ Criticality & Prioritisation

    Equipment Criticality & Work Priority

    planningengineeringprocess safety
    Definition
    Criticality ranks equipment by the consequence of its failure; priority ranks individual work requests by urgency and risk.
    Why it matters
    Resources are limited. Criticality and priority make sure safety-critical and production-critical work is done first, and that low-risk work does not crowd out important jobs.
    Explanation
    Criticality assessment scores consequences (safety, environment, production, cost) and likelihood, often giving classes A (high), B, C. A priority matrix combines the consequence of not doing the job with how soon failure may happen: e.g. P1 emergency (now), P2 urgent (24–72 h), P3 planned (within weeks), P4 opportunity/shutdown.
    Types
    Equipment criticality (static ranking), work-order priority (dynamic), SCE flag.
    Components
    Criticality matrix, SCE list, priority matrix, equipment master data in CMMS.
    Process / workflow
    Criticality assigned once and reviewed periodically → each notification gets a priority using the matrix → planner and operations agree in the daily meeting.
    O&G example
    Three requests: firewater pump fails weekly run test (SCE → P1/P2), export pump B vibration rising with A in service (P2/P3), office AC fault (P4). The firewater pump is first because the platform's fire protection depends on it.
    Common mistakes
    Everything marked P1 (“priority inflation”); criticality never reviewed after modifications.
    Best practices
    Audit priority use; challenge P1s in the daily meeting; flag SCEs clearly in the CMMS.
    🧪 Practice activity: Rank it
    Rank: (a) gas detector faulty in a compressor house, (b) spare lube-oil pump leaking slightly, (c) paint touch-up on a handrail.
    Answer: (a) first — SCE, compensate immediately (portable detection); (b) second — planned; (c) last — opportunity work.

    🗺️ Maintenance Planning

    Job Planning & Work Packs

    planningresource allocationdocumentation
    Definition
    Planning decides what and how: the steps, crafts, hours, materials, tools, permits, isolations and safety precautions for a job, collected in a work pack.
    Why it matters
    Studies of maintenance productivity show crews waste much of their shift waiting for parts, permits, access or information. A good plan removes these delays and makes the job safer.
    Explanation
    The planner visits the job (scoping), reads history and OEM manuals, builds a job plan (steps and durations), reserves materials, books specialist tools, cranes and scaffolds, identifies permits and isolation points from P&IDs, and attaches drawings and checklists. Re-usable standard job plans are stored in the CMMS.
    Types
    Routine/PM plans, corrective job plans, shutdown work packs, emergency (minimal planning, still safe).
    Components
    Job steps, labour estimate, materials/BOM, tools, permits/isolations, risk assessment, drawings, quality hold points.
    Process / workflow
    Scope in field → job plan → materials reserved and kitted → permits identified → work pack ready → status “ready to schedule”.
    O&G example
    Planning a reciprocating compressor valve change: valve kits and gaskets reserved, special valve-seat tool booked, isolation list from P&ID (suction, discharge, vent, drain, motor), gas freeing requirement, OEM torque values, 2 mechanics × 10 h.
    Common mistakes
    Planning from the desk without a field visit; no materials check; vague steps.
    Best practices
    Plan at least a week ahead; kit materials before scheduling; capture feedback from technicians to improve job plans.
    🧪 Practice activity: Work pack contents
    List six things in a work pack for a pump seal change.
    Answer: Job steps, isolation list, permit requirements and JSA, seal kit and gaskets (reserved), seal drawing/OEM procedure, torque and alignment values, labour estimate, test/hand-back requirements.

    📅 Scheduling & Backlog

    Scheduling, Backlog & Schedule Compliance

    schedulingprogress trackingExcel/ERP
    Definition
    Scheduling decides when and who: ready jobs are placed on a weekly schedule matching available crews and equipment access agreed with operations.
    Why it matters
    A firm weekly schedule raises productivity and lets operations prepare equipment. Backlog shows whether the team has enough resources.
    Explanation
    Backlog (in crew-weeks) = total estimated hours of approved work ÷ weekly crew capacity. Healthy backlog is commonly 2–4 weeks of ready work. Schedule compliance = scheduled jobs completed ÷ scheduled jobs × 100 (target often ≥ 85–90%). Break-ins (unplanned emergency work) reduce compliance.
    Types
    Weekly schedule, daily schedule, look-ahead, shutdown schedule (critical path).
    Components
    Ready backlog, crew capacity, operations constraints, equipment outage windows, schedule meeting.
    Process / workflow
    Thursday: draft next week's schedule from ready backlog → schedule meeting with operations → freeze → daily schedule → track completion and break-ins → report compliance.
    O&G example
    Mechanical crew of 6 × 40 h = 240 h/week. Ready backlog = 960 h → 4 weeks. Last week 42 of 48 scheduled jobs done → compliance 87.5%. Break-ins took 60 h — mostly one bad-actor pump (sent for RCA).
    Common mistakes
    Scheduling jobs not ready (no parts); over-scheduling beyond capacity; not tracking break-ins.

    🔐 Permit to Work & Isolation

    Permit to Work & Energy Isolation

    process safetyP&IDsafety
    Definition
    A Permit to Work is a formal written authorisation for hazardous work; isolation separates equipment from every energy source so it can be worked on safely.
    Why it matters
    Piper Alpha (North Sea, 6 July 1988, 167 killed): a condensate pump's PSV had been removed for maintenance and a blind flange fitted. The night shift did not know, started the pump, the flange leaked and the gas ignited. Permit and handover failures were central causes.
    Explanation
    Isolation hierarchy: positive isolation (spades/blinds, removed spool) → double block and bleed → single valve (low risk only). Electrical: LOTO at the switchgear and “try” to start. Every isolation point is listed on an isolation certificate (from the P&ID), locked, tagged and verified. Cross-referencing links permits that affect the same equipment.
    Types
    Hot work, cold work, confined space, electrical, line-break, radiography, diving (offshore), excavation permits.
    Components
    Permit, JSA, isolation certificate, LOTO, blind list, gas test record, permit board/log, signatures (issuer, performer, area authority), hand-back.
    Process / workflow
    Request → JSA → operations prepares (depressure, drain, purge) → isolate and verify → gas test → issue at site → toolbox talk → work → suspend/hand-back → de-isolate.
    O&G example
    A pump is isolated for a seal job: motor locked at MCC and start button tried; suction/discharge valves locked closed; casing drained and vented to closed drain; gas test 0% LEL; permit cross-referenced with the operations shift log.
    Common mistakes
    Permits issued from the control room; missing small lines (seal flush, drains); suspended permits not communicated at shift change.
    Best practices
    Site verification; numbered blinds; permit status in every shift handover.
    🧪 Practice activity: Piper Alpha lesson
    Name two permit-system controls that would have prevented the Piper Alpha restart of the pump.
    Answer: Cross-referencing the PSV-removal permit with the pump's equipment status (pump locked out while its PSV was off); effective shift handover of suspended permits; physical tags/locks on the pump.

    ✅ Workmanship Quality & Inspection Points

    Quality of Maintenance Work

    quality plansinspectionchecklistsmeasurements
    Definition
    Making sure maintenance work meets specifications, OEM requirements and codes — right first time — and proving it with records.
    Why it matters
    Maintenance-induced failures (wrong gasket, poor alignment, missed bolt) cause leaks and early repeat failures — “we broke it while fixing it”.
    Explanation
    The Quality Engineer defines an inspection and test plan (ITP) with hold points (work stops until inspected) and witness points. The Inspector checks against drawings/specifications with checklists and measurements: material certificates (positive material identification, PMI), weld NDT, torque records, clearances, alignment readings.
    Types
    Self-check, peer check, inspector verification, client/third-party witness.
    Components
    ITP, checklists, calibrated instruments, material certificates, NCR process.
    Process / workflow
    Hold points in work pack → inspect → record → release or raise NCR → final dossier.
    O&G example
    PMI on a replacement spool for a hot hydrogen line finds carbon steel instead of the specified chrome-moly. The spool is rejected before installation — the hold point prevented a future high-temperature hydrogen attack failure.
    Common mistakes
    Skipping hold points under schedule pressure; signing checklists without measurements.
    Best practices
    Hold points in the plan, respected by supervisors; record actual values, not ticks.
    🧪 Practice activity: Hold or witness?
    Which should be a hold point: (a) final alignment of a critical compressor, (b) painting a handrail, (c) PMI of alloy spools?
    Answer: (a) and (c) — errors there are costly or dangerous and hard to detect later.

    🔁 Post-Maintenance Testing & Hand-back

    Post-Maintenance Testing & Hand-back

    equipment/process operationprocess operationsreporting
    Definition
    Tests that prove equipment works and is leak-tight after maintenance, and the formal return of equipment to operations.
    Why it matters
    Many leaks and trips occur in the first hours after maintenance. Testing finds problems before full production.
    Explanation
    Typical tests: leak test (N2 or service test), run test with vibration and temperature checks, valve stroke test, trip/function test, insulation test for motors, PSV set certificate. Hand-back confirms all isolations removed in order, blinds removed per list, tools cleared, permits closed, and records complete.
    Types
    Mechanical run test, leak/tightness test, functional test, SIS proof test.
    Components
    Test procedure, acceptance criteria, hand-back form, blind list reconciliation.
    Process / workflow
    Work complete → housekeeping → de-isolate per certificate → test with operations → accept → close permit and work order.
    O&G example
    After a valve repack, an N2 leak test at operating pressure finds a small gland leak — corrected before hydrocarbons are introduced.
    Common mistakes
    “It'll be fine” hand-back without test; blinds left in; records incomplete.
    Best practices
    Test criteria in the job plan; operations witness; blind list signed off.

    Answer: Permits closed/suspended correctly; isolations removed in order; blinds removed and list reconciled; leak/run test passed; area clean and guards refitted; records and work order updated.

    Working with Operations

    Why it matters

    Explanation

    Definition
    Health, safety and environmental rules that apply to maintenance work in oil and gas facilities.
    Why it matters

    📡 Condition Monitoring & Predictive Maintenance

    Condition Monitoring Techniques

    measurementstrend analysispreventive maintenance
    Definition
    Measuring indicators of equipment health over time so that maintenance is done when deterioration begins, before functional failure.
    Why it matters
    The P-F interval — the time between a detectable potential failure (P) and functional failure (F) — is the window to plan a repair calmly instead of reacting to a breakdown.
    Explanation
    • Vibration analysis: unbalance, misalignment, bearing defects, looseness (ISO 10816/20816 severity guidance).
    • Oil analysis: wear metals, water, viscosity, particle count.
    • Thermography: hot electrical connections, blocked coolers, insulation loss.
    • Ultrasound: valve passing, gas leaks, bearing lubrication.
    • Performance monitoring: compressor efficiency, pump head/flow, exchanger fouling.
    • Motor current signature analysis and insulation tests for motors.
    Types
    Periodic route-based, continuous online (critical machines with protection systems), operator-based (rounds).
    Components
    Sensors/portable collectors, alarm limits, trend database, analyst reports, recommendations to CMMS.
    Process / workflow
    Baseline → collect → compare to alarm limits and trend → diagnose → recommend → plan work → verify after repair.
    O&G example
    Oil analysis on a gas turbine gearbox shows iron and chromium rising across three samples; vibration shows a developing gear-mesh sideband. The gearbox is inspected at a planned outage two weeks later — a pitted gear is replaced before failure.
    Common mistakes
    Collecting data nobody reviews; no baseline after repair; monitoring interval longer than the P-F interval.
    Best practices
    Interval shorter than P-F; recommendations tracked to closure in CMMS.
    🧪 Practice activity: Pick the technique
    Choose a technique: (a) loose cable lug in a switchboard, (b) passing valve on a flare line, (c) water in compressor lube oil.
    Answer: (a) Thermography; (b) acoustic/ultrasound; (c) oil analysis.

    🧰 Equipment Troubleshooting

    Systematic Equipment Troubleshooting

    troubleshootingproblem-solvingP&ID
    Definition
    A structured, evidence-based method for finding the cause of an equipment problem, rather than replacing parts until it works.
    Why it matters
    “Parts-swapping” wastes spares, time and money, and often leaves the true cause in place.
    Explanation
    Define the problem (what, when, how much, what changed) → make safe → collect data (trends, readings, history, process conditions from P&ID) → verify instruments → list causes → test from most likely/easiest → fix → verify → record. Remember the process can cause equipment symptoms: low suction level causes pump cavitation, not a bad impeller.
    Types
    Mechanical, electrical, instrument, process-induced.
    Components
    Trends, vibration/oil data, P&IDs, OEM troubleshooting guides, work history.
    Process / workflow
    See explanation; document as a short troubleshooting record linked to the work order.
    O&G example
    A booster pump trips on high temperature. Two motors were replaced with no change. A troubleshooting review finds the minimum-flow recirculation valve stuck closed — the pump was running near shut-off and overheating.
    Common mistakes
    Changing several things at once; ignoring process conditions; no record.
    Best practices
    “What changed?” first; one change at a time; involve operations.
    🧪 Practice activity: Process or machine?
    A centrifugal pump is noisy with fluctuating discharge pressure only when the suction tank level is low. Cause?
    Answer: Cavitation from low NPSH available — a process condition, not a machine defect.

    🔍 Root Cause Analysis & Bad Actors

    RCA & CAPA for Equipment Failures

    RCACAPAdata analysisreporting
    Definition
    Root cause analysis (RCA) finds the underlying reasons a failure happened; CAPA (corrective and preventive action) removes them. Bad actors are equipment items with repeated failures.
    Why it matters
    A small number of bad actors often cause a large share of maintenance cost and downtime (Pareto principle).
    Explanation
    Methods: 5 Whys, fishbone (man, machine, method, material, measurement, environment), fault tree, failure analysis of parts (metallurgy, wear patterns). Root causes are usually physical (what broke), human (what someone did) and latent/system (why the system allowed it).
    Types
    Quick 5-whys for minor failures; formal team RCA for high-consequence or repeat failures.
    Components
    Failed parts (preserve them!), data, timeline, team, report, CAPA register, effectiveness check.
    Process / workflow
    Trigger (bad-actor list, high-consequence failure) → preserve evidence → analyse → root causes → CAPA with owners/dates → verify effectiveness.
    O&G example
    5 Whys on repeat bearing failures of a cooling fan: bearing failed → lubricant degraded → over-greasing caused churning heat → PM task said “grease monthly” without quantity → task list written generically. CAPA: grease quantity per OEM in task list, training, ultrasound-guided greasing.
    Common mistakes
    Stopping at “operator error”; throwing away failed parts; CAPA never verified.
    Best practices
    Monthly bad-actor review; keep failed parts; verify CAPA with MTBF data.
    🧪 Practice activity: Three levels
    A coupling failed because bolts were not torqued. Give physical, human and latent causes.
    Answer: Physical: loose bolts fatigued. Human: technician didn't torque. Latent: no torque value in the job plan, no calibrated wrench available, no inspection hold point.

    🧠 RCM & FMEA

    Reliability-Centred Maintenance & FMEA

    advanced technical analysisengineeringdesign review
    Definition
    RCM is a structured method (SAE JA1011) to decide the right maintenance for each function and failure mode. FMEA (failure modes and effects analysis) lists how things fail and what happens.
    Why it matters
    It replaces habit-based maintenance with tasks that actually address real failure modes — often removing useless PMs and adding missing ones.
    Explanation
    RCM asks: functions → functional failures → failure modes → effects → consequences → which task (condition-based, time-based, failure-finding, redesign, run-to-failure). Failure-finding tasks (e.g. proof tests) are needed for hidden failures in protective devices.
    Types
    Full RCM for critical systems, streamlined RCM/PMO (PM optimisation) for less critical ones.
    Components
    Team (operations, maintenance, engineering), FMEA worksheets, decision logic, updated task lists.
    Process / workflow
    Select system → FMEA → decision logic → tasks and intervals → load into CMMS → review with failure data.
    O&G example
    An RCM review of a water injection pump train removes a monthly strip-down inspection (which itself caused failures) and adds online vibration monitoring and quarterly oil analysis.
    Common mistakes
    RCM done only by engineers without operators; results never loaded into the CMMS.
    Best practices
    Start with critical equipment; include technicians; close the loop with failure data.
    🧪 Practice activity: Hidden failure
    Why does a standby firewater pump need failure-finding tasks?
    Answer: Its failure is hidden — nobody notices until it is needed in a fire. Periodic run/performance tests reveal the failure before the demand.

    🚨 Abnormal Conditions & Breakdown Response

    Breakdown & Abnormal Condition Response

    process safetycommunicationtroubleshooting
    Definition
    How maintenance responds when equipment fails or behaves abnormally during operation.
    Why it matters
    Pressure to restore production fast leads to shortcuts — the most dangerous moment for maintenance crews.
    Explanation
    Priorities stay the same: people → environment → asset → production. Emergency work skips detailed planning but never skips permit, isolation and gas testing. Contain first (operations makes the plant safe), then diagnose, then repair.
    Types
    Trip of critical equipment, leak, alarm on SCE, utility failure, abnormal noise/vibration.
    Components

    Process / workflow

    Common mistakes
    Repairing live leaks without engineering approval; restarting equipment after a trip without finding the cause.
    Best practices
    Investigate before restart; keep emergency spares; debrief every major breakdown.
    🧪 Practice activity: Restart?
    A critical compressor tripped on high vibration; production wants an immediate restart. What do you do?
    Answer: Check trip data and machine condition (vibration history, bearing temperatures, oil) with the rotating equipment engineer; restart only when the cause is understood and it is safe.

    🛡️ Asset Integrity & Safety-Critical Elements

    Asset Integrity Management & SCEs

    process safetyinspectiondocumentation
    Definition
    Asset integrity is the ability of an asset to perform its function effectively and efficiently while protecting health, safety and the environment. Safety-critical elements (SCEs) are equipment whose failure could cause, or fail to prevent or limit, a major accident.
    Why it matters
    Major accidents happen when several barriers fail together. Maintenance keeps those barriers working.
    Explanation
    Each SCE has a performance standard: what it must do (functionality), how reliably (availability), and survivability. Examples: PSVs, ESD valves, F&G detectors, firewater pumps, deluge systems, blowdown valves, pressure envelope (vessels/piping), emergency power. SCE maintenance tasks are tracked separately and deferral requires formal risk assessment.
    Types
    Prevention barriers (containment, PSVs), detection (F&G), control (ESD, blowdown), mitigation (firewater, deluge), emergency response (lifeboats, alarms).
    Components
    SCE register, performance standards, assurance tasks (inspection, testing), verification by an independent body, barrier status dashboard.
    Process / workflow
    Identify SCEs → write performance standards → assign assurance tasks in CMMS → execute and record → verify → report barrier health.
    O&G example
    A deluge valve test shows water reaching nozzles in 45 s against a performance standard of 30 s. The SCE is recorded as failed, compensating measures are set (fire team on standby, restrict hot work) and the valve is repaired as P1.
    Common mistakes
    Treating SCE tests like routine PMs; passing tests without measuring against the standard.
    Best practices
    100% SCE compliance target; failed SCE = immediate compensating measures.
    🧪 Practice activity: Is it an SCE?
    Which are SCEs: (a) firewater pump, (b) canteen dishwasher, (c) blowdown valve, (d) gas detector?
    Answer: (a), (c) and (d).

    🔁 Process Safety in Maintenance & MOC

    Management of Change in Maintenance

    process safetydesign reviewtechnical documentation
    Definition
    MOC is the formal review of any change that is not replacement in kind — materials, design, operating limits, procedures, temporary repairs and bypasses.
    Why it matters
    Flixborough (1974, 28 killed) followed a temporary bypass pipe installed during maintenance without proper engineering — the classic maintenance-driven change.
    Explanation
    Replacement in kind (identical part, same spec) needs no MOC. Different material, gasket type, seal plan, clamp/temporary repair, setpoint, or removing equipment needs MOC: hazard review, engineering calculation, approval, document updates (P&ID, datasheets, CMMS), training, PSSR.
    Types
    Permanent, temporary (with expiry), emergency MOC.
    Components
    MOC form, risk review, approvals, document updates, training records, close-out.
    Process / workflow
    Propose → review hazards → approve → implement → update documents → train → PSSR → close.
    O&G example
    The specified seal isn't in stock; a “similar” seal from another pump is proposed. It is a different seal plan — MOC review finds it is not suitable for the fluid temperature. Correct seal is expedited instead.
    Common mistakes
    “Equivalent” parts without review; temporary clamps that stay for years; drawings not updated.
    Best practices
    Clear replacement-in-kind rules; temporary MOC register with expiry dates.
    🧪 Practice activity: MOC or not?
    (a) Same-part-number bearing; (b) leak clamp on a gas line; (c) PSV set pressure raised 5%.
    Answer: (a) No; (b) Yes (temporary MOC with engineering assessment and expiry); (c) Yes.

    ⚖️ HAZOP & Risk Assessment for Maintenance

    Risk Assessment for Maintenance Work

    P&IDsafetyadvanced technical analysis
    Definition
    Identifying hazards of maintenance tasks and their effect on the process, and confirming controls reduce risk to an acceptable level (ALARP).
    Why it matters
    Maintenance creates temporary states the original HAZOP never considered: isolations, removed PSVs, open systems, bypassed trips.
    Explanation
    Task-level: JSA/JHA and toolbox risk assessment. Process-level: HAZOP (guidewords + parameters on P&ID nodes), What-If for non-routine maintenance (e.g. hot tapping), bow-tie to show which barriers are degraded during the work, LOPA for trip bypass decisions. Risk matrix = likelihood × severity.
    Types
    JSA, HAZOP, What-If, bow-tie, LOPA, SIMOPS review.
    Components
    Hazard list, risk matrix, barriers, compensating measures, approvals.
    Process / workflow
    Identify hazards → evaluate → controls → residual risk → approval → review when conditions change.
    O&G example
    What-If for removing a PSV online: “What if pressure rises while the PSV is off?” → controls: spare PSV in service, or unit pressure limited and high-pressure trip verified, operator monitoring, time limit, permits cross-referenced.
    Common mistakes
    Generic JSAs copied from old jobs; no re-assessment when scope changes.
    Best practices
    Involve the work team; review at the worksite; stop and re-assess on change.
    🧪 Practice activity: Score it
    Hot work 10 m from a gas compressor: likelihood 2, severity 5 on a 5×5 matrix. Score and action?
    Answer: 10 — medium-high: proceed only with added controls (habitat or shutdown of nearby equipment, continuous gas monitoring, fire watch) and senior approval.

    ⏳ Deferral of Maintenance

    Maintenance Deferral Management

    planningreportingprocess safety
    Definition
    The formal process for postponing maintenance beyond its due date — especially SCE and statutory tasks.
    Why it matters
    Uncontrolled deferral slowly removes barriers. Many investigations find long lists of overdue safety-critical maintenance before an accident.
    Explanation
    Each deferral needs: reason, risk assessment, compensating measures, new due date, approval at the right level (higher for SCEs), and tracking. Repeat deferrals trigger escalation.
    Types
    Non-critical deferral (supervisor/planner), SCE deferral (senior engineer/asset manager, sometimes verifier/regulator notification).
    Components
    Deferral form, risk matrix, compensating measures, deferral register, KPI.
    Process / workflow
    Request → assess → compensate → approve → track → complete → review trends.
    O&G example
    A firewater pump annual test is due during a crane outage. Deferral for 2 weeks is approved with a weekly run test and the second pump confirmed available.
    Common mistakes
    Deferrals as routine; no compensating measures; same item deferred repeatedly.
    Best practices
    Report deferred SCE tasks monthly to management as a leading indicator.
    🧪 Practice activity: Compensate
    Suggest one compensating measure for a deferred gas detector calibration in a compressor house.
    Answer: Portable gas monitoring/patrols in the area, bump test of the detector, or restricting hot work until it is calibrated.

    🏗️ Shutdown & Turnaround Coordination

    Shutdown & Turnaround Management

    planningschedulingproject executionresource allocation
    Definition
    A planned stop of a facility or unit to complete maintenance, inspection and modifications that cannot be done while running.
    Why it matters
    Turnarounds are the largest maintenance events — high cost, high risk (many contractors, SIMOPS) and every extra day is lost production.
    Explanation
    Phases: scope development & freeze (12–18 months ahead for large TAs; only work that truly needs shutdown) → planning (work packs, critical path, materials, contractors, blind lists) → pre-shutdown work → execution (24/7, daily progress meetings) → startup → post-TA review. Typical critical paths: compressor/turbine overhauls, vessel internal inspections, exchanger bundle work, catalyst changes.
    Types
    Full facility shutdown, unit turnaround, short planned outage, campaign maintenance.
    Components
    Worklist, schedule (Primavera/MS Project), resource histogram, materials plan, permit office, blind list, KPIs (safety, schedule, cost, scope growth).
    Process / workflow
    Challenge scope → freeze → plan and procure → readiness reviews → execute → hand back → lessons learned.
    O&G example
    An offshore platform 21-day shutdown: gas compressor overhaul is the critical path. Inspection discovers corroded piping — emergent work is assessed against the critical path; pre-fabricated spools (ordered after last year's UT) avoid an extension.
    Common mistakes
    Late scope additions; materials arriving late; compressing startup to recover schedule.
    Best practices
    Scope freeze discipline; materials on site before shutdown; never shortcut startup.
    🧪 Practice activity: Scope challenge
    Which item should NOT be in shutdown scope: (a) vessel internal inspection, (b) replacing a lube oil filter on a spare pump, (c) exchanger bundle pull?
    Answer: (b) — it can be done online, so keep it out of the shutdown.

    ▶️ Startup Readiness & Hand-back

    Startup Readiness & PSSR

    checklistsinspectionprocess safety
    Definition
    Confirming all maintenance work is complete, tested and documented, and that the plant is safe to start; the Pre-Startup Safety Review (PSSR) is the formal check.
    Why it matters
    Restarts after maintenance are high-risk: leaking joints, blinds left in, PSVs not reinstalled, trips left bypassed.
    Explanation
    Readiness: all work orders complete, punch list A-items closed, blind list reconciled, PSVs installed with isolation valves car-sealed open, SIS overrides removed and proof tests done, leak tests passed, MOC documents updated, operators briefed.
    Types
    Mechanical completion, PSSR, leak test, readiness walkdown.
    Components
    Punch list, blind list, PSV register, override register, test records, hand-back certificates.
    Process / workflow
    Walkdown → punch list → close A-items → PSSR sign-off → leak test → hand back to operations → startup.
    O&G example
    The PSSR walkdown finds a PSV inlet valve still closed and unsealed after reinstallation. It is opened and car-sealed — the vessel would have had no overpressure protection.
    Common mistakes
    PSSR without walkdown; startup with open A-items; overrides left on.
    Best practices
    Independent walkdown; reconcile every blind and seal; review the override register before startup.
    🧪 Practice activity: A-item or B-item?
    Classify: (a) missing handrail on a platform walkway, (b) PSV not installed, (c) missing paint on a support.
    Answer: (a) A-item — safety for people (fix before startup or barricade); (b) A-item — must close before startup; (c) B-item — can be closed after startup.

    🏛️ Maintenance Strategy & Asset Management

    Integrated Asset Management

    advanced technical analysisleadershipengineering
    Definition
    Asset management (ISO 55000) is the coordinated activity of an organisation to realise value from its assets over their whole life — balancing cost, risk and performance.
    Why it matters
    Maintenance decisions affect production, safety, emissions and lifetime cost. Experts link daily work to asset-level goals.
    Explanation

    🧩 Advanced Troubleshooting

    Chronic & Complex Failures

    troubleshootingdesign reviewRCA
    Definition
    Solving failures that repeat despite repairs, or whose causes lie in design, process conditions or system interactions.
    Why it matters
    Chronic failures are accepted as “normal” and quietly cost the most.
    Explanation
    Experts look beyond the component: process conditions (operating far from best efficiency point, two-phase flow), design (wrong seal plan, pipe strain, resonance), installation (foundation, grouting), and organisation (no spares, rushed jobs). Tools: operating deflection shape and modal analysis, failure metallurgy, process data correlation, OEM consultation.
    Types
    Design-related, process-induced, installation-related, maintenance-induced.
    Components
    Long-term history, process trends, specialist tests, cross-discipline team.
    Process / workflow
    Gather history → map failure timeline against process/maintenance events → hypotheses → specialist tests → design/process fix → verify MTBF.
    O&G example
    A reciprocating compressor's pipework cracks every year at the same small-bore connection. Vibration testing shows a pulsation resonance at running speed. Adding a support and orifice plate (via MOC) removes the resonance; no cracks since.
    Common mistakes
    Replacing like-for-like forever; ignoring small-bore connections.
    Best practices
    Escalate any failure repeated three times to formal RCA.
    🧪 Practice activity: Operating point
    A pump's seals fail often and it runs at 30% of design flow. What is the likely link?
    Answer: Running far left of best efficiency point causes recirculation, vibration and shaft deflection, damaging seals and bearings. Solutions: smaller impeller/pump, minimum-flow recirculation, or operating change.

    🧭 Maintenance Decision Making

    Repair, Replace, Run or Defer?

    problem-solvingcalculationsprocess safety
    Definition
    Choosing between repair, replacement, continued operation with monitoring, or deferral — under time and budget pressure.
    Why it matters
    Wrong decisions either waste money or let risk grow until failure.
    Explanation
    Priority order never changes: people → environment → asset → production. Consider risk (consequence × likelihood), life-cycle cost (repair cost vs replacement vs downtime), spares lead time, condition data and trend, and whether a safe operating window exists. Use pre-defined limits (e.g. vibration danger level → shut down) so decisions are not made under emotion.
    Types
    Run with monitoring, planned repair, immediate shutdown, replace/upgrade, formal deferral.
    Components
    Condition data, risk matrix, cost comparison, authority levels.
    Process / workflow
    Assess → options (including “stop”) → risk/cost each → decide at the right level → set monitoring triggers → review.
    O&G example
    A gas export compressor shows rising vibration (alert, not danger). Expert decision: continue with daily vibration and oil checks, set a shutdown trigger at the danger level, and plan bearing replacement in the outage 10 days away — spares confirmed.
    Common mistakes
    Running to danger level “for production”; no triggers; decisions by the wrong authority.
    Best practices
    Agreed triggers; document decisions and their basis.
    🧪 Practice activity: Repair or replace?
    A pump has failed 5 times in 2 years at €20k each; a better-designed replacement costs €60k and is expected to fail once in 5 years. Decision?
    Answer: Replace — ~€50k/year in repairs (plus downtime) versus €60k once; strong case on cost and reliability.

    📉 Operational Risk Management

    Cumulative Risk & Barrier Health

    advanced technical analysisreportingprocess safety
    Definition
    Ongoing control of the combined risk from degraded barriers, deferred maintenance, temporary repairs, overrides and simultaneous operations.
    Why it matters
    Each item may look acceptable; together they can remove all protection for one hazard.
    Explanation
    Registers: SCE failures, deferrals, temporary repairs/clamps, overrides. A cumulative risk review per area checks which barriers on the same hazard are degraded. SIMOPS planning prevents conflicting activities (e.g. hot work near a line being opened).
    Types
    Barrier degradation, SIMOPS, people/competence risk.
    Components
    Barrier dashboard, risk registers, review meeting, escalation thresholds.
    Process / workflow
    Log → assess individually → review cumulatively → compensate → escalate → restore.
    O&G example
    In one module: a gas detector faulty, deluge test deferred, a clamp on a gas line and planned hot work. The cumulative review stops the hot work and prioritises the detector and deluge.
    Common mistakes
    Reviewing items one by one; registers without owners.
    Best practices
    Weekly cumulative review; visible barrier dashboard.
    🧪 Practice activity: SIMOPS
    Why should line-breaking and hot work in the same area be reviewed together?
    Answer: Line-breaking can release hydrocarbons; hot work is an ignition source. Together they create a fire risk that neither permit alone would show.

    Scenario B · Repeated flange leaks after turnaround
    Situation: Six joints leak at restart.
    Expert response: Safe isolation and repair; RCA finds untrained contractors and uncontrolled torque tools; CAPA: joint integrity programme, certified technicians, joint tags, tool calibration control.

    Situation: 23 overdue SCE tasks, mostly F&G detectors.
    Expert response: Escalate to management, risk-assess each, compensate, dedicated instrument campaign, root cause (resources? access? planning?) and weekly reporting until zero.

    Situation: In-line inspection shows 55% wall loss at three locations.
    Expert response: Fitness-for-service assessment, possible pressure reduction, sleeve/clamp repairs via MOC, review cathodic protection and inhibitor programme, schedule re-inspection.

    🧭 Fundamentals

    What are the main types of maintenance?
    Corrective (after failure), preventive (time-based), predictive/condition-based (based on monitoring), statutory, shutdown/turnaround, and structured approaches such as RCM that choose the right one per failure mode.
    What is a CMMS and why does data quality matter?
    A Computerised Maintenance Management System (e.g. SAP PM, Maximo) holds equipment, plans, work orders and history. Reliability analysis, KPIs and audits depend on accurate tags, completion notes and failure codes.
    What is a safety-critical element?
    Equipment whose failure could cause, or fail to prevent or limit, a major accident — PSVs, ESD and blowdown valves, F&G detection, firewater pumps, deluge, the pressure envelope. They have performance standards and assurance tasks.
    Why must maintenance people understand P&IDs and the process?
    To define complete isolations, know what fluid and pressure are behind each joint, recognise process-induced failures and understand how their work affects safety and production.

    ⚙️ Equipment & Techniques

    A pump keeps failing its mechanical seal. How do you investigate?
    Check operating point vs BEP, seal flush plan and flow, alignment and pipe strain, bearing condition and vibration, seal selection for the fluid, installation records — then RCA with failed parts.
    How do you distinguish unbalance from misalignment by vibration?
    Unbalance shows mainly 1× running speed radially; misalignment often shows high axial vibration and strong 2× components.
    How do you maintain a PSV?
    Under permit: remove, as-found pop test, overhaul, set and seal, certify, reinstall, car-seal isolation valves open, update the PSV register — never leave equipment unprotected without a risk-assessed plan.
    What is important when maintaining Ex equipment?
    Only competent persons; isolate electrically; keep certification intact — correct glands, all bolts, flamepaths clean and within gap, certified like-for-like parts; record in the Ex register.
    How do you assemble a flange joint correctly?
    Clean and inspect faces, correct new gasket and bolts, lubricate, tighten in a cross pattern over several passes to the specified torque with a calibrated tool, record and tag the joint, leak test.

    🛡️ Planning, Safety & Quality

    What makes a job “ready to schedule”?
    Scoped in the field, job plan with steps and labour, materials kitted, tools/cranes/scaffold booked, permits and isolations identified, operations aware of required outage.
    What is backlog and how do you interpret it?
    Approved work not yet done, in crew-weeks (hours ÷ weekly capacity). Around 2–4 weeks of ready work is healthy; much higher suggests resource shortage, much lower suggests idle crews or under-reporting.
    What lessons does Piper Alpha teach maintenance?
    A PSV was removed and blinded; the night shift didn't know and started the pump. Lessons: cross-referenced permits, robust handover of suspended work, locking out equipment whose protection is removed.
    When does a maintenance change need MOC?
    Whenever it isn't replacement in kind — different material, design, seal plan, gasket type, setpoints, temporary repairs/clamps or bypasses.
    What is a hold point?
    A step in an ITP where work must stop until the inspection is performed and accepted — e.g. final alignment of a critical machine or PMI of alloy parts.

    🏆 Advanced & Expert

    How would you reduce reactive maintenance from 60% to 25%?
    Criticality review, PM optimisation/RCM on critical equipment, condition monitoring, planning and scheduling discipline, bad-actor elimination with RCA, spares strategy, and KPI tracking with management review.
    How do you decide whether to defer a safety-critical task?
    Only with formal risk assessment, compensating measures, approval at the right authority level, a firm new date and tracking; repeated deferral escalates. If risk cannot be controlled, don't defer.
    Which KPIs show a healthy maintenance organisation?
    High PM and 100% SCE compliance, schedule compliance ≥ ~85–90%, 2–4 weeks ready backlog, low reactive %, rising MTBF, low rework, cost in line with benchmarks, and good data quality.
    How do you calculate availability from MTBF and MTTR?
    Availability = MTBF ÷ (MTBF + MTTR). For MTBF 2,000 h and MTTR 30 h: 2,000 ÷ 2,030 ≈ 98.5%.

    👥 Role-Based & Behavioural

    (Supervisor) Your crew wants to skip gas testing for a “5-minute job”. What do you do?
    Strong answer: Stop; gas test and permit as required regardless of duration; explain why; record and coach.
    Weak answer: “If it's quick and nobody smells gas, it's fine.”
    (Planner) How do you build next week's schedule?
    Strong answer: Only ready work, by priority and criticality, matched to crew capacity and operations outage windows, agreed in the schedule meeting, tracked for compliance and break-ins.
    Weak answer: “I list everything open and see what gets done.”
    (Quality Engineer) Several flanges leaked after a shutdown. What do you do?
    Strong answer: Make safe, RCA (joint records, tools, training, gaskets), CAPA (joint integrity programme, tags, calibration control), verify at next startup.
    Weak answer: “Re-tighten them and move on.”
    (Analyst) What would your maintenance dashboard show?
    Strong answer: PM/SCE compliance, schedule compliance, backlog, reactive %, MTBF of critical equipment, top bad actors with owners, cost vs budget — each with a trend and an action.
    Weak answer: “All the data we have.”
    (Behavioural, STAR) Tell me about a breakdown you handled.
    Situation (compressor trip at night), Task (restore safely), Action (made safe with operations, permit and isolation, diagnosed failed coupling, replaced and aligned, tested), Result (back in 9 hours, RCA found missing torque values in job plan — task list corrected).

    1️⃣ Scenario 1 · Abnormal Equipment Condition

    Situation: Export compressor K-101 vibration rose from 3.0 to 6.8 mm/s over 5 days (alert 7.1, trip 11.0). Oil analysis shows increasing iron. A planned outage is in 12 days.
    Q. What is the best decision?
    • A Ignore it until the trip level
    • B Increase monitoring, set a shutdown trigger, prepare spares and work pack, and bring the outage forward if the trend accelerates
    • C Shut down immediately without analysis
    • D Raise the alarm limits
    ✍️ What would you prepare now so the repair is quick?
    Model answer: Diagnosis with the rotating equipment engineer (spectrum, bearing frequencies); bearing and seal spares confirmed in stock; OEM procedure and clearances; vendor support if needed; isolation list from P&ID; permits; crane/lifting plan; crew and shift plan; operations agreement on outage timing.

    2️⃣ Scenario 2 · Equipment Failure

    Situation: Produced-water pump P-401A has had its mechanical seal fail for the 4th time this year. P-401B is running. The pump runs at about 35% of design flow.
    Q. What is the best next step?
    • A Replace the seal again and close the work order
    • B Switch seal supplier without review
    • C Repair the seal and start a formal RCA as a bad actor, including the low-flow operating point
    • D Run P-401A to failure
    ✍️ List likely root causes and possible CAPA.
    Model answer: Causes: low-flow operation (recirculation, vibration, shaft deflection), blocked/insufficient seal flush, solids in produced water, misalignment, wrong seal type. CAPA: minimum-flow recirculation or re-rated impeller, appropriate seal plan (e.g. with filtration/cyclone separator), alignment check, operating guidance — via MOC; verify by MTBF.

    3️⃣ Scenario 3 · Process Deviation

    Situation: UT on the export pipeline launcher piping shows 45% wall loss; inspection says corrosion is internal and active. The line operates at 70 bar.
    Q. What is the right immediate response?
    • A Engineering fitness-for-service assessment, possibly reduce pressure, plan repair via MOC and review the corrosion inhibition programme
    • B Keep operating normally until the next shutdown
    • C Weld a patch on the live line immediately
    • D Paint the area to stop corrosion
    ✍️ What information does the engineer need for the assessment?
    Model answer: Remaining wall thickness map, corrosion rate history, design pressure and material, operating pressure/temperature, defect length and shape, inhibitor injection records, fluid analysis (CO2, H2S, water), and API 579 / pipeline code methods.

    4️⃣ Scenario 4 · Quality Issue

    Situation: After a weekend of maintenance, four flanged joints leak on restart. The joint records show different technicians, and one torque wrench was out of calibration.
    Q. What is the best Quality Engineer response?
    • A Re-tighten them live and move on
    • B Blame the technicians
    • C Accept small leaks as normal after maintenance
    • D Make safe and repair, raise an NCR, RCA and CAPA covering tool calibration, training and joint records
    ✍️ Write three CAPA actions.
    Model answer: (1) Tool calibration control — tags, register, quarantine of out-of-date tools; (2) joint integrity training/certification for all who make joints; (3) joint register and tags with torque records, plus N2 leak test before restart; review joints made with the uncalibrated tool.

    5️⃣ Scenario 5 · Maintenance Coordination

    Situation: A heavy valve must be replaced on the slug catcher. It needs a 50 t crane, a vendor technician, a 12-hour operations outage and scaffold. The crane is available only Tuesday or Friday.
    Q. What should the Coordinator and Planner do?
    • A Book the crane first and tell the others later
    • B Agree one date with operations, then align crane, vendor, scaffold, materials, permits and lift plan for that date
    • C Let the vendor pick the date
    • D Do the job without an outage
    ✍️ List the items to confirm 48 hours before the job.
    Model answer: Valve and gaskets/bolts on site and inspected; crane and lift plan approved; rigging certified; vendor confirmed; scaffold erected and tagged; isolation list and permits prepared; operations outage confirmed; weather forecast; SIMOPS check with other work in the area.

    6️⃣ Scenario 6 · Safety Incident

    Situation: During flange breaking on a sour gas line, a technician's H2S monitor alarms at 15 ppm. The isolation certificate shows a single closed valve upstream.
    Q. What should happen first?
    • A Finish the job quickly
    • B Tighten the flange and continue later
    • C Stop work, move upwind, raise the alarm; operations secures the line; investigate the isolation (single valve passing)
    • D Remove the gas monitor
    ✍️ What should the investigation recommend?
    Model answer: Minimum double block and bleed (or positive isolation) for sour/hazardous service; verification of zero pressure and gas-free before breaking; BA for first break; isolation standard review; permit issuer training; check why a single-valve isolation was approved.

    7️⃣ Scenario 7 · Shutdown

    Situation: A 14-day plant shutdown is planned in 4 months. The worklist has 640 items; 180 could be done online.
    Q. What should the planning team do with the 180 online-capable items?
    • A Remove them from shutdown scope and schedule them online before or after
    • B Keep them — more work in the shutdown is better
    • C Cancel them
    • D Add them to the critical path
    ✍️ List four key shutdown readiness checks one month before.
    Model answer: Scope frozen and work packs complete; long-lead materials on site; contractors mobilised and inducted; critical path schedule and resource histogram approved; blind lists and isolation plans ready; permit office staffed; shutdown/startup procedures reviewed.

    8️⃣ Scenario 8 · Startup & Readiness

    Situation: Shutdown is complete, one day late. Open items: two PSVs still in the workshop, 3 blinds unconfirmed, and a deluge valve test not done. Production wants to start tonight.
    Q. What is the right decision?
    • A Start up and fit the PSVs later
    • B Blind the PSV nozzles and start
    • C Skip the deluge test because it passed last year

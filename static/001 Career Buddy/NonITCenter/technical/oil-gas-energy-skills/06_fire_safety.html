    Fire Safety Fundamentals in Oil & Gas

    Start from zero: why fire is the defining hazard of oil and gas, how fire starts and spreads, the fire classes and agents, the special fire and explosion types of hydrocarbons, and the basic rules everyone follows.

    🔥 What is Fire Safety in Oil & Gas?

    Fire Safety Function

    risk assessmentsafety proceduresincident/emergency response
    Definition
    Fire safety is the prevention of fires and explosions, their early detection, control and extinguishment, and the protection and safe escape of people — across wells, platforms, plants, pipelines, tank farms and terminals.
    Why it matters
    Oil and gas facilities hold large amounts of flammable liquids and gases, often under pressure and above their flash points. A single ignition can escalate to explosions, structural collapse and loss of life within minutes.
    Explanation
    Fire safety works in layers: prevent leaks and ignition → detect gas and fire early → control by isolation and shutdown → protect with passive and active systems → respond with trained teams → escape, evacuate, rescue. Fire safety teams combine engineering, inspection and testing of fire systems, procedures, training and emergency response.
    Types
    Fire prevention, fire protection engineering, fire system inspection/testing/maintenance, firefighting and emergency response, fire safety training and assurance.
    Components
    Fire risk assessment, ignition control, fire & gas detection, ESD, passive fire protection, firewater and foam systems, extinguishers, fire teams, emergency response plan, drills.
    Process / workflow
    Identify fire hazards → prevent and control ignition → install and maintain protection → train and drill → respond → investigate and improve.
    O&G example
    On a gas platform, a small flange leak is detected by gas detectors at 20% LEL. ESD isolates the module, ignition sources are shut down and deluge is ready. The release disperses without ignition — layered fire safety worked.
    Common mistakes
    Relying only on firefighting; letting fire system maintenance slip; treating “minor” hot work casually.
    Best practices
    Prevention first; keep detection and protection systems tested; drill realistic scenarios.
    🧪 Practice activity: Which layer?
    Classify: (a) gas detector, (b) fireproofing on steel, (c) hot work permit, (d) fire team.
    Answer: (a) detection; (b) passive protection; (c) prevention/ignition control; (d) response.
    EXAMPLE · One fire pump, many hands

    The Engineer calculates firewater demand; the Planner schedules the weekly pump test; the Technician runs it; the Inspector records pressures; the Quality Engineer tracks failed tests to closure; the Analyst trends pump availability. All ten Fire Safety roles keep one barrier ready.

    🔺 Fire Triangle & Combustion Basics

    Figure 1.1 · Remove any side and the fire goes out

    Combustion Basics

    risk assessmentengineering
    Definition
    Fire is a rapid chemical reaction between a fuel and oxygen, started by heat/ignition and sustained by a chain reaction (the fire tetrahedron).
    Why it matters
    Every fire prevention and extinguishing method works by removing one element: fuel (isolation), oxygen (smothering, foam, CO2), heat (cooling with water), or the chain reaction (dry chemical powder).
    Explanation
    • Flash point: lowest temperature at which a liquid gives off enough vapour to ignite briefly (petrol well below 0 °C, diesel above ~55 °C).
    • Auto-ignition temperature (AIT): temperature at which a substance ignites without a flame or spark — hot surfaces and hot oil leaks can self-ignite.
    • LEL/UEL: a gas burns only between its lower and upper explosive limits (methane ≈ 5–15% in air).
    • Minimum ignition energy: hydrogen and ethylene ignite with tiny sparks.
    Types
    Flaming combustion, smouldering, deflagration, detonation.
    Components
    Fuel properties (SDS), ignition sources, ventilation, confinement.
    Process / workflow
    Know the fuel properties → identify ignition sources → keep fuel and ignition apart → plan how to remove a side of the triangle.
    O&G example
    Hot residue oil above its auto-ignition temperature leaks from a pump seal and ignites immediately without any spark — the ignition source was the oil's own heat.
    Common mistakes
    Assuming no flame = no ignition; ignoring hot surfaces and static.
    Best practices
    Use SDS data (flash point, AIT, LEL) in risk assessments and permits.
    🧪 Practice activity: Which side?
    Which side of the triangle does each remove: (a) closing an ESD valve, (b) water cooling, (c) foam blanket?
    Answer: (a) fuel; (b) heat; (c) oxygen (and suppresses vapour).

    💥 Hydrocarbon Fire & Explosion Types

    Hydrocarbon Fire Scenarios

    risk assessmentincident/emergency responseadvanced technical analysis
    Definition
    The characteristic fire and explosion events that follow a release of hydrocarbons.
    Why it matters
    Each type needs different protection and response; using the wrong tactic can make it worse.
    Explanation
    • Pool fire: burning liquid pool — high radiant heat, smoke.
    • Jet fire: pressurised gas/liquid release ignited — intense, erosive flame that can fail steel within minutes.
    • Flash fire: delayed ignition of a vapour cloud burning back without significant overpressure.
    • Vapour cloud explosion (VCE): ignition of a vapour cloud in congested/confined areas producing damaging overpressure.
    • BLEVE: boiling liquid expanding vapour explosion — a pressurised liquefied gas vessel (LPG) weakened by fire ruptures, creating a fireball.
    • Boilover: in a burning crude tank, a hot zone reaches water at the bottom, which flashes to steam and violently ejects burning oil.
    Types
    Fires (pool, jet, flash) and explosions (VCE, BLEVE, confined explosions).
    Components
    Release rate, ignition timing, congestion, wind, inventory.
    Process / workflow
    Identify credible scenarios → consequence modelling → protection design → response pre-plans.
    O&G example
    Buncefield (UK, 2005): a petrol storage tank overfilled, forming a large vapour cloud that ignited in a massive explosion and fire; 43 people were injured and the fire burned for days. It changed tank overfill protection practice.
    Common mistakes
    Planning only for pool fires; ignoring jet fire impingement on vessels; no BLEVE cooling plan.
    Best practices
    Scenario-specific protection and pre-incident plans.
    🧪 Practice activity: Name it
    An LPG sphere engulfed in fire suddenly ruptures with a huge fireball. What event?
    Answer: BLEVE — prevented by cooling water on the vessel shell and pressure relief/depressuring.

    🛑 Basic Fire Safety Rules

    • Know your alarms, escape routes and muster point on day one.
    • No smoking or naked flames except in designated areas; no unapproved electrical equipment (phones, cameras) in hazardous areas.
    • Hot work only under permit, with gas testing and a fire watch.
    • Never block fire equipment, escape routes or fire doors.
    • Report leaks, damaged fire equipment and impairments immediately.
    • On alarm: stop work, make your job safe if possible, go to muster, report for headcount.
    • Fight a fire only if trained, it is small, you have an escape route behind you and the alarm has been raised.
    EXAMPLE · Small fire decision

    A small fire starts in oily rags near a workshop bench. A trained worker raises the alarm, keeps the exit behind them and uses a nearby extinguisher — it's out in seconds. If it had spread beyond the bench, the right action would be to leave and let the fire team respond.

    Fire Risk Assessment & Prevention

    Prevent fires before they start: assess fire risk, control ignition sources, understand hazardous areas, estimate fire and explosion consequences, and manage hot work, housekeeping and storage.

    ⚖️ Fire Risk Assessment

    Fire Risk Assessment

    risk assessmentinspectiondocumentation
    Definition
    A systematic review of fire hazards (fuels, ignition sources, oxygen), people at risk, existing controls and the actions needed to reduce fire risk.
    Why it matters
    It is the basis for every fire precaution — and a legal requirement in many countries for workplaces.
    Explanation
    Steps: (1) identify fuels and ignition sources; (2) identify people at risk (incl. contractors, visitors, people with limited mobility); (3) evaluate and reduce risk — prevention, detection, protection, escape; (4) record findings and plan; (5) review regularly and after changes. For process plants, this links to HAZID, fire and explosion risk assessment (FERA) and consequence modelling.
    Types
    Building/workplace fire risk assessment, process fire and explosion risk assessment, task fire risk assessment (hot work).
    Components
    Site walk, fuel/ignition inventory, escape route review, protection systems review, action plan.
    Process / workflow
    Walk → identify → evaluate → act → record → review.
    O&G example
    A warehouse storing drums of lubricants and solvents next to a battery charging area: the assessment separates them, adds ventilation and a fire detection upgrade, and marks a clear escape route.
    Common mistakes
    Desk-only assessments; not reviewing after changes; ignoring contractors.
    Best practices
    Walk the site; involve users; review annually and after modifications.
    🧪 Practice activity: Spot the fuel and ignition
    A diesel generator sits next to a store of cardboard packaging. Identify fuel and ignition sources.
    Answer: Fuels: diesel, cardboard. Ignition: hot exhaust, electrical faults. Action: separate storage, keep combustibles away from hot surfaces, provide extinguishers and detection.

    🗺️ Hazardous Area Classification

    Hazardous Area (Zone) Classification

    drawings/specificationsrisk assessmenttechnical design
    Definition
    Dividing a facility into zones according to how likely an explosive gas atmosphere is (IEC 60079-10-1; US uses Class/Division or Zones).
    Why it matters
    It decides where certified (Ex) equipment is required and which activities need special permits.
    Explanation
    Zone 0: explosive atmosphere present continuously or for long periods (inside tanks). Zone 1: likely in normal operation (around vents, sample points). Zone 2: not likely, and only briefly if it occurs (around flanges and valves). Equipment must be certified for the zone, gas group and temperature class (T-class must be below the gas's auto-ignition temperature).
    Types
    Gas zones 0/1/2; dust zones 20/21/22.
    Components
    Area classification drawings, Ex equipment register, inspection records.
    Process / workflow
    Identify release sources → grade → ventilation → zone extent → drawings → equipment selection → inspection.
    O&G example
    A contractor brings a non-Ex electric fan into a Zone 1 area to cool a confined space. The Fire Safety Inspector stops its use and supplies an Ex-certified air mover.
    Common mistakes
    Non-certified portable equipment; outdated drawings after modifications.
    Best practices
    Portable equipment control; keep drawings current through MOC.
    🧪 Practice activity: Zone
    Inside the vapour space of a fixed-roof petrol tank — which zone?
    Answer: Zone 0.

    Consequence Assessment

    calculationsadvanced technical analysisrisk assessment
    Definition
    Estimating the effects of fires and explosions — radiant heat, flame size, overpressure, smoke — on people, equipment and buildings.
    Why it matters
    It defines safe distances, escape routes, muster locations, building design and where to apply protection.
    Explanation
    Fire Safety Engineers use consequence models (e.g. PHAST, FLACS, or simpler correlations) for pool and jet fires (flame length, radiation contours) and explosions (overpressure contours). Results feed the fire and explosion risk assessment (FERA), passive fire protection extents and escape/evacuation analysis.
    Types
    Radiation, overpressure, toxic and smoke modelling.
    Components
    Release scenarios, weather data, layout, model outputs, criteria.
    Process / workflow
    Scenarios → model → compare with criteria → design actions.
    O&G example
    Modelling shows a muster point would receive more than 5 kW/m² from a tank pool fire. The muster point is moved behind a building out of the radiation contour.
    Common mistakes
    Single weather case; ignoring escalation to neighbouring equipment.
    Best practices
    Several wind directions and speeds; check escalation targets.
    🧪 Practice activity: Muster check
    Why must muster points be outside predicted high-radiation zones?
    Answer: People at muster are unprotected and waiting; radiation above a few kW/m² causes pain and burns within seconds to minutes.

    🔥 Hot Work Fire Prevention

    Hot Work Controls

    safety proceduressafetychecklists
    Definition
    Controls for welding, cutting, grinding and other work that produces heat or sparks.
    Why it matters
    Hot work is a leading cause of industrial fires; sparks can travel many metres and ignite vapours or combustibles hidden behind walls or in drains.
    Explanation
    Hierarchy: avoid hot work (cold cutting, bolted connections) → move it to a safe workshop → control it on site with a hot work permit: gas test (typically 0% LEL before start and continuous monitoring), remove/cover combustibles (common guidance: within about 11 m / 35 ft), cover drains, fire watch during and for a period after (commonly at least 30–60 minutes), extinguisher/hose ready, welding habitat in hazardous areas.
    Types
    Open-flame, spark-producing, habitat (pressurised enclosure) hot work.
    Components
    Permit, gas detector, fire watch, fire blankets, extinguishers, habitat.
    Process / workflow
    Justify → plan → prepare area → gas test → issue permit → fire watch → monitor → post-work fire watch → close.
    O&G example
    Grinding on a pipe rack: sparks fall through grating onto an oily drain two levels below. Covering drains and using spark containment blankets is part of the permit.
    Common mistakes
    Gas test done hours earlier; fire watch leaving with the welder; drains uncovered.
    Best practices
    Continuous monitoring; post-work fire watch; avoid hot work where possible.
    🧪 Practice activity: Fire watch
    Why does the fire watch stay after the hot work ends?
    Answer: Smouldering fires from hot slag or sparks can start minutes later in hidden places.

    🧹 Housekeeping & Storage

    • Keep oily rags in closed metal bins; empty daily.
    • Store flammable liquids in approved cabinets or stores, away from ignition sources and exits; limit quantities at workplaces.
    • Separate oxidisers (oxygen cylinders) from fuel gas cylinders; keep cylinders upright and secured.
    • Keep escape routes, fire doors, hydrants, monitors and extinguishers clear.
    • Clean up spills immediately; control vegetation around tank farms and pipelines.
    EXAMPLE · Cylinder storage

    An inspection finds oxygen and acetylene cylinders stored together next to an exit. They are separated (or divided by a fire-rated barrier), secured upright, and moved away from the escape route.

    Fire Detection & Protection Systems

    The engineered barriers that detect and fight fire: fire and gas detection, passive fire protection, firewater pumps and networks, deluge and sprinklers, foam, and gaseous suppression.

    📡 Fire & Gas Detection

    Fire & Gas (F&G) Systems

    technical designequipment/process operationincident/emergency response
    Definition
    Detectors and logic that sense flammable/toxic gas, flame, heat or smoke and trigger alarms and automatic actions (ESD, deluge, ventilation shutdown).
    Why it matters
    Early detection gives time to isolate leaks before ignition and to escape before fire escalates.
    Explanation
    • Gas detectors: point catalytic/IR detectors and open-path IR beams; typical alarm settings around 20% LEL (low) and 40–60% LEL (high) — company-specific.
    • Flame detectors: UV, IR, UV/IR and multi-spectrum IR — fast detection of hydrocarbon flames.
    • Heat detectors: fusible links, rate-of-rise, linear heat cable.
    • Smoke detectors: buildings, control rooms, electrical rooms.
    • Voting (e.g. 2-out-of-N confirmed detection) reduces spurious trips for executive actions.
    Types
    Point and open-path; conventional and addressable; manual call points.
    Components
    Detectors, F&G logic controller, cause-and-effect chart, alarms (audible/visual), interfaces to ESD and deluge.
    Process / workflow
    Mapping study (coverage) → design → install → commission → test (calibration, functional tests) → maintain.
    O&G example
    A detector-mapping study for a compressor module finds a coverage gap behind a large vessel; two additional IR point detectors are added.
    Common mistakes
    Detectors blocked or covered during maintenance and not restored; overdue calibration; inhibited detectors forgotten.
    Best practices
    Inhibit register with time limits; calibration schedule; coverage mapping after layout changes.
    🧪 Practice activity: Detector choice
    Which detector best protects an outdoor gas turbine enclosure against fire: smoke detector or flame detector?
    Answer: Flame detector (plus heat/gas detection) — smoke detectors work poorly in open, ventilated hydrocarbon areas.

    🧱 Passive Fire Protection

    Passive Fire Protection (PFP)

    drawings/specificationsinspectionengineering
    Definition
    Fire protection that works without activation: fireproofing coatings on steel, fire-rated walls and doors, insulation of vessels, cable fire protection.
    Why it matters
    It buys time — keeping structures and vessels intact long enough for isolation, depressuring and escape.
    Explanation
    Ratings describe the fire type and duration, e.g. H-120 (hydrocarbon pool fire curve, 120 minutes), J-rated (jet fire resistance), A-60 (cellulosic fire division). Materials: cementitious, intumescent and epoxy intumescent coatings, mineral wool, fire-rated panels. PFP must be maintained — damage and water ingress can cause corrosion under fireproofing.
    Types
    Structural fireproofing, fire walls/divisions, vessel and support protection, cable/ESD valve protection.
    Components
    PFP specification, extent drawings, inspection records.
    Process / workflow
    Fire scenarios → PFP extent → specification → application QA → periodic inspection and repair.
    O&G example
    An inspection finds cracked fireproofing on pipe-rack columns with rust stains. Repair is planned and the columns inspected for corrosion under the fireproofing.
    Common mistakes
    Removing PFP for maintenance and not replacing it; ignoring cracks.
    Best practices
    PFP register and inspection plan; reinstatement as a hold point after maintenance.
    🧪 Practice activity: Decode
    What does H-120 mean?
    Answer: Resistant to a hydrocarbon fire (H curve) for 120 minutes.

    🚰 Firewater Systems & Pumps

    Firewater Supply

    equipment/process operationcalculationspreventive maintenance
    Definition
    The water supply, fire pumps and distribution network (ring main, hydrants, monitors, hose reels) that feed firefighting and fixed systems.
    Why it matters
    Without reliable water at the right pressure and flow, deluge, monitors and foam systems fail.
    Explanation
    Design to the largest credible fire demand plus hose allowance for the required duration. Fire pumps (NFPA 20 / EN 12845 principles) usually include electric and diesel drivers for redundancy, a jockey pump to keep the main pressurised, and automatic start on pressure drop. Ring mains have sectional isolation valves so a break doesn't take out the whole system. Testing per NFPA 25 principles: weekly/periodic churn runs, annual flow tests.
    Types
    Seawater systems (offshore), tank/reservoir supply (onshore), town main backup.
    Components
    Tanks, fire pumps, jockey pump, controllers, ring main, hydrants, monitors, valves.
    Process / workflow
    Demand calculation → design → commissioning flow test → routine testing → impairment management.
    O&G example
    The diesel fire pump fails to start during its weekly test (flat battery). It is declared impaired, the electric pump confirmed available, compensating measures applied, and the battery replaced the same day.
    Common mistakes
    Fire pumps left in manual; missed weekly tests; closed ring-main valves after maintenance.
    Best practices
    Automatic mode always; valve position checks; trend pump test results.
    🧪 Practice activity: Demand
    A deluge zone needs 10.2 L/min/m² over 400 m², plus 2,000 L/min hose allowance. Total demand?
    Answer: 10.2 × 400 = 4,080 L/min + 2,000 = 6,080 L/min.

    🫧 Foam Systems

    Foam Firefighting

    incident/emergency responsecalculationstechnical design
    Definition
    Firefighting foam is a blanket of bubbles made from foam concentrate, water and air that smothers flammable liquid fires, suppresses vapours and cools.
    Why it matters
    Water alone spreads burning hydrocarbons; foam is the main agent for liquid pool and tank fires.
    Explanation
    Concentrate types: AFFF/AR-AFFF (film-forming; alcohol-resistant versions for polar solvents), fluoroprotein, and newer fluorine-free foams replacing PFAS-containing foams for environmental reasons. Proportioning at 1%, 3% or 6%. Application rate and duration come from standards such as NFPA 11 (e.g. a minimum around 4.1 L/min/m² for some fixed tank systems, higher for monitors). Systems: tank rim seal foam, subsurface injection, foam pourers, monitors, bund foam.
    Types
    Low, medium and high-expansion foam; fixed, semi-fixed and mobile systems.
    Components
    Concentrate tanks, proportioners, foam makers/pourers, monitors, concentrate quality tests.
    Process / workflow
    Calculate area × rate × duration → concentrate volume → design → test proportioning and concentrate quality annually.
    O&G example
    A 40 m diameter tank (1,257 m²) at 4.1 L/min/m² needs about 5,150 L/min of foam solution; with 3% concentrate for 55 minutes that's ~8,500 L of concentrate (before allowances).
    Common mistakes
    Wrong concentrate for the fuel; degraded concentrate; proportioners never tested.
    Best practices
    Annual concentrate testing; realistic proportioning tests; plan PFAS transition.
    🧪 Practice activity: Why not water?
    Why is a water jet dangerous on a burning oil pool?
    Answer: Water sinks and can boil violently, splashing and spreading burning oil; foam floats and smothers it.

    🧪 Gaseous & Special Suppression

    • CO2 systems: flood enclosed rooms (e.g. turbine enclosures, switch rooms); lethal to people — lock-off and pre-discharge alarms required during occupancy/maintenance.
    • Clean agents (inert gases, chemical agents): computer and control rooms.
    • Dry chemical powder: fast knock-down of hydrocarbon fires, e.g. at well sites and helidecks, often combined with foam.
    • Portable and wheeled extinguishers: first-aid firefighting (Module 04).
    ⚠️
    Before entering a space protected by CO2, the system must be locked off and the entry controlled by permit — accidental discharges have caused fatalities.

    🧯 Portable Extinguishers

    Using Portable Extinguishers

    incident/emergency responsetoolssafety
    Definition
    Hand-held and wheeled extinguishers for first-aid firefighting of small fires.
    Why it matters
    A trained person with the right extinguisher can stop a small fire in seconds — before it escalates.
    Explanation
    Types: water (A), foam (A, B), dry powder (A, B, C, electrical), CO2 (B, electrical), wet chemical (F/K). Technique PASS: Pull the pin, Aim at the base of the fire, Squeeze the handle, Sweep side to side. Rules: raise the alarm first, keep an escape route behind you, approach upwind, back away if it doesn't go out quickly.
    Types
    Hand-held (e.g. 6–9 kg), wheeled units (50 kg+) for process areas and helidecks.
    Components
    Cylinder, agent, gauge (stored pressure), pin and tamper seal, hose, inspection tag.
    Process / workflow
    Alarm → select correct extinguisher → PASS → watch for re-ignition → report and replace.
    O&G example
    A small fire on a diesel spill near a generator: a trained operator uses a dry powder extinguisher from upwind, aiming at the base — out in seconds; the generator is isolated and the area cooled and cleaned.
    Common mistakes
    Aiming at the flames, not the base; using water on oil or live electrics; fighting a fire that's too big.
    Best practices
    Hands-on training with live fire props; know locations; never fight alone without raising alarm.
    🧪 Practice activity: Pick the extinguisher
    Fire in an energised electrical panel. Which extinguisher?
    Answer: CO2 (or suitable dry powder) — and isolate the power as soon as possible.

    ITM Programme

    preventive maintenanceschedulingequipment/process operation
    Definition
    The planned schedule of inspections, tests and maintenance that keeps fire systems ready to perform.
    Why it matters
    Fire systems sit idle for years; hidden failures are only found by testing — or when it's too late.
    Explanation

    Types
    Inspection (visual), testing (function), maintenance (repair/replace).
    Components
    Equipment register, task lists, CMMS, test records, failure follow-up.
    Process / workflow
    Schedule → perform → record results vs criteria → fail = impairment and repair → trend.
    O&G example
    Annual flow test shows a hydrant delivering 30% less flow than design; investigation finds a partially closed sectional valve after maintenance.
    Common mistakes
    Tick-box testing; tests overdue; failures not followed up.
    Best practices
    Record measured values; 100% compliance target for safety-critical fire systems.

    ⛔ Fire System Impairment Management

    Impairment Management

    risk assessmentsafety procedurescommunication
    Definition
    The formal process for controlling risk when a fire protection system is out of service — planned (maintenance) or unplanned (failure).
    Why it matters

    Components

    Process / workflow

    O&G example

    Common mistakes
    Silent impairments; long-term impairments with no end date.
    Best practices
    Daily impairment review; escalate impairments past their expiry.

    🧑‍🚒 Fire Response Organisation

    Incident Command for Fires

    incident/emergency responseleadershipcommunication
    Definition
    The structure that commands and coordinates fire response — on-scene commander, fire/emergency response teams (ERT), operations, medical and support.
    Why it matters
    Clear command avoids duplicated effort, unsafe actions and lost information in the first critical minutes.
    Explanation
    Typical roles: incident commander (overall control), fire team leader, operations (isolation, ESD, depressuring), medical, logistics, communications, liaison with public fire services and mutual aid. Size-up: what is burning, what is exposed, is the source isolated, wind direction, people missing, resources available.
    Types
    Site fire brigade, ERT of trained workers, contracted fire service, mutual aid, municipal fire service.
    Components
    Command post, radios, pre-incident plans, resource lists, status boards.
    Process / workflow
    Alarm → size-up → isolate and protect people → set strategy (offensive/defensive) → tactics → monitor → handover/recovery.
    O&G example
    At a refinery pump fire, the incident commander confirms operations has isolated the pump suction, assigns one crew to cool the adjacent column and another to apply foam, and reports to the emergency management team every 15 minutes.
    Common mistakes
    Everyone fighting the fire, nobody commanding; no accountability for firefighters.
    Best practices
    Single commander; accountability boards; pre-incident plans.
    🧪 Practice activity: Size-up
    List five size-up questions for a process fire.
    Answer: What is burning and how much? Is the source isolated/can it be? What is exposed (vessels, people)? Wind direction? Anyone missing/injured? Available water, foam, crews?

    🚒 Hydrocarbon Firefighting Tactics

    PriorityAction
    1 · Life safetyRescue, evacuation, accountability
    2 · Isolate fuelESD, block valves, depressuring — the most effective “extinguishment”
    3 · Protect exposuresCool vessels, structures and pipe racks with water/deluge
    4 · Control/extinguishFoam for liquid fires; let gas fires burn until isolated

    ⚠️
    Choosing a defensive strategy (protect exposures, let fuel burn out) can be the correct decision when isolation is impossible or extinguishing would create a greater hazard.

    🛢️ Storage Tank Fires

    Tank Fire Response

    incident/emergency responsecalculationsadvanced technical analysis
    Definition
    Fires in atmospheric storage tanks — from small rim-seal fires on floating-roof tanks to full-surface fires.
    Why it matters
    Full-surface tank fires are among the largest and hardest fires; crude tanks can boil over.
    Explanation
    Rim-seal fires (lightning is a common cause) are handled with fixed rim-seal foam systems. Full-surface fires need large foam monitors at calculated rates, enough concentrate and water for the full duration, and cooling of adjacent tanks. Boilover risk in crude/heavy oils: a hot zone moves down and reaches water at the tank bottom — burning oil can be thrown far beyond the bund. Signs include increased flame intensity and frothing; evacuation distances must be large.
    Types
    Rim seal, vent, full surface, bund (dike) fires.
    Components
    Rim-seal systems, foam pourers, large-capacity monitors, concentrate stock, cooling water.
    Process / workflow
    Size-up → isolate/transfer product → cool exposures → assemble foam resources → attack only with full resources → maintain blanket.
    O&G example
    A lightning strike starts a rim-seal fire on a floating-roof crude tank. The fixed rim-seal foam system extinguishes it within minutes; the team checks the roof seal and pontoons afterwards.
    Common mistakes
    Starting foam attack without enough concentrate; underestimating boilover distance.
    Best practices
    Pre-calculated tank fire plans; mutual aid for large-capacity resources.
    🧪 Practice activity: Foam demand
    Tank area 1,000 m², rate 6.5 L/min/m² by monitors, 3% concentrate, 65 minutes. Concentrate needed?
    Answer: 6,500 L/min solution × 3% = 195 L/min × 65 min ≈ 12,700 L (plus reserves).

    💨 Gas Fires & BLEVE Prevention

    Gas Jet Fires & Pressurised Vessels

    incident/emergency responserisk assessmentengineering
    Definition
    Response to fires fed by pressurised gas or liquefied gas, and protection of pressurised vessels (LPG spheres, bullets) from BLEVE.
    Why it matters
    Extinguishing an unisolated gas fire can create an explosive cloud; flame impingement on a vessel above its liquid level can cause rupture in minutes.
    Explanation
    Gas fires: isolate and depressure, let it burn while cooling exposures; extinguish only to allow isolation if safe. BLEVE prevention: water cooling on vessel shells (especially vapour space), depressuring, PFP on supports and shells, relief valves. If a vessel is engulfed and cooling cannot be applied, withdraw to a safe distance — responders have died near BLEVEs.
    Types
    Jet fires, flange/valve fires, LPG vessel exposure fires.
    Components
    ESD and blowdown, deluge on vessels, unmanned monitors, evacuation distances.
    Process / workflow
    Isolate → cool with fixed systems/unmanned monitors → depressure → withdraw if cooling fails.
    O&G example
    A jet fire impinges on an LPG bullet. Deluge activates automatically, the bullet is depressured to flare, and fire crews set unmanned monitors and withdraw outside the BLEVE hazard distance.
    Common mistakes
    Putting out the flame and leaving gas flowing; staying close to an engulfed vessel.
    Best practices
    Unmanned monitors; pre-planned withdrawal distances.
    🧪 Practice activity: Why cool the vapour space?
    Why is cooling the vapour space of an LPG vessel especially important?
    Answer: The steel above the liquid level isn't cooled by the liquid, so it heats quickly, loses strength and can fail while internal pressure is high — causing a BLEVE.

    🦺 Firefighter Safety & PPE

    • Structural/industrial firefighting PPE: fire-resistant turnout gear, helmet, gloves, boots, SCBA.
    • Accountability: tag/board system showing who is where and for how long (air time).
    • Rehab and heat stress management for crews.
    • Approach upwind and uphill; watch for flashback, wind shifts and structural collapse.
    • Never enter areas with possible gas release without gas monitoring.
    EXAMPLE · Air management

    A crew's SCBA low-air alarm sounds during an interior search; the crew leader withdraws the whole team, and the accountability officer confirms all are out before the next crew enters.

    🗺️ Emergency Response Plan & Pre-Fire Plans

    ERP & Pre-Incident Plans

    incident/emergency responseplanningdocumentation
    Definition
    The ERP defines the overall emergency organisation; pre-fire (pre-incident) plans give area-specific tactics: hazards, inventories, isolation points, water supplies, access routes and exposures.
    Why it matters
    In a fire, the commander needs facts in seconds — pre-fire plans provide them.
    Explanation
    Pre-fire plan content: plot plan, inventories and fuel types, isolation and ESD points, firewater/foam resources and capacities, credible scenarios and tactics, exposure protection priorities, evacuation distances, mutual aid. Kept in command vehicles and control rooms; reviewed after changes.
    Types
    Site ERP, unit pre-fire plans, tank fire plans, mutual aid agreements.
    Components
    Drawings, data sheets, resource calculations, contact lists.
    Process / workflow
    Scenarios → resources → tactics → document → train → test → update.
    O&G example
    A tank farm pre-fire plan pre-calculates foam and water demand for the largest tank; it shows on-site concentrate is insufficient, so a mutual aid agreement with a neighbouring terminal is signed.
    Common mistakes
    Plans out of date after modifications; resources never checked against demand.
    Best practices
    Annual review; check every plan in drills.
    🧪 Practice activity: Pre-fire plan
    List five items in a pre-fire plan for a pump house.
    Answer: Fluids and inventories, isolation/ESD valve locations, nearest hydrants/monitors and foam, electrical isolation points, access and escape routes, exposures, special hazards (H2S).

    🏃 Evacuation, Escape & Muster

    • Escape routes: at least two directions where practicable, marked, lit (emergency lighting) and kept clear.
    • Muster points / temporary refuge: outside fire and smoke effect zones; offshore, the temporary refuge (TR) protects people while evacuation is organised.
    • Headcount: card/electronic POB systems; missing persons trigger search and rescue.
    • Evacuation means offshore: lifeboats, liferafts, helicopters — escape, evacuation and rescue (EER) analysis checks they work in credible scenarios.
    • Special needs: visitors, contractors, people with limited mobility.
    EXAMPLE · Escape route check

    An escape route review after a module extension finds one route now ends at a dead end behind new equipment. A new stairway is added before the module is put in service.

    🛡️ Fire Systems as Safety-Critical Elements

    Performance Standards for Fire Systems

    risk assessmentdesign reviewdocumentation
    Definition
    Fire and gas detection, ESD, deluge, firewater pumps, PFP and escape systems are safety-critical elements (SCEs) with written performance standards.
    Why it matters
    Performance standards turn “we have a deluge system” into “water reaches every nozzle within 30 s at the required density” — measurable and testable.
    Explanation
    Each standard covers functionality (what it must do), availability/reliability, survivability (will it work after the explosion?) and dependencies (power, water). ITM tasks test against these criteria; failures are reported as barrier impairments.
    Types
    Detection, control, mitigation and EER barriers.
    Components
    SCE register, performance standards, assurance tasks, verification reports.
    Process / workflow
    Identify SCEs → write standards → test → record pass/fail → act on failures → independent verification.
    O&G example
    A deluge test shows water reached nozzles in 48 s against a 30 s standard; it is recorded as failed, compensating measures applied, and the valve actuator repaired.
    Common mistakes
    Passing tests without measuring against the standard; deferring SCE tests without risk assessment.
    Best practices
    Measured values on test records; barrier health dashboard.
    🧪 Practice activity: Survivability
    Why does a firewater ring main need a survivability requirement?
    Answer: An explosion could damage the main before the fire; sectional valves, routing and protection ensure enough water still reaches the fire area.

    🔎 Fire Safety Audits & Assurance

    • Audit fire risk assessments, ITM records, impairment management, hot work permits, training and drills against company standards and regulations.
    • Use third parties (insurers, certifying bodies, regulators) for independent assurance.
    • Insurer loss-prevention surveys often recommend upgrades (e.g. tank rim-seal systems, firewater capacity).
    • Track audit findings to closure with evidence; trend repeat findings.
    EXAMPLE · Insurer recommendation

    An insurer survey finds the firewater supply cannot meet the largest tank fire demand. The Fire Safety Senior Engineer prepares an upgrade case — a new firewater tank — with risk and cost justification.

    🏗️ Fire Safety in Shutdowns & Turnarounds

    • Many hot work jobs, open equipment, pyrophoric scale and temporary facilities — fire risk peaks.
    • Plan fire watches, extra extinguishers, temporary fire detection in work areas, standby fire crews for high-risk jobs.
    • Manage fire system impairments carefully — planned isolation of deluge/fire mains must have compensating measures.
    • Before startup: all fire systems restored and tested, impairments closed, escape routes clear, pre-fire plans updated.
    ⚠️
    Opening equipment from sour service can expose pyrophoric iron sulfide, which self-heats and ignites in air. Keep it wet, remove it carefully and dispose of it safely.

    🏛️ Fire Safety Strategy & Engineering

    Fire & Explosion Strategy

    advanced technical analysisdesign reviewrisk assessment
    Definition
    The overall plan for how a facility prevents, detects, controls and mitigates fires and explosions and protects people — based on a fire and explosion risk assessment (FERA).
    Why it matters
    Fire systems added one by one without a strategy leave gaps or waste money; a strategy links scenarios to barriers.
    Explanation
    Strategy elements: inherent safety (layout, spacing, inventory reduction, separation of hazardous and occupied areas), detection and ESD philosophy, PFP extents, active protection philosophy (where deluge vs monitors vs none), firewater demand, EER (escape, evacuation and rescue), emergency response capability and mutual aid. Revisited when the facility changes.
    Types
    Fire protection philosophy, FERA, EER analysis, firewater study.
    Components
    Scenario list, consequence results, barrier allocation, performance standards.
    Process / workflow
    Scenarios → consequences → frequency → barrier selection → ALARP review → performance standards.
    O&G example
    A FERA for a new compressor station shows explosion overpressure could damage the control room. The layout is changed to move the control room farther away and strengthen it, instead of adding more active protection.
    Common mistakes
    Protection by habit; ignoring explosion effects on fire systems.
    Best practices
    Inherent safety first; documented strategy; update with MOC.
    🧪 Practice activity: Inherent first
    Give two inherently safer layout choices for a tank farm.
    Answer: Adequate spacing between tanks and from occupied buildings; bunds sized and sloped to drain spills away from tanks; locating ignition sources far from tanks.

    🧭 Fire Response Decision Making

    Offensive vs Defensive Decisions

    incident/emergency responseleadershipproblem-solving
    Definition
    Deciding whether to attack a fire (offensive), protect exposures and let it burn (defensive), or withdraw (non-intervention).
    Why it matters
    Wrong decisions can kill responders (BLEVE, boilover) or let a fire escalate.
    Explanation
    Factors: life at risk, fuel isolation possible?, resources sufficient for the full attack (foam, water, duration)?, escalation risk (vessels, boilover), environmental impact (firewater run-off, smoke), responder safety. Rule of thumb: commit to extinguishment only with enough resources to finish; otherwise protect exposures.
    Types
    Offensive, defensive, non-intervention; controlled burn.
    Components
    Size-up information, pre-fire plans, resource tallies, risk-benefit assessment.
    Process / workflow
    Size-up → risk-benefit → decide → communicate → reassess continuously.
    O&G example
    A full-surface crude tank fire with only half the required foam on site: the commander chooses defensive tactics (cool adjacent tanks, transfer product out) while mutual-aid foam arrives, then attacks with full resources.
    Common mistakes
    Partial foam attacks that waste concentrate; staying near vessels at BLEVE risk.
    Best practices
    Resource calculations before attack; continuous reassessment.
    🧪 Practice activity: Controlled burn
    Why might a commander let a small isolated fire burn out?
    Answer: If fuel is limited and isolated, exposures are protected, and extinguishing would create risks (e.g. unburnt vapours, contaminated firewater run-off into a river).

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer. Role-based questions show what a strong and a weak answer look like.

    🧭 Fundamentals

    Explain the fire triangle and how extinguishing methods relate to it.
    Fire needs fuel, oxygen and heat (plus a chain reaction — the tetrahedron). Isolation removes fuel, foam/CO2 remove oxygen, water removes heat, dry powder interrupts the chain reaction.
    What is the difference between flash point and auto-ignition temperature?
    Flash point is the lowest temperature at which a liquid gives off enough vapour to ignite with a source; auto-ignition temperature is where it ignites by itself without a flame or spark.
    Name the main hydrocarbon fire and explosion types.
    Pool fire, jet fire, flash fire, vapour cloud explosion, BLEVE and boilover (crude tanks).
    Why shouldn't you extinguish a gas fire before isolating the source?
    Unburnt gas keeps flowing and can form a flammable cloud that may re-ignite explosively; a controlled flame is safer until isolation.

    🛡️ Prevention & Protection Systems

    What are hazardous area zones?
    Zone 0 — explosive atmosphere continuously present; Zone 1 — likely in normal operation; Zone 2 — unlikely and brief. Equipment must be certified (Ex) for the zone, gas group and temperature class.
    What is the difference between passive and active fire protection?
    Passive (fireproofing, fire walls) works without activation; active (deluge, sprinklers, foam, gaseous) must detect and operate.
    How does a deluge system work?
    Open nozzles connected to a deluge valve; when F&G or manual signal opens the valve, water flows from all nozzles to cool and protect equipment.
    Why use foam on hydrocarbon fires?
    Foam floats on the fuel, smothers the fire, suppresses vapour release and cools; water alone can spread burning oil.
    What controls apply to hot work?
    Avoid if possible; permit; gas testing (typically 0% LEL and continuous); remove/cover combustibles and drains; fire watch during and after; extinguishers; habitats in hazardous areas.

    🔧 Equipment, ITM & Procedures

    Describe the PASS technique.
    Pull the pin, Aim at the base of the fire, Squeeze the handle, Sweep side to side — after raising the alarm and with an escape route behind you.
    What is fire system impairment management?
    Formal control when a fire system is out of service: tag and notify, risk assess, compensating measures (fire watch, restrictions), time limit, restore and test, close.
    What tests keep a fire pump reliable?
    Regular run (churn) tests, annual flow tests, diesel engine and battery checks, controller checks — and keeping it in automatic mode.
    What is a performance standard for a fire system?
    A measurable statement of what the system must do (functionality), how reliably, its survivability and dependencies — tested during ITM.

    🚒 Response & Expert

    What are the priorities in a hydrocarbon fire?
    Life safety → isolate fuel → protect exposures → control/extinguish → prevent re-ignition.
    What is boilover and how do you manage the risk?
    In crude tank fires, the hot zone reaches bottom water which flashes to steam and ejects burning oil. Manage by large evacuation distances, monitoring, and full-resource foam attack or defensive tactics.
    How do you prevent a BLEVE?
    Cool the vessel (especially the vapour space) with fixed systems or unmanned monitors, depressure, use PFP and relief valves, and withdraw people if cooling can't be maintained.
    When would you choose a defensive strategy?
    When resources are insufficient to finish an attack, the fuel can't be isolated, or responder risk is too high — protect exposures and wait or let it burn out.

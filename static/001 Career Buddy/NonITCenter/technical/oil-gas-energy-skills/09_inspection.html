    Inspection Fundamentals & the 10 Roles

    Start from zero: where Inspection sits in Oil & Gas / Energy Skills, what its ten roles do, which skills each role needs, and the words every inspection conversation uses.

    🧭 What is the Inspection department?

    Oil & Gas / Energy Skills is a skill group made up of 9 departments. Inspection is one of them. Pressure vessels, pipes, tanks, boilers and structures wear out, corrode and crack over time. Inspection finds out what condition the equipment is in, whether it is still safe, and when it must be checked or fixed again. The department has 10 roles, from Engineer to Analyst. This course teaches what each role does, which skills it needs and how the roles work together safely.

    📐
    Plan the inspection
    Engineers and planners decide what to inspect, how and when.
    🔍
    Examine and measure
    Inspectors and technicians look, measure and test the equipment.
    📋
    Judge and control
    Quality engineers raise NCRs, find root causes and control corrective action.
    📅
    Manage and report
    Supervisors, coordinators, planners and analysts keep work, records and KPIs on track.
    EXAMPLE · One vessel, four people

    An Engineer sets the thickness points and limit for a vessel. A Technician opens and cleans it. An Inspector measures and records the readings. An Analyst later shows how fast the wall is thinning across similar vessels. One vessel, four roles.

    🧰 The Oil & Gas / Energy Skills group

    Inspection sits inside a group of 9 departments that serve plants that process or generate energy. It supports every other department, because every plant needs proof that its equipment is safe. These terms are often mixed up, so learn them first.

    EXAMPLE · Which word fits?

    Measuring wall thickness at fixed points every two years is condition monitoring. Checking a new weld for cracks with MT is NDT examination. A yearly audit of whether inspection records are complete is quality assurance.

    Inspection Programmes, Risk & Planning

    How inspection work is chosen, risk-ranked, planned, tracked and reported, with the numbers and documents used at each step.

    🏭 Why equipment is inspected

    Equipment fails slowly and often without warning. Inspection looks for the damage early, while a repair is still simple and safe.

    Damage typeWhat happensWhere to look
    General corrosionWall thins evenlyVessel shells, tank walls
    PittingSmall deep holesStagnant areas, bottom of vessels
    Corrosion under insulation (CUI)Hidden corrosion beneath insulationInsulated pipes and vessels, low points
    ErosionWall wears from fast or dirty flowElbows, valves, pump discharge
    CrackingCracks from fatigue, stress or environmentWelds, nozzles, bends
    Creep and overheatingMetal weakens at high temperatureFurnace tubes, hot piping
    Fouling and blockageDeposits reduce flow and heat transferExchangers, filters
    EXAMPLE · Early beats late

    A pinhole leak found by a visual check is a small repair. The same leak found after a gas release is an incident. Inspection finds problems while they are small.

    🗂️ Types of inspection

    TypeWhenPurpose
    External inspectionWhile runningCheck for leaks, corrosion, supports, insulation damage
    On-stream inspectionWhile runningMeasure thickness from outside
    Internal inspectionEquipment shut down and openedLook at inside surfaces, linings and internals
    Construction / fabrication inspectionDuring buildingCheck welds, materials, dimensions and tests
    Vendor / third-party inspectionAt the supplier or by an independent partyWitness tests and release shipment
    Turnaround inspectionDuring a planned shutdownOpen and inspect many items in a short window
    EXAMPLE · Choosing a type

    A vessel cannot stop for two years. External UT thickness checks and an external visual inspection are done on-stream; the internal inspection is saved for the next shutdown.

    ⚖️ Risk-based inspection

    Risk-based inspection (RBI) focuses effort where failure would be most likely and most harmful. Risk combines two things.

    Part of riskMeaningExample of high
    Probability of failureHow likely damage is to cause a failureFast corrosion rate, old equipment, poor inspection history
    Consequence of failureHow bad a failure would beToxic or flammable fluid, high pressure, near people
    RiskProbability × consequenceHigh probability and high consequence = inspect most often
    EXAMPLE · Two lines, two plans

    Line A carries water at low pressure with a slow corrosion rate: low risk, long interval. Line B carries hot sour gas with fast corrosion and is near a walkway: high risk, short interval and extra monitoring.

    📌
    RBI changes where and how often inspection is done. It never removes the need for the legal and code requirements that apply.

    📐 Corrosion rate, remaining life and intervals

    Thickness readings turn into three key numbers.

    QuantityFormulaMeaning
    Corrosion rate(previous thickness − current thickness) ÷ timeHow fast the wall is thinning
    Remaining life(current thickness − minimum required thickness) ÷ corrosion rateTime before the wall is too thin
    Next inspectionNot more than a set fraction of remaining life (often half), within code limitsKeeps a safety margin
    EXAMPLE · Worked example

    A vessel wall was 9.0 mm six years ago and is 8.4 mm now. Corrosion rate = (9.0 − 8.4) ÷ 6 = 0.1 mm/year. Minimum required thickness is 6.0 mm. Remaining life = (8.4 − 6.0) ÷ 0.1 = 24 years. Half is 12 years, but the code caps this interval at 10 years for internal inspection, so the next internal inspection is within 10 years.

    📌
    Limits and intervals come from the governing code and the equipment’s design data. The numbers here are illustrations only.

    📍 Condition monitoring locations and data

    A condition monitoring location (CML), also called a thickness monitoring location (TML), is a marked point where thickness is measured each time. Same point, same method, so the trend is reliable.

    ItemWhat to knowExample
    Location choiceMore points where damage is likelyElbows, injection points, dead legs, nozzles
    MarkingEach CML has an ID and a mark on the equipment and drawingCML 14 on the isometric
    Same method each timeSame probe, same procedure, same calibrationKeeps readings comparable
    Short-term and long-term rateShort-term uses the last interval; long-term uses the whole historyA sudden jump in short-term rate triggers review
    Data storageReadings held in a database by tag and CMLAllows trending
    EXAMPLE · A jump in the data

    CML 14 reads 8.9 mm, 8.8 mm, then 8.0 mm. The last interval rate is much higher than before. The Engineer reviews, the Inspector re-measures with a second gauge and a cause is sought (a change in process fluid or a bad reading).

    📅 Inspection planning and due dates

    Every item has a due date set from code intervals and RBI. The plan makes sure the work is done before the date, using the available people, access and shutdown windows.

    IdeaPlain meaningExample
    Due dateLatest date by which an inspection must be doneVessel V-101 due 30 June
    OverduePast the due dateA safety concern and often a legal one
    Critical pathLongest chain of linked jobs; sets the end dateIsolate → open → clean → inspect → close
    FloatSpare time before the end date is affectedA piping check with 8 days of float
    BaselineThe approved plan used to measure progressYear plan frozen in January
    Look-aheadDetailed plan for the next few daysThis week’s jobs by crew
    EXAMPLE · Compliance in numbers

    The plan has 100 inspections due this quarter. 92 are done on time. Plan compliance = 92 ÷ 100 = 92%. The 8 late items are listed with owners and new dates.

    🚩
    An overdue inspection is a risk, not just a late task. Extension of a due date needs a documented technical assessment and approval.

    💰 KPIs, cost and change

    Inspection performance is tracked with simple numbers.

    KPIFormulaMeaning
    Plan complianceInspections done on time ÷ inspections dueHigher is better; target near 100%
    Overdue countNumber of items past due dateShould be zero or tightly controlled
    NCR closure rateNCRs closed on time ÷ NCRs dueShows how well findings are followed up
    Repeat NCR rateNCRs repeating a known cause ÷ total NCRsShows if CAPA works

    EXAMPLE · Weld reject rate

    A fabricator’s RT shows 3 rejected welds out of 150 tested. Reject rate = 3 ÷ 150 = 2%. The Quality Engineer asks whether a welder or a procedure is the cause.

    Methods, Procedures, Standards & NCR/CAPA

    The inspection methods, written procedures, standards, certification levels and NCR/CAPA process that make inspection results trustworthy.

    🛡️ Safety during inspection

    Inspectors work in hazardous places: inside vessels, at height, near live plant and next to radiography. Safe work comes first.

    HazardExampleControl
    Confined spaceEntering a vessel or tankPermit, isolation, gas test, standby person, rescue plan
    RadiationRadiography nearbyBarriers, signs, timed exclusion zone; time, distance, shielding
    HeightScaffold or rope accessTagged scaffold, harness, tool tethers
    Hot or cold surfacesInspecting hot pipingWait for safe temperatures; PPE
    Toxic gasH2S or benzene near opened equipmentDetectors, breathing apparatus where needed
    Insulation and dustRemoving insulation on old linesDust control, correct PPE, waste handling
    ⚠️
    Never enter a confined space to rescue someone first. Raise the alarm and let the trained rescue team act.
    EXAMPLE · Radiography clash

    An Inspector plans to enter a vessel at 22:00 when radiography is planned next to it. The SIMOPS plan moves radiography to another area, and the Coordinator informs both crews.

    🔍 NDT and inspection methods

    MethodWhat it findsTypical use
    VT (visual)Surface damage, leaks, deformationFirst check on everything
    UT thicknessWall thickness lossVessels, pipes, tanks
    UT flaw / TOFD / phased arrayInternal cracks and weld flawsWelds in pressure equipment
    RT (radiography)Internal flaws and corrosion profileWelds, small-bore piping, CUI screening
    MT (magnetic particle)Surface and near-surface cracksCarbon steel welds
    PT (dye penetrant)Surface cracksNon-magnetic metals
    Eddy currentTube wall loss and cracksExchanger tubes
    PMIAlloy gradeChecking materials
    Hardness testingHeat-treatment problemsWelds and repairs
    ThermographyHot spots, insulation damageFurnaces, electrical, CUI screening
    💡
    Method choice depends on the damage type and the metal. Visual inspection is cheap and comes first.
    EXAMPLE · Choosing a method

    Question: “Does this weld have a surface crack on carbon steel?” Answer: MT. For stainless steel, PT instead, because stainless is not magnetic.

    📖 Inspection procedures and calibration

    An inspection procedure is a written method that makes the work repeatable. Two inspectors following it should get the same result.

    Procedure sectionWhat it saysWhy it matters
    Scope and applicationWhat equipment and conditions it coversPrevents wrong use
    PersonnelRequired qualification levelOnly qualified people sign
    Equipment and calibrationInstruments, blocks, checks before and after useReadings can be trusted
    Surface preparationHow clean and at what temperaturePoor surfaces give poor results
    Method stepsScan pattern, sensitivity, coverageRepeatability
    Acceptance criteriaLimits for accept and rejectClear decisions
    ReportingWhat must be recordedComplete records
    EXAMPLE · UT thickness arithmetic

    A UT gauge measures the round-trip time of a sound pulse. Thickness = velocity × time ÷ 2. In steel the velocity is about 5,900 m/s. A round trip of 3.4 µs gives 5,900 × 3.4×10⁻⁶ ÷ 2 ≈ 10.0 mm. A wrong velocity setting gives a wrong thickness, which is why calibration on a block of the same material comes first.

    🚩
    An uncalibrated instrument makes every reading doubtful. If a gauge is found out of calibration, readings taken since the last good check must be reviewed.

    🎓 Personnel qualification

    Level IIIWrite procedures, qualify methods and oversee the systemApproves a phased-array procedure
    EXAMPLE · Who signs

    A Level I technician takes UT readings for a Level II inspector. The Level II reviews them, evaluates against the limit and signs the report. A certificate for each method and level is held on file.

    ⚖️ Acceptance criteria and evaluation

    ResultMeaningNext step
    AcceptableWithin limitsRecord; set the next date
    EvaluateNear or at a limit; needs engineering judgementEngineer reviews; add monitoring
    RejectOutside limitsRaise an NCR; repair, rework or re-rate
    EXAMPLE · Reading against a limit

    Minimum thickness is 6.0 mm. Readings: 7.4, 6.8, 6.1, 5.8 mm. The first three pass; 6.1 is near the limit and is flagged to the Engineer; 5.8 is below minimum and gets an NCR. All four are recorded.

    📌
    The inspector reports and applies the stated limit. Choosing a different limit or accepting a reject is an engineering decision, not an inspector’s choice.

    📋 The NCR process

    A non-conformance report (NCR) records something that does not meet requirements and controls what happens to it.

    An NCR runs from detection to closure with evidence.
    DispositionMeaningExample
    Use as isAccept with engineering justificationMinor dimension variation that does not affect function
    RepairBring to an acceptable condition by an approved methodWeld repair of a defect
    ReworkRedo to meet the original requirementRe-machine a flange face
    Reject / scrapCannot be usedWrong material grade
    EXAMPLE · A complete NCR

    NCR-045: wall at CML 14 is 5.8 mm, minimum 6.0 mm. Photos and readings attached. Disposition by the Senior Engineer: temporary clamp, replacement in 3 months. Verified by re-measurement after the clamp. Closed with evidence.

    🔧 RCA and CAPA

    An NCR fixes one item. Root-cause analysis (RCA) finds why it happened, and corrective and preventive action (CAPA) stops it happening again.

    StepWhat it doesExample
    ContainmentStop further harm nowTake the gauge out of use
    RCAFind the real cause, e.g. 5-whysGauge was not on the calibration list
    Corrective actionFix this causeAdd the gauge to the list and calibrate it
    Preventive actionStop similar problems elsewhereCheck all gauges against the list monthly
    Effectiveness checkProve it workedNo overdue calibration for three months
    EXAMPLE · 5-whys

    Why was a thickness reading wrong? The gauge was out of calibration. Why? It was not on the list. Why? New gauges are not added when issued. Why? No one owns the step. Why? The procedure is silent. Fix: add the step and an owner to the procedure.

    🗃️ Records and traceability

    An inspection result is only useful if it can be traced to the equipment, the method and the person.

    🧭 Fundamentals

    What is the Inspection department, and where does it sit?
    It is one of nine departments in Oil & Gas / Energy Skills. It has ten roles (Engineer, Senior Engineer, Project Engineer, Supervisor, Coordinator, Planner, Quality Engineer, Inspector, Technician, Analyst) and all share three skills: inspection procedures, quality standards and NCR/CAPA.
    What is the difference between inspection and NDT?
    Inspection is the overall activity of checking equipment against requirements. NDT is a set of test methods used within it, such as UT, MT, PT and RT, that find defects without damaging the part.
    What is a CML?
    A condition monitoring location: a marked point where thickness is measured each time so that trends are reliable.
    How do you calculate corrosion rate and remaining life?
    Corrosion rate = (previous − current thickness) ÷ time. Remaining life = (current − minimum required thickness) ÷ corrosion rate.
    What is risk-based inspection?
    A method that sets inspection scope and frequency by risk, which is probability of failure multiplied by consequence of failure.
    What is QA versus QC?
    QA is the system that plans and controls quality (procedures, audits). QC is checking that a specific job or product meets requirements.
    Why must an instrument be calibrated?
    An uncalibrated instrument can give wrong readings, so every result is doubtful. Calibration against a known block is checked before and after use.
    Name the three skills in all ten roles, and why.
    Inspection procedures, quality standards and NCR/CAPA. Procedures make work repeatable, standards set the limits, and NCR/CAPA controls what fails.

    📐 Engineering roles

    What does an Engineer do in inspection?
    Designs the inspection scheme, sets limits, calculates corrosion rates and remaining life, and writes requirements.
    How do you decide the next inspection date?
    From remaining life and risk, using the code interval limit. For example, if remaining life is 24 years, half is 12 years, but a code cap of 10 years for internal inspection applies.
    What does a Senior Engineer add?
    Fitness-for-service, design review, troubleshooting of repeat damage and leadership.
    What is fitness-for-service?
    An engineering method to judge whether damaged equipment can stay in service safely, and for how long.
    What does a Project Engineer own?
    A package such as the vessel inspection programme: contractors, drawings, dates, cost and reports.
    How do you handle a request to extend an overdue due date?
    Do a documented technical assessment, consider risk, get approval from the authorised person and record the new date.

    👷 Site, planning and coordination roles

    What does a Supervisor check before inspection starts?
    Permits valid, isolation in place, access safe, procedure current, gauges calibrated and crew qualified.
    What does a Coordinator do all day?
    Runs meetings, keeps registers, arranges access and permits, chases actions and collects reports.
    What is plan compliance?
    Inspections completed on time divided by inspections due, shown as a percentage.
    What does a Planner use to set due dates?
    Code intervals, RBI results and shutdown windows, combined with crew availability.
    What is the critical path?
    The longest chain of linked jobs; any delay on it delays the end date.
    How do you recover a late critical job?
    Check the logic, add resources or shifts, remove waiting time and agree changes with the team.

    ✅ Quality and inspection roles

    What is an ITP?
    An inspection and test plan: checks, hold and witness points, methods and acceptance limits for a job.
    What are NCR dispositions?
    Use as is (with justification), repair, rework or reject. The decision is made by an authorised engineer.
    What is the difference between RCA and CAPA?
    RCA finds why a non-conformance happened. CAPA fixes it and prevents recurrence, then checks effectiveness.
    What is the 5-whys method?
    Asking ‘why?’ repeatedly until the underlying process cause is found, not just the first symptom.
    What goes in an inspection record?
    Tag and CML ID, date, inspector and level, procedure and revision, instrument and calibration, result against limit, and photos.
    What would you do if a reading is below minimum thickness?
    Record it exactly, mark and photograph the area, raise an NCR, tell the Engineer immediately and do not change the limit.

    🔧 Technician and analyst roles

    How do you prepare a vessel for internal inspection?
    Isolate with blinds, drain, flush, ventilate, gas-test, get a permit, open, clean the surface and set up access and lighting.
    How does UT thickness measurement work?
    A pulse travels through the wall and back. Thickness = velocity × round-trip time ÷ 2. Steel is about 5,900 m/s.
    Why must surfaces be cleaned before MT or PT?
    Dirt, paint or scale can hide cracks or cause false indications.
    What KPIs does an Analyst track?
    Plan compliance, overdue items, NCR closure and repeat rates, weld reject rate, corrosion trends and SPI/CPI.
    How do you spot a repeat NCR?
    Group NCRs by cause. If the same cause appears again, the CAPA was not effective.
    What would you do if a gauge was found out of calibration?
    Stop using it, tag it, review readings taken since its last good check, raise an NCR and recheck where needed.

    🏗️ Scenario: Annual inspection programme of a gas plant

    You run the annual inspection programme of a gas plant: 120 pressure vessels, 6,000 piping thickness points and 12 tanks. Some work is on-stream; some needs a shutdown. Contractors supply NDT crews. Safety, quality and due dates all matter. Answer each task in your own words, then compare with the model answer.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Limits and calculations

    ✍️ A vessel wall was 9.0 mm six years ago and is 8.4 mm now. Minimum required thickness is 6.0 mm. What are the corrosion rate and remaining life, and what do you recommend as Engineer?
    Model answer: Corrosion rate = (9.0 − 8.4) ÷ 6 = 0.1 mm/yr. Remaining life = (8.4 − 6.0) ÷ 0.1 = 24 years. Half is 12 years, but the code cap on internal inspection interval (10 years in this example) applies, so set the next internal inspection within 10 years, keep external checks at the code interval, and record the calculation and data.

    ✍️ Eight vessels are within 60 days of their due dates but the NDT contractor has only one crew. What do you do as Planner and Coordinator?
    Model answer: List the items by due date and risk, check float and the shutdown window, ask for a second crew or extended shifts, group vessels by area to reduce moves, agree access and permits in advance, and escalate if a due date cannot be met so that a documented technical assessment can be made.

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Safety and access

    ✍️ A crew wants to enter a vessel that is closed by one valve while radiography is planned next to it. What do you do as Supervisor?
    Model answer: Stop entry. A single valve is not a reliable isolation. Use blinds or double block and bleed, gas-test, get a confined-space permit with a standby person and rescue plan, and separate the work from the radiography zone using the SIMOPS plan.

    • D Radiography is quick

    4️⃣ Task 4 · Procedures and calibration

    ✍️ A UT gauge is found with no calibration tag halfway through a week of thickness readings. What happens next?
    Model answer: Stop using the gauge and tag it out. Check it on a calibration block. Review all readings taken since its last good check and re-measure where needed, raise an NCR, find why the tag was missing (RCA) and add a regular gauge calibration check (CAPA).
    Q. What is the right first action?
    • A Continue and note it later
    • B Stop using it and review readings taken with it
    • C Add 5% to every reading
    • D Ignore it if readings look normal

    5️⃣ Task 5 · NCR and CAPA

    ✍️ Five inspection reports in a month have wrong equipment tags. How do you respond as Quality Engineer?
    Model answer: Raise NCRs, correct each report, find the root cause (for example a free-text template), change the template to a drop-down list, train the team, check the next 30 reports for effectiveness and close the CAPA with evidence.
    Q. What makes the CAPA effective?
    • A Correcting each report only
    • B Fixing the root cause and checking it works
    • C Blaming the inspectors
    • D Closing the NCRs quickly

    ✍️ This quarter 92 of 100 inspections were done on time, 8 are overdue and 12% of NCRs repeat a known cause. What should each role do?

Senior Engineer: assess the overdue items for risk and approve any extension. Quality Engineer: review the repeat NCR causes and strengthen CAPA.

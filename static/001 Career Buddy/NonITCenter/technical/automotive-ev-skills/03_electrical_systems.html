    What are Electrical Systems?

    Start from zero: how electrical power is generated, distributed and used safely, and what an Electrical Systems Engineer does.

    🧭 What is an Electrical System?

    An electrical system brings power from a source to the loads that use it, safely and reliably. It includes supply, transformers, switchgear, cables, protection, control panels, motors and lighting. An Electrical Systems Engineer designs, calculates, documents, tests and supports these systems.

    ⚡ Power circuits

    Carry energy to motors, heaters, lighting and equipment (230 V single-phase, 415 V three-phase).

    🎛️ Control circuits

    Low-voltage signals (24 V DC, PLC I/O) that start, stop and monitor equipment.

    💡
    Memory aid: Source → Protect → Distribute → Use → Control.

    Module 02 · Basic · Calculations

    Circuits, Power & Calculations

    The formulas every electrical engineer uses daily.

    🧮 Core Formulas

    QuantityFormula
    Ohm's lawV = I × R
    Power (single-phase)P = V × I × cosφ
    Power (three-phase)P = √3 × VL × IL × cosφ
    Apparent powerS = P / cosφ (kVA)
    EnergykWh = kW × hours
    Example · Ohm's law

    230 V across a 46 Ω heater gives I = 230 / 46 = 5 A, so P = 230 × 5 = 1,150 W.

    📊 Power Factor

    Low power factor means more current for the same real power, causing heating, voltage drop and utility penalties. Capacitor banks improve it.

    Example · kVA

    A 100 kW load at power factor 0.8 needs S = 100 / 0.8 = 125 kVA.

    📏 Motor Current and Voltage Drop

    Example · Motor full-load current

    37 kW, 415 V, pf 0.88, efficiency 0.93: I = 37,000 / (√3 × 415 × 0.88 × 0.93) ≈ 63 A.

    Example · Voltage drop

    Cable 25 mm² Cu, R ≈ 0.93 Ω/km, 100 m run, 63 A: Vd ≈ √3 × 63 × 0.93 × 0.1 ≈ 10 V (2.4% of 415 V). Many designs limit drop to about 3–5%; check the applicable code.

    Module 03 · Intermediate · Drawings

    Electrical Drawings & Documentation

    Read, check and maintain the drawings and records that define the system.

    📐 Types of Electrical Drawings

    DrawingShows
    Single-line diagram (SLD)Source, transformers, breakers, feeders, major loads.
    SchematicCircuit logic: contactors, relays, interlocks, control.
    Wiring / connection diagramTerminal numbers, wire IDs, cable routing.
    Panel layout (GA)Physical positions and dimensions of components.
    Cable scheduleCable type, size, length, route, from-to.
    Load listEvery load, rating and demand factor.

    🔣 Symbols, Standards and Revisions

    Drawings use standard symbols (IEC 60617 or ANSI/IEEE). Always check the title block: drawing number, revision, date, approval. Build from the latest approved revision only.

    🚨
    Mark up as-built changes immediately. Out-of-date drawings cause wrong wiring and unsafe work.

    📝 Technical Documentation

    Keep calculation sheets, datasheets, test reports, inspection checklists, manuals and the as-built dossier. Good documentation lets another engineer understand, test and safely maintain the system.

    Module 04 · Intermediate · Design

    Power Distribution & Protection Design

    Choose and coordinate the equipment that keeps power available and safe.

    🏭 Distribution Equipment

    🔌
    Transformer
    Steps voltage up or down (e.g. 11 kV → 415 V).
    🗄️
    Switchgear / MCC
    Switch, protect and control feeders and motors.
    🧵
    Cables & busbars
    Carry current; sized by current, drop and short-circuit.
    🔁
    Capacitor bank
    Improves power factor.
    🧰
    VFD / soft starter
    Controls motor speed and starting current.
    🔋
    UPS / DG
    Backup supply for critical loads.

    🛡️ Protection Devices

    DeviceProtects against
    FuseOvercurrent and short circuit; single-use.
    MCB / MCCBOverload and short circuit; resettable.
    Overload relayMotor overload.
    RCD / RCCB (30 mA)Earth leakage and shock risk.
    Surge protectionVoltage surges and lightning effects.
    💡
    Selectivity: only the breaker nearest the fault should trip, so the rest of the plant stays on.

    🧾 Cable Sizing Steps

    Load current → current rating (with derating) → voltage drop check → short-circuit withstand → protection match

    Module 05 · Advanced · Testing

    Testing & Commissioning

    Prove that the installed system matches the design and is safe to energise.

    🧪 Common Tests

    TestPurpose
    ContinuityConductors and earth bonds are intact.
    Insulation resistance (megger)Insulation is healthy; typical 500 V DC test, ≥1 MΩ for LV circuits.
    Earth resistanceEarth electrode meets the design value.
    Polarity & phase sequenceCorrect connection and motor rotation.
    Functional testInterlocks, trips and control logic work.
    ThermographyFinds hot connections under load.

    ✅ Commissioning Flow

    Visual check → De-energised tests → Energise in stages → Functional tests → Load tests → Handover

    FAT is done at the vendor; SAT is done on site.

    🔍 Troubleshooting Method

    Understand the symptom → check the obvious (supply, fuses, loose terminals) → use drawings and measurements → isolate the fault → fix → test and document. Never reset a tripping breaker repeatedly without finding the cause.

    Module 06 · Advanced · Safety

    Electrical Safety

    Rules that keep people alive: non-negotiable for every electrical task.

    🔒 Lockout / Tagout

    Identify the source → notify → switch off → isolate → lock and tag → release stored energy → test before touch. Each worker applies their own lock.

    🚨
    Never assume a circuit is dead. Prove it with a rated tester, checked before and after use.

    🦺 Safe Practices

    🧤
    PPE
    Insulated gloves, face shield, arc-rated clothing for the hazard level.
    🌍
    Earthing
    Bond and earth exposed metal.
    💧
    IP ratings
    IP65 is dust-tight and protected against water jets.
    ⚡
    Arc flash
    Study incident energy; follow NFPA 70E-type practice.
    📜
    Standards
    IEC 60364, IEC 61439, NFPA 70E, IS 732 / NEC (BIS).
    🧯
    Emergency
    Know the isolator, first aid and fire response.

    Module 07 · Practice

    Practice: Motor Feeder Tripping

    Apply calculation, design, testing and safety thinking to a real scenario.

    🏗️ Scenario

    A new 37 kW, 415 V pump motor trips its MCCB on start-up. The feeder is a 100 m, 25 mm² Cu cable. The drawing on site is Rev B, while the approved set is Rev C. A recent insulation test was not recorded.

    0 / 0 correct

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Calculations

    ✍️ Estimate the full-load current and check the voltage drop.
    Model answer: I ≈ 37,000 / (√3 × 415 × 0.88 × 0.93) ≈ 63 A. Vd ≈ √3 × 63 × 0.93 × 0.1 ≈ 10 V (≈2.4%), acceptable at rated current. Starting current (6–7×) may cause a larger transient drop.
    Q. Starting current of a DOL induction motor is roughly…
    • A equal to full load
    • B 6–7 times full-load current
    • C half of full load
    • D zero

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Investigation

    ✍️ List safe steps to investigate the trip.
    Model answer: Apply LOTO, prove dead, check the drawing revision (get Rev C), inspect terminals, run insulation and continuity tests, check the MCCB trip curve vs motor starting, and consider soft-starter or VFD.
    Q. Which drawing should you use?
    • A Any copy
    • B The oldest
    • C The latest approved revision
    • D The one in the toolbox

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Fix and Record

    ✍️ What do you document after the fix?
    Model answer: Updated drawing/as-built, test results (insulation, continuity), breaker settings, root cause, corrective action and handover notes.

    • C They slow work

    Module 01 · Basic · Foundations

    What is Industrial Automation?

    From sensor to actuator to operator screen: how plants are measured, controlled and monitored safely.

    🧭 What is Industrial Automation?

    Industrial automation uses sensors, controllers, drives, networks and software to run machines and processes with minimal manual intervention. The core stack is field devices → I/O → PLC/DCS → network → SCADA/HMI → MES/ERP.

    LevelExamples
    FieldTransmitters, switches, valves, drives, encoders
    ControlPLC, DCS controller, safety PLC
    SupervisorySCADA, HMI, historian, alarm server
    EnterpriseMES, ERP, Power BI dashboards

    Module 02 · PLC, DCS & SCADA

    PLC, DCS and SCADA Systems

    Programming, scan behaviour, networks, redundancy, alarm management and cyber security.

    💾 PLC Programming (IEC 61131-3)

    Languages: LD, FBD, ST, SFC (IL is deprecated). Each scan: read inputs → run logic → write outputs → communications/housekeeping. Use edge detection for counters, TON/TOF/TP timers for delays, and structured tags with UDTs. A scan exceeding the watchdog causes a CPU fault.

    🌐 Industrial Networks and Protocols

    ProtocolMediumTypical use
    Modbus RTURS-485Simple device polling, 120 Ω termination at both ends
    Profibus DP / PARS-485 / MBPDrives, remote I/O, process instruments
    Profinet / EtherNet/IPEthernetReal-time I/O, drives, PLC-to-PLC
    OPC UATCP/IPSecure vendor-neutral SCADA/MES data
    Foundation FieldbusH1Digital process instrumentation

    🖥️ DCS, SCADA and Alarm Management

    A DCS suits large continuous plants with integrated database, redundant controllers and loop faceplates. SCADA suits distributed assets with historians and remote telemetry. ISA-18.2 / EEMUA 191: aim for about 1 alarm per 10 min, use priorities, deadband, on-delay and shelving, and review bad actors regularly.

    ⚠️
    Alarms must be actionable, with a defined operator response, otherwise they are just noise.

    🔐 Redundancy and Cyber Security

    Use redundant CPUs, power supplies and ring networks (MRP/RSTP) for availability. IEC 62443: divide the plant into zones and conduits, use firewalls and DMZ, role-based access, patch management, backup and removable-media control.

    Module 03 · Control Systems & Design

    Control Systems, Tuning and Drives

    PID, tuning, advanced strategies, stability, drives and motion.

    🎚️ PID Control and Tuning

    u = Kc[e + (1/Ti)∫e dt + Td·de/dt]. P gives speed with offset, I removes offset but adds lag and windup risk, D anticipates but amplifies noise. Gain Kc = 100/PB. Ziegler-Nichols closed-loop: PID Kp = 0.6Ku, Ti = Pu/2, Td = Pu/8. Use anti-windup and derivative on PV.

    LoopTypical tuning
    FlowFast: PI, low Kc, small Ti (seconds)
    PressurePI, moderate
    LevelP-only or PI, depends on surge needs
    TemperaturePID, slow, deadtime-dominated

    🧩 Advanced Control Strategies

    🪜
    Cascade
    Fast inner loop rejects disturbances for the slower outer loop.
    ➡️
    Feedforward
    Acts on a measured disturbance before the PV moves.
    ⚖️
    Ratio
    Maintains a flow or fuel/air ratio.
    ⏱️
    Smith predictor
    Compensates for long dead time.
    🔀
    Split-range
    One controller drives two valves or ranges.
    🧠
    MPC
    Handles multivariable constraints.

    📈 Stability, Sampling and Digital Control

    Targets: gain margin ≥ 6 dB, phase margin 30–60°. Sample at ≥ 5–10× the loop bandwidth, and above 2× the highest signal frequency (Nyquist) with anti-alias filtering. Dead time θ vs time constant τ (θ/τ > 1) makes loops hard to tune.

    ⚙️ Drives and Motion

    Synchronous speed Ns = 120f/P. Pump/fan power ∝ N³ (affinity laws), so VFDs save energy. Encoders: 4× quadrature counts = 4 × PPR. Servo loops cascade position → velocity → current. Select drive size by torque, duty cycle and regenerative energy.

    Module 04 · Instrumentation Troubleshooting

    Instrumentation, Measurement and Troubleshooting

    Signals, sensors, calibration, valves, loop checks and fault finding.

    📟 Signals and Scaling

    4–20 mA: % = (mA−4)/16 × 100. A 250 Ω resistor gives 1–5 V. NAMUR NE43: < 3.6 mA or > 21 mA means failure. HART adds digital data over the loop. Isolate, shield (ground at one end) and separate signal from power wiring.

    🌡️ Measurement Technologies

    VariableTechnologyKey point
    TemperatureRTD Pt100, thermocouple3/4-wire for RTD, cold-junction compensation for TC
    FlowDP (√ΔP), Coriolis, magmeter, vortexSquare-root extraction for DP
    LevelDP, radar, ultrasonic, floath = ΔP/(ρg), account for density
    PressureStrain gauge, capacitanceZero/span, impulse line checks

    🔧 Calibration, Valves and Fault Finding

    Record as-found then as-left at 0/25/50/75/100% up and down (hysteresis). Valves: choose fail-safe action, check positioner, stiction, Cv and cavitation. Method: check power → wiring/continuity → signal → configuration → process (impulse lines, fouling) → device.

    💡
    Use a calibrated loop calibrator and HART communicator, and always record tool IDs.

    Module 05 · Drawings, Safety & Quality

    Drawings, Safety and Quality

    P&IDs, loop diagrams, functional safety, FAT/SAT, inspection, RCA and CAPA.

    📑 Drawings and Specifications

    Read P&ID (ISA tags such as FT-101, LIC-102, PDT-205), loop diagrams, wiring and terminal drawings, I/O lists, cause-and-effect charts, logic narratives and instrument datasheets. Always work from the latest approved revision.

    🛡️ Functional Safety and Work Safety

    IEC 61511: SIL is set by risk reduction. PFDavg(1oo1) ≈ λDU·T/2, and SIL 2 is 10⁻³–10⁻². Test safety functions at proof-test intervals. Never bypass an interlock without an approved permit. LOTO, isolation of stored energy, arc-flash PPE and permit-to-work apply.

    🧪 Inspection, FAT/SAT, Quality, RCA and CAPA

    FAT at vendor site, SAT on site, then loop checks, I/O checkout, interlock tests and documentation. A quality plan defines checks, acceptance and records. RCA tools: 5-Why, fishbone, fault tree. Flow: contain → root cause → CAPA → verify effectiveness.

    Module 07 · Practice

    Practice: Hunting Tank-Level Loop

    Apply scaling, tuning, instrumentation, safety and CAPA thinking to a real scenario.

    🏗️ Scenario

    A tank level loop (LIC-101) oscillates with a 90 s period. The DP transmitter is 0–50 kPa on a 4–20 mA loop, the outlet valve is air-to-open, and the PLC runs PID with Kc = 5, Ti = 20 s. Operators report the valve sticks, and the last calibration was not recorded.

    0 / 0 correct

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Calculations

    ✍️ The transmitter reads 12 mA in a tank of water. What is the DP, and what level is it?
    Model answer: DP = 50 × (12−4)/16 = 25 kPa. h = 25,000/(1000 × 9.81) ≈ 2.55 m (above the low tap).
    Q. A 12 mA reading on a 0–50 kPa transmitter equals…
    • A 12.5 kPa
    • B 25 kPa
    • C 37.5 kPa
    • D 50 kPa

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Investigation

    ✍️ List a safe troubleshooting plan.
    Model answer: Check permits and isolate if needed, review the trend (hunting with stiction shows a saw-tooth PV and square-wave output), put the loop in manual, stroke-test the valve with positioner feedback, inspect packing and the positioner, verify the transmitter zero/span with a loop calibrator and record as-found, then retune (reduce gain, increase Ti) only after the hardware is healthy.
    Q. Valve stiction typically produces…
    • A Limit-cycle oscillation
    • B A perfectly smooth response
    • C No effect
    • D Higher accuracy

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Fix and Record

    ✍️ Describe the CAPA and the records you would close with.
    Model answer: Corrective: repair/replace the valve positioner or packing, calibrate the transmitter, and retune with anti-windup. Preventive: add valve diagnostics and a PM task, include stiction checks in commissioning, and set trend-based alarms. Records: as-found/as-left data, tool IDs, tuning values, MOC, NCR/CAPA and updated loop sheet.

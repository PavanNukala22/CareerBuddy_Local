    What is Maintenance Engineering and Operations?

    Start from zero: why maintenance matters, the ten roles in a maintenance organisation, the skills each role needs, and the core vocabulary.

    🧭 What is Maintenance?

    Maintenance is the set of activities that keep machines, equipment, utilities and facilities in a condition where they can perform their required function safely, reliably and at the lowest total cost. When a critical machine stops, production, quality, safety and delivery all suffer, so maintenance protects the whole business — not just the machine.

    🚒 Reactive (Breakdown)

    Wait for the failure, then fix. Unplanned stops, rushed repairs, higher cost, higher safety risk. "Fix it when it breaks."

    🛡️ Proactive (Planned)

    Inspect, service and analyse before failure. Planned stops, controlled cost, safer work. "Stop it from breaking."

    💡
    Memory aid: The best maintenance job is the failure that never happens. Every role in this module — from technician to analyst — works toward that.

    Preventive Maintenance and CMMS

    The skills every role shares: how PM programmes are built, and how the CMMS records everything that happens to an asset.

    🗓️ Building a Preventive Maintenance Programme

    A good PM programme is built from evidence, not habit. Inputs: OEM manual, failure history, asset criticality, operating conditions, and legal/safety requirements.

    Asset list → Criticality → Failure modes → PM tasks → Frequency → Job plan → CMMS PM schedule

    PM elementWhat it contains
    Task listClear steps: inspect, clean, lubricate, tighten, adjust, replace.
    FrequencyTime-based (weekly, monthly) or usage-based (hours, cycles, km).
    StandardsAcceptance limits: temperature, vibration, clearance, torque values.
    Tools and partsGrease type, filters, belts, special tools, measuring instruments.
    SafetyLOTO points, PPE, permits.
    Skill and timeCraft required and estimated hours.
    Example · Conveyor gearbox PM

    Every 500 operating hours: check oil level and colour, take an oil sample, check for leaks, grease output bearing with the specified grease, check coupling bolts, record temperature. Acceptance: oil temp ≤ 80 °C; no leaks; bolts at specified torque.

    ⚠️
    Too many PMs waste labour; too few cause breakdowns. Review PM tasks using failure history and RCA at least yearly.

    💻 CMMS and the Work Order Lifecycle

    A CMMS is the digital memory of maintenance. Main modules: Asset register Work orders PM scheduler Spare parts inventory Purchasing Reports and KPIs

    Work order stageWhat happens
    RequestProblem or need is logged with asset ID, symptom and priority.
    Review / approveSupervisor or Planner validates and prioritises.
    PlanTasks, parts, tools, permits and hours are defined.
    ScheduleDate and crew assigned with production agreement.
    ExecuteWork performed; findings and measurements noted.
    CloseActual hours, parts, failure code, cause and remarks recorded.
    ReviewQuality check; data feeds KPIs and RCA.
    ⚠️
    If it is not in the CMMS, it did not happen. Missing failure codes and vague remarks make analysis impossible.

    🗂️ Maintenance Documentation and Records

    • Equipment history card: every repair, modification and part change for the asset.
    • SOPs and checklists: current revision available at the point of use.
    • Drawings and specifications: latest approved versions for repair and modification.
    • Permits and LOTO records: evidence of safe work.
    • Calibration records: for gauges, torque wrenches and test instruments.
    • Management of change: any modification is reviewed, approved and documented.

    Inspection, Troubleshooting, Tools and Safety

    Do the hands-on work correctly and safely: inspect with checklists, measure with the right tool, troubleshoot logically.

    🔍 Inspection Rounds and Checklists

    Inspection finds small defects before they become failures. A good checklist has clear check points, acceptance criteria and space to record results.

    TechniqueWhat it detects
    Visual / operator roundsLeaks, loose parts, damage, abnormal noise, guards missing.
    Vibration analysisImbalance, misalignment, bearing wear, looseness.
    Infrared thermographyHot connections, overloaded motors, blocked cooling.
    Oil analysisContamination, wear metals, degraded lubricant.
    UltrasoundAir/steam leaks, bearing lubrication condition, electrical discharge.
    💡
    Record the measured value, not just "OK". Trends in values warn you early.

    📏 Measuring Tools, Drawings and Calibration

    ToolUse
    Vernier / micrometer / dial gaugeShaft, bore, wear and runout measurements.
    Feeler gauge / straight edgeGaps, flatness, coupling alignment checks.
    Multimeter / clamp meterVoltage, resistance, current without breaking the circuit.
    Insulation tester (megger)Motor and cable insulation resistance.
    Torque wrenchTighten fasteners to specified torque.
    IR thermometer / thermal cameraSurface temperature.
    Vibration pen / analyserOverall vibration and spectrum.

    Reading drawings and specifications: check part number, revision, dimensions, tolerance, material and fits before repair or replacement.

    Example · Shaft tolerance

    Drawing: Ø20 ± 0.05 mm. Acceptable range is 19.95 – 20.05 mm. A measured value of 19.92 mm is worn below the limit — reject or repair.

    Always check the calibration sticker. A gauge past its due date can give wrong readings and invalidate earlier decisions.

    🧩 Structured Troubleshooting

    Observe → Define the symptom → Check the basics → Isolate (half-split) → Fix → Test → Document

    • Check the basics first: power, air, fuses, lubrication, interlocks, sensors, settings.
    • Half-split method: divide the system in two and test which half contains the fault.
    • Change one thing at a time and note the result.
    • Document symptom, cause and fix in the CMMS so the next person benefits.
    Example · Motor trips on overload
    1. Check overload setting against nameplate current.
    2. Measure running current with a clamp meter — higher than nameplate.
    3. Check the driven load: gearbox turns hard — low oil level found.
    4. Fix: refill oil, find and repair leak. Test: current normal.

    🦺 Safety: LOTO, PPE and Permits

    LOTO (Lockout-Tagout) sequence: Prepare and notify → Shut down → Isolate → Lock and tag → Release stored energy → Verify zero energy

    • Identify all energy sources: electrical, pneumatic, hydraulic, mechanical, thermal, gravity.
    • Each worker applies their own lock; only the owner removes it.
    • Verify by trying to start the equipment (try-out) after isolation.
    • Use permits for hot work, confined space, work at height and electrical work.
    ⚠️
    Never bypass interlocks or guards to "save time". No job is so urgent that it cannot be done safely.

    Planning, Scheduling, Coordination and Supervision

    Turn work requests into well-prepared, well-timed, safely executed jobs.

    🗓️ Planning vs Scheduling

    📝 Planning (What and How)

    Define tasks, sequence, parts, tools, permits, safety, skills and estimated hours. Done before the job is scheduled.

    📅 Scheduling (When and Who)

    Assign a date, crew and time slot using priority, production windows and resource availability.

    MetricFormula / meaning
    Schedule complianceScheduled jobs completed on time ÷ jobs scheduled × 100
    PM compliancePMs completed on time ÷ PMs due × 100
    Backlog (weeks)Outstanding job hours ÷ weekly crew hours available
    Wrench timeHands-on working time ÷ total paid time
    Planned work %Planned job hours ÷ total job hours
    💡
    A planned job has parts kitted, permits ready and tools staged. Poor planning is the biggest reason for low wrench time.

    📊 Resources, Excel/ERP and Progress Tracking

    • Resource allocation: match craft skills, hours available and priority; avoid overloading the best people.
    • Excel / ERP: track job status, planned vs actual hours, parts reservations, purchase requests and costs.
    • Progress tracking: Gantt chart or status board; update daily; flag delays early.
    • Spare parts: reserve and kit parts before the job; escalate long-lead items.
    Example · Weekly schedule

    Crew: 6 technicians × 40 h = 240 h. Approved backlog: 720 h. Backlog = 720 ÷ 240 = 3 weeks. Emergency work takes ~20% of crew time, so real capacity for planned work is ~192 h per week.

    🤝 Coordination and Project Execution

    • Cross-team coordination: production (downtime windows), stores (parts), purchasing (long-lead items), contractors, safety and quality.
    • Shutdown / turnaround planning: scope freeze, critical path, permits, contractor mobilisation, daily review meetings.
    • Project execution: scope, drawings and specifications, budget, schedule, installation, commissioning, handover and documentation.
    • Reporting: short, factual updates — status, risks, decisions needed.

    Critical path: the longest chain of dependent tasks; it decides the shutdown duration. Delay on it delays everything.

    🧑‍✈️ Supervising the Team

    🧭

    Module 05 · Advanced · Quality Engineer

    Quality, RCA and CAPA in Maintenance

    Make maintenance repairs right the first time, find real root causes and make sure failures never come back.

    ✔️ Quality in Maintenance Work

    Poor-quality maintenance creates failures: wrong grease, over-tightened bolts, misalignment, missed steps. A maintenance quality plan prevents this.

    Quality elementExample
    Inspection planCritical repairs inspected against drawings, specs and acceptance criteria.
    Hold pointsMandatory stop where work cannot continue until inspection approves (e.g. alignment check before guard fitting).
    ChecklistsStep-by-step PM and overhaul checklists with recorded values.
    Calibration controlTorque wrenches and gauges within calibration.
    DocumentationSigned records, as-built drawings, updated CMMS history.
    Quality KPIsFirst-time-fix rate, repeat failure rate, rework %.

    🔎 Root Cause Analysis Tools

    ToolUse
    Is / Is-NotDefine what, where, when and how big.
    5 WhysDrill from symptom to systemic cause.
    Ishikawa (6M)Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, Environment.
    ParetoPrioritise the few assets or causes behind most downtime.
    Fault tree / FMEAStructured analysis of failure modes and effects.
    Failed-part analysisExamine the failed component: wear pattern, fracture, heat, contamination.

    Common failure mechanisms: Lack of lubrication Contamination Misalignment Imbalance Overload Fatigue Corrosion Wrong part or procedure

    ⚠️
    Find two causes: the occurrence cause (why it failed) and the escape cause (why the PM or inspection did not catch it). Stopping at "operator error" is not a root cause.
    Example · Repeated bearing failure
    1. Why did the bearing fail? Overheated and lost lubrication.
    2. Why no lubrication? Grease quantity was guessed.
    3. Why guessed? No lubrication standard on the PM task.
    4. Why none? PM was copied from a similar machine without review.
    5. Root cause: PM task lacks grease type, quantity and interval. Escape: inspection did not include a temperature check.

    🧩 CAPA: Corrective and Preventive Action

    • Correction: fix the immediate problem (replace the bearing).
    • Corrective action: remove the cause (add lube standard and temperature check to the PM).
    • Preventive action: stop the same cause elsewhere (apply to all similar gearboxes — horizontal deployment).
    • Effectiveness check: monitor recurrence for a defined period (e.g. 3 months) before closing.
    Action strengthExample
    Strongest — design outFit grease nipples with metered auto-lubricators.
    Strong — error-proofColour-coded grease guns and fittings prevent wrong grease.
    Medium — detectAdd temperature or vibration check limits to rounds.
    Weak — people onlyRetraining or reminder posters on their own.

    Close CAPA by updating the PM task, SOP, CMMS record and spare-parts list, not just the report.

    Engineering, Reliability and Analytics

    Move from fixing machines to improving the system: calculations, design review, KPIs and reliability-centred strategy.

    📐 Engineering Calculations and Design Review

    Maintenance Engineers support decisions with numbers and sound technical documentation.

    MetricFormula
    MTBFTotal operating time ÷ number of failures
    MTTRTotal repair downtime ÷ number of repairs
    AvailabilityMTBF ÷ (MTBF + MTTR), or uptime ÷ total time
    OEEAvailability × Performance × Quality
    Failure rate (λ)Failures ÷ operating time = 1 ÷ MTBF
    Example · Pump availability

    In 720 h a pump fails 4 times, each repair taking 4 h. Downtime = 16 h; operating time = 704 h. MTBF = 704 ÷ 4 = 176 h. MTTR = 16 ÷ 4 = 4 h. Availability = 176 ÷ 180 = 97.8%.

    Design review for maintainability: access for tools and lifting, standardised parts, easy isolation points, lubrication from outside guards, condition-monitoring points, clear tagging and complete documentation.

    💡
    Every modification needs updated drawings, specifications, PM tasks, spare parts and training — treat documentation as part of the job.

    📈 KPIs, Excel / Power BI and Trend Analysis

    KPIWhat it tells you
    PM complianceAre planned PMs done on time? (typical target ≥ 90%)
    Planned vs unplanned workProactive vs reactive balance; higher planned % is better.
    MTBF / MTTR / AvailabilityReliability, repair speed and uptime.
    Backlog (weeks)Is capacity matching demand?
    Repeat failure rateAre RCA and CAPA effective?
    Maintenance cost per unitCost efficiency of the programme.
    Wrench timeHow much time is spent actually working.
    • Excel: pivot tables, Pareto by asset and failure code, formulas for MTBF and MTTR.
    • Power BI: live dashboards from CMMS data with drill-down by area and asset.
    • Trend analysis: rising MTTR, shrinking MTBF or climbing repeat failures are early warnings.
    📌
    Data quality is critical: consistent failure codes, accurate closing data and complete asset hierarchy make dashboards trustworthy.

    🏆 Reliability Strategy and Leadership

    • Criticality analysis: rank assets by consequence of failure (safety, production, cost, environment) to focus effort.
    • RCM: Reliability-Centred Maintenance picks the right task (PM, predictive, redesign, run-to-failure) for each failure mode.
    • Bathtub curve: early failures → useful-life random failures → wear-out; each phase needs a different strategy.
    • Condition monitoring: vibration, thermography, oil analysis to act just in time.
    • TPM: operators share ownership of basic care.
    • Leadership and reporting: clear, data-based reports; mentor others; share lessons across sites.
    🏆
    Expert behaviour: decisions based on data, verified root causes, standardised fixes in the CMMS, and horizontal deployment of lessons learned.

    Practice: Maintenance in a Bottling Plant

    Work through a realistic scenario covering planning, safety, RCA, CAPA and KPIs.

    🏗️ Scenario: Repeated Gearbox and Bearing Failures

    A bottling plant has a main conveyor drive gearbox that failed 3 times in 2 months, each time with a bearing failure and 6 hours of unplanned downtime. The CMMS shows 30% of PMs overdue, lubrication is done "as needed" without a standard, work orders are closed with remarks like "done", and the vibration pen used on rounds is past its calibration date. Production wants a fix without losing more shifts.

    0 / 0 correct

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Response and Planning

    ✍️ List your immediate actions and how you will plan the repair.
    Model answer: Make the area safe (LOTO, permit); inspect the failed bearing and keep it for analysis; plan the job (parts, tools, grease, alignment tool, hours, crew); schedule with production for the shortest window; kit parts; assign a skilled crew; record findings in the CMMS; quarantine the out-of-date vibration pen and check results taken with it; check similar gearboxes.
    Q. What is the best first step regarding the overdue vibration pen?
    • A Quarantine it and review readings taken with it
    • B Keep using it
    • C Delete its records
    • D Ignore it until the next audit

    2️⃣ Task 2 · RCA

    ✍️ Use 5 Whys and 6M to find the occurrence and escape causes.
    Model answer: Occurrence: lubrication is guesswork (no standard for grease type, quantity, interval); PM tasks overdue and not reviewed; possible misalignment after repairs. Escape: rounds do not record temperature/vibration values, the vibration pen is uncalibrated, and CMMS closing data is vague so repeat failures were not flagged. Systemic: PM programme not based on failure history or criticality.
    Q. Which cause is an escape cause?
    • A Inspection did not record measured values or catch rising vibration
    • B Grease quantity was guessed
    • C Bearings were wrong size
    • D Operator was new

    3️⃣ Task 3 · CAPA and KPIs

    ✍️ Propose permanent actions and how you will verify effectiveness.
    Model answer: Create a lubrication standard (grease type, quantity, interval) and fit auto-lubricators or colour-coded tools; add temperature and vibration limits to rounds; add alignment check as a hold point; calibrate and control instruments; enforce failure codes and detailed closing remarks in the CMMS; recover overdue PMs by criticality; deploy to similar gearboxes; verify with MTBF, MTTR, repeat failure rate and PM compliance for 3 months.
    Q. Which KPI best shows whether the CAPA worked on this gearbox?
    • A Repeat failure rate and MTBF of the asset
    • B Number of employees
    • C Electricity bill
    • D Number of meetings

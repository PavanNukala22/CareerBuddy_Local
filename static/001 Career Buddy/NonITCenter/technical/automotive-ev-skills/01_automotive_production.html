    What is Automotive Quality Engineering?

    Start from zero: what quality means in a car plant, what a Production Quality Engineer does every day, and the core vocabulary.

    🧭 What is Automotive Quality Engineering?

    Automotive quality engineering ensures every part, sub-assembly and vehicle meets customer, safety and regulatory requirements — at production volume, shift after shift. Vehicles contain thousands of parts, so one weak process can multiply into thousands of defective cars. Quality engineers therefore focus on preventing defects, not just finding them.

    🛡️ Quality Assurance (QA)

    Process and system: quality plans, FMEA, audits, supplier approval. "Are we building it the right way?"

    🔍 Quality Control (QC)

    Product checks: inspection, measurement, testing, sorting. "Is this part right?"

    💡
    Memory aid: QA builds the fence; QC checks nothing escaped. An automotive Quality Engineer works on both.

    👷 The Role: Responsibilities and Daily Work

    The Automotive Production Quality Engineer sits between production, maintenance, engineering, suppliers and the customer.

    🔍
    Inspection
    Defines and verifies in-process and final checks, first-off approval, audits.
    📋
    Quality Plans
    Builds and maintains Control Plans, work instructions, inspection standards.
    🧩
    RCA and CAPA
    Contains problems, finds root causes, drives 8D / CAPA to closure.
    📈
    Production KPIs
    Tracks FTQ, PPM, scrap, rework, OEE, Cpk and customer complaints.
    📝
    Documentation
    Keeps records, traceability, PPAP files and controlled documents.
    ♻️
    Lean / 5S and Improvement
    Eliminates waste, standardises work and runs improvement projects.
    A typical dayWhat the engineer does
    Start of shiftReview overnight defects, scrap and customer alerts; confirm first-off approvals.
    On the lineGemba walk; check inspection stations, gauges, Lean/5S compliance.
    Problem solvingContain nonconforming parts; lead RCA; update FMEA and Control Plan.
    End of dayUpdate KPI boards, close actions, brief the next shift.

    📖 Key Terms (What is What)

    NonconformanceAny result that fails a requirement.
    ContainmentImmediate action to stop suspect parts reaching the customer.
    FTQ / FPYFirst-time-quality: good first time with no rework.
    PPMDefective parts per million delivered.
    TraceabilityAbility to follow a part back to material, process and shift.
    Takt timeAvailable time / customer demand.

    Objective evidenceMeasurements, records and photos, not opinions.

    Quality Standards, Plans and Documentation

    Know what you are checking against: IATF 16949 and the core tools, Control Plans and controlled documentation.

    📜 Quality Standards in Automotive

    Standard / ToolPurpose
    ISO 9001General quality management system.
    IATF 16949Automotive QMS: defect prevention, variation and waste reduction.
    CSRsCustomer-specific requirements added by each OEM.
    APQPPhased product/process planning, from concept to feedback.
    PFMEA / DFMEARisk analysis of process / design failures.
    MSAProves the measurement system is reliable (e.g. Gauge R&R).
    SPCMonitors and controls process variation with charts.
    PPAPEvidence the process produces conforming parts at rate.
    📌
    IATF 16949 and these five core tools (APQP, FMEA, MSA, SPC, PPAP) are the backbone of the Quality Engineer's toolbox.

    📋 Quality Plans: from APQP to the Control Plan

    A quality plan makes quality requirements executable on the line. The chain is: Process Flow → PFMEA → Control Plan → Work Instructions / Inspection Standards

    Control Plan columnMeaning
    Process step / machineWhere the control applies.
    Characteristic (product / process)What is controlled; special characteristics are flagged.
    Specification / toleranceThe acceptance limits from the drawing.
    Evaluation / measurement methodGauge, vision system, visual standard.
    Sample size / frequencye.g. 5 pcs per hour or 100%.
    Control methodSPC, poka-yoke, checklist.
    Reaction planWhat to do if out of tolerance (stop, contain, escalate).

    APQP phases: 1 Plan 2 Product design 3 Process design 4 Validation (PPAP) 5 Launch and feedback

    🗂️ Documentation and Document Control

    • Procedures and work instructions must be the current revision at the point of use.
    • Records (inspection sheets, calibration, NCR, 8D) are kept for the period the customer/IATF requires.
    • Lot and serial traceability links material, machine, shift and inspections to shipped parts.
    • Change control: process or tooling changes need approval and, often, customer notification.
    ⚠️
    Undocumented checks are treated as not done. If it is not recorded, there is no objective evidence.

    Inspection, Measurement and Testing

    Perform inspection correctly: right method, right gauge, right frequency, and trustworthy data.

    🔍 Types of Inspection in Production

    TypeWhenAutomotive example
    IncomingMaterial/component receiptCheck steel coil thickness and certificate.
    First-offAfter setup or changeoverApprove first panel after die change.
    In-processDuring productionCheck spot-weld count and torque on assembly.
    Final / end-of-lineBefore shipmentLeak, function and appearance test.
    AuditPeriodicProduct audit of finished vehicles; LPA at the line.
    • Variable data: measured values (e.g. 25.03 mm) enable SPC.
    • Attribute data: pass/fail or counts (e.g. scratch present) are less sensitive.

    📏 Measurement Tools and MSA

    ToolUse
    Vernier / micrometerDimensions and thickness.
    Go / No-Go gauge, fixtures (checking fixtures)Fast fit/function checks.
    CMMPrecise 3D measurement of complex parts.
    Torque tester, hardness testerFastener and material properties.
    Vision systemAutomated presence/position/defect detection.

    MSA asks: is the measurement system itself good enough? Key terms: bias, linearity, stability, repeatability, reproducibility. Gauge R&R: <10% good, 10–30% conditional, >30% unacceptable.

    💡
    Always check calibration status before use. An out-of-calibration gauge can invalidate earlier inspection results.

    📊 Sampling and SPC Basics

    SPC separates normal (common-cause) variation from signals (special causes). Use X-bar/R charts for variable data and p/np charts for attributes.

    • Stable chart = predictable; capable process = fits within specification.
    • Cp = spread vs tolerance; Cpk also includes centring.
    • Typical requirements: Ppk ≥ 1.67 (initial), Cpk ≥ 1.33 (ongoing), unless the CSR says otherwise.
    Example · Door hinge hole

    Specification Ø10.00 ± 0.05 mm. Mean 10.02, σ = 0.01 → Cpk = min(10.05−10.02, 10.02−9.95)/(3σ) = 0.03/0.03 = 1.0. The process is stable but marginal; centre it to 10.00.

    Lean/5S and Production KPIs

    Keep the line organised and efficient, and read the numbers that show quality performance.

    🧹 Lean and 5S on the Shop Floor

    5SMeaningExample
    Sort (Seiri)Remove what is not needed.Remove obsolete jigs from the cell.
    Set in order (Seiton)A place for everything.Shadow boards for torque wrenches.
    Shine (Seiso)Clean and inspect.Daily cleaning reveals oil leaks.
    Standardize (Seiketsu)Make the best way the rule.Visual standards and checklists.
    Sustain (Shitsuke)Discipline and audits.Monthly 5S audit with scorecards.

    Lean wastes (TIMWOODS): Transport Inventory Motion Waiting Overproduction Over-processing Defects Skills (unused)

    Related tools: Andon (stop and call for help), Jidoka (stop on defect), Poka-yoke, Standard work, Kanban, SMED (fast changeover).

    📈 Production Quality KPIs

    KPIFormula / meaning
    FTQ / FPYGood units first time ÷ units started
    PPMDefective parts ÷ delivered parts × 1,000,000
    Scrap rateScrapped units ÷ total produced
    Rework rateReworked units ÷ total produced
    OEEAvailability × Performance × Quality
    CpkProcess capability index
    Customer complaints / warrantyField and OEM feedback (e.g. R/1000, 3MIS)
    COPQCost of scrap, rework, warranty, recalls
    Example · OEE

    Availability 90%, Performance 95%, Quality 98% → OEE = 0.90 × 0.95 × 0.98 = 83.8%. Quality losses (2%) are small, but availability (10% loss) is the biggest lever.

    🗣️ Using KPI Boards: Trend, Pareto, Action

    • Display KPIs daily at the cell (visual management) and review in short tier meetings.
    • Use Pareto to choose the top defect, trend charts to see drift, and action logs with owner and due date.
    • Escalate when the target is missed for several consecutive days.

    Problem Solving: Nonconformance, RCA and CAPA

    Contain problems, find real root causes, and make sure they never come back.

    🚨 Handling a Nonconformance

    Flow: Detect → Contain → Record (NCR) → Disposition → Root Cause → CAPA → Verify

    DispositionMeaning
    Use-as-isAccept with documented justification (and customer approval if required).
    Rework / repairBring to specification using an approved procedure.
    Sort100% inspection to separate good from bad.
    ScrapDestroy or reject; record quantity and cost.
    Return to supplierFor supplier-caused issues with an SCAR.

    🔎 Root Cause Analysis Tools

    ToolUse
    Is / Is-NotDefine what, where, when, how big.
    5 WhysDrill from symptom to systemic cause.
    Ishikawa (6M)Brainstorm causes: Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, Environment.
    ParetoPrioritise.
    Fault tree / FMEAStructured failure analysis.
    Comparison (good vs bad)Find what differs between good and bad parts.
    ⚠️
    Find two causes: the occurrence cause (why it was made) and the escape cause (why it was not detected).
    Example · Door panel gap out of tolerance
    1. Why is the gap wide? Fixture clamp is loose.
    2. Why loose? Locating pin worn.
    3. Why worn? No preventive maintenance interval defined.
    4. Why not defined? PFMEA did not list wear of the pin.
    5. Root cause: PFMEA/PM plan missing. Escape: sampling frequency too low (1/shift).

    🧩 8D and CAPA

    8DAction
    D0Emergency response, plan
    D1Form the team
    D2Describe the problem
    D3Interim containment
    D4Root cause and escape point
    D5Choose permanent corrective actions
    D6Implement and validate
    D7Prevent recurrence (FMEA, Control Plan, horizontal deployment)
    D8Recognise the team and close
    • Correction: fix the part. Corrective action: remove the cause. Preventive action: prevent the same type of cause elsewhere.
    • Effectiveness check: monitor KPIs for a defined period (e.g. 3 months) before closing.
    • Prefer error-proofing (design out > prevent > detect) over “retraining”.

    Expertise: Process Improvement and Quality Leadership

    Move from solving problems to improving systems: DMAIC, DOE, capability, audits and quality culture.

    🚀 DMAIC Improvement Projects

    PhaseKey activityTools
    DefineProblem, goal, scope, voice of the customerProject charter, SIPOC
    MeasureBaseline and validate measurementMSA, process map, data plan
    AnalyzeFind vital few causesPareto, regression, hypothesis tests
    ImproveTest and implement solutionsDOE, pilot, poka-yoke
    ControlHold the gainSPC, Control Plan, audits, A3
    Example · Reduce paint defects from 4,500 to < 1,500 PPM

    Pareto shows dirt is 60% of defects. DOE tests filter change interval and booth airflow. The best setting reduces dirt defects by 55%. Control: update the PM plan, add booth cleanliness checks to the LPA and monitor on the KPI board.

    🏭 Systems, Audits and Supplier Quality

    • Process audit verifies that the process follows the Control Plan; product audit verifies the output; system audit checks IATF/ISO compliance.
    • Supplier quality: incoming controls, PPAP, SCAR, development plans; shift from inspection to supplier process control.
    • Quality gates in launches: no passage to the next gate until capability and MSA are demonstrated.

    🌐 Smart Quality and Culture

    • Vision systems, traceability linked to VIN/serial and real-time SPC give 100% data, but the systems must still be validated (MSA) and included in the Control Plan.
    • Build a quality culture: stop-the-line authority, openness about defects, recognition of prevention, regular Gemba walks.
    • Use lessons learned to update FMEA templates and design standards for future programs.
    🏆
    Expert-level behaviour: decisions based on data, root cause verified, results standardised, and fixes deployed horizontally.

    Practice: Quality Engineering in a Body Shop

    Work through a realistic scenario covering planning, inspection, RCA, CAPA and KPIs.

    🏗️ Scenario: Spot-Weld Strength Problem

    A body-shop line welds side-frame reinforcements for an SUV program (1,200 units per day). In week 2, the OEM reports undersized weld nuggets on 3 vehicles (PPM = 1,800; target ≤ 50). Internally, FTQ drops from 98.5% to 94%. A torque/peel tester used for audits is found 2 weeks past its calibration due date, and the Control Plan sample frequency for peel tests is 1/shift.

    0 / 0 correct

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Containment and Planning

    ✍️ List your containment actions in the first 24 hours.
    Model answer: Stop shipment of suspect lots; check WIP, warehouse and in-transit stock; 100% inspect/sort affected production with a certified method and tag; notify the customer; implement interim controls (increase peel-test frequency, add 100% weld-count check); record in 8D D3.
    Q. Which is the best first step regarding the overdue tester?
    • A Quarantine it and assess the impact on previous results
    • B Keep using it
    • C Delete its records
    • D Ignore until the next audit

    2️⃣ Task 2 · RCA

    ✍️ Use 5-Why and 6M to find the occurrence and escape causes.
    Model answer: Occurrence: electrode tip wear not controlled (no tip-dress/change by weld count) and a worn current setting after changeover; Escape: peel-test frequency of 1/shift and a tester out of calibration allowed the problem to pass; PFMEA did not rate electrode wear with proper detection controls.
    Q. Which cause is the escape cause?
    • A Sampling frequency and gauge status failed to detect
    • B Electrodes wore
    • C Current dropped
    • D Operator was new

    3️⃣ Task 3 · CAPA and KPIs

    ✍️ Propose permanent actions and how you will verify effectiveness.
    Model answer: Install weld-controller stepper/monitoring for automatic tip dressing and change; add current/force monitoring with alarms; update PFMEA, Control Plan (higher peel frequency until Cpk ≥ 1.33), work instructions and PM; calibrate and control gauges; horizontal deployment to other weld cells; verify with FTQ, PPM and Cpk for 3 months and LPA checks.
    Q. Which KPI best shows customer impact?
    • A PPM
    • B OEE only
    • C Headcount
    • D Energy cost

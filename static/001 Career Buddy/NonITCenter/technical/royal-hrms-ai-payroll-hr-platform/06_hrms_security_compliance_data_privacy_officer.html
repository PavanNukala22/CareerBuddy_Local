    Module 01

    The Security, Compliance & Privacy Officer Role

    Why HR data is a prime target, what the officer is accountable for, and how the four pillars — GDPR/SOC 2/ISO 27001 governance, PII encryption, audit logging and RBAC — form one security programme for Royal HRMS.

    🛡️ Course Overview: What the Role Is

    The HRMS Security, Compliance & Data Privacy Officer protects the most sensitive data a company holds — salaries, bank accounts, national IDs (Aadhaar, PAN, SSN), health and family details — for every tenant on Royal HRMS. You design controls, prove them to auditors and customers, respond to incidents, and ensure privacy rights are honoured.

    📜
    Data governance
    GDPR, India DPDP Act, SOC 2 and ISO 27001 programmes.
    🔐
    PII encryption
    Field-level encryption, key management, tokenisation, masking.
    🧾
    Audit logs
    Tamper-evident records of who accessed or changed what.
    👥
    RBAC
    Least-privilege roles for employees, managers, HR, payroll and support.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Classify HR data and apply controls matched to sensitivity.
    2. Explain and operationalise GDPR, India's DPDP Act, SOC 2 and ISO 27001 for an HR SaaS platform.
    3. Design field-level and envelope encryption, key management, masking, tokenisation and crypto-shredding.
    4. Design tamper-evident audit logging and SIEM detections for HR data access.
    5. Design RBAC with scopes, field-level permissions, segregation of duties, ABAC and access reviews.
    6. Handle DSARs, DPIAs, security questionnaires and audits.
    7. Lead incident response for HR data breaches and fraud attempts.

    💡

    ⚖️ Intermediate: GDPR Essentials for HR

    • Lawful basis: employment contract and legal obligation (payroll, tax) usually; consent is rarely appropriate for employees because of power imbalance.
    • Principles: lawfulness, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity & confidentiality, accountability.
    • Rights: access, rectification, erasure (limited by legal retention), restriction, portability, objection.
    • Breach notification: supervisory authority within 72 hours; individuals without undue delay if high risk.
    • Transfers: outside the EEA via adequacy, SCCs + transfer impact assessment.

    Module 03

    Employee PII Encryption

    Protecting identity, bank and salary data — encryption in transit and at rest, field-level and envelope encryption, KMS and key rotation, tokenisation, masking, searchable encryption trade-offs and crypto-shredding.

    🔐 Basic: Layers of Encryption

    Figure 3.1 · Encryption layers

    ✅ Field-level encryption

    Protects data even from DB admins, backups and SQL injection dumps — only the app with key access can decrypt.

    ❌ Disk encryption only

    Protects stolen disks, but anyone who can query the DB sees plaintext PII.

    🔑 Intermediate: Envelope Encryption

    Figure 3.2 · Envelope encryption
    pythonimport os
from cryptography.hazmat.primitives.ciphers.aead import AESGCM

def encrypt_field(plaintext: str, dek: bytes, aad: bytes) -> bytes:
    nonce = os.urandom(12)                                  # unique per encryption
    ct = AESGCM(dek).encrypt(nonce, plaintext.encode(), aad)
    return nonce + ct

def decrypt_field(blob: bytes, dek: bytes, aad: bytes) -> str:
    return AESGCM(dek).decrypt(blob[:12], blob[12:], aad).decode()

aad = b"tenant=acme|table=employee_bank|field=account_no"     # binds ciphertext to context
    💡
    Use AAD (associated data) with tenant and field names. A ciphertext copied into another tenant's row will fail to decrypt — an extra isolation guarantee.

    🎭 Intermediate: Masking & Tokenisation

    TechniqueWhat it doesExample
    MaskingShows partial data in UIAccount XXXXXX4821, PAN ABCDE****F
    TokenisationReplaces value with a random token; vault maps backBank account → tok_9fa2… for analytics
    Hashing (keyed)One-way, for matching/dedupHMAC of PAN to detect duplicates without storing PAN in analytics
    PseudonymisationReplace identifiers in datasetsAnalytics uses employee_key, not name
    pythonimport hmac, hashlib
def pan_fingerprint(pan: str, key: bytes) -> str:
    return hmac.new(key, pan.upper().encode(), hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()   # keyed, not plain SHA
def mask_account(acct: str) -> str:
    return "X" * (len(acct) - 4) + acct[-4:]

    🔄 Advanced: Key Management & Rotation

    • Keys live in KMS/HSM; applications have permission to use keys, not export them.
    • Separate keys per tenant (KEK) and per environment; production keys inaccessible from staging.
    • Rotation: rotate KEKs yearly (KMS keeps old versions for decryption); re-wrap DEKs; re-encrypt data lazily or in batches.
    • BYOK/HYOK for enterprise tiers; document the effect of customer key revocation (service unavailable for that tenant).
    • Monitor KMS usage: spikes in decrypt calls can signal data exfiltration.

    🔎 Advanced: Searching Encrypted Data

    NeedApproachTrade-off
    Exact match on PANBlind index (HMAC column)Reveals equality; no partial search
    Search by last 4 digitsSeparate masked columnLimited leakage
    Range queries on salaryKeep salary in restricted table with strong RBAC rather than encrypting for rangePolicy over crypto
    ⚠️
    Avoid "home-made" crypto and deterministic encryption without understanding leakage. Use vetted libraries (e.g., Tink, AWS Encryption SDK) and review designs with security engineering.

    🏆 Expert: Crypto-Shredding & Lifecycle

    When a tenant offboards, deleting every copy (including backups) is hard. Crypto-shredding destroys the tenant's KEK: all their ciphertext — in DBs, backups, exports — becomes permanently unreadable. Combine with documented deletion certificates for customers.

    Audit Log Systems

    Designing audit trails that answer "who did what, to whose data, when, from where, and why" — event schemas, what to log, tamper-evidence, retention, privacy of logs, SIEM detection and evidence for auditors.

    🧾 Basic: Audit Logs vs Application Logs

    ✅ Audit log

    Business-meaningful, immutable record: "HR admin A viewed salary of employee B". Retained for years, used for compliance and investigations.

    ❌ Debug log

    Technical, noisy, short-lived, may contain PII by accident. Not evidence.

    Must logExample
    Authentication eventsLogin, MFA, failed attempts, SSO changes
    Access to restricted dataView salary, export bank details, download Form 16 for someone else
    ChangesSalary revision, bank change, role grants
    Admin & configurationPayroll rule edits, integration credentials changed
    Support accessRoyal HRMS support impersonation sessions

    💡
    Log masked values for sensitive fields — the audit log must not become a new copy of the PII it protects.

    🔗 Advanced: Tamper-Evidence

    Attackers and insiders try to erase traces. Make logs append-only and verifiable.

    Figure 4.1 · Tamper-evident pipeline
    pythonimport hashlib, json
def chain(prev_hash: str, event: dict) -> str:
    payload = prev_hash + json.dumps(event, sort_keys=True, separators=(",", ":"))
    return hashlib.sha256(payload.encode()).hexdigest()
# store event + its hash; any modification breaks every later hash
    • Store in WORM (write-once-read-many) storage with object lock / retention mode.
    • Separate duties: application teams cannot delete audit storage.
    • Publish signed daily digests so integrity can be verified independently.

    🚨 Advanced: Detection with SIEM

    Detection ruleSignal
    Bulk salary viewing> 50 salary views by one user in 10 min
    Off-hours exportBank-details export outside business hours from new IP
    Privilege escalationRole grant to self or by non-admin
    Bank change burstMany bank changes before payroll cut-off
    Impossible travelLogins from distant countries within an hour

SELECT actor_id, tenant_id, COUNT(*) AS views
FROM audit_event
WHERE action = 'employee.salary.view' AND ts > now() - interval '10 minutes'
GROUP BY actor_id, tenant_id HAVING COUNT(*) > 50;

    🏆 Expert: Customer-Facing Audit & Retention

    • Tenant admins can search their own audit trail and export it (customer SIEM integration via streaming).
    • Retention aligned with contracts and law (e.g., 1–7 years) and cost tiers (hot 90 days, cold archive).
    • Access to logs is itself logged; investigations follow chain-of-custody.
    • Auditors receive samples showing events for control tests (e.g., all role grants in a quarter with approvals).

    Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)

    Designing who can see and do what in an HR system — roles, permissions and scopes, manager hierarchies, segregation of duties, ABAC extensions, access reviews, break-glass and support access.

    👥 Basic: RBAC Concepts

    Figure 5.1 · RBAC model
    RoleTypical permissionsScope
    EmployeeView own payslip, apply leave, update own addressSelf
    ManagerApprove leave, view team attendanceDirect and indirect reports
    HRBPView profiles, initiate transfersAssigned business units
    Payroll adminRun payroll, view salaryAssigned legal entities
    Payroll approverApprove runsAssigned legal entities
    Tenant adminConfigure roles and settingsWhole tenant (no salary by default)

    🧩 Intermediate: Permissions, Scopes & Field-Level Control

    pythonfrom dataclasses import dataclass
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Grant:
    permission: str        # "salary.view", "leave.approve"
    scope: str             # "self" | "reports" | "business_unit:BU-7" | "entity:IN01"

def can(user, permission, target_employee):
    for g in user.grants:
        if g.permission != permission: continue
        if g.scope == "self" and target_employee.id == user.employee_id: return True
        if g.scope == "reports" and target_employee.id in org.reports_of(user.employee_id): return True
        if g.scope.startswith("entity:") and target_employee.legal_entity == g.scope[7:]: return True
    return False
    • Field-level permissions: an HRBP may see an employee profile but not the bank account or salary fields.
    • Enforce authorization server-side in one policy layer — the UI hiding a button is not security.

    ⚖️ Intermediate: Segregation of Duties (SoD)

    ConflictRiskControl
    Change bank details + approve payrollDivert salary to own accountDifferent people; system blocks combined roles
    Create employee + run payrollGhost employee fraudSeparate onboarding and payroll roles
    Grant roles + audit rolesCover tracksSecurity team owns audit
    ⚠️
    SoD must be checked at role assignment time (prevent toxic combinations) and continuously (detect drift).

    🧠 Advanced: ABAC & Policy-as-Code

    Pure RBAC causes "role explosion" (HRBP-India-Sales-L5…). ABAC adds attributes: user's country, employee's legal entity, data classification, time, device posture.

    text# Rego (OPA) style policy: payroll admin can view salary only for their entities, on managed devices
allow {
  input.action == "salary.view"
  input.user.roles[_] == "payroll_admin"
  input.resource.legal_entity == input.user.entities[_]
  input.context.device_managed == true
}

    🔁 Advanced: Access Reviews & Lifecycle

    Figure 5.2 · Joiner-mover-leaver access
    • Quarterly manager/owner certification of privileged roles.
    • Automatic removal of access when HR status changes (Royal HRMS is the HR source — use it!).
    • Dormant account detection (no login 90 days → disable).

    🏆 Expert: Break-Glass & Support Access

    • Break-glass emergency admin accounts: vaulted credentials, MFA, time-bound, alerting, mandatory post-use review.
    • Support impersonation: customer must grant time-boxed consent; read-only by default; masked restricted fields; every action audited and visible to tenant admins.
    • Just-in-time (JIT) access for engineers to production data with approvals and auto-expiry.
    ✅
    Expert outcome: zero standing access to production tenant data for Royal HRMS staff.

    🔒 Automated Authorization Tests

    pythonROLES = ["employee", "manager", "hrbp", "payroll_admin"]
@pytest.mark.parametrize("role", ROLES)
def test_salary_field_visibility(role, client, employee_other_bu):
    r = client(role).get(f"/v1/employees/{employee_other_bu.id}")
    if role in ("employee", "manager", "hrbp"):
        assert "salary" not in r.json()            # field-level restriction

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions for security, GRC and privacy roles in HR SaaS.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.What's the difference between GDPR and ISO 27001?
    GDPR is EU law governing personal data processing; ISO 27001 is a voluntary, certifiable standard for an information security management system. They overlap on security controls.
    IQ2.What is least privilege?
    Granting only the minimum access needed for a job, for the minimum time.
    IQ3.Why is consent rarely the lawful basis for employee data?
    The employer-employee power imbalance makes consent hard to be freely given; contract and legal obligation are typical bases.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.Explain SOC 2 Type I vs Type II.
    Type I assesses control design at a point in time; Type II tests operating effectiveness over a period (3–12 months).
    IQ5.How would you encrypt bank details in Royal HRMS?
    Field-level AES-GCM with per-tenant DEKs wrapped by KMS KEKs, AAD binding, masked display, blind index for matching, access logged, rotation.
    IQ6.What should an audit event contain?
    Timestamp, tenant, actor (id, role, IP, session), action, target, masked changes, reason, result, request ID.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you make audit logs tamper-evident?
    Hash chaining, WORM storage with object lock, separation of duties, signed periodic digests, verification jobs.
    IQ8.Design RBAC for an HRMS.
    Roles with permissions and scopes (self, reports, BU, entity), field-level restrictions for restricted data, SoD rules, ABAC for context, server-side policy engine, access reviews.
    IQ9.How do you handle a DSAR as a processor?
    Notify/assist the controller per DPA, locate data across systems, redact third parties, deliver securely within deadlines, log the request.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.A customer asks how you'd prove no Royal HRMS staff can read their salaries.
    Explain field encryption with tenant keys (optionally BYOK), zero standing access, JIT with customer consent, masked support views, audit trails visible to them, SOC 2 evidence and pen-test results.
    IQ11.Walk me through a breach response affecting EU and Indian employees.
    Contain, preserve evidence, scope, assess risk, notify customers (controllers) without undue delay so they can meet GDPR 72-hour and DPDP Board/individual notification duties, remediation, RCA, lessons learned.
    IQ12.How do you run continuous compliance?
    Unified control framework mapped to all regimes, automated evidence collection, control owners and KPIs, continuous monitoring, internal audits, management review.

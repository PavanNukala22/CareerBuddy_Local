    ⚖️ People-Analytics Ethics (Read First)

    ⚠️
    Predictions about people can harm careers. Royal HRMS policy: scores are decision support, never automatic decisions; no use of protected attributes (gender, religion, caste, age, disability) as features; minimum group sizes in dashboards; employees informed through the privacy notice.

    ✅ Responsible use

    Use flight risk to trigger stay conversations, career moves, workload reviews. Aggregate by team for leadership.

    ❌ Harmful use

    Denying promotions because a model says "will leave"; showing individual risk to peers; using health or family data.

    Module 02

    Churn Prediction Modeling

    Build an attrition model step by step — defining the target, creating point-in-time employee snapshots, engineering features, training and evaluating classifiers, survival models, calibration and deployment.

    🚪 Basic: Defining Attrition

    Before modelling, agree on the target. "Attrition" usually means voluntary exits (resignations) within a horizon such as 6 months. Involuntary exits (terminations, retirements, contract ends) are different processes and should be excluded or modelled separately.

    MetricFormulaExample
    Monthly attrition rateExits in month / average headcount18 / 1,200 = 1.5%
    Annualised attritionSum of 12 monthly rates (approx.)≈ 18%
    Regretted attritionExits of high performers / exits40% of exits regretted
    Early attritionExits within first 6–12 months / hires22% of hires
    sql-- monthly voluntary attrition per tenant
SELECT tenant_id, date_trunc('month', exit_date) AS month,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE exit_type = 'resignation')::numeric
       / NULLIF(AVG(headcount), 0) AS voluntary_rate
FROM exits JOIN monthly_headcount USING (tenant_id, month)
GROUP BY 1, 2;

    🧊 Intermediate: Point-in-Time Snapshots

    The most common bug in churn models is leakage: using information that would not have been known at prediction time. Build an employee-month snapshot table: one row per employee per month, features computed as of that date, label = "resigned within next 6 months".

    Figure 2.1 · Snapshot construction
    pythonimport pandas as pd

def build_snapshot(as_of: pd.Timestamp, emp, events):
    active = emp[(emp.hire_date <= as_of) & ((emp.exit_date.isna()) | (emp.exit_date > as_of))].copy()
    past = events[events.date <= as_of]                       # only past data -> no leakage
    active["tenure_m"] = (as_of - active.hire_date).dt.days / 30.4
    lv = past[past.type == "leave"].groupby("emp_id").size()
    active["leaves_90d"] = active.emp_id.map(
        past[(past.type == "leave") & (past.date > as_of - pd.Timedelta(days=90))]
        .groupby("emp_id").size()).fillna(0)
    horizon = as_of + pd.DateOffset(months=6)
    active["label"] = ((active.exit_type == "resignation")
                       & (active.exit_date > as_of) & (active.exit_date <= horizon)).astype(int)
    active["as_of"] = as_of
    return active
    ⚠️
    Features like "notice period served" or "exit interview score" are perfect predictors — and pure leakage. If a feature only exists because the person is leaving, drop it.

    🤖 Advanced: Training & Evaluating Models

    Attrition data is imbalanced (perhaps 8% positive). Accuracy is misleading — a model predicting "nobody leaves" is 92% accurate and useless.

    pythonfrom sklearn.model_selection import TimeSeriesSplit
from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score, average_precision_score
import lightgbm as lgb

X, y = df[FEATURES], df["label"]
# time-based split: train on older snapshots, validate on newer ones
train = df.as_of < "2025-07-01"; valid = df.as_of >= "2025-07-01"
model = lgb.LGBMClassifier(n_estimators=400, learning_rate=0.03,
                           num_leaves=31, class_weight="balanced")
model.fit(X[train], y[train])
p = model.predict_proba(X[valid])[:, 1]
print("ROC-AUC", roc_auc_score(y[valid], p))
print("PR-AUC ", average_precision_score(y[valid], p))
    MetricWhy use it
    PR-AUCFocuses on the rare positive class
    Precision@top 10%How many of the flagged top decile really leave — matches HRBP capacity
    Recall@top 10%Share of all leavers captured in the top decile
    Calibration (Brier score)A 30% score should mean ~30% actually leave
    📌
    Always use time-based validation, never random splits — random splits leak future patterns into training and inflate metrics.

    ⏱️ Advanced: Survival Analysis

    Classification answers "will they leave in 6 months?". Survival analysis answers "when are they likely to leave?" and handles employees who have not left yet (censored observations) correctly.

cph.fit(tenure_df[["tenure_months", "left", "compa_ratio", "months_since_promo", "overtime_trend"]],
        duration_col="tenure_months", event_col="left")

    🏆 Expert: Calibration, Drift & Multi-Tenant Models

    • Calibrate probabilities (isotonic or Platt) so HRBPs can trust "35% risk".
    • Global vs per-tenant models — small tenants lack data; use a global model with tenant-level features, fine-tune for large tenants.
    • Drift monitoring — feature distributions and actual attrition change (e.g., after a market hiring boom); retrain quarterly or on drift alerts.
    • Fairness audit — compare false positive rates across gender and age groups even though they are not features.

    🎯 Advanced: Measuring Interventions (Uplift)

    High risk does not mean an intervention will help. Uplift modelling estimates the change in leave probability caused by an action, so HR spends effort where it matters.

    🟢
    Persuadables
    Stay only if we act — highest value.
    ⚪
    Sure things
    Stay anyway — don't spend budget.
    ⚫
    Lost causes
    Leave anyway — limited value in acting.
    🔴
    Do-not-disturb
    Acting increases risk — avoid.
    python# simple two-model (T-learner) uplift estimate
m_treat = lgb.LGBMClassifier().fit(X[treated], y[treated])
m_ctrl  = lgb.LGBMClassifier().fit(X[~treated], y[~treated])
uplift = m_ctrl.predict_proba(X_now)[:, 1] - m_treat.predict_proba(X_now)[:, 1]
# positive uplift = intervention reduces leave probability
    📌
    Run randomised pilots (e.g., stay-interview program for half of high-risk employees) to measure true effect before scaling.

    Headcount Planning Algorithms

    Forecasting how many people each team needs — supply/demand models, attrition-adjusted hiring plans, time-series forecasting, driver-based demand, scenario planning and optimisation under budget constraints.

    📈 Basic: The Headcount Equation

    Every planning model starts from a simple flow equation:

    textHeadcount(end) = Headcount(start) + Hires + Internal moves in − Exits − Internal moves out
Required hires  = Target headcount − Projected headcount after attrition
    Figure 4.1 · Supply vs demand
    TermMeaning
    SupplyPeople you will have if you do nothing
    DemandPeople the business needs (driven by volume, projects, ratios)
    GapDemand − supply → hire, redeploy or reduce
    Time-to-fillDays from requisition to joining — shifts hiring earlier

    🧮 Intermediate: Attrition-Adjusted Hiring Plan

    pythonimport numpy as np
start = 420                       # current headcount
monthly_attr = 0.015              # forecast voluntary + involuntary
target = [430, 440, 450, 460, 470, 480]
time_to_fill_m = 2                # requisitions must open 2 months earlier
hc, plan = start, []
for m, t in enumerate(target):
    exits = round(hc * monthly_attr)
    hires = max(0, t - (hc - exits))
    hc = hc - exits + hires
    plan.append({"month": m + 1, "exits": exits, "hires": hires,
                 "open_req_in_month": max(1, m + 1 - time_to_fill_m)})
print(plan[:3])
    💡
    Forecast attrition by segment (role, location, tenure) — a single company-wide rate hides that call-centre attrition may be 4%/month while engineering is 1%.

    📉 Intermediate: Time-Series Forecasting

    For demand driven by volume (e.g., stores, call centres) forecast the driver, then convert to headcount with productivity ratios.

    🏆 Expert: Optimisation Under Constraints

    With a fixed budget, which mix of hires, contractors, overtime and redeployments meets demand at lowest cost? This is a linear programming problem.

    pythonfrom scipy.optimize import linprog
# decision vars: [fte_hires, contractors, overtime_fte]
cost = [12.0, 15.0, 9.0]                     # lakh per FTE-equivalent per year
A_ub = [[-1, -1, -1],                        # meet capacity gap >= 40
        [0, 0, 1],                           # overtime cap 8 FTE-equivalent (labour law)
        [0, 1, 0]]                           # contractor cap 15
b_ub = [-40, 8, 15]
res = linprog(cost, A_ub=A_ub, b_ub=b_ub, bounds=[(0, None)] * 3)
print(res.x)   # -> optimal mix
    ⚠️
    Optimisers follow constraints literally. Encode legal limits (overtime caps, working-hour laws) and human limits (sustained overtime causes attrition) or the model will recommend burnout.

    Building pipelines that turn pulse surveys, open comments, exit interviews and HR tickets into sentiment scores and themes — preprocessing, multilingual models, topic modelling, aggregation, anonymity and production MLOps.

    💬 Basic: Sources & Outputs

    SourceVolumeSignal
    Pulse surveys (eNPS + comment)MonthlyEngagement and reasons
    Exit interviewsPer exitRoot causes of attrition
    HR helpdesk ticketsDailyPain points with policies & payroll
    Internal Q&A, town hallsEvent-basedOrganisational mood

    Outputs: sentiment (positive / neutral / negative or a score), themes/topics (pay, manager, workload, growth), emotion where useful, and trends by team over time.

    🔁 Intermediate: Pipeline Architecture

    Figure 5.1 · Sentiment pipeline
    pythonimport re
EMAIL = re.compile(r"[\w.+-]+@[\w-]+\.[\w.]+")
PHONE = re.compile(r"\+?\d[\d\s-]{8,}\d")
def redact(text: str, names: set[str]) -> str:
    text = EMAIL.sub("[EMAIL]", text)
    text = PHONE.sub("[PHONE]", text)
    for n in names:                       # known employee/manager names in tenant
        text = re.sub(rf"\b{re.escape(n)}\b", "[PERSON]", text, flags=re.I)
    return text
    ⚠️
    Comments often name managers ("Ravi never listens"). Redact names before storage and modelling, and never show raw comments for groups smaller than the anonymity threshold (e.g., 5).

    🧠 Intermediate: Models — From Lexicons to Transformers

    ApproachProsCons
    Lexicon (VADER)Fast, no trainingMisses context, sarcasm, Indian English and code-mixed text
    Classic ML (TF-IDF + logistic regression)Cheap, explainableNeeds labelled data per language
    Transformer (fine-tuned multilingual BERT/XLM-R)Context-aware, multilingualGPU cost, needs MLOps
    LLM zero-shot classificationFast to start, theme extractionCost, consistency, data-privacy controls

    🗂️ Advanced: Topic Modelling & Theme Tagging

    Sentiment alone says "negative"; themes say about what. Use BERTopic (embeddings + clustering) for discovery, then a supervised multi-label classifier on a stable taxonomy for reporting.

    Figure 5.2 · HR theme taxonomy

topic_model = BERTopic(language="multilingual", min_topic_size=15)

    📏 Advanced: Aggregation & Statistical Care

    • Report sentiment as share negative with confidence intervals, not single averages.
    • Use minimum group size (n ≥ 5) and suppress smaller cells.
    • Compare to baseline (team's previous quarters, company average) to avoid over-reacting.
    • Weight by response rate — a team with 20% response is less reliable.

    Module 08

    Real-World Scenarios & Troubleshooting

    Production problems and case studies from workforce analytics at Royal HRMS — leaking models, drifting forecasts, misleading sentiment and fairness incidents.

    🕵️ Troubleshooting: A Model That Is "Too Good"

    Symptom: validation ROC-AUC 0.97; in production, top-decile precision is only 18%.

    CheckFinding
    Split methodRandom split mixed future snapshots into training
    Feature auditlast_working_day_set flag present for leavers
    FixTime-based validation; remove post-resignation features; re-evaluate (AUC 0.78, precision@10% 41%)
    💡
    A realistic attrition model has ROC-AUC around 0.70–0.85. Anything far higher should trigger a leakage hunt.

    📉 Troubleshooting: Forecast Misses After Market Shift

    Symptom: attrition forecast 1.2%/month; actual 2.4% for engineers for 4 months. Root cause: hiring boom in the market — a regime change absent from training data. Fix: add external drivers (job-posting index), retrain more frequently, show uncertainty bands and segment-level drift alerts.

    💬 Troubleshooting: Sentiment Says Everyone Is Angry

    Symptom: a new tenant's sentiment is 70% negative. Investigation shows comments are in Marathi and the model, trained mostly on English/Hindi, defaults to negative. Fix: language detection → route to supported models; mark unsupported languages "not scored"; add labelled Marathi data.

    ⚖️ Case Study: Fairness Review Before Launch

    A churn model flagged women returning from maternity leave as high risk 2.1× more often, driven by "leave days in last 180d". The team removes long statutory leave from leave features, adds "returner" support playbooks, and documents the result in the model card. Post-fix, FPR ratio is 1.1×.

    ✅
    The fairness audit was a launch gate, so the issue never reached managers.

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Technical and ethical questions asked for people-analytics and HR data-science roles.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.How do you calculate attrition rate?
    Exits in a period divided by average headcount in that period; specify voluntary vs involuntary and annualise for comparison.
    IQ2.Why is accuracy a bad metric for attrition?
    Classes are imbalanced; predicting "no one leaves" scores high accuracy but is useless. Use PR-AUC, precision/recall at top-k, calibration.
    IQ3.What is data leakage?
    Using information in training that would not be available at prediction time, inflating offline metrics and failing in production.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you build features for churn without leakage?
    Point-in-time snapshots: compute every feature as of the prediction date using only prior data; labels from the future window; time-based validation.
    IQ5.When would you use survival analysis instead of classification?
    When timing matters or many employees are still employed (censored); it predicts hazard and survival curves over time.
    IQ6.How would you forecast call-centre headcount?
    Forecast call volume with seasonal time-series, convert to agents using handle time, service levels and shrinkage, adjust for attrition and time-to-fill.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.Explain SHAP to an HR leader.
    It splits each person's risk into how much each factor pushed it up or down relative to average — like an itemised bill for the score.
    IQ8.How do you know an intervention works?
    Randomised pilots or quasi-experimental designs; uplift modelling to target persuadables; measure retention difference with confidence intervals.
    IQ9.How do you handle multilingual comments?
    Language detection, multilingual transformers or language-specific models, golden labelled sets per language, mark unsupported languages rather than guess.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you ensure a people-analytics model is fair?
    Exclude protected attributes as features, audit outcomes by group (FPR, recall, calibration), detect proxies, mitigate, document model cards, governance sign-off and periodic re-audit.
    IQ11.Global model or per-tenant models for 600 tenants?
    Global model with tenant-level features and calibration for small tenants; fine-tuned or separate models for large tenants with enough data; monitor per-tenant performance; respect contracts on data use.
    IQ12.A CHRO wants individual flight-risk scores shown to all managers. Your response?
    Explain harm and stigma risks, propose HRBP-only individual access, team-level insights for managers with suggested actions, audit logging, and pilot with measured outcomes.

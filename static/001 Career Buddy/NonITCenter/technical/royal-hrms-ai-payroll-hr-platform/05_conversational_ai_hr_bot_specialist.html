    🤖 Course Overview: What the Role Is

    An HR Bot Specialist designs, trains and operates the conversational assistant that answers employee questions — "How many leaves do I have?", "Why is my salary lower this month?", "Update my bank account" — and hands over to HR when needed. You own understanding quality, conversation design, automation flows and channel integrations.

    Natural Language Understanding (NLU)

    How the assistant understands employees — text normalisation, intent classification, entity extraction, confidence thresholds, multilingual and code-mixed text, and the hybrid NLU + LLM architecture.

    🧠 Basic: What NLU Does

    NLU converts free text into structured meaning the bot can act on.

    json{
  "text": "apply 2 days casual leave from next monday",
  "intent": {"name": "apply_leave", "confidence": 0.94},
  "entities": [
    {"entity": "leave_type", "value": "casual"},
    {"entity": "duration_days", "value": 2},
    {"entity": "start_date", "value": "2026-10-12"}
  ]
}

    🔤 Intermediate: Entity Extraction

    Entity typeTechniqueExample
    Dates & durationsRule/duckling-style parsers"next Monday", "2 days"
    Closed listsLookup with synonymssick = SL = "medical leave"
    Free valuesTrained NERManager names, project codes
    Regex patternsRegex with validationIFSC ^[A-Z]{4}0[A-Z0-9]{6}$, employee ID E-\d{4}
    pythonimport re
SYNONYMS = {"sl": "sick", "medical": "sick", "cl": "casual", "pl": "privilege", "el": "privilege"}
def leave_type(text: str):
    for tok in re.findall(r"[a-z]+", text.lower()):
        if tok in SYNONYMS: return SYNONYMS[tok]
        if tok in {"sick", "casual", "privilege", "maternity", "paternity"}: return tok
    return None
print(leave_type("Need 1 CL tomorrow"))   # casual
    💡
    HR language is full of abbreviations and local terms — CL, SL, PL, LOP, F&F, Form 16, HRA. A synonym dictionary per tenant is one of the cheapest accuracy wins.

    🎚️ Intermediate: Confidence Thresholds & Disambiguation

    ✅ Calibrated behaviour

    ≥ 0.80 act; 0.50–0.80 confirm ("Do you want to check your leave balance or apply for leave?"); < 0.50 fallback with suggestions.

    ❌ Over-confident bot

    Always acts on the top intent — applies leave when the user only asked about policy.

    ⚠️
    For actions with side effects (apply leave, change bank details), always confirm with a summary before executing, regardless of confidence.

    🌐 Advanced: Multilingual & Code-Mixed Text

    Indian employees often type Hinglish ("mera salary kab aayega"), regional languages, or transliterated text. Options: multilingual encoders (LaBSE, XLM-R, multilingual-e5), language detection + translation, or training data covering code-mix.

    pythonfrom sentence_transformers import SentenceTransformer
import numpy as np
enc = SentenceTransformer("sentence-transformers/LaBSE")
examples = {"payslip_query": ["where is my payslip", "salary slip download", "mera payslip kahan hai"],
            "salary_date":   ["when will salary be credited", "salary kab aayega"]}
labels, vecs = [], []
for intent, utts in examples.items():
    for u in utts:
        labels.append(intent); vecs.append(enc.encode(u, normalize_embeddings=True))
q = enc.encode("salary kab credit hoga", normalize_embeddings=True)
scores = np.array(vecs) @ q
print(labels[int(scores.argmax())], round(float(scores.max()), 2))   # salary_date

    🔀 Advanced: Hybrid NLU + LLM

    Classic intent classifiers are fast, cheap and predictable for transactional intents. LLMs excel at open questions over policies. Royal HRMS uses a router.

    Figure 2.2 · Hybrid routing
    NeedBest tool
    "Apply leave", "Download payslip"Intent + slot-filling flow (deterministic, auditable)
    "Is paternity leave paid for adoptive parents?"RAG over tenant policy docs with citations
    "I'm being harassed by my manager"Sensitive-topic detector → immediate human escalation

    🏆 Expert: Guardrails

    • Never let the LLM invent numbers: salary, balances and dates come only from APIs.
    • Ground policy answers in retrieved passages; refuse when nothing relevant is found.
    • Detect sensitive topics (harassment, health, self-harm, legal disputes) and route to trained humans with care.
    • Prompt-injection defence: treat retrieved documents and user text as data, not instructions.

    Module 03

    Employee Query Intent Training

    Designing the HR intent taxonomy, collecting and labelling utterances, training and evaluating classifiers, confusion analysis, active learning from fallbacks, and keeping the model healthy across tenants.

    🏷️ Basic: Designing the Intent Taxonomy

    Figure 3.1 · HR intent domains
    DomainExample intents
    Leave & attendancecheck_leave_balance, apply_leave, cancel_leave, regularise_attendance, holiday_list
    Payrollpayslip_download, salary_date, salary_deduction_query, reimbursement_status
    Taxform16_download, investment_declaration, regime_choice_help
    Profileupdate_bank_account, update_address, download_letter
    💡
    Intents should be mutually exclusive and actionable. If two intents trigger the same response, merge them; if one intent needs very different fulfilment, split it.

    📝 Intermediate: Collecting & Labelling Data

    • Sources: historical HR tickets, emails, search logs, pilot conversations (with PII redacted).
    • Aim for diverse utterances: short ("payslip"), long, misspelled, Hinglish, polite and angry variants.
    • 30–100 utterances per intent to start; more for confusable intents.
    • Write labelling guidelines with edge cases; measure inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's kappa).
    yamlintent: salary_deduction_query
examples:
  - why is my salary less this month
  - deduction in salary kyu hua
  - what is this LOP deduction
  - net pay dropped by 3000 explain
  - my in hand is lower than last month
counter_examples:            # belong elsewhere
  - when will salary come     # -> salary_date

    🧪 Intermediate: Training & Evaluation

    pythonfrom sklearn.feature_extraction.text import TfidfVectorizer

    MetricMeaning
    Per-intent precision / recallWhere the model is wrong and how
    Macro-F1Balanced view across rare and frequent intents
    Confusion matrixWhich pairs get mixed (e.g., salary_date vs salary_deduction_query)

    📌
    Keep a frozen test set from real traffic. Never tune on it; otherwise your metrics stop reflecting reality.

    🏢 Advanced: Multi-Tenant Intent Models

    • Core taxonomy shared across tenants; tenants add custom intents (e.g., "canteen coupon balance").
    • Tenant synonyms and entity lists (locations, leave types) loaded per tenant.
    • Evaluate per tenant; a global improvement can regress a tenant with unusual vocabulary.

    PracticeWhy
    Versioned datasets and modelsReproduce any past behaviour

    A good handoff never makes the employee repeat themselves. The bot creates a structured ticket with intent, entities, conversation transcript and employee context.

    pythonfrom dataclasses import dataclass
@dataclass
class InboundMessage:            # every channel maps into this
    tenant_id: str
    channel: str                 # "teams" | "slack" | "whatsapp" | "app"
    channel_user_id: str
    text: str
    attachments: list

def handle(msg: InboundMessage):
    employee = identity.resolve(msg.tenant_id, msg.channel, msg.channel_user_id)   # mapped via SSO
    reply = brain.respond(employee, msg.text)
    return adapters[msg.channel].render(reply)     # adaptive card / Block Kit / WhatsApp list

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked for conversational AI and HR chatbot roles.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.What is the difference between an intent and an entity?
    Intent is the goal (apply_leave); entities are parameters needed to fulfil it (dates, leave type).
    IQ2.What is a fallback and how do you reduce it?
    The bot can't understand or answer; reduce via better training data, synonyms, new intents from clustering fallbacks, and clarifying prompts.
    IQ3.What metrics define a good HR bot?
    Containment, fallback rate, CSAT, ticket deflection, task completion, latency.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you build an intent taxonomy?
    From real ticket/query data, group by required fulfilment, ensure intents are distinct and actionable, define guidelines and examples per intent.
    IQ5.How do you evaluate an intent model?
    Frozen real-traffic test set, per-intent precision/recall, macro-F1, confusion matrix, out-of-scope detection.
    IQ6.Design a bot-to-ticket handoff.
    Confirm, create structured ticket with intent/entities/transcript/context, route by rules, communicate SLA, track status in chat, close the loop.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.When would you use an LLM versus a classifier?
    Classifier/flows for transactional actions needing predictability and audit; LLM with RAG for open policy questions; router decides; guardrails for numbers and sensitive topics.
    IQ8.How do you secure bot access to HR data?
    Delegated per-user tokens, RBAC enforced at API, no super-admin accounts, tenant-isolated stores, PII masking, signed webhooks, audit logs.
    IQ9.How do you handle multilingual employees?
    Language detection, multilingual encoders, code-mixed training data, translated reviewed content, per-language evaluation.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you prevent hallucinated policy answers?
    Retrieval with tenant/audience filters and current versions, grounded prompts with citations, refusal thresholds, evaluation sets, human review of new policy domains.
    IQ11.How would you run a multi-tenant bot platform?
    Shared core models and taxonomy, tenant configuration for intents/synonyms/channels/escalations, per-tenant indices, per-tenant evaluation, canary releases, residency controls.
    IQ12.Containment is 40%; you must reach 65% in two quarters. Plan?
    Analyse top fallback clusters and ticket categories, add flows for top 10 transactional intents, RAG for policies, proactive messages for predictable spikes, channel expansion, measure weekly and iterate.

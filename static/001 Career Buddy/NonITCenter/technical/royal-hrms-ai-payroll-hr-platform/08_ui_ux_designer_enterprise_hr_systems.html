    Module 01

    The Enterprise HR UI/UX Designer

    What makes designing for HR and payroll different from consumer apps, who you design for, the design process, and the four core skills: workflow simplification, dashboard prototyping, mobile HR design and design systems.

    🎨 Course Overview: What the Role Is

    The UI/UX Designer for Enterprise HR Systems makes complex HR and payroll work feel simple — for an employee applying leave on a crowded bus, a manager approving 30 requests, and a payroll admin validating 50,000 payslips. You research users, simplify workflows, prototype dashboards and mobile flows, and maintain the Royal HRMS design system.

    🔀
    Workflow simplification
    Turning 12-step, multi-approver processes into clear, guided flows.
    📊
    Dashboard prototyping
    Self-service and admin dashboards that answer questions at a glance.
    📱
    Mobile HR app design
    Fast, accessible, offline-tolerant flows for every employee.
    🧩
    Design systems
    Tokens, components and patterns shared across web and mobile.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Explain what makes enterprise HR UX different and design for distinct HR personas.
    2. Map and simplify complex multi-actor HR workflows using proven techniques.
    3. Prototype role-based self-service dashboards with appropriate visualisations and states.
    4. Design mobile HR flows that are fast, accessible, localised and offline-tolerant.
    5. Build and govern a design system with tokens, components and multi-tenant theming.
    6. Test designs with usability, accessibility and analytics methods.
    7. Measure and communicate the business impact of UX work.

    Module 02

    Complex Workflow Simplification

    Making multi-step, multi-actor HR processes easy — mapping current flows, task analysis, progressive disclosure, smart defaults, error prevention, approvals and status visibility, measured with usability metrics.

    🗺️ Basic: Map the Current Flow

    You cannot simplify what you have not mapped. Start with journey maps and service blueprints for real processes — e.g., an employee reimbursement claim today:

    Figure 2.1 · Reimbursement claim today

    Pain points: 6 hand-offs, no status visibility, policy violations caught late, receipts lost. Each pain point is a design opportunity.

    🔍 Intermediate: Simplification Techniques

    TechniqueHR example
    Remove stepsAuto-route to approver from org chart instead of choosing manually
    Smart defaultsLeave type defaulted to the one with balance; dates from calendar tap
    Progressive disclosureShow advanced options (half-day, partial hours) only when needed
    Inline validation"Exceeds monthly meal limit by ₹420" while typing, not after submit
    AutomationOCR reads receipt amount and date
    Status visibilityTimeline: Submitted → Manager approved → Finance → Paid in Oct payroll
    Figure 2.2 · Redesigned claim
    💡
    Count interaction cost: clicks, fields, decisions, waiting time. A 40% reduction in fields usually beats any visual polish.

    🧭 Intermediate: Designing Approvals

    📬
    Context in the request
    Show balance, team calendar and overlap when approving leave.
    ✅
    Batch actions
    Approve 12 regularisations at once with filters.
    🔔
    Actionable notifications
    Approve/Reject directly from push or email.
    ⏳
    SLAs visible
    "Pending 2 days — auto-escalates tomorrow".
    🔁
    Delegation
    Out-of-office delegation in one tap.

    ⚠️ Advanced: Error Prevention & Recovery

    ✅ Prevent & explain

    Disable impossible dates, show why ("Holiday"), confirm high-impact actions with a summary, allow undo within 30 seconds.

    ❌ Punish after the fact

    Generic "Error 400" after a 3-page form; data lost on back button; no explanation of policy.

    html<!-- inline, accessible validation message -->
<label for="amount">Claim amount (₹)</label>
<input id="amount" inputmode="decimal" aria-describedby="amount-hint amount-error" aria-invalid="true">
<p id="amount-hint" class="hint">Meal limit: ₹1,500 per day</p>
<p id="amount-error" class="error" role="alert">₹1,920 exceeds the daily limit by ₹420. Add a reason or reduce the amount.</p>

    📏 Advanced: Measuring Simplification

    MetricBeforeAfter (target)
    Task success rate72%≥ 95%
    Time on task (claim)6 min 40 s< 2 min
    Fields per claim146
    Policy violations at finance stage18%< 3%
    SUS score58≥ 75

    🏆 Expert: Configurable Workflows Without Chaos

    Every tenant configures workflows differently. Experts design pattern-level components (stepper, timeline, approval card, rule summary) that render any configured workflow consistently, plus an admin workflow builder with previews and validation.

    ⚠️
    A common mistake is designing the "happy path" for one demo customer. Test designs with the most complex tenant configurations — multiple approvers, conditional steps, delegation.

    Module 03

    Self-Service Dashboard Prototyping

    Designing employee, manager and admin dashboards — choosing what to show, information hierarchy, data visualisation for HR, empty and error states, prototyping fidelity, testing with real data, and permission-aware layouts.

    📊 Basic: Dashboards Answer Questions

    A dashboard is not a collection of charts; it answers the top questions of a role in under 10 seconds.

    RoleTop questionsDashboard elements
    EmployeeWhen is payday? How much leave? Anything pending?Payday countdown, leave balances, to-dos
    ManagerWho's out today? What needs my approval? Any risks?Team availability, approvals queue, overtime alerts
    Payroll adminIs this month's run on track? Exceptions?Run status stepper, exception counts, variance summary
    HR leaderHeadcount, attrition, hiringKPI cards with trends, drill-down

    🧱 Intermediate: Layout & Hierarchy

    Figure 3.1 · Employee home dashboard
    • Use a 12-column grid on desktop, single column on mobile, consistent 8-pt spacing.
    • Lead with actionable items; informational items below.
    • One primary action per card.

    📈 Intermediate: Data Visualisation for HR

    DataGood chartAvoid
    Leave balance by typeSimple bars or numbers3D pies
    Attendance over monthCalendar heat-mapLine charts for categorical days
    Attrition trendLine with benchmarkDual axes without labels
    Headcount by departmentSorted horizontal barsUnsorted pies with 12 slices
    Payroll run progressStepper with countsGauge charts
    💡
    Always show comparison (vs last month, vs target) and units. "Attrition 1.8%" means nothing without "monthly, vs 1.4% last quarter".

    🧪 Intermediate: Prototyping Fidelity

    FidelityToolUse for
    Sketch / low-fiPaper, FigJamExploring many layouts fast
    Mid-fi wireframeFigmaInformation architecture, card priority
    High-fi interactiveFigma prototype with variablesUsability testing, stakeholder review
    Coded prototypeHTML/React with sample dataData-heavy dashboards, performance, responsiveness
    📌
    Prototype with realistic data — long names ("Venkata Subrahmanya Lakshmi Narasimha"), 0 and 999 values, 15 pending approvals. Lorem ipsum hides layout breakage.

    🫙 Advanced: Empty, Loading, Error & Permission States

    🫙
    Empty
    "No pending approvals 🎉" with next best action.
    ⏳
    Loading
    Skeletons matching final layout; no layout shift.
    ⚠️
    Error
    Explain and retry: "Couldn't load payslips. Retry".
    🔒
    No permission
    Hide restricted widgets; never show "Access denied" teasers of salary data.
    🧪
    Partial data
    "Attendance syncing from device — last updated 08:42".

    🏆 Expert: Configurable, Permission-Aware Dashboards

    • Widgets declare required permissions; layout reflows when a widget is hidden.
    • Tenants configure which widgets appear for which roles; designers define rules (min/max widgets, ordering) to keep quality.
    • Instrument dashboards: widget click-through, time to first action, unused widgets → remove.
    json{
  "widget": "team_availability",
  "requires": ["attendance.view:reports"],
  "sizes": ["1x1", "2x1"],
  "empty_state": "No team members scheduled today",
  "refresh": "5m"
}

    Module 04

    Mobile HR App Design

    Designing the Royal HRMS app for every employee — mobile-first task flows, attendance check-in UX, thumb zones and touch targets, offline and low-bandwidth, notifications, accessibility, localisation and security UX.

    📱 Basic: Mobile-First Principles

    • Design for one-handed use: primary actions in the thumb zone (bottom of screen).
    • Touch targets ≥ 44×44 pt (iOS) / 48×48 dp (Android).
    • One primary task per screen; bottom navigation for 3–5 top areas (Home, Attendance, Leave, Pay, More).
    • Readable type: body ≥ 16 px equivalent; support dynamic type.
    Figure 4.1 · Mobile top tasks

    ⏰ Intermediate: Attendance Check-in UX

    Check-in happens dozens of times a month under time pressure — it must be fast and forgiving.

    StateUX
    Inside geofence, face match OKOne tap → confirmation with time in < 3 s, haptic feedback
    Outside geofenceShow map with distance; offer "request regularisation"
    Low light / face failGuidance ("Move to brighter area"), retry, fallback PIN
    OfflineQueue punch with device time; "Will sync when online" badge
    Mock location detectedClear, non-accusatory message and help link
    ⚠️
    Never just say "Check-in failed". Tell the employee why and what to do next — otherwise they raise a ticket or lose pay.

    🌐 Intermediate: Offline & Low Bandwidth

    📦
    Offline queue
    Actions saved locally and synced with clear status.
    🪶
    Lightweight screens
    Compressed images, lazy loading, < 1 MB first load.
    🔁
    Optimistic UI
    Show success immediately with rollback on failure.
    🧭
    Sync indicators
    "Last synced 10:02" on balances.

    🔔 Advanced: Notifications That Help

    NotificationGoodBad
    Payday"Salary credited — view payslip" with deep link"New update available"
    Approval"Asha requests 2 days CL (12–13 Oct). Approve / Reject""You have a pending task"
    Reminder"Submit tax proofs by 15 Jan to avoid higher TDS"Daily spam
    💡
    Let users control categories and quiet hours. Notification fatigue leads to disabled notifications — and missed approvals.

    ♿ Advanced: Accessibility & Localisation

    • WCAG 2.2 AA: contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for text, labels for screen readers, no colour-only status ("Approved ✓" not just green).
    • Support TalkBack/VoiceOver, dynamic font sizes, reduced motion.
    • Localisation: Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Arabic (RTL mirroring), Indian number format (1,00,000), local date formats.
    • Allow text expansion: German or Tamil labels can be 30–40% longer than English.

    🏆 Expert: Security UX

    NeedDesign
    Sensitive screens (payslip)Biometric unlock, blur in app switcher, auto-lock after inactivity
    Bank changeStep-up OTP, show masked old account, notify via other channel
    Shared devicesQuick logout, kiosk mode, no cached payslips
    Phishing resistanceConsistent branding, in-app messages instead of links in SMS

    Design Systems Management

    Running the Royal HRMS design system — foundations and design tokens, component libraries in Figma and code, documentation, multi-tenant theming, accessibility baked in, contribution and governance, versioning and adoption metrics.

    🧩 Basic: What a Design System Contains

    Figure 5.1 · Royal HRMS design system

    🎛️ Intermediate: Design Tokens

    Tokens are named design decisions stored as data and shared by Figma and code. Use tiers: primitive → semantic → component.

    json{
  "color": {
    "navy": {"900": {"value": "#14213D"}},
    "amber": {"500": {"value": "#FCA311"}},
    "action": {"primary": {"value": "{color.navy.900}"}},
    "status": {"approved": {"value": "#0F7B0F"}, "rejected": {"value": "#E03E3E"}}
  },
  "space": {"1": {"value": "4px"}, "2": {"value": "8px"}, "4": {"value": "16px"}},
  "radius": {"control": {"value": "8px"}},
  "font": {"family": {"base": {"value": "'Noto Sans', system-ui, sans-serif"}}}
}
    css:root {
  --color-action-primary: #14213D;
  --color-status-approved: #0F7B0F;
  --space-2: 8px;
  --radius-control: 8px;
  --font-base: 'Noto Sans', system-ui, sans-serif;
}
.btn-primary { background: var(--color-action-primary); border-radius: var(--radius-control); padding: var(--space-2) calc(var(--space-2) * 2); }
    💡
    Components should reference semantic tokens (action.primary), never raw hex. That is what makes rebranding and dark mode cheap.

    🏷️ Intermediate: Component Specifications

    Spec itemExample: Approval card
    AnatomyAvatar, requester, request type, dates, context line, actions
    VariantsLeave, attendance, expense, transfer
    StatesDefault, hover, focus, loading, approved, rejected, expired
    BehaviourSwipe actions on mobile; keyboard A/R shortcuts on desktop
    AccessibilityFocus order, ARIA labels, live region announcements on action
    ContentMax 2 lines context; dates in locale format

    🎨 Advanced: Multi-Tenant Theming

    Enterprise customers want their brand in Royal HRMS. Allow controlled theming: logo, primary/accent colours, maybe font — validated for contrast automatically.

    pythondef contrast_ratio(hex1, hex2):
    def lum(h):
        r, g, b = [int(h[i:i+2], 16) / 255 for i in (1, 3, 5)]
        f = lambda c: c / 12.92 if c <= 0.03928 else ((c + 0.055) / 1.055) ** 2.4
        return 0.2126 * f(r) + 0.7152 * f(g) + 0.0722 * f(b)
    a, b = sorted([lum(hex1), lum(hex2)], reverse=True)
    return (a + 0.05) / (b + 0.05)
print(round(contrast_ratio("#FFFFFF", "#14213D"), 1))   # ~15.6 -> passes AA
    ⚠️
    Without guardrails, a tenant's light-yellow brand colour on white buttons fails accessibility. Auto-derive accessible text colours or reject the theme.

    📉 Scenario: Leave Requests Abandoned on Mobile

    Data: 38% abandonment on the leave form. Session recordings show users stuck on the "Leave type" dropdown with 11 options and no balances shown.

    • Redesign: show types as cards with balances; default to most-used with balance; hide types not applicable to the employee.
    • Result: abandonment 38% → 9%; time on task 74 s → 22 s.

    🌏 Scenario: Arabic RTL Launch

    Mirrored layouts broke progress steppers and charts. Fix: logical CSS properties (margin-inline-start), mirrored icons only where directional (arrows, not checkmarks), charts kept LTR for time axes with RTL labels, testing with native speakers.

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions for enterprise UX and product design roles in HR tech.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.How is enterprise UX different from consumer UX?
    Mandatory use, complex roles and permissions, dense data, configurable policies, high-stakes accuracy; success measured in efficiency and error reduction.
    IQ2.What is progressive disclosure?
    Revealing advanced or rarely used options only when needed to keep primary flows simple.
    IQ3.What makes a good empty state?
    Explains why it's empty, reassures, and offers the next best action.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How would you simplify a multi-approver workflow?
    Map current flow, remove unnecessary steps, auto-route, give approvers context, batch actions, status timeline, SLAs and delegation.
    IQ5.How do you design dashboards for different roles?
    Identify top questions per role, prioritise actionable items, appropriate visualisations with comparisons, permission-aware widgets, test with real data.
    IQ6.Which usability metrics do you use?
    Task success rate, time on task, error rate, SUS, CES, plus product metrics like tickets and abandonment.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you design for frontline workers?
    Research context, mobile-first, large targets, icons + text, local languages, offline, low data, kiosk/shared-device modes.
    IQ8.Explain design tokens and their tiers.
    Named design decisions as data; primitive values, semantic meanings and component-specific tokens; shared across Figma and code for consistency and theming.
    IQ9.How do you ensure accessibility?
    Build it into components and tokens, WCAG 2.2 AA criteria, automated and manual audits with screen readers, inclusive research, regression checks.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you govern a design system across 15 squads?
    Clear ownership, contribution model, semver and deprecation, office hours, documentation, metrics, visual regression tests, roadmap aligned with product needs.
    IQ11.How do you prove UX impact to leadership?
    Tie design changes to business metrics: tickets deflected, approval time, payroll corrections, adoption, with before/after data and experiments.
    IQ12.How do you support multi-tenant branding without harming usability?
    Limited, validated theming via semantic tokens, automatic contrast enforcement, previews, fixed semantics for status colours.

    Course Overview

    Immigration & Visa Processing Specialist: overview, objectives and prerequisites.

    🧭 Course Overview

    This course teaches how an Immigration and Visa Processing Specialist moves employees and candidates across borders legally. It covers three skills: work permit and visa application processing (with H-1B, Tier 2, Iqama and Golden Visa as examples), consulate coordination and legal brief filing.

    The role matters because one missed document or deadline can delay a start date, stop a person from working, or put the employer's sponsor status at risk. Done well, people arrive on time with the right status and the employer stays compliant. Done badly, projects stall and legal exposure grows.

    Each skill has four levels, formulas and worked examples. The extra material adds tools, testing, good practice, projects, troubleshooting and interview practice. Figures are illustrative. Immigration rules, fees, processing times and route names change often and differ by country, so this course teaches the working method, not current law. A specialist always follows the current official guidance and works under a licensed immigration lawyer or authorised adviser where the law requires it.

    When you finish this course you will be able to:

    1. Explain the main work routes: H-1B, UK Skilled Worker (formerly Tier 2), Saudi iqama and UAE Golden Visa.
    2. Build a complete document pack and measure its completeness.
    3. Work backwards from a start date to a latest filing date.
    4. Calculate approval rate, RFE rate and selection rate and read them correctly.

    🛂 Fundamentals: What work permits and visas are, and the main routes

    A visa lets a person enter or stay in a country for a stated purpose. A work permit lets them work. In some countries these are one document, in others two. The specialist makes sure the person has the right permission before starting work, because working without permission can harm the person and the employer.

    Routes differ by country. The US H-1B is for specialty occupations and is sponsored by an employer, with a yearly numerical limit and a registration step for most new cases. The UK Skilled Worker route replaced the older Tier 2 (General) route and needs a licensed sponsor and a Certificate of Sponsorship. In Saudi Arabia the iqama is the residence permit linked to the employer and the work visa. The UAE Golden Visa is a long-term residence option for groups such as investors and certain skilled professionals. Names, limits and rules change, so always check current official guidance.

    RouteCountryCore idea
    H-1BUnited StatesEmployer-sponsored specialty occupation, annual limit, registration, labor condition application
    Skilled Worker (formerly Tier 2)United KingdomLicensed sponsor, Certificate of Sponsorship, salary and skill checks
    IqamaSaudi ArabiaResidence permit tied to the work visa and the employer, renewed regularly
    Golden VisaUnited Arab EmiratesLong-term residence for eligible groups, not tied to one employer in the usual way
    formulaCost per case = Government fees + Legal fees + Other costs (translation, courier)
    🧪
    Practical Example: A work visa case has government fees of $2,000, legal fees of $3,500 and translation and courier of $300. Cost per case = 2,000 + 3,500 + 300 = $5,800.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Using an old route name such as Tier 2 as if it were still current
    • Assuming one country's rules apply in another
    • Starting work before the permit is valid
    Hands-on PracticeGovernment fees are $1,500, legal fees $3,000 and courier $200. What is the cost per case?
    1,500 + 3,000 + 200 = $4,700.

    📑 Core Concepts: Eligibility and the document pack

    Every application proves three things: the employer is allowed to sponsor, the job qualifies, and the person qualifies. Each needs documents. Typical documents are a valid passport, the job offer or contract, an employer letter, degree and experience proofs, a clean record or police certificate where required, a medical report, photographs and fee payment proof.

    Many countries require a passport that stays valid for a minimum period beyond the stay, often six months, so confirm the rule and check the date first. Degrees and certificates often need attestation, apostille or legalisation, which can take weeks, so start those early.

    DocumentPurposeWhat to check
    PassportIdentity and travelValidity period, blank pages, name spelling
    Job offer or contractProves role and payTitle, salary, dates match the application
    Degree and experience proofsProves qualificationAttested or apostilled, translated if needed
    Employer letterConfirms sponsorshipSigned, dated, on letterhead
    Police and medical certificatesCharacter and health checksIssued within the allowed period
    Photographs and fee proofApplication completenessRight size, right background, receipt attached
    formulaDocument completeness rate = Documents complete ÷ Documents required × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: A pack needs 14 documents and 12 are ready. Completeness = 12 ÷ 14 × 100 = 85.7 percent. Two items are still missing, so filing waits.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Checking passport validity only at the end
    • Letting names differ between documents
    • Submitting uncertified copies when originals are required
    Hands-on PracticeA pack needs 20 documents and 17 are ready. What is the completeness rate?
    17 ÷ 20 × 100 = 85 percent.

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Processing an application step by step

    A clean process has eight stages: intake of the case, eligibility check, document collection, form preparation, review by the responsible lawyer or adviser, filing with fees, tracking and biometrics, and decision with follow-up such as visa stamping or card collection. Each stage has an owner and an output.

    Plan from the start date backwards. Add the processing time, a buffer for questions from the authority and time for pre-steps such as attestation. Processing times change, so use the authority's current published estimate or your provider's latest experience, not an old figure.

    StageOwnerOutput
    IntakeCoordinatorCase record and target start date
    Eligibility checkSpecialist and counselGo or no-go with route chosen
    Document collectionCoordinator and candidateComplete, certified pack
    Preparation and reviewSpecialist and counselSigned forms and support letters
    Filing and feesSpecialistReceipt and reference number
    Tracking and biometricsCoordinatorAppointment confirmed, status updated
    Decision and follow-upSpecialistApproval notice and entry or stamping done
    formulaLatest filing date = Required start date − (Processing days + Buffer days)
    🧪
    Practical Example: The required start date is 1 November. Processing is 45 days and the buffer is 15 days, a total of 60 days. Latest filing date = 1 November − 60 days = 2 September.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Using last year's processing time
    • No buffer for requests for more information
    • Filing before the lawyer's review is finished
    Hands-on PracticeThe start date is 1 April (non-leap year), processing is 30 days and the buffer is 10 days. What is the latest filing date?
    Total 40 days. 1 April − 40 days = 20 February.

    📈 Intermediate Concepts: Case KPIs and real scenarios

    Track a few numbers each month. Approval rate shows outcomes. RFE rate (the share of cases where the authority asked for more evidence) shows the quality of the first filing. Cycle time shows speed. Selection rate shows luck and planning in lottery-style routes such as the H-1B cap registration.

    Read numbers with care. A high approval rate can hide cases that were never filed because they looked weak. A rising RFE rate usually points to unclear evidence or weak job descriptions.

    KPIFormulaUse
    Approval rateApproved ÷ DecidedOutcome quality
    RFE rateRFEs received ÷ Cases filedFirst-filing quality
    Selection rateSelected ÷ RegisteredLottery-style routes
    Cycle timeDecision date − Filing dateSpeed
    On-time start rateStarted on planned date ÷ CasesBusiness impact
    formulaApproval rate = Approved ÷ Decided × 100   |   RFE rate = RFEs ÷ Filed × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: In a quarter, 120 cases were decided and 114 were approved: 114 ÷ 120 = 95 percent. Of 120 cases filed, 24 received an RFE: 24 ÷ 120 = 20 percent. Separately, 18 of 50 registrations were selected: 36 percent. The 20 percent RFE rate shows evidence packs need work.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Reading approval rate without the RFE rate
    • Comparing lottery selection rate with approval rate
    • Not recording the reason for each RFE
    Hands-on Practice200 cases were filed and 30 received an RFE. What is the RFE rate?
    30 ÷ 200 × 100 = 15 percent.

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: edge cases, sponsor duties and risk

    Experts handle cases outside the normal path: employer changes, cap-exempt or special categories, extensions that overlap with travel, name changes, dependants, and people who must stay in country while a renewal is pending. Each needs a lawyer-reviewed plan.

    Sponsors carry duties. Examples include keeping records, reporting changes and, in some systems, public access files or workforce quota rules such as Saudi nationalisation bands. Overstay, unauthorised work and misstatements are serious risks for the person and the employer. Build alerts for expiry and keep a clear audit trail.

    RiskControl
    Expired permit while workingExpiry alerts at 120, 90 and 60 days
    Wrong route chosenCounsel review before filing
    Sponsor duty missedDuty checklist per route, reviewed quarterly
    Misstatement in formsTwo-person check against source documents
    Quota or cap shortfallPlan earlier and keep alternatives ready
    formulaOverstay risk exposure = Cases within 90 days of expiry with no renewal filed ÷ Active cases × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: There are 300 active cases and 18 are within 90 days of expiry with no renewal filed. Exposure = 18 ÷ 300 × 100 = 6 percent. Those 18 cases get priority action.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Relying on the employee to report expiry
    • Letting a pending renewal replace a legal check of working rights
    • Keeping records in personal files
    Hands-on PracticeThere are 250 active cases and 20 are within 90 days of expiry with no renewal filed. What is the exposure?
    20 ÷ 250 × 100 = 8 percent.

    🏛️ Fundamentals: What consulates do and key terms

    An embassy represents a country in another country's capital. A consulate serves people in other cities and handles most visa and document work. Many countries also use outsourced visa application centres to collect forms, fingerprints and photographs, while the decision stays with the government.

    Coordination matters because appointments, biometrics and passport handling are fixed steps with limited slots. A missed appointment can add weeks. The specialist books, prepares and tracks so the applicant arrives with a perfect pack.

    TermMeaning
    EmbassyMain diplomatic mission in the capital
    ConsulateOffice handling visas and documents in other cities
    Visa application centreOutsourced site that collects applications and biometrics
    BiometricsFingerprints and photo taken at an appointment
    ApostilleCertificate that authenticates a public document for other member countries of the Hague Apostille Convention
    LegalisationChain of certification for use in countries outside that convention
    formulaAppointment lead time = Appointment date − Request date
    🧪
    Practical Example: A request is made on 3 January and the first slot is on 21 January. Lead time = 18 days.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Booking an appointment before the pack is complete
    • Using the wrong consulate for the applicant's place of residence
    • Ignoring appointment slot limits in busy seasons
    Hands-on PracticeA request is made on 10 February and the appointment is on 5 March (non-leap year). What is the lead time?
    18 days to 28 February plus 5 days = 23 days.

    🧭 Core Concepts: Types of consular processes

    Not every case goes through a consulate the same way. Some need an in-person interview, some only a document drop-off, some are fully online as an e-visa, some are granted on arrival, and some are changes of status inside the country with no consulate involved. Choose the route first because it decides the steps and the lead time.

    Many applications also need documents certified before submission. The usual chain is notarisation, then apostille or consular legalisation, then certified translation. Each step adds days, so map the chain and add the times.

    PathWhen usedTypical extra step
    Consulate interviewMany work and long-stay visasAppointment and interview preparation
    Visa application centreCollection of forms and biometricsCourier of passports
    E-visa or onlineShort stays or simple routesUpload quality checks
    Visa on arrivalLimited countries and purposesPre-approval letter if required
    In-country change of statusPerson already lawfully in countryStatus and travel check
    formulaDocument chain time = Notary days + Apostille or legalisation days + Translation days + Other days
    🧪
    Practical Example: Notary takes 2 days, apostille 5 days, translation 3 days and consulate legalisation 7 days. Total = 2 + 5 + 3 + 7 = 17 days.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Assuming every country accepts an apostille
    • Translating before legalising when order matters
    • Not adding courier time between steps
    Hands-on PracticeNotary takes 1 day, apostille 4 days, translation 3 days and legalisation 10 days. What is the total chain time?
    1 + 4 + 3 + 10 = 18 days.

    📋 Intermediate Concepts: Coordinating a consular application step by step

    Follow seven steps. Confirm the route and the correct consulate. Book the appointment and record the confirmation. Build an applicant pack with a checklist and a spare set of copies. Brief the applicant on dress, timing, what to carry and what questions may come. Track the submission receipt. Chase the status at planned intervals. Confirm passport return and check the visa details.

    Passports can be held by the consulate or courier for days or weeks, so tell the applicant not to plan travel until the passport is back. Check every detail on the visa, such as name, dates and category, as soon as it is received.

    StepOwnerCheck
    Confirm route and consulateSpecialistWritten confirmation from official source
    Book appointmentCoordinatorConfirmation saved with date and place
    Build applicant packCoordinatorChecklist 100 percent complete
    Brief applicantSpecialistBriefing sheet sent and acknowledged
    Submit and trackCoordinatorReceipt and tracking number logged
    Passport return and checkCoordinatorVisa details match the application
    formulaPassport-out days = Passport return date − Submission date
    🧪
    Practical Example: A passport is submitted on 5 March and returned on 19 March. Passport-out days = 14. The applicant is told not to book travel before 19 March.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Letting the applicant book flights before the visa arrives
    • Not keeping copies of everything submitted
    • Not checking visa details on receipt
    Hands-on PracticeA passport is submitted on 12 June and returned on 30 June. What are the passport-out days?
    30 − 12 = 18 days.

    📈 Intermediate Concepts: Consulate KPIs and scenarios

    Track appointment lead time, first-submission acceptance rate (cases accepted without a request to fix or add documents), visa turnaround, passport-out days, no-show rate and cost per applicant. Review them by consulate and by month.

    Scenario: acceptance falls at one consulate. Read the returned-document reasons, find the common cause, such as photo size or missing translations, and fix the checklist for that location.

    🧪

    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Reporting only averages and hiding a weak consulate
    • No reminder for the applicant before the appointment
    • Not recording reasons for returned documents
    Hands-on Practice80 applicants submitted and 68 were accepted first time. What is the acceptance rate?

    🌍 Advanced Concepts: Expert: edge cases, delays and risk

    Experts plan for closures, backlogs, new rules at short notice, applicants who must apply in their country of residence, name transliteration differences, emergency or expedited requests and sanctions-related restrictions. They keep alternate routes ready and talk to the consulate through proper channels.

    Passports and personal data are sensitive. Use secure couriers and tracked handovers, limit who can see applicant files and delete copies when no longer needed under the data rules.

    RiskControl
    Consulate closure or backlogAlternate city or route, early booking
    Rule change at short noticeSource check before every filing
    Name mismatch across documentsName sheet agreed before submission
    Passport lost in transitTracked courier and insured handover
    Personal data exposureAccess limits and retention rules

    🧪

    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Zero slack in the plan
    • Emailing passport scans without protection
    • Not keeping a name sheet
    Hands-on PracticeRequired arrival 31 October, appointment 5 October, turnaround 12 days, travel prep 4 days. What is the slack?
    Ready date = 21 October. Slack = 10 days.

    ⚖️ Fundamentals: What legal briefs and filings are, and key terms

    In immigration, a filing is any document sent to the authority: a petition, an extension, a response to a request for evidence, an appeal or a motion. A legal brief is the written argument that explains why the case meets the law, with exhibits as proof. A licensed lawyer or authorised representative decides the legal position and signs where required. The specialist prepares, organises and tracks.

    Deadlines are strict. A late filing can end a case and cannot always be cured. That is why calendaring, internal deadlines and proof of delivery matter.

    RFERequest for evidence from the authority

    ExhibitEvidence attached to a brief
    Power of attorney or representation formAuthority for a representative to act

    🗂️ Core Concepts: Types of filings and their contents

    Different filings answer different needs. An initial petition starts the case. An extension keeps lawful status. An amendment reports a change. An RFE response answers specific questions. An appeal or motion asks for another review. Each has a standard content list.

    A good packet has a cover letter, a list of issues, the brief, an indexed set of exhibits and the required forms with fees. A clear index and page numbers help the reviewer find proof quickly.

    FilingPurposeTypical content
    Initial petitionStart the caseForms, support letter, evidence of role and qualification
    ExtensionContinue stayUpdated forms, proof of continued need
    AmendmentReport a changeNew terms, new evidence
    RFE responseAnswer the authority's questionsCover letter, brief, indexed exhibits
    Appeal or motionSeek reviewBrief, grounds, evidence, fee proof
    formulaPacket pages = Brief pages + Exhibit pages + Form pages + Index and cover pages
    🧪
    Practical Example: A brief is 12 pages, exhibits 85 pages, forms 10 pages and index and cover 3 pages. Packet = 12 + 85 + 10 + 3 = 110 pages.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • No index, so the reviewer cannot find proof
    • Attaching irrelevant evidence
    • Leaving out a required form or fee
    Hands-on PracticeA brief is 8 pages, exhibits 60, forms 6 and index and cover 2. How many pages in total?
    8 + 60 + 6 + 2 = 76 pages.

    🗓️ Intermediate Concepts: Preparing and filing step by step

    Work in eight steps. Calendar the official deadline and set an earlier internal deadline. Read the notice and write a list of every issue it raises. Gather evidence for each issue. Draft the brief with the lawyer. Index and number the exhibits. Run a quality check against the notice. Obtain signatures and fees. File by the required method and keep proof of delivery.

    Never let the final week be the working week. Build in a review step by someone who did not draft the packet.

    Evidence gatheringSpecialist and clientDocuments mapped to issues
    Draft briefCounselSigned-off argument
    Index and quality checkSecond reviewerChecklist with no gaps
    Filing and proofSpecialistReceipt, tracking or confirmation saved
    formulaInternal deadline = Official deadline − Safety margin (days)
    🧪
    Practical Example: The official deadline is 30 June and the safety margin is 7 calendar days. Internal deadline = 23 June. Work is complete and reviewed by then.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Setting the internal deadline equal to the official one
    • No second reviewer
    • Not saving proof of filing
    Hands-on PracticeThe official deadline is 31 August and the safety margin is 10 days. What is the internal deadline?
    31 August − 10 days = 21 August.

    Measure on-time filing rate, first-pass quality (filings returned for formal defects), RFE response success rate, cycle time and cost per filing. Review defect reasons every month.

    Scenario: two packets were rejected for a missing signature. Add a signature check to the quality list and a second reviewer for every filing.

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: edge cases, ethics and risk

    Experts handle conflicting evidence, short deadlines that cross public holidays and time zones, multi-country filings and privileged communications. They never misstate facts, never invent evidence and escalate any doubt to the responsible lawyer. They also respect limits on what a non-lawyer may do in each country.

    Build a template library and a lessons file so each case improves the next. Protect client data with access limits and secure transfer.

    Misstatement or weak evidenceSource check and lawyer sign-off
    Unauthorised practice of lawClear role split with the responsible lawyer

    Security & Compliance

    Data protection, access, fraud and legal risk for this role. Each skill has a security question in the Assessment.

    🔐 Security & Compliance: the big picture

    Immigration work handles passports, birth and marriage certificates, criminal record checks, medical results and salary letters. A leak can lead to identity theft and can harm the applicant's case. A false or altered document can lead to refusal, bans and legal penalties for the person and the employer.

    Security here means protecting documents, keeping every statement truthful, controlling who can see files, and keeping accurate records of filings and deadlines. Rules differ by country, so confirm them with an immigration lawyer.

    Security riskWhy it mattersControl
    Document leakIdentity theft and harm to the applicantEncrypted storage and secure sharing
    False or altered documentRefusal, bans and penaltiesVerify originals and never alter papers
    Missed deadlineLoss of status or rightsCentral calendar with alerts
    Scam sites and fake agentsMoney and data lossUse official government portals and approved providers
    Unauthorised accessStaff see files they do not needRole-based access and logs
    Data kept too longBreaks retention rulesRetention schedule and secure deletion

    🛡️ Security for: Work permit and visa application processing (H-1B, Tier 2, Iqama, Golden Visa)

    • Collect only the documents the application needs and store them in an encrypted folder.
    • Never submit a document you have not checked against the original.
    • Use official portals only and keep payment receipts.

    🛡️ Security for: Consulate coordination

    • Share appointment details and passports only through secure channels.
    • Keep a log of who handled each passport when it travels to a consulate.
    • Beware of fake appointment sites that charge extra fees.

    🛡️ Security for: Legal brief filing

    • Legal briefs hold privileged information, so mark them confidential and limit access.
    • Keep copies of what was filed, with proof of delivery and time.
    • Make sure every statement in a brief is true and supported.

    🧪 Measuring security

    formulaDeadline compliance = Deadlines met ÷ Deadlines due × 100
    🧪
    Worked example: Of 60 filing deadlines, 57 are met. Compliance = 57 ÷ 60 × 100 = 95 percent. The three misses are reviewed and calendar alerts are added.

    Security checklist

    • Documents are stored in encrypted, access-controlled folders.
    • Originals are checked before submission.
    • Deadlines are tracked on one calendar with alerts.
    • Only official portals and approved providers are used.
    • Access is role-based and logged.
    • Files are deleted on schedule.

    Interview Preparation

    Five questions per skill with model answers: concept, scenario and numeric.

    💬 Interview Questions: Work permit and visa application processing (H-1B, Tier 2, Iqama, Golden Visa)

    IQ1.What is the difference between a visa and a work permit?
    A visa is permission to enter or stay for a stated purpose. A work permit is permission to work. Some countries combine them and others keep them separate, so the specialist checks which applies.
    IQ2.What happened to the UK Tier 2 route?
    Tier 2 (General) was replaced by the Skilled Worker route. It needs a licensed sponsor and a Certificate of Sponsorship. Always confirm the current name and rules from the official source.
    IQ3.A manager wants an employee to start work while the permit is pending. What do you do?
    Check the rules for that country and route with the responsible lawyer. Working without valid permission is a serious risk, so the answer is not to allow work until it is confirmed lawful.
    IQ4.The RFE rate has risen to 25 percent. How do you respond?
    Review the RFE reasons, find the common causes, such as thin job descriptions or missing evidence, and fix the templates and checklists. Track the rate monthly to confirm improvement.
    IQ5.The start date is 1 April, processing is 30 days and the buffer is 10 days. What is the latest filing date?
    Total 40 days. 1 April minus 40 days is 20 February in a non-leap year.

    💬 Interview Questions: Consulate coordination

    IQ1.What does an apostille do?
    It authenticates a public document for use in other member countries of the Hague Apostille Convention. For countries outside it, a consular legalisation chain is used instead.
    IQ2.Why book appointments only when the pack is complete?
    Slots are limited. Arriving with missing items wastes the slot and adds weeks.
    IQ3.An applicant's passport is delayed at a consulate. What do you do?
    Check tracking, contact the consulate or centre through the proper channel and tell the applicant not to travel. Update the case plan and the manager with the new date.
    IQ4.The visa shows a wrong date of birth. What now?
    Do not travel. Contact the consulate or authority to correct it, using the original documents. Record the cause and add a name and date check to the checklist.
    IQ5.Notary 1 day, apostille 4 days, translation 3 days, legalisation 10 days. What is the total?
    1 + 4 + 3 + 10 = 18 days.

    💬 Interview Questions: Legal brief filing

    IQ1.What is an RFE?
    A request for evidence is a notice from the authority asking for more proof before it decides the case. The response has a strict deadline.
    IQ2.Why set an internal deadline?
    It creates a safety margin for review, signatures and delivery problems, so the filing is never made on the last day.
    IQ3.You discover two days before a deadline that a key exhibit is missing. What do you do?
    Tell the responsible lawyer at once, look for substitute evidence, and plan a complete filing before the official date. Record the cause and fix the checklist.
    IQ4.A colleague wants to leave out a weak fact from a filing. How do you respond?
    Raise it with the responsible lawyer. Facts must not be misstated, and the lawyer decides how to present the case honestly.
    IQ5.50 RFE responses were filed and 38 were approved. What is the success rate?
    38 ÷ 50 × 100 = 76 percent.

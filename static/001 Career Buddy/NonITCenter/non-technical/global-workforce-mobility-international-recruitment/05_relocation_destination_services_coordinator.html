    Module 01

    Course Overview

    Relocation & Destination Services Coordinator: overview, objectives and prerequisites.

    🧭 Course Overview

    This course teaches how a Relocation and Destination Services Coordinator helps employees and families settle in a new country. It covers four skills: housing search oversight, school search assistance, international transit logistics and shipment of household goods tracking.

    The role matters because a move succeeds or fails on practical details. A family with no suitable home, no school place, a missed connection or a lost shipment is unhappy and unproductive. A good coordinator plans early, controls cost, tracks every step and keeps the family informed.

    Each skill has four levels, formulas and worked examples. The extra material adds tools, testing, good practice, projects, troubleshooting and interview practice. Figures are illustrative and use US dollars. Costs, customs rules and school systems differ by country, so confirm current details with suppliers and local advisers.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    When you finish this course you will be able to:

    1. Explain the housing search process and set a budget against an allowance.
    2. Calculate rent-to-allowance ratio, total monthly cost, viewings needed and upfront cash.
    3. Explain school types and curricula and compare school costs.
    4. Calculate applications needed, placement rate and sibling-discount cost.
    5. Plan travel legs, calculate door-to-door time and chargeable weight.
    6. Measure on-time arrival and expected disruption cost.
    7. Calculate container fill, transit days and cost per cubic metre.
    8. Track a shipment through its milestones and handle customs documents.
    9. Calculate damage claim rate and insurance premium.
    10. Report relocation results with clear key performance indicators.
    11. Protect family, child and housing data and prevent scams and fraud in relocation.

    🏠 Fundamentals: What housing search oversight is and why it matters

    Housing search oversight means guiding and checking the search for a home in the new country. The coordinator sets the budget, briefs a local agent, checks the shortlist and makes sure the lease is fair and safe. The coordinator does not usually choose the home but protects the employee from poor or costly choices.

    It matters because housing is the biggest single cost of a move and the biggest cause of early stress. A good search saves money and lets the employee start work calmly.

    TermMeaning
    Housing allowanceAmount the employer pays towards rent
    Rent-to-allowance ratioRent as a percentage of the allowance
    ShortlistHomes chosen for viewing
    LeaseContract to rent a home
    DepositRefundable sum held against damage
    Home finding agentLocal supplier who finds and shows homes
    formulaRent-to-allowance ratio = Monthly rent ÷ Housing allowance × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: The rent is $3,000 and the allowance is $3,500. Ratio = 3,000 ÷ 3,500 × 100 = 85.7 percent. The home is inside the allowance with $500 to spare.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Starting the search before the budget is agreed
    • Ignoring the daily commute
    • Not telling the employee what the allowance covers
    Hands-on PracticeThe rent is $2,400 and the allowance is $3,000. What is the ratio?
    2,400 ÷ 3,000 × 100 = 80 percent.

    📍 Core Concepts: Needs, location and the true monthly cost

    List the needs before searching: number of bedrooms, distance to work and school, transport links, safety, pets and parking. Rank them as must-have or nice-to-have. The advertised rent is only part of the cost, so add utilities and service charges.

    Compare homes on total monthly cost, not on rent alone. A cheaper rent far from work can cost more once travel is added.

    Cost itemIncluded in rent?Action
    UtilitiesOften notAsk for average monthly bills
    Service or building chargeSometimesAsk for the amount
    Local taxesVaries by countryConfirm who pays
    Internet and phoneUsually notAdd an estimate
    Transport to workNeverEstimate monthly cost
    formulaTotal monthly housing cost = Rent + Utilities + Service charge
    🧪
    Practical Example: A flat has rent of $2,800, utilities of $250 and a service charge of $150. Total = 2,800 + 250 + 150 = $3,200 per month.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Comparing rents only
    • Not asking what is included
    • Forgetting the cost of travel to work
    Hands-on PracticeRent is $2,200, utilities $180 and service charge $120. What is the total monthly cost?
    2,200 + 180 + 120 = $2,500.

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Running a housing search step by step

    Follow seven steps. Agree the budget and needs with the employee. Brief the home finding agent in writing. Review the shortlist for price, area and fit. Arrange viewings, often in one trip of two or three days. Help compare and negotiate. Review the lease before signing. Support the move-in with an inventory and photos.

    Plan the number of viewings from past results. If only some viewings produce a good option, you need enough viewings to find the target number of strong choices.

    StepOwnerOutput
    BriefCoordinatorWritten needs and budget
    ShortlistAgentHomes within budget
    ViewingsEmployee and agentNotes and photos
    NegotiationAgentAgreed terms
    Lease reviewCoordinator and adviserChecked contract
    Move-inEmployeeInventory report
    formulaViewings needed = Target good options ÷ Share of viewings that give a good option
    🧪
    Practical Example: The target is 3 good options and 30 percent of viewings give one. Viewings = 3 ÷ 0.30 = 10.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • No written brief
    • Booking too few viewing days
    • Signing before the lease is reviewed
    Hands-on PracticeThe target is 4 good options and 40 percent of viewings give one. How many viewings?
    4 ÷ 0.40 = 10 viewings.

    📈 Intermediate Concepts: Housing KPIs and scenarios

    Track days to lease, share of leases within the allowance, number of viewings per lease, employee satisfaction and complaints after move-in. Compare agents and cities to see where the process is slow or costly.

    Scenario: leases take too long. Check whether briefs are late, whether the budget is too low for the city and whether the agent shows too few homes.

    KPIFormulaUse
    Days to leaseSigning date − Search start dateSpeed
    Within-allowance rateLeases within allowance ÷ LeasesBudget control
    Viewings per leaseViewings ÷ LeasesAgent quality
    SatisfactionAverage scoreExperience
    Move-in issuesIssues ÷ Move-insQuality
    formulaDays to lease = Lease signing date − Search start date
    🧪
    Practical Example: The search starts on 3 March and the lease is signed on 24 March. Days to lease = 24 − 3 = 21 days.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Judging only on speed
    • Not recording the allowance at the start
    • Ignoring complaints after move-in
    Hands-on PracticeThe search starts on 5 May and the lease is signed on 30 May. How many days?
    30 − 5 = 25 days.

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: lease risk and upfront cash

    Experts read the lease for the term, break clause, notice period, rent increase rule, repair duties, deposit return and who pays agency fees. They also plan upfront cash because the first payment can be many times the monthly rent.

    They check local law, avoid scams such as paying a deposit before seeing a home, and advise the employee in clear language. They also check that the home is safe and legally rentable.

    RiskControl
    Scam or false listingUse trusted agents and view before paying
    Unfair break clauseAdviser reviews the lease
    Deposit not returnedInventory and photos at move-in
    Cash shortfallAdvance payment or loan plan
    Unsafe homeSafety checklist at viewing
    formulaUpfront cash = Deposit + First month rent + Agency fee
    🧪
    Practical Example: Rent is $2,800. The deposit is 2 months, the first month is paid in advance and the agency fee is 1 month. Upfront cash = 5,600 + 2,800 + 2,800 = $11,200.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Not planning upfront cash
    • Paying a deposit before a viewing
    • Skipping the inventory report
    Hands-on PracticeRent is $2,000. The deposit is 1 month, the first month is paid in advance and the agency fee is half a month. What is the upfront cash?
    2,000 + 2,000 + 1,000 = $5,000.

    🎒 Fundamentals: What school search assistance is and why it matters

    School search assistance helps relocating families find, compare and secure school places for their children. The coordinator explains the local school system, matches schools to the family's needs and supports applications and admission steps.

    It matters because the children's schooling often decides whether a family accepts a move and stays. Places can be limited, deadlines can be early and costs can be high.

    TermMeaning
    Education allowanceEmployer payment towards school fees
    CurriculumThe programme of study, such as national, IB or British
    International schoolSchool teaching in English or another language for expatriates
    WaitlistList of applicants waiting for a place
    Offer rateShare of applications that give an offer
    TuitionYearly teaching fee
    formulaTuition share = Tuition ÷ Education allowance × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: Tuition is $24,000 and the allowance is $30,000. Share = 24,000 ÷ 30,000 × 100 = 80 percent. The rest of the allowance covers other costs.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Starting too late
    • Ignoring the child's curriculum and language
    • Looking at tuition only
    Hands-on PracticeTuition is $18,000 and the allowance is $24,000. What is the share?
    18,000 ÷ 24,000 × 100 = 75 percent.

    📚 Core Concepts: School types, curricula and total cost

    Families can choose public, private, international or religious schools. Think about the child's age, language, previous curriculum and plans for later study. A change of curriculum can be hard for older children.

    Compare total yearly cost: tuition plus registration, transport, uniform and other fees. Many schools also charge a one-off enrolment or capital fee.

    School typeFeaturesWatch-out
    PublicLow or no fees, local languageLanguage gap, zoning rules
    Private nationalLocal curriculum, moderate feesEntry tests
    InternationalFamiliar curriculum, EnglishHigh fees, long waitlists
    ReligiousFaith-based ethosAdmission rules
    Home or onlineFlexibleLocal law may limit it
    formulaTotal school cost = Tuition + Registration + Transport + Uniform
    🧪
    Practical Example: Tuition is $22,000, registration $1,500, transport $1,200 and uniform $800. Total = 22,000 + 1,500 + 1,200 + 800 = $25,500.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Comparing tuition only
    • Forgetting enrolment fees
    • Not checking the curriculum match
    Hands-on PracticeTuition is $15,000, registration $1,000, transport $900 and uniform $600. What is the total?
    15,000 + 1,000 + 900 + 600 = $17,500.

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Running a school search step by step

    Follow seven steps. Collect each child's age, grade, records, language and needs. Brief the family on the local system and the deadlines. Shortlist schools by fit, distance and cost. Book visits or online tours. Submit applications to more than one school. Track offers, tests and waitlists. Confirm the place, pay fees on time and plan the first day.

    Apply to enough schools. If only some applications give an offer, you must apply to more schools than the number of places needed.

    StepOwnerOutput
    Child profileCoordinator and parentsRecords and needs
    ShortlistCoordinatorSchools by fit and cost
    VisitsParentsNotes and ranking
    ApplicationsCoordinator and parentsSubmitted forms
    Offer trackingCoordinatorStatus list
    EnrolmentParentsConfirmed place
    formulaApplications needed = Target offers ÷ Offer rate
    🧪
    Practical Example: The family needs 2 offers and the offer rate is 40 percent. Applications = 2 ÷ 0.40 = 5.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Applying to only one school
    • Missing document deadlines
    • Not translating records
    Hands-on PracticeThe family needs 3 offers and the offer rate is 50 percent. How many applications?
    3 ÷ 0.50 = 6 applications.

    📈 Intermediate Concepts: School search KPIs and scenarios

    Track placement rate, days to confirmed place, share of placements within the education allowance, family satisfaction and number of changes of school in the first year. Compare cities because waitlists differ.

    Scenario: a city has long waitlists. Apply earlier, add a second-choice school and ask the employer to consider a later start date.

    KPIFormulaUse
    Placement rateChildren placed ÷ Children needing a placeSuccess
    Days to placeConfirmation date − Start dateSpeed
    Within-allowance ratePlacements within allowance ÷ PlacementsCost control
    SatisfactionAverage scoreExperience

    formulaPlacement rate = Children placed ÷ Children needing a place × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: In one quarter 18 of 20 children are placed. Placement rate = 18 ÷ 20 × 100 = 90 percent.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Counting only offers, not enrolments
    • No second-choice school
    • Ignoring families who left
    Hands-on Practice38 of 40 children are placed. What is the placement rate?
    38 ÷ 40 × 100 = 95 percent.

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: special needs, waitlists and multiple children

    Experts plan for children with special learning needs by contacting schools early, as support rules and places vary widely. They handle waitlists, mid-year starts and families with several children. Many schools give a sibling discount.

    They also protect privacy: children's records and needs are shared only with consent and only with schools involved.

    CaseAction
    Special learning needsAsk about support early and get written confirmation
    WaitlistJoin several lists and keep a back-up school
    Mid-year startAsk about entry points and catch-up help
    Several childrenAsk about sibling discounts and same-site places
    RecordsTranslate and share only with consent
    formulaTotal for two children = First child fee + Second child fee × (1 − Sibling discount)
    🧪
    Practical Example: Each child costs $20,000 and the sibling discount is 10 percent. Total = 20,000 + 20,000 × 0.90 = 20,000 + 18,000 = $38,000.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Asking about special needs late
    • Sharing records without consent
    • Assuming the discount applies to all fees
    Hands-on PracticeEach child costs $15,000 and the sibling discount is 20 percent. What is the total for two children?
    15,000 + 15,000 × 0.80 = 15,000 + 12,000 = $27,000.

    ✈️ Fundamentals: What international transit logistics is and why it matters

    International transit logistics covers the journey of the employee and family and of their personal baggage: tickets, routes, connections, documents, airport help and arrival transfers. The coordinator plans the trip so it is safe, reasonable in cost and easy for the family.

    It matters because travel problems at the start of a move shape the family's view of the whole assignment. A missed document or connection can cost money and cause real distress.

    TermMeaning
    ItineraryFull plan of the journey with times
    Connection timeTime between two flights
    Excess baggageBaggage above the free allowance
    TransferGround transport between airport and home
    Travel policyCompany rules on class, cost and booking
    Entry documentsPassport, visa and any other papers needed to enter
    formulaTotal travel cost = Fare × Persons + Excess baggage
    🧪
    Practical Example: Four people travel at $1,200 each and excess baggage is $300. Total = 4 × 1,200 + 300 = $5,100.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Booking before the visa is approved
    • Ignoring baggage limits
    • Choosing very short connections
    Hands-on PracticeThree people travel at $900 each and excess baggage is $150. What is the total?
    3 × 900 + 150 = $2,850.

    📦 Core Concepts: Air baggage and freight weight

    Airlines and carriers charge by chargeable weight. This is the higher of the actual weight and the volumetric weight. Volumetric weight turns the size of a box into a weight, because bulky light boxes take up space. A common factor is 6,000 for centimetres.

    Check the carrier's own factor and limits. Pack items so the family knows what is allowed and what is not, such as liquids, batteries and restricted goods.

    Weight typeMeaningUse
    Actual weightWhat the box weighs on a scaleCompare with volumetric
    Volumetric weightLength × Width × Height ÷ 6,000Bulky boxes
    Chargeable weightThe higher of the twoPrice basis
    Free allowanceWeight carried at no extra chargePlan baggage
    Restricted itemsGoods not allowed or limitedBriefing list
    formulaVolumetric weight (kg) = Length × Width × Height (cm) ÷ 6,000
    🧪
    Practical Example: A box is 60 cm × 50 cm × 40 cm and weighs 15 kg. Volumetric = 120,000 ÷ 6,000 = 20 kg. Chargeable weight is the higher figure, 20 kg.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Using actual weight only
    • Not checking the carrier's factor
    • Packing restricted items
    Hands-on PracticeA box is 80 × 60 × 50 cm and weighs 35 kg. What is the chargeable weight?
    Volumetric = 240,000 ÷ 6,000 = 40 kg. Chargeable weight = 40 kg.

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Planning a journey step by step

    Follow seven steps. Check entry documents and the travel policy. Choose routes that suit children, pets and special needs. Book flights, transfers and temporary housing. Send the family an itinerary with all contacts. Confirm 72 hours before travel. Monitor the journey and help if something goes wrong. Confirm arrival and settle the family.

    Add up the time of every leg to give the family a realistic door-to-door time and to check that rest breaks are possible.

    StepOwnerOutput
    Document checkCoordinatorPassport and visa confirmed
    Route choiceCoordinator and travellerItinerary
    BookingTravel agentTickets and transfers
    BriefingCoordinatorItinerary and contacts
    MonitoringCoordinatorStatus updates
    Arrival checkDestination agentConfirmed arrival
    formulaDoor-to-door time = Sum of all legs and waiting times
    🧪
    Practical Example: Home transfer 2 hours, flight 9 hours, layover 1.5 hours, second flight 3 hours and arrival transfer 1 hour. Total = 2 + 9 + 1.5 + 3 + 1 = 16.5 hours.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Not sending the family a full itinerary
    • No plan for delays
    • Ignoring the needs of young children
    Hands-on PracticeTransfer 1.5 hours, flight 7 hours, layover 2 hours, second flight 2.5 hours and arrival transfer 1 hour. What is the total?
    1.5 + 7 + 2 + 2.5 + 1 = 14 hours.

    📈 Intermediate Concepts: Transit KPIs and scenarios

    Track on-time arrival rate, cost per traveller against policy, number of rebookings, share of travellers who received an itinerary 7 days before and traveller satisfaction. Compare routes and suppliers.

    Scenario: a flight is cancelled. The coordinator rebooks the family, informs the receiving contact, extends temporary housing if needed and records the cost for any claim.

    KPIFormulaUse
    On-time arrival rateOn-time arrivals ÷ JourneysReliability
    Cost against policyActual ÷ Policy limitCost control
    Rebooking rateRebookings ÷ JourneysDisruption
    Itinerary on timeItineraries sent on time ÷ JourneysService
    SatisfactionAverage scoreExperience
    formulaOn-time arrival rate = On-time arrivals ÷ Journeys × 100
    🧪
    Practical Example: In one month 46 of 50 journeys arrive as planned. Rate = 46 ÷ 50 × 100 = 92 percent.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Counting arrivals but not delays
    • No record of rebooking cost
    • Not asking travellers for feedback
    Hands-on Practice57 of 60 journeys arrive as planned. What is the rate?
    57 ÷ 60 × 100 = 95 percent.

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: disruption, insurance and duty of care

    Experts plan for disruption. They choose routes with back-up options, buy travel insurance that covers delay and baggage loss, and keep a 24-hour contact for travellers. They also know the travel advice for the route and destination.

    They estimate the expected cost of disruption to set a budget reserve and to decide whether insurance is worth buying.

    RiskControl
    Cancelled flightRebooking rules and a 24-hour contact
    Lost baggageInsurance and an essentials allowance
    Missed connectionLonger connection time
    Document problem at the borderDocument check before travel
    Health emergencyMedical cover and emergency contacts
    formulaExpected disruption cost = Journeys × Probability × Cost per event
    🧪
    Practical Example: There are 200 journeys, a 4 percent chance of disruption and a cost of $500 per event. Expected cost = 200 × 0.04 × 500 = $4,000.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • No 24-hour contact
    • Choosing the cheapest route with no back-up
    • Buying no cover for baggage loss
    Hands-on PracticeThere are 150 journeys, a 6 percent chance and $400 per event. What is the expected cost?
    150 × 0.06 × 400 = $3,600.

    🚢 Fundamentals: What household goods shipment tracking is and why it matters

    Household goods shipment tracking means following a family's belongings from packing in the origin country to unpacking at the new home. The coordinator works with the mover, checks the paperwork and keeps the family informed at every milestone.

    It matters because the family's belongings are valuable and personal. A late, damaged or lost shipment costs money, causes stress and may delay the family's settling in.

    TermMeaning

    Cubic metre (cbm)Unit of shipment volume

    🗓️ Core Concepts: Shipping modes, milestones and transit time

    Household goods go by sea, air or road and rail. Sea is cheapest but slowest, air is fast but costly, and road suits nearby countries. Each shipment passes milestones: survey, packing, pick-up, export clearance, departure, arrival, import clearance, delivery and unpacking.

    Measure transit time from departure to arrival. Plan for delays at ports and in customs, and tell the family a range, not a single date.

    ModeSpeedCostUse
    Sea FCLSlowLow per cbm for large loadsFull homes
    Sea LCLSlowModerateSmall loads
    Air freightFastHighUrgent items
    RoadModerateModerateNearby countries
    RailModerateModerateSome Europe–Asia routes
    formulaTransit days = Arrival date − Departure date
    🧪
    Practical Example: A ship leaves on 4 January and arrives on 14 February. January has 27 days left after the 4th, plus 14 days in February. Transit days = 27 + 14 = 41.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes
    • Giving the family one fixed date
    • Not allowing for customs delay
    • Using air freight for everything
    Hands-on PracticeA ship leaves on 10 March and arrives on 5 May. How many days?
    March: 31 − 10 = 21. April: 30. May: 5. Total = 56 days.

    🛠️ Intermediate Concepts: Tracking a shipment step by step

    Follow eight steps. Get at least three quotes and compare them like for like. Hold a pre-move survey and agree the inventory. Supervise packing and check the inventory with the family. Confirm export documents and the booking. Track the shipment weekly and report to the family. Prepare import clearance papers, such as passport copies, inventory and visa or residence proof. Arrange delivery, unpacking and inspection. Close the file with a damage report.

    Compare quotes by cost per cubic metre as well as by total, but check what is included, such as packing, insurance and delivery.

    🛡️ Advanced Concepts: Expert: insurance, claims and customs

    Experts arrange insurance that covers the replacement value of the goods, not just the mover's limited liability. They keep photos and a signed inventory, and tell the family to note damage on the delivery receipt before signing. Claims have strict time limits.

    They also check customs rules: some countries limit imports of goods such as food, plants, medicines or electronics, and duty relief often needs proof of residence or a minimum period abroad. Always confirm the current rules with the customs broker.

    Security & Compliance

    Data protection, access, fraud and legal risk for this role. Each skill has a security question in the Assessment.

    🔐 Security & Compliance: the big picture

    Relocation coordinators handle passports, children's school records, medical needs, home addresses and travel plans. They also direct payments to landlords, schools and movers. This data is sensitive, especially for children, and payments are a target for fraud.

    Security here means sharing the minimum data, using secure channels, checking suppliers and payments, protecting children's information with consent, and keeping the family's travel details private. Confirm local rules with your privacy and legal teams.

    Security riskWhy it mattersControl
    Housing scam or fake listingDeposit lostView before paying and use trusted agents
    Children's data leakSerious harm to the childConsent, minimum sharing and secure transfer
    Supplier payment fraudMoney lostVerify bank details and use two approvals
    Travel details exposedPersonal safety riskShare itineraries only with those who need them
    Unvetted supplierPoor service and data riskSupplier checks and contracts
    Unclaimed damageCost and distressInventory, photos and claims log

    🛡️ Security for: Housing search oversight

    • Never ask the employee to pay a deposit before a viewing through a trusted agent.
    • Check the landlord's identity and right to rent the property.
    • Keep copies of the lease and inventory securely.

    🛡️ Security for: School search assistance

    • Get parental consent before sharing records and send only what each school needs.
    • Keep special needs information strictly limited.
    • Use official school channels and check payment details.

    🛡️ Security for: International transit logistics

    • Do not post itineraries or boarding passes on social media.
    • Send travel documents through secure channels.
    • Check that transfer drivers are from approved suppliers.

    🛡️ Security for: Shipment of household goods tracking

    • Check the mover's licence and insurance before booking.
    • Keep the inventory and customs papers secure, as they list the family's belongings.
    • Beware of requests to pay extra fees to release a shipment.

    🧪 Measuring security

    formulaVendor access removal on time = Accesses removed within 5 days ÷ Leavers × 100
    🧪
    Worked example: 20 vendor staff leave a project and access is removed within 5 days for 18. Rate = 18 ÷ 20 × 100 = 90 percent. The two late cases are reviewed and removal is added to the exit checklist.

    Security checklist

    • Children's data is shared only with consent.
    • Payments to suppliers need verification and two approvals.
    • No deposits are paid before viewing.
    • Itineraries are shared only with those who need them.
    • Suppliers are vetted and under contract.
    • Inventories and photos are kept for claims.

    💬 Interview Questions: Housing search oversight

    IQ1.Why compare total monthly cost, not rent?
    Utilities, service charges and travel can make a cheap rent cost more. Total cost gives a fair comparison.
    IQ2.What do you check in a lease?
    Term, break clause, notice, rent increases, repair duties, deposit return and who pays fees. A local adviser should review it.
    IQ3.An employee wants to pay a deposit for a home they have not seen. What do you say?
    Do not pay. This is a common scam sign. View the home through a trusted agent first.
    IQ4.Rent is $2,400 and the allowance is $3,000. What is the ratio?
    2,400 ÷ 3,000 = 80 percent.
    IQ5.Rent $2,000, deposit 1 month, first month in advance and agency fee half a month. Upfront cash?
    2,000 + 2,000 + 1,000 = $5,000.

    💬 Interview Questions: School search assistance

    IQ1.Why start the school search early?
    Places and deadlines are limited, and international schools often have waitlists. Early action gives more choice.
    IQ2.How do you compare schools?
    Compare curriculum fit, language, distance and total yearly cost, not just tuition.
    IQ3.A child has special learning needs. What do you do?
    Contact schools early, ask for written confirmation of support and share records only with parental consent.
    IQ4.A family needs 3 offers and the offer rate is 50 percent. How many applications?
    3 ÷ 0.50 = 6.
    IQ5.Two children cost $15,000 each with a 20 percent sibling discount. Total?
    15,000 + 12,000 = $27,000.

    💬 Interview Questions: International transit logistics

    IQ1.What is chargeable weight?
    The higher of the actual weight and the volumetric weight. Carriers charge on it.
    IQ2.A flight is cancelled while the family is travelling. What do you do?
    Rebook, tell the receiving contact, extend temporary housing if needed, support the family and log costs.
    IQ3.Why send the itinerary 7 days early?
    The family can check documents and plan. It also leaves time to fix errors.
    IQ4.A box is 80 × 60 × 50 cm and weighs 35 kg. Chargeable weight?
    240,000 ÷ 6,000 = 40 kg, so 40 kg.
    IQ5.57 of 60 journeys arrive on time. Rate?
    57 ÷ 60 = 95 percent.

    IQ1.Why get three quotes?
    To compare cost per cbm and inclusions. A low total may leave out packing or insurance.
    IQ2.What is the risk of relying on the mover's liability?
    It is usually limited by weight and pays little. Insurance at replacement value protects the family better.
    IQ3.A container is held in customs. What do you do?
    Check documents, contact the broker, tell the family the expected delay and arrange temporary furniture if needed.
    IQ4.A ship leaves on 10 March and arrives on 5 May. How many days?

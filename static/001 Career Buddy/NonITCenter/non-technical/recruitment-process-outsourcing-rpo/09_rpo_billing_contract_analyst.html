    Module 01 · Foundations

    The RPO Billing & Contract Analyst

    The person who turns the RPO contract into correct numbers: calculating fees, matching hires to charges, tracking service-level credits and bonuses, and sending invoices clients can approve first time.

    🔎 What is an RPO Billing & Contract Analyst?

    In an RPO engagement, the Billing & Contract Analyst reads the commercial terms, collects hiring and cost data, and makes sure the client is charged exactly what the contract says, no more and no less. They also flag where the contract and day-to-day reality have drifted apart.

    🧮
    Fee calculator
    Works out the recurring management fee from the contract model.
    🔗
    Reconciler
    Matches every hire to the right placement charge.
    ⚖️
    SLA scorekeeper
    Tracks penalties and bonuses against agreed targets.
    🧾
    Invoice owner
    Produces clear, accurate invoices that get paid on time.

    Module 02 · Base fee

    Management-Fee Calculation

    Work out the recurring fee the client pays for the RPO service itself, separate from the fees for individual hires.

    🧱 Common fee models

    The management fee pays for the RPO team, tools and oversight. The contract decides how it is calculated, so always start from the signed terms.

    ModelHow it worksBest when
    Fixed monthly feeSame amount each month, regardless of hiring volumeVolume is steady and predictable
    Cost-plusActual team and overhead cost plus an agreed marginClient wants cost transparency
    Tiered by volumeFee changes as hires or requisitions cross set bandsVolume varies through the year
    Per requisitionA set fee for each requisition opened or workedHiring is project-based

    🧮 Worked example

    Cost-plus fee = (team cost + overhead + tools) × (1 + margin)

    🧮
    Example: team cost $90,000 + overhead $10,000 + tools $5,000 = $105,000. With a 12% margin, the management fee is $105,000 × 1.12 = $117,600 for the month.
    1. Find the model and the fee period in the contract.
    2. Gather inputs: headcount, rates, volumes or requisition counts for the period.
    3. Apply the formula and any caps, floors or annual increases.
    4. Pro-rate partial months, such as team members who start or leave mid-month.
    5. Record the working so the number can be re-checked later.

    ✅ Keep it accurate

    • Calculation uses the current signed version of the contract
    • Annual indexation or rate changes applied from the right date
    • Caps, floors and volume bands checked for the period
    • Partial months pro-rated consistently
    • Working saved with the invoice for audit
    ⚠️
    Do not guess at unclear terms. If the contract is ambiguous, raise it with account leadership and legal, and agree the reading in writing with the client.
    Module 03 · Variable fees

    Placement-Fee Reconciliation

    Match every hire in the applicant tracking system to a placement charge, so each fee is billed once, at the right amount and at the right time.

    🔗 What is reconciliation?

    A placement fee is charged when a hire meets the contract's conditions, often a percentage of first-year base salary or a fixed amount per hire. Reconciliation compares three lists: hires recorded by recruiters, hires confirmed by the client, and fees on the invoice.

    🧮
    Example: an 8% placement fee on a $90,000 base salary is $90,000 × 0.08 = $7,200.

    ⚙️ Steps to reconcile

    StepWhat to do
    Pull hiresExport hires for the period from the tracking system, with start dates and salaries
    Confirm with the clientCheck that each hire started and the salary matches the offer
    Apply the rulesCheck billable status, fee rate, exclusions and any guarantee period
    Match to chargesCompare against previous invoices to avoid double-billing or missed hires
    Resolve gapsList each difference with a reason and an owner

    🔎 Common mismatches

    • Missed hires: started in the period but never invoiced
    • Duplicates: same person billed twice, for example after a requisition was reopened
    • Wrong salary: offer changed after the data was entered
    • Early leavers: a hire who leaves inside the guarantee period may need a credit or replacement, depending on the contract
    • Non-billable hires: internal transfers or sources the contract excludes
    📏
    Reconcile every period. Small errors found monthly are quick to fix; errors found at year end become disputes.
    Module 04 · Performance

    Performance Penalty / Bonus Tracking

    Measure service-level results against the contract, then calculate any credit owed to the client or bonus earned by the provider.

    ⚖️ How performance terms work

    Many contracts put part of the fee at risk. If service-level targets are missed, the client receives a credit; if targets are beaten, the provider may earn a bonus.

    ElementWhat it means
    KPI or SLAA measurable target, such as time to fill or offer acceptance rate
    WeightingShare of the at-risk amount linked to each target
    ThresholdThe level at which a penalty or bonus starts
    CapThe maximum penalty or bonus for the period
    ExclusionsAgreed reasons that stop the clock, such as late client feedback

    🧮 Worked example

    🧮
    Example: monthly management fee is $100,000, with 10% at risk, so $10,000. The time-to-fill SLA carries 40% of that amount. If it is missed, the credit is $10,000 × 0.40 = $4,000.
    1. Collect results from the reporting system for each KPI.
    2. Remove exclusions that the contract allows, with evidence.
    3. Compare results with threshold and target.
    4. Apply weighting and caps to calculate the credit or bonus.
    5. Agree the result with the delivery lead before it reaches the invoice.

    🛡️ Tracking tips

    • Use the same definitions as the contract, for example when the clock starts and stops
    • Keep a log of exclusions with dates and approvals
    • Share results with the delivery team before invoicing, so no one is surprised
    • Show penalties and bonuses as separate lines, not hidden inside other charges
    ⚠️
    Never adjust results to reach a target. Data should come from the agreed system and be reported as it is, including misses.
    Module 05 · Delivery

    Commercial Invoicing

    Bring fees, credits and pass-through costs into one clear invoice that matches the contract and passes the client's approval first time.

    🧾 What goes on the invoice

    LineSource
    Management feeCalculated from the contract model (Tab 2)
    Placement feesReconciled hires for the period (Tab 3)
    Pass-through costsJob boards, assessments, background checks and similar, at agreed mark-up if any
    Penalties and bonusesCredits and earned bonuses from SLA tracking (Tab 4)
    TaxesSales tax or VAT, as the law and contract require

    ✅ Invoice checklist

    • Correct client legal entity, address and tax details
    • Purchase order number and cost-centre codes where required
    • Billing period and invoice date match the contract schedule
    • Each line has a description, quantity, rate and amount
    • Supporting schedule attached, such as hire list and SLA report
    • Payment terms, due date and bank details clearly shown

    💳 Getting paid

    • Send invoices on the agreed date, using the client's required portal or format
    • Track due dates and follow up politely before and after they pass
    • Record disputes with the invoice number, the issue and the owner
    • Issue a credit note for agreed corrections rather than editing a sent invoice
    • Review ageing reports monthly with your finance team
    💡
    Make it easy to approve. A clear summary page and a supporting schedule let the client's approver check the invoice in minutes instead of raising questions.

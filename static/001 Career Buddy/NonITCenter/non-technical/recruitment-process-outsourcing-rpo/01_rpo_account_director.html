    Module 01 · Foundations

    The RPO Account Director Role

    The person accountable for the whole client relationship: trusted by the client's leadership, responsible for delivery against the contract, and clear about what the account earns.

    🧭 What is an RPO Account Director?

    Recruitment Process Outsourcing (RPO) is an arrangement where a company hands all or part of its hiring process to a specialist provider, which runs it as an extension of the client's own team. The Account Director is the senior leader on the provider's side who owns that client relationship from signature to renewal.

    The role sits between three worlds that rarely speak the same language: the client's executives (who think in business outcomes), the delivery team (who think in requisitions and pipelines), and the provider's own finance and leadership (who think in margin and growth). The Account Director translates between them.

    🤝
    Relationship owner
    Single point of accountability for the client at executive level.
    📏
    Delivery guardian
    Makes sure service levels and KPIs in the contract are actually met.
    💹
    Commercial steward
    Keeps the contract profitable without hurting the client's experience.
    🔁
    Renewal driver
    Builds the evidence, quarter by quarter, that earns the next term.

    🔄 The RPO Model at a Glance

    The client keeps the strategy and the final hiring decision. The RPO provider runs the process: sourcing, screening, coordinating interviews, managing offers, and reporting on results.

    Core skill 1
    Module 02 · Relationships

    Client C-suite Stakeholder Management

    Executives do not want to hear about requisitions. They want to hear what hiring means for revenue, cost, risk and speed. This module is about speaking their language and earning a seat at their table.

    🎯 What Each Executive Cares About

    The same hiring data lands differently depending on who is reading it. Frame it for the person in front of you.

    ExecutiveTypical concernsHow to frame hiring
    CEOGrowth targets, competitive position, key leadership hires"These roles unlock the plan, and here is the risk if they stay open."
    CHRO / Head of TalentEmployer brand, quality of hire, candidate and manager experience, diversity goals"Here is how the process feels to candidates and managers, and what we are changing."
    CFOCost per hire, budget accuracy, contract value, predictability"Here is spend against plan, and what drives variance."
    COO / business unit headsVacancies hurting operations, ramp-up time, coverage"Here is when each critical team reaches full strength."
    ProcurementContract compliance, pricing, vendor risk"Here is delivery against every contractual commitment."

    🌱 Building Trust Over Time

    Executive trust is built through small, repeated behaviors rather than one great presentation.

    ⏰
    Be predictable
    Same cadence, same format, same people. Executives relax when they know what is coming.
    🔍
    Know their business
    Read their results, their earnings calls and their press. Reference them naturally.
    🗣️
    Be candid
    Share the real picture, including problems, before someone else does.
    💬
    Bring ideas
    Suggest improvements nobody asked for, grounded in their data.

    ⚠️ Delivering Bad News

    Sooner or later a critical hire falls through or a service level is missed. How you handle that moment shapes the relationship more than any good quarter.

    ❌ What erodes trust

    • Waiting for the client to notice
    • Explaining before stating the problem
    • Blaming the market or the hiring manager
    • Arriving with a problem but no plan

    ✅ What builds it

    • Raising it early, directly and by phone first
    • Stating impact on the business in one sentence
    • Owning your part of the cause
    • Presenting a recovery plan with dates
    🧯
    Rule of thumb: an executive should never learn about a problem from someone other than you. When in doubt, call.

    ✉️ Writing for Executives

    Executive updates are short, lead with the conclusion, and make any ask explicit. A reliable shape:

    templateSubject: [Account] hiring update – on track / at risk / action needed

Headline:  One sentence on where we stand and why it matters.
Evidence:  Two or three numbers that support the headline.
Risk:      The biggest threat to the plan, and what we are doing about it.
Ask:       The one decision or action we need from you, and by when.

    If the reader stops after the first line, they should still know the state of play.

    Module 03 · Delivery

    SLA/KPI Delivery Governance

    Governance is the habit of measuring delivery against the contract, on a fixed rhythm, with clear owners for every gap. Done well, it makes the client's confidence in you independent of anyone's opinion.

    ⚖️ SLA, KPI and the Difference

    The two terms are often used loosely. In an RPO contract they usually mean different things.

    TermMeaningTypical consequence
    SLA Service Level AgreementA contractually agreed level of service, with a defined measurement methodCredits, penalties or formal remediation if missed
    KPI Key Performance IndicatorA measure of performance that both sides track, often without a financial penaltyDiscussion, improvement plan, input to the QBR
    OLA Operational Level AgreementCommitments the client makes to enable delivery, such as feedback turnaround timeUsed to show when delays are not on the provider
    ⚠️
    Read the definitions closely. "Time to fill" can start at requisition approval, requisition open, or first sourcing. Each gives a different number. Agree the clock start and stop in writing, once, early.

    📐 Core RPO Measures

    MeasureWhat it tells you
    Time to fillHow long a role takes from opening to an accepted offer
    Time to submit / shortlistHow quickly the first qualified candidates reach the hiring manager
    Offer acceptance rateShare of offers that candidates accept
    Quality of hireHow well new hires perform and stay, usually measured at set points after joining
    Cost per hireTotal hiring cost divided by hires made
    Hiring manager satisfactionHow managers rate the process and the candidates
    Candidate experienceHow candidates rate communication and fairness
    Diversity of slate / hiresProgress against the client's stated representation goals

    Which of these are contractual SLAs and which are tracked KPIs is specific to each contract.

    Module 04 · Commercials

    Contract Profitability Tracking

    A contract that delights the client but loses money cannot be sustained. This module covers how RPO contracts are priced, how to measure margin, and how to protect it without cutting corners on service.

    💰 How RPO Contracts Earn Money

    Pricing models differ, and the model decides which risks sit with you.

    ModelHow the provider is paidMain risk for the provider
    Per-hire feeA fee for each completed hire, often tiered by role levelHiring volume falls, but the team cost stays
    Management feeA fixed monthly fee for running the service, often covering a dedicated teamVolume rises beyond what the team was sized for
    HybridA base management fee plus a per-hire componentBalanced, but needs careful volume bands
    Pass-through costsThird-party costs such as job boards or assessments, billed at or near costUnbilled or under-recovered spend
    📌
    Know your contract's model before anything else. The same drop in hiring volume is a revenue problem under per-hire pricing and a cost-efficiency opportunity under a fixed management fee.

    🧮 The Margin Formula

    Margin shows how much of each unit of revenue remains after the cost of serving the client.

    formulaContract margin   = Revenue − Cost to serve
Margin %          = (Revenue − Cost to serve) ÷ Revenue × 100

# Cost to serve usually includes:
#   delivery team salaries and benefits (recruiters, sourcers, coordinators)
#   technology and sourcing tools allocated to the account
#   job boards, assessments, and other third-party spend not recovered
#   management time and overhead allocated to the account
    Figure 4.1 · Numbers are for illustration. Your cost structure will differ.

    🕳️ Where Margin Leaks

    Most lost margin does not disappear in one big event. It leaks out slowly in places nobody is watching.

    📈
    Scope creep
    Extra roles, locations or services delivered without a change request or a price.
    👥
    Team sized for the peak
    Staff hired for a busy period remain after volume falls.
    🧾
    Unrecovered pass-through
    Job board or assessment spend that was never billed back.
    🔁
    Rework and fallout
    Candidates who drop out late force the team to restart the search.
    ⚖️
    SLA credits
    Credits owed for missed service levels reduce what you invoice.
    ⌛
    Slow invoicing
    Delays in billing or disputes over supporting data stretch cash collection.

    📒 Tracking Profitability Monthly

    Check margin on a fixed monthly rhythm, shortly after the books for the month close. A simple tracker is enough to start, with the key question in the last column.

    LinePlanActualVarianceQuestion to ask
    Revenue———Is volume or mix different from plan?
    Delivery team cost———Is capacity matched to current demand?
    Tools and sourcing———Is any spend unbilled or avoidable?
    SLA credits———Which misses drove them, and are they fixed?
    Margin %———Is the trend improving or sliding?
    🔭
    Look at the trend over three to six months, not only the latest number. One weak month is noise. Three in a row is a signal.

    🎛️ Levers You Can Pull

    • Change control: when requests go beyond the agreed scope, document them and price them, or decline them.
    • Capacity planning: forecast demand with the client and flex the team up or down against it.
    • Process efficiency: automate scheduling and screening admin so the same team handles more.
    • Pass-through discipline: approve third-party spend only when the billing route is clear.
    • Commercial reviews: use the renewal or a volume change to revisit pricing bands honestly.
    ⚠️
    Protect margin without cutting the service the client is paying for. Reducing the team to hit a margin target while SLAs slip trades a small gain now for a lost renewal later.

    Module 05 · Reviews

    Quarterly Business Reviews (QBRs)

    The QBR is where the other three skills come together in front of the client's leadership. It should leave everyone clear on what happened, why it happened, and what happens next.

    🎯 What a QBR Is For

    A QBR is not a longer monthly performance meeting. It has a different job:

    🗓️ Monthly review

    Looks backwards at the scorecard. Fixes operational issues. Audience: client talent leaders.

    🧭 QBR

    Looks at the quarter and the next one. Connects hiring to business outcomes. Decides priorities and investments. Audience: client executives.

    📋 A QBR Agenda That Works

    Ninety minutes is a common length. Hold the time for discussion, not slides.

    PartTimePurpose
    1. Headlines5 minOverall status and the three things that matter most
    2. Results against targets15 minSLAs and KPIs, with trends rather than single points
    3. What we learned15 minCauses behind the numbers, from candidate and manager feedback
    4. Market and talent insight15 minWhat is changing in the talent market that affects the client's plans
    5. Commercial health10 minVolumes, spend against plan, and any scope or pricing matters (shown at the right level for the audience)
    6. Next quarter plan20 minPriorities, improvements and the decisions needed from the client
    7. Actions and owners10 minRead back every action with an owner and a date

    ✅ Preparing: The Checklist

    • Agree the attendee list and agenda with the client sponsor at least two weeks ahead
    • Confirm every figure with finance and delivery before it goes in the deck
    • Pre-brief the key executives one to one, so nothing in the meeting is a surprise
    • Prepare an honest view on each miss, with cause and recovery plan
    • Bring at least two ideas for improvement based on the client's own data
    • List the decisions you need from the client, and make each one specific
    • Agree who from your team presents which section, and rehearse the transitions

    📖 Telling the Story

    Executives remember a clear storyline, not a collection of charts. A reliable sequence:

    Figure 5.1 · Results, insight, decision
    💡
    Open with the headline, including bad news. When the executives see you are not hiding anything in the first five minutes, they engage with the rest far more openly.

    📬 After the Meeting

    The QBR only counts if it changes what happens next.

    • Within 24 hours: send a short summary with decisions and every action, owner and date.
    • Within a week: update the account plan and brief your delivery team on what the client said.
    • At the next monthly review: report progress against each QBR action first.
    • At the next QBR: open by showing what was done about last quarter's commitments.
    🏁
    That closes the loop. Every QBR feeds the stakeholder conversations, delivery priorities and commercial decisions of the next quarter. Take the assessment next to check how much has stuck.

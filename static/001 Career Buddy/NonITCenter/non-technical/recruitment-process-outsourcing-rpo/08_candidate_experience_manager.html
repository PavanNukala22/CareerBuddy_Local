    Module 01 · Foundations

    The Candidate Experience Manager

    The person who makes sure every candidate, hired or not, is treated clearly, quickly and respectfully, and who uses feedback and data to keep improving the process.

    🔎 What is a Candidate Experience Manager?

    In an RPO engagement, the Candidate Experience Manager looks at hiring through the candidate's eyes. They map each step, measure how candidates feel, check that surveys work, and redesign the interview process so it is fair, fast and easy to follow.

    🗺️
    Journey mapper
    Shows every step a candidate takes and where it hurts.
    📈
    Score keeper
    Measures candidate Net Promoter Score and tracks it over time.
    🔍
    Survey auditor
    Checks that feedback surveys are fair, timely and useful.
    🔁
    Loop optimizer
    Shortens and sharpens the interview process.

    🔄 Where Experience Sits in the Hiring Flow

    Candidate experience is not one stage. It runs through every touchpoint, from the first job ad to the first week at work, and also covers the people who are not hired.

    Module 02 · Understanding

    Candidate Journey Mapping

    Lay out every step a candidate goes through, what they do, think and feel at each one, and where the process loses them.

    🗺️ What a journey map shows

    A journey map lists each stage, touchpoint, candidate feeling and owner in one view. It turns a vague sense that "candidates are unhappy" into specific moments you can fix.

    StageCandidate questionCommon problem
    DiscoverIs this a place I want to work?Unclear role, pay or location
    ApplyHow hard is this form?Long forms, no confirmation
    ScreenDid anyone read my application?Silence for days
    InterviewWhat should I expect?Poor scheduling, unprepared interviewers
    OfferIs this decision fair and clear?Slow offers, vague terms
    JoinWill I be ready on day one?No contact before the start date

    ⚙️ Build steps

    1. Pick a persona or role family, such as graduates or engineers, because journeys differ.
    2. List every touchpoint: emails, calls, portal screens, interviews and offer messages.
    3. Gather evidence from candidate interviews, survey comments and data such as days waiting.
    4. Mark the moments that cause drop-off or frustration.
    5. Assign owners and fixes, then review the map every quarter.

    ⚖️ Map the whole journey, including rejection

    🟢 Hired candidates

    Tell you what worked, how smooth onboarding was and what made them accept.

    🟠 Rejected and withdrawn

    Show the largest group, and the one most likely to share a bad story or to apply again if treated well.

    ⚠️
    Do not map from the inside only. A map built from the recruiter's point of view hides what candidates see. Test the real application and talk to real candidates.
    Module 03 · Measurement

    Net Promoter Score (cNPS) Measurement

    Track one simple number that shows whether candidates would recommend your hiring process, and learn why the score moves.

    📈 The formula

    Ask: "How likely are you to recommend our hiring process to a friend?" on a scale of 0 to 10. cNPS = % promoters − % detractors.

    GroupScoreMeaning
    Promoters9 to 10Happy and likely to recommend
    Passives7 to 8Satisfied but not enthusiastic; not counted in the formula
    Detractors0 to 6Unhappy and likely to discourage others
    🧮
    Example: out of 200 responses, 110 are promoters (55%) and 40 are detractors (20%). cNPS = 55 − 20 = +35.

    ⚙️ How to run it

    • Survey at key moments: after applying, after interviews, after an offer or rejection
    • Include everyone: hired, rejected and withdrawn candidates
    • Add one open question: "What is the main reason for your score?"
    • Segment the results by stage, role family, location and hiring manager
    • Close the loop: follow up with detractors where they agree to be contacted

    🔎 Reading the score

    📏
    Watch the trend and the sample size. A score from 12 responses can swing wildly. Report the number of responses with every score and compare like with like.
    • Compare hired vs not hired, since rejected candidates usually score lower
    • Read the comments to find themes behind the number
    • Link cNPS to outcomes such as offer acceptance and re-application rates
    Module 04 · Trust

    Post-Interview Survey Auditing

    Check that your surveys are well timed, well worded, representative and acted on, so the scores you report can be trusted.

    🔍 Why audit your surveys

    A survey can look healthy and still mislead. If only happy candidates answer, or questions push a certain reply, the score is wrong and the wrong problems get fixed.

    🧾 What to check

    AreaWhat to look for
    TimingSent soon after the interview, but not before the candidate hears an outcome that may colour the answer
    WordingNeutral, one idea per question, no leading phrases
    LengthShort enough that people finish it, ideally under three minutes
    Response rateEnough responses, and from all groups, not only the happiest
    CoverageAll stages and all candidate outcomes are surveyed
    PrivacyClear consent, safe storage and no identifiable comments shared widely
    ActionResults reach owners and lead to visible changes
    ⚠️
    Watch for bias. If recruiters pick who receives the survey, or interviewers coach candidates to give high scores, the data is no longer honest.

    ✅ Audit checklist

    • Survey is sent automatically to every candidate at every chosen stage
    • Questions are neutral and reviewed at least once a year
    • Response rate and sample size are shown next to each score
    • Rejected and withdrawn candidates are included
    • Comments are tagged by theme and shared with the right owners
    • Data handling follows local law and the client's policy
    Module 05 · Improvement

    Interview Loop Optimization

    Redesign the sequence of interviews so it is as short as it can be, fair to every candidate, and still gives managers what they need to decide.

    🔁 What the loop is

    The interview loop is the full set of interviews and assessments a candidate completes before a decision. Each added round costs candidate time, delays the offer and risks losing the person to another employer.

    🔴 Long, unclear loop

    Six or more rounds, repeated questions, slow feedback and no explanation of what comes next.

    🟢 Focused loop

    Few rounds, each testing something different, with a clear schedule and quick decisions.

    ⚙️ How to optimize

    StepWhat to do
    MeasureTrack days between stages, number of rounds and drop-off after each one
    Remove overlapGive each interviewer a different skill to assess, so questions are not repeated
    StructureUse consistent questions and scoring rubrics for every candidate
    Schedule fastOffer the full loop on one or two days where possible, with self-scheduling
    Enforce feedbackAgree a deadline, such as 24 hours, for interviewers to submit scores
    Prepare candidatesShare who they will meet, the format and how to prepare
    💡
    Test changes. Trial a shorter loop on one team, compare time to fill, cNPS and quality of hire against the old loop, then roll it out.

    🎯 Good loop principles

    • Every round has a clear purpose that no other round covers
    • Interviewers are trained on structured, fair and respectful interviewing
    • Candidates always know what happens next and when
    • Reasonable adjustments are offered to candidates who need them
    • Every candidate receives a timely outcome, including a kind and clear rejection

    Assessment

    Test yourself on journey mapping, cNPS, survey audits and interview loops. Multiple-choice questions, timed like a real screening test. Difficulty ranges from quick checks to genuinely tricky judgement calls.

    Test what you've learned
    A timed, exam-style mock test. Some questions are quick concept checks; others describe a realistic situation and ask what you would do.
    • Multiple-choice questions with a mix of concept and scenario items
    • Difficulty from Simple to Hard
    • Covers journey mapping, cNPS, survey audits and interview loops

    🔒 Exam Rules & Security

    • The test runs in fullscreen with a countdown timer; it auto-submits when time is up.
    • No tab or window switching and no exiting fullscreen. After 3 warnings the test auto-submits.
    • Screenshots, copy, cut, print and right-click are disabled; refreshing or closing the page asks for confirmation.
    • Navigate freely, mark questions for review, then submit. Results and explanations appear only after submission.
    • Answers are graded on the LMS server when available, so they are never present in the page.

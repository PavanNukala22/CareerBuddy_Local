    Module 01 · Foundations

    The RPO Implementation Manager Role

    The person who takes a signed RPO contract and turns it into a working service: redesigned processes, a configured and migrated system, service levels that measure correctly, and a client team ready to use it all.

    🏗️ What is an RPO Implementation Manager?

    When a company outsources hiring to an RPO provider, the first months decide whether the partnership works. The Implementation Manager leads that transition, from contract signature to a stable, running service, and then hands it to the Account Director and delivery team.

    It is a project role with a specific shape: a fixed start, a go-live date, many dependencies, and a client whose own people and systems are changing at the same time.

    🧩
    Process designer
    Turns the client's current hiring process into a leaner, agreed future process.
    💾
    Technology lead
    Runs the ATS/CRM setup, data migration, integrations and cutover.
    ⏱️
    Service-level builder
    Makes sure contract SLAs are defined precisely and measured by the system.
    🤝
    Change leader
    Brings the client's talent team along so the new way of working sticks.

    Module 02 · Process

    Process Re-engineering

    Putting a broken process into a new system only makes it fail faster. Re-engineering means looking at how hiring really works today, removing what adds no value, and designing a process that both the client and the provider can run.

    🎯 Why Re-engineer?

    Most clients arrive with a hiring process that grew over years: steps added after each problem, approvals nobody remembers the reason for, and differences between teams and countries. Moving that process unchanged into a new system carries all of it forward.

    ⚠️
    Do not simply "lift and shift". If the old process was slow because of extra approvals, a faster ATS will not fix it. The design work has to come before the configuration work.

    Good re-engineering balances four goals, and a design that ignores one of them usually fails in practice:

    ⚡
    Speed
    Fewer days between a role opening and a hire starting.
    🎓
    Quality
    The right candidates, assessed fairly and consistently.
    😊
    Experience
    Easy for candidates and hiring managers to take part in.
    🛡️
    Compliance
    Meets legal, data protection and client policy requirements.

    🗺️ Mapping the As-Is Process

    Before designing anything, document how hiring actually works today. "Actually" matters: the written policy and the real practice are often different.

    1. Interview the people who do the work, not only the managers who describe it. Recruiters, coordinators and hiring managers each see a different part.
    2. Walk through real requisitions from open to hire and note every step, handoff and wait.
    3. Record who does each step, which system or tool they use, and how long it takes.
    4. Collect the data: time between steps, drop-off rates, and the number of requisitions that follow each path.
    5. Capture the variations across regions, business units and job types.
    🔍
    Ask "why" three times. When someone says "we always get VP approval at this step", ask what happens if it is skipped, and when the rule began. Often the original reason no longer applies.

    🗑️ Finding Waste

    Once the as-is map exists, look for the places where time and effort are lost. The same patterns appear in most organizations.

    ⏳
    Waiting
    Requisitions or candidates sitting idle for approval or feedback.
    🔄
    Rework
    Steps repeated because information was missing or wrong the first time.
    📋
    Duplicate entry
    The same data typed into several systems or spreadsheets.
    🏃
    Handoffs
    Each handoff between people or teams adds delay and risk of error.
    ✍️
    Extra approvals
    Sign-offs that add time but no real control.
    ❓
    Unclear ownership
    Nobody knows who is responsible, so nothing moves.

    ✨ Designing the To-Be Process

    The to-be design removes the waste while keeping the controls the client genuinely needs. Here is a simplified example of what the change can look like for requisition approval.

    Figure 2.1 · Illustrative example. Your client's controls will differ.

    ✅ Good design principles

    • Fewer handoffs and approvals, each with a clear purpose
    • Standard steps with agreed local exceptions
    • Data entered once and reused
    • Decision owners named for every stage

    ❌ Common design mistakes

    • Designing without the people who do the work
    • Removing a control the client legally needs
    • Building a different process for every region
    • Producing a design too complex to train

    🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Defining Roles and Ownership

    A process is only as good as the clarity of who does what. A RACI table is the simplest way to record it: Responsible (does the work), Accountable (owns the outcome), Consulted (gives input) and Informed (kept up to date).

    StepHiring managerRPO recruiterClient HRImplementation / delivery lead
    Raise requisitionA / RICI
    Intake and job briefCA / RII
    Source and screenIA / RIC
    Interview and decideA / RRII
    Offer approvalRCAI

    This table is an example. Agree the real version with the client in the design phase, and make sure every row has exactly one A.

    Module 03 · Technology

    ATS/CRM Technology Migration

    Moving a client's hiring onto a new Applicant Tracking System and Candidate Relationship Management tool is the riskiest part of an implementation. This module covers how to plan it, move the data cleanly, connect the systems, and switch over without losing a single candidate.

    🖥️ ATS and CRM: What Each Does

    SystemMain purposeTypical data
    ATS Applicant Tracking SystemManages the active hiring process, from requisition to offer and hireRequisitions, applications, interview feedback, offers, compliance records
    CRM Candidate Relationship ManagementBuilds and nurtures talent pools before roles existProspects, talent communities, engagement history, campaigns

    Some platforms combine both. Others are separate tools that must be connected. Either way, the migration has to decide which system is the source of truth for each type of data.

    📌
    In many RPO engagements the provider uses its own ATS for the service, while in others it works inside the client's system. That choice changes the whole migration plan, so settle it before anything else.

    🧭 The Migration Approach

    A migration is a project within the project. Keep it structured, and keep each stage's exit criteria written down.

    Figure 3.1 · Scope, configure, migrate, test, cut over

    📦 Data Migration

    Data is where most migrations go wrong. Moving poor data into a new system only gives you the same problems in a new place.

    Decide what to move

    ➡️ Usually migrate

    • Open requisitions and their candidates in progress
    • Recent candidate records within retention limits
    • Offer and hire records needed for reporting or compliance
    • Talent pool contacts with valid consent

    🗄️ Usually archive or leave

    • Records past the legal retention period
    • Duplicates and obviously invalid contacts
    • Candidates without consent to be contacted
    • Old, closed requisitions with no reporting value

    Map, clean and reconcile

    1. Field mapping: for each field in the old system, state where it goes in the new one, and how values are converted.
    2. Cleansing: remove duplicates, fix formats (dates, phone numbers, country names) and fill required fields.
    3. Trial loads: run the migration into a test environment, at least twice, before the real one.
    4. Reconciliation: compare counts and sample records between old and new, and sign off the differences.
    mapping exampleOld system field        →  New system field        Rule
Cand_Status = "Intvw 1"   →  Stage = "First interview"   Map 14 old statuses to 9 new stages
Phone                    →  Mobile                  Standardize to international format
Applied_On               →  Application date        Keep the original date; do not overwrite
    🔐
    Data protection comes first. Candidate data is personal data. Confirm with the client's legal and privacy teams what can be moved, where it may be stored, and how consent and retention rules apply, before any data leaves the old system.

    🔌 Integrations

    The ATS rarely works alone. Each connection is a mini-project with its own owner, testing and risk.

    Connects toWhyCheck carefully
    Client HRISPass hired candidates in as employees, and sync org and headcount dataWhich system owns each field, and sync timing
    Job boards and career sitePost roles and receive applicationsPosting rules, duplicate applications, branding
    Assessment and interview toolsSend tests and scheduling links, and bring results backCandidate data sharing and result mapping
    Background check and onboardingTrigger checks and onboarding tasks at the right stageConsent, regional rules, and error handling
    Single sign-on and access controlLet the right people in, and no one elseRoles, permissions, and joiner/leaver process

    🧪 Testing and Cutover

    Cutover is the moment the old system stops being used and the new one takes over. Prepare for it like a launch, with a written plan and a way back.

    • User acceptance testing completed with real recruiters and hiring managers, with defects triaged
    • Two full trial data loads reconciled and signed off by the client
    • Every integration tested end to end, including failure cases
    • Security, access roles and data protection checks completed
    • Reports and SLA measures verified against known sample data (see Tab 4)
    • Cutover plan written, with times, owners and a communication schedule
    • Rollback plan agreed: what triggers it, and how it is done
    • Support team ready for the first days, with a clear escalation route
    🧯
    Plan the quiet weekend. Many teams cut over outside peak hiring periods, freeze changes before the date, and keep the old system read-only for a defined period afterward so nothing is lost.

    ⚠️ Common Risks

    🧹
    Dirty data
    Duplicates and gaps surface only after go-live, when it is hard to fix.
    🎛️
    Scope creep
    "Just one more field" requests that push the date or the budget.
    ⏰
    Late IT dependencies
    Client IT access, security reviews or HRIS changes arriving after the plan assumed.
    🧪
    Short testing
    Testing squeezed to protect the date, so defects reach live users.
    🙋
    No business owner
    Nobody on the client side with authority to decide on design questions.

    Keep a risk log from the first week, with an owner and a response for each risk, and review it in every status meeting.

    Module 04 · Service levels

    SLA Configuration

    A service level written in a contract is only useful if the system can measure it, exactly as written, without manual workarounds. SLA configuration turns contract language into stages, timestamps, rules and reports.

    📜 From Contract to Configuration

    Contracts describe SLAs in business language, such as "time to shortlist" or "offer acceptance rate". A system cannot measure an idea. It measures events with timestamps. Your job is to translate the first into the second.

    Figure 4.1 · Four steps from a contract sentence to a trusted report

    📐 Defining Each SLA Precisely

    For every SLA, write a one-page specification covering the points below, and have the client sign it off. If two reasonable people could read a definition two ways, it is not finished.

    SLA specificationName:             Time to shortlist
Contract clause:   (reference to the exact clause)
Target:            (as stated in the contract)
Clock starts:      (the exact event, e.g. requisition status = "Approved and open")
Clock stops:       (the exact event, e.g. first qualified candidate submitted to the manager)
Calendar:          Business days or calendar days? Which holidays?
Pauses:            When the clock stops for client delay (e.g. intake not completed)
Exclusions:        Roles or situations outside the SLA (e.g. on-hold requisitions)
Data source:       Which system field(s) the measure is calculated from
Reported:          Frequency, format, and who receives it
Sign-off:          Client owner and provider owner, with date
    ⚠️
    Watch for client-side delays. If the client takes a week to give feedback, the provider should not be penalized for it. Agree in advance which delays pause the clock, and make the system able to record them.

    ⚙️ Building It in the System

    Once definitions are signed off, build them into the ATS. The aim is that SLA data is recorded automatically as a by-product of normal work, not typed in afterwards.

    🔖
    Status and stage model
    Stages that match the agreed process, with clear entry and exit rules.
    🕒
    Timestamps
    Every event an SLA depends on is time-stamped automatically.
    🔒
    Required fields
    Mandatory data, such as pause reasons, so recruiters cannot skip them.
    🔔
    Alerts
    Warnings when a requisition is close to breaching its target.
    📊
    Reports and dashboards
    Scorecards built from the same logic the specification describes.

    Keep the setup as simple as the measures allow. Every extra mandatory field is something recruiters have to fill in under time pressure.

    🧾 Reporting and Reconciliation

    Before anyone relies on an SLA report, prove it is right. Take a sample of real requisitions, calculate each measure by hand from the raw records, and compare with what the report shows.

    CheckWhat to confirm
    Hand calculationThe report matches a manual calculation on a sample of requisitions
    Edge casesPaused clocks, holidays, cancelled and reopened requisitions behave as specified
    TotalsCounts in reports match counts in the system's own lists
    Both sides agreeClient and provider both accept the sample results in writing
    💡
    A report that only the provider trusts is a source of argument. Walk the client's analysts through the sample calculations so they can verify the logic for themselves.

    🧪 The Pre Go-Live SLA Dry Run

    Run the new process and reports in a pilot or parallel period, ahead of go-live, with contract SLAs measured but not yet penalized. This reveals three things:

    • Definition gaps: situations the specification did not cover.
    • Data gaps: recruiters who are not recording the events that SLAs depend on.
    • Realistic targets: whether the contract's targets are achievable with the redesigned process.
    ✅
    Exit rule: do not go live until every contractual SLA has a signed definition, a working measure, and at least one reconciled report. Hand the Account Director a measurement system they can trust on day one.

    Module 05 · People

    Change Management for Client Talent Teams

    The client's recruiters and coordinators are being asked to change how they work, and sometimes their role itself. A technically perfect implementation still fails if those people do not adopt it, or quietly keep working around it.

    🧠 Why People Resist Change

    Resistance is rarely stubbornness. It usually has an understandable reason, and the reasons are different for different people.

    😟
    Job security
    Outsourcing can feel like a threat to the client team's own roles.
    🧭
    Loss of control
    People who ran the process now share it, or no longer own it.
    ❓
    Uncertainty
    Not knowing what will change, or when, creates anxiety.
    📉
    Extra workload
    Learning a new system while still delivering today's hires.
    🏛️
    Past experience
    Earlier projects that promised much and delivered little.
    💡
    Listen first. Run short, open conversations with the client's team early, and ask what worries them. Concerns you hear in week one are far cheaper to address than ones that surface after go-live.

    🔬 Assessing the Impact

    For each group of people, work out what changes for them, how big the change is, and what they will need. A simple impact table keeps the plan honest.

    GroupWhat changesLikely concernWhat helps
    Client recruitersNew ATS, new process, new roles alongside the RPO teamRole clarity and job securityClear role descriptions, early involvement in design, honest messages from leadership
    Recruiting coordinatorsScheduling and admin moves to new toolsLearning curve and workloadHands-on training, quick-reference guides, a named helper
    Hiring managersRaise requisitions and give feedback in the system, within agreed timingsExtra work and loss of controlShort sessions, simple steps, evidence that it saves them time
    Client HR leadersNew reports and governanceVisibility and trust in the dataEarly sight of dashboards and the SLA definitions

    📣 Communication Plan

    Communicate earlier, more often, and more simply than feels necessary. Use the client sponsor's voice for the "why", and your team's voice for the "how".

    Figure 5.1 · Announce, involve, prepare, launch, reinforce

    🎓 Training That Works

    Training is where people decide whether the new way of working is manageable. Make it practical, role-specific and close to the go-live date.

    ✅ What works

    • Role-based sessions: each group sees only what it needs
    • Hands-on practice in a safe test environment
    • Short job aids and quick-reference guides
    • Super users on each team who can help colleagues
    • Training held within a couple of weeks of go-live

    ❌ What does not

    • One generic demo for everyone
    • Training months before people use the system
    • Slides with no chance to try the tool
    • No support route after the session

    📈 Adoption and Hypercare

    Hypercare is the heightened support period straight after go-live, when questions and small problems peak. Staff it generously, and keep the hand-over to steady state conditional on stability.

    Measure adoption, not just attendance at training:

    Adoption measureWhat it tells you
    Share of requisitions raised in the system, not by emailWhether managers are using the new process
    Share of interview feedback given within the agreed timeWhether client-side commitments are being met
    Number and type of support requestsWhere training or design needs fixing
    Workarounds spotted (spreadsheets, side emails)Places where the process or system does not fit real work
    User feedback and satisfaction pulseHow the client's team feels about it
    🏁
    That completes the set. Redesigned process, migrated system, measurable SLAs, and a client team that uses it all. Take the assessment next to check how much has stuck.

    Module 06

    Assessment

    Test yourself on process, technology, SLAs and change management. Multiple-choice questions, timed like a real screening test. Difficulty ranges from quick checks to genuinely tricky judgement calls.

    Test what you've learned
    A timed, exam-style mock test. Some questions are quick concept checks; others describe a realistic situation and ask what you would do.
    • Multiple-choice questions with a mix of concept and scenario items
    • Difficulty from Simple to Hard
    • Covers process, technology, SLAs and change management

    🔒 Exam Rules & Security

    • The test runs in fullscreen with a countdown timer; it auto-submits when time is up.
    • No tab or window switching and no exiting fullscreen. After 3 warnings the test auto-submits.
    • Screenshots, copy, cut, print and right-click are disabled; refreshing or closing the page asks for confirmation.
    • Navigate freely, mark questions for review, then submit. Results and explanations appear only after submission.
    • Answers are graded on the LMS server when available, so they are never present in the page.

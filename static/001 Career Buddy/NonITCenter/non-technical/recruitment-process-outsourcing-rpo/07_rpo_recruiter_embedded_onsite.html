    Module 01 · Foundations

    The RPO Recruiter (Embedded Onsite)

    A recruiter employed by the RPO provider who works inside the client's offices, as part of the business unit, advising hiring managers and delivering hires against agreed service levels.

    🔎 What is an Embedded Onsite RPO Recruiter?

    In a Recruitment Process Outsourcing (RPO) engagement, the embedded onsite recruiter sits with the client's team every day. You know the business unit's goals, speak with its hiring managers in person, and run the hiring process end to end, while following your RPO provider's methods and reporting.

    🏢
    Business partner
    Understands the unit's strategy, teams and hiring plans.
    🧭
    Hiring consultant
    Advises managers on role design, market and process.
    📝
    Intake leader
    Runs the meeting that sets up every search for success.
    ⏱️
    SLA owner
    Delivers on the speed, quality and service promises in the contract.

    🔄 Where You Sit in the Hiring Flow

    You are involved at every stage, from the moment a hiring manager opens a requisition to the day the new hire joins.

    Module 02 · Context

    Deep Client Business Unit Integration

    Become a trusted part of the business unit, so you can recruit with the same knowledge, language and priorities as the people you serve.

    🧱 What integration means

    Integration goes beyond having a desk at the client's site. It means you understand what the unit does, how it is organised, and what success looks like this year, and hiring managers treat you as one of their own team.

    AreaWhat to learn
    StrategyGrowth plans, new products, restructures and budget limits
    Teams and rolesOrg chart, role families, career paths and team culture
    PeopleHiring managers, their styles, and who else influences decisions
    ProcessApproval steps, interview habits and offer rules
    Talent marketWhere the unit's candidates come from and who competes for them

    ⚙️ How to build it

    1. Shadow the unit: attend team meetings and business reviews where you are invited.
    2. Meet every hiring manager one to one in your first weeks, and ask what has worked and failed before.
    3. Learn the language: job titles, tools and acronyms the team uses.
    4. Share market insight regularly, such as pay trends and candidate availability.
    5. Stay independent: follow both the client's rules and your RPO provider's standards.

    ⚖️ Embedded or remote?

    🏢 Embedded onsite

    Face-to-face access, faster decisions, deeper knowledge of the team and a stronger relationship with managers.

    💻 Remote RPO team

    Wider reach and lower cost, but less day-to-day context and fewer informal conversations.

    ⚠️
    Do not "go native" completely. Keep your provider's process, data and compliance rules, even when a manager asks you to skip them.
    Module 03 · Advice

    Hiring Manager Consulting

    Move from order-taker to adviser: use data and market knowledge to help managers define the right role, see the right candidates and decide with confidence.

    🧭 The consultant mindset

    🔴 Order-taker

    Posts the role as written, forwards every résumé and waits for feedback.

    🟢 Consultant

    Questions the brief, brings market facts, recommends a plan and keeps the manager informed.

    🧾 Where managers need advice

    TopicHow you help
    Role designSeparate must-have from nice-to-have skills; suggest alternative titles or levels
    Market realityShare candidate supply, pay ranges and competing employers
    Interview processRecommend fewer stages, structured questions and fast feedback
    Candidate feedbackExplain why a shortlist was or was not right, and adjust together
    Offer and closeAdvise on pay position and how to handle counter-offers
    💡
    Bring evidence. "Candidates with this exact mix of skills are rare; widening to related experience gives us 4 times the pool" is stronger than "this will be hard".

    🗣️ Handling hard conversations

    • Unrealistic brief: show market data, then offer two or three options
    • Slow feedback: link delay to lost candidates and agree a feedback deadline
    • Bypassing the process: explain the compliance and fairness reasons, and offer a fast, compliant route
    • Pay below market: show comparable offers and the likely rejection rate
    Module 04 · Alignment

    Intake Meeting Leadership

    Lead the meeting that sets the search up properly: agree the profile, the process, the timeline and each person's responsibilities.

    📝 Why the intake meeting matters

    Most slow or failed searches trace back to an unclear start. A strong intake meeting saves weeks later by making sure you and the hiring manager want the same person.

    📏
    Keep it short and focused. Aim for 30 to 45 minutes, send a summary within 24 hours and ask the manager to confirm it.

    🎬 Running the meeting

    StepWhat to do
    PrepareReview the requisition, team data and similar past hires
    OpenState the purpose, the time available and the outcome you want
    ExploreAsk about the business need, day-one tasks, team and success in 12 months
    CalibrateReview sample profiles and agree must-haves versus nice-to-haves
    PlanAgree interview panel, stages, feedback deadlines and target dates
    ConfirmSend a written summary and get the manager's agreement

    ✅ Intake checklist

    • Business reason for the role and the problem it solves
    • Must-have and nice-to-have skills, written separately
    • Pay range, location and work-pattern approved
    • Interview panel named, with availability and agreed feedback times
    • Target dates for shortlist, interviews and offer
    • Written summary sent and confirmed by the manager
    Module 05 · Delivery

    SLA Adherence

    Meet the service levels in the RPO contract by tracking them daily, raising risks early and being open with the client about performance.

    📜 What an SLA is

    A Service Level Agreement (SLA) sets measurable promises between the RPO provider and the client, often tied to fees, bonuses or penalties. Your daily work is where those promises are kept or broken.

    📊 Common SLA measures

    MeasureWhat it tracks
    Time to submitDays from intake to the first qualified shortlist
    Time to fillDays from requisition approval to offer acceptance
    Submittal-to-interview ratioCandidate quality, as seen by the hiring manager
    Offer acceptance rateHow often offers are accepted
    Manager satisfactionSurvey score from hiring managers
    ComplianceCorrect process, records and data in the system
    🧮
    Example: if the SLA is 21 days to first shortlist and intake happened on day 0, then day 14 is your checkpoint. If you have no shortlist by then, raise the risk with the manager and your delivery lead.

    🛡️ Staying on track

    • Update the system every day, because reports depend on your records
    • Review all open roles weekly and flag the ones at risk
    • Report misses honestly, with the cause and a recovery plan
    • Agree exclusions, such as on-hold roles or late feedback, in writing
    • Use review meetings to propose process fixes, not only to report numbers
    ⚠️
    Never hit a number by cutting corners. Rushing shortlists or skipping checks may meet an SLA today and damage quality and trust tomorrow.

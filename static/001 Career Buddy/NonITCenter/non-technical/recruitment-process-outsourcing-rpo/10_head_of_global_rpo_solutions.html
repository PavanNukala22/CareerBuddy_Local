    The Head of Global RPO Solutions

    The senior leader who designs multi-country hiring solutions for large clients, prices them in a way both sides can live with, and leads the global pursuit that wins the deal.

    🔎 What is a Head of Global RPO Solutions?

    In a Recruitment Process Outsourcing (RPO) provider, the Head of Global RPO Solutions owns how the service is shaped and sold to enterprise clients. They turn a client's hiring problem into a designed solution, a commercial model and a winning proposal, and hand a deal to delivery that can actually be run.

    Module 02 · Design

    Enterprise Solution Design

    Shape a hiring service that fits a large client across countries, business units and hiring types, and that delivery can run well.

    🏗️ What a solution contains

    An enterprise solution describes what is delivered, by whom, from where, with which tools and under what governance. Start from the client's business goals, then design backwards.

    ElementQuestions to answer
    ScopeWhich roles, countries and volumes? Which are in or out?
    Delivery modelOnsite, regional hubs, shared service centre or a mix?
    ProcessIntake, sourcing, screening, offers and onboarding handoffs
    Technology and dataApplicant tracking, reporting and integration with client systems
    GovernanceReview meetings, escalation paths and a joint leadership group
    ComplianceLocal employment law, data privacy and fair hiring rules per country
    TransitionHow the service moves from the current model without losing momentum

    ⚙️ Design steps

    1. Discover the client's hiring volumes, pain points, systems and business goals.
    2. Segment hiring by role type, country and complexity, because one size will not fit all.
    3. Choose delivery models for each segment and check them with regional delivery leaders.
    4. Test the design against risks: volume swings, local law and key-person dependence.
    5. Document the solution in plain language with a clear picture of the roles and handoffs.

    ⚖️ One model or many?

    🟦 One global standard

    Consistent process, data and cost across countries, and simpler governance. Less fit with local markets.

    🟧 Local variations

    Better fit with local law, language and talent markets. More complexity to manage and report.

    📏
    A common answer is both. Keep one global core for process, data and governance, and allow local variations where law or the talent market requires them.
    Module 03 · Commercials

    RPO Pricing Models

    Understand how cost-per-slate, cost-per-hire and management-fee models work, what each rewards, and how to choose between them.

    💰 The three models

    ModelHow the client paysClient riskProvider risk
    Management feeA fixed recurring fee for running the servicePays even if hiring is lowAbsorbs cost if volume is higher than planned
    Cost-per-hireA fee for each completed hirePays only for results; fee may rise with volumeEarns nothing if hires do not complete
    Cost-per-slateA fee for each shortlist of qualified candidates deliveredPays even if the hiring manager rejects the slatePaid for effort and quality before the decision
    📏
    Define a slate precisely. Agree how many candidates it contains, what "qualified" means and how a rejected slate is handled, or disputes will follow.

    🧮 Worked examples

    📅
    Management fee
    $150,000 per month × 12 = $1.8M a year, whatever the number of hires.
    🧑‍💼
    Cost-per-hire
    600 hires × $4,500 = $2.7M. If only 400 are hired, the cost is $1.8M.
    📋
    Cost-per-slate
    1,000 slates × $1,200 = $1.2M, paid when each slate is delivered.
    💡
    Compare on the same volume. Always model each option at low, expected and high hiring volumes. The cheapest model at one volume can be the most expensive at another.

    🧩 Choosing and combining

    • Stable, predictable hiring: management fee, often with a smaller per-hire element
    • Volatile or project-based hiring: cost-per-hire, so the client pays only when it hires
    • Hard-to-fill, specialist roles: cost-per-slate, to reward the search itself
    • Large global programmes: a hybrid with a base management fee plus per-hire fees by region or role band
    ⚠️
    Check the margin at every volume. A pricing model that looks attractive at expected volume can lose money if hiring volume or role mix changes. Test the downside before offering it. Local tax, currency and law also affect pricing in each country.
    Module 04 · Winning

    Global Pursuit Leadership

    Lead the cross-country team and the executive effort that turns a request for proposal or a client conversation into a signed global deal.

    🌍 What a global pursuit is

    A pursuit is the organised effort to win a specific opportunity. For a global client, it involves solution, pricing, legal, delivery and sales people in many time zones, all working to one story and one deadline.

    🧭
    This guide reads "global pursuit of leadership" as leading global pursuits. That is, taking the lead on multi-country bids and proposals.

    🎬 Leading the pursuit

    StepWhat to do
    QualifyDecide bid or no bid using client fit, win chance, risk and margin
    OrganiseName a pursuit lead and owners for solution, pricing, legal and each region
    Shape the storyAgree win themes: why this client should choose you
    Build the responseWrite to the client's questions, with proof from similar clients
    ReviewHold review sessions before submission to challenge gaps and risks
    PresentPrepare the team for orals and demos with a clear, rehearsed message
    Negotiate and learnProtect agreed terms, then review the result whether you win or lose

    ✅ Pursuit checklist

    • Bid or no-bid decision recorded, with the reasons
    • One accountable leader and a regional contact for each country
    • Win themes agreed before writing starts
    • Pricing approved by finance, with margin tested at low and high volume
    • Local legal, tax and data privacy points reviewed per country
    • Time zones and deadlines planned, with buffer before submission
    • Post-decision review shared, win or lose
    ⚠️
    Do not promise what delivery cannot run. A deal that is won on an unrealistic solution or price harms the client, the delivery team and your reputation.

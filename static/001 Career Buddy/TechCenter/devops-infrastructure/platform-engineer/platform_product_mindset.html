    Platform as a Product

    Your developers are your customers. A platform nobody wants to use has failed, however good the technology.

    🗺️ Big picture diagram Basic

    The platform product loop

    👥 Know your users Basic

    Create personas: e.g. Priya, backend dev (wants a new service in an hour), Sam, data engineer (needs pipelines and GPUs), Lee, SRE (wants consistent observability).

    ☕
    Analogy: a cafe. A good cafe watches what customers order and where queues form. A platform team watches tickets, Slack questions and onboarding time.

    🧠 Cognitive load Basic

    📚
    Intrinsic
    Skills needed for the task itself (writing code).
    🌀
    Extraneous
    Pain from tools, YAML, tickets. Platform removes this.
    🧩
    Germane
    Learning that helps the business. Keep this.

    🔄 Platform lifecycle and who the stakeholders are Basic

    📖 What it is

    A platform product moves through discovery, build, launch, growth and sunset. Users are developers; stakeholders are security, finance and leadership who also need outcomes.

    🎯 Why it matters

    Serving only developers can fail on compliance; serving only compliance kills adoption.

    🧩 Types

    • Users: product engineers, data engineers, SREs.
    • Customers (sponsors): engineering leadership funding the team.
    • Stakeholders: security, compliance, finance.
    • Contributors: other teams adding plugins and templates.

    🧪 Example

    Security wants all images scanned. The platform bakes scanning into the Golden Path so developers do nothing extra and security gets evidence.

    🧱 Product vocabulary for platform teams Basic

    📖 What it is

    Vision, strategy, roadmap, backlog and OKRs are the same terms product managers use.

    🎯 Why it matters

    Speaking this language helps you ask for budget and prioritise honestly.

    🧩 Types

    • Vision: one sentence: every team ships to production safely on day one.
    • OKR: Objective plus measurable Key Results.
    • MVP: smallest release that tests an assumption.
    • Backlog: ordered list of problems to solve.

    🧪 Example

    OKR: Objective reduce wait for infrastructure. KR1 median database request time from 2 days to 10 minutes. KR2 80% of new services use the Golden Path.

    Gathering Feedback and Measuring Value

    Turn opinions into evidence and decide what to build next.

    🗣️ Feedback channels Intermediate

    30-minute chats with 5 developers reveal most pain.
    📋
    Surveys
    Quarterly NPS / satisfaction pulse.
    💬
    Support channel
    Tag every question; repeated tags = missing feature or doc.
    🖱️

    Portal clicks, template runs, failed deployments.

    🎙️ Running a developer interview Intermediate

    📖 What it is

    A structured 30-minute conversation focused on behaviour, not opinions about your tools.

    🎯 Why it matters

    Developers will say what they think you want to hear unless questions ask about real past events.

    🧩 Types

    • Ask: Walk me through the last time you created a new service.
    • Probe: What did you do next? Where did you wait?
    • Avoid: leading questions like Wouldn't a portal be great?

    🧪 Example

    Result: 4 of 6 developers copied an old repo because the template was outdated. Insight: the template is the problem, not the lack of a portal.

    🔢 Calculating NPS and adoption Intermediate

    📖 What it is

    NPS asks How likely are you to recommend the platform (0-10)?. Promoters score 9-10, detractors 0-6.

    🎯 Why it matters

    It is a simple trend line; the free-text comments tell you why.

    🧪 Example

    NPS = %promoters - %detractors. 40 responses: 18 promoters (45%), 14 passives, 8 detractors (20%). NPS = 45 - 20 = 25.

    Adoption = teams using Golden Path ÷ total teams = 36 ÷ 48 = 75%.

    🧪 Experiments and A/B rollouts Intermediate

    📖 What it is

    Treat platform changes as hypotheses and test with a pilot group before a wide release.

    🎯 Why it matters

    It limits blast radius and gives evidence for the roadmap.

    🧩 Types

    • Pilot: 2 volunteer teams use the new feature.
    • Feature flags: enable by team.
    • Success criteria: defined before the pilot starts.

    🧪 Example

    Hypothesis: preview environments reduce review time. Pilot 3 teams for 4 weeks. Result: PR cycle time dropped 30%. Decision: roll out.

    Expert

    Module 03 · Leadership

    Platform Strategy and Operating Model

    Run the platform like a business: roadmap, team shape, communication and saying no.

    🗺️ Roadmap and thinnest viable platform Expert

    Start with the smallest platform that removes the biggest pain (often just one Golden Path), then grow by evidence. Prioritise with RICE: Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort.

    Discover→Prioritise→Build thin slice→Launch→Measure→Iterate

    🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Team Topologies Expert

    Team typeRole
    Stream-alignedProduct teams that deliver to customers
    PlatformProvides internal services as a product
    EnablingCoaches teams to adopt new capabilities
    Complicated-subsystemDeep specialists (e.g. ML infra)

    Interaction modes: X-as-a-Service is the goal; use collaboration briefly when building something new.

    📣 Adoption and communication Expert

    • Public changelog and release notes for every platform change.
    • Office hours and an internal champions network.
    • Deprecation policy with dates and migration help.
    • SLOs for the platform itself (portal uptime, provisioning time).
    ⚠️
    Pitfalls: ivory-tower design, forced migration without value, vanity metrics (number of templates), no owner for docs.

    💰 Business case and ROI Expert

    📖 What it is

    A business case translates platform benefits into time and money saved.

    🎯 Why it matters

    Platform teams are cost centres; without numbers they are the first to be cut.

    🧪 Example

    200 developers waste 3 hours/week on setup and tickets. Platform removes 2 hours. 200 × 2 × 48 weeks = 19,200 hours/year. At 60 USD/hour = 1.15M USD/year saved, against a platform team costing 600k USD. ROI ≈ 1.9x.

    🧠 Measuring developer experience Expert

    📖 What it is

    DevEx has three dimensions: flow state, feedback loops and cognitive load. Combine surveys with system data.

    🎯 Why it matters

    Telemetry alone says what happens; surveys say how it feels.

    🧩 Types

    • Perceptual: quarterly survey: I can deploy without help (1-5).
    • Workflow: CI duration, time to first review, deploy lead time.
    • Combine: if CI time is fast but satisfaction is low, look at flaky tests or unclear errors.

    🧪 Example

    Survey shows low scores on understanding errors. Action: standardise pipeline failure messages with links to fix guides.

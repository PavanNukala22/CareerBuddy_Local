    Cloud Pricing Foundations

    Before buying any discount you must understand what you pay by default, how rates are measured, and the one number that decides every commitment: break-even utilization.

    💳 What are cloud pricing models?

    A pricing model is the contract that decides how much you pay per unit of compute, storage or service. The same virtual machine can cost 3–4 different prices depending on how you buy it. FinOps engineers pick the cheapest model that still matches how steadily the workload runs.

    🛒
    On-Demand
    Pay per second/hour. No commitment, highest unit price, full flexibility.
    📜
    Commitments
    RIs, Savings Plans, CUDs: promise usage for 1–3 years for a lower rate.
    🎲
    Spot / Preemptible
    Spare capacity at deep discount; can be reclaimed with short notice.
    🤝
    Negotiated rates
    Enterprise-wide private pricing (EDP / PPA / MACC) layered on top.

    BASIC The pricing spectrum

    ModelCommitmentTypical saving*RiskBest for
    On-DemandNone0% (baseline)Highest costSpiky, new, short-lived workloads
    Savings Plan / CUD (spend)$/hour, 1–3 yr~20–66%Under-use of commitmentSteady, changing compute
    Reserved Instance / resource CUDSpecific capacity, 1–3 yr~30–72%Lock-in to type/regionVery stable, known shapes
    Spot / PreemptibleNone~60–90%InterruptionBatch, CI, stateless, fault-tolerant

    *Illustrative ranges. Discounts vary by provider, service, region, term and payment option — always confirm on the current provider pricing page.

    📏 Key vocabulary every FinOps engineer uses

    TermMeaning
    List / on-demand ratePublic price before any discount
    Effective rateWhat you actually paid per unit after all discounts: total cost ÷ usage
    Amortized costUpfront payments spread across the term so each day shows its true share
    Coverage% of eligible usage that is covered by commitments
    Utilization% of purchased commitment that was actually used
    Break-even utilizationMinimum utilization at which a commitment is cheaper than on-demand
    ESR (Effective Savings Rate)Realized savings ÷ what on-demand would have cost
    🧮 Example — break-even

    On-demand = $0.100/hr. 1-year commitment effective rate = $0.062/hr (paid for every hour, used or not).
    Break-even utilization = 0.062 ÷ 0.100 = 62%. If the instance runs more than 62% of the hours (≈ 15 h/day), commit; below that, stay on-demand.

    💡
    Rule of thumb: commit to the floor of your usage (the part that is always on), never to the peak.

    ⚠️ Common beginner mistakes

    • Buying commitments for workloads about to be migrated, resized or shut down.
    • Judging savings by the invoice instead of effective rate (upfront fees distort the month).
    • Ignoring coverage and utilization together: 100% utilization with 20% coverage still leaves most spend at list price.
    ⚠️
    Optimize usage first (rightsizing, scheduling, deleting idle resources), then buy commitments for what remains. Committing to waste locks the waste in for 1–3 years.

    Module 02 · Commitment: capacity-based

    Reserved Instances (RIs)

    RIs are the original commitment product: you reserve a specific instance footprint for 1 or 3 years and receive a billing discount. Learn the types, the payment options, and how portfolios are managed.

    📘 What is a Reserved Instance?

    An RI is not a physical server; it is a billing discount (and optionally a capacity reservation) applied automatically to matching on-demand usage. If no matching usage exists in an hour, you still pay — that is waste.

    🗓️
    Term
    1 year or 3 years. Longer term = bigger discount, bigger lock-in.
    💵
    Payment option
    All Upfront, Partial Upfront, or No Upfront (monthly).
    🔄
    Class
    Standard (max discount) vs Convertible (exchangeable).
    📍
    Scope
    Regional (any AZ in region) vs Zonal (one AZ + capacity reservation).

    BASIC Types at a glance

    DimensionOption AOption B
    ClassStandard: highest discount, limited changes, can be sold on RI Marketplace (AWS)Convertible: lower discount, exchange for different family/OS/tenancy
    ScopeRegional: size-flexible, any AZ, no capacity guaranteeZonal: locked to one AZ, reserves capacity
    PaymentAll Upfront: biggest discount, cash out on day 1No Upfront: smallest discount, no cash outlay
    Term1-year: lower risk3-year: deepest discount
    📝
    Other clouds: Azure calls them Reservations (VMs, SQL, Storage, Cosmos DB…). GCP has no classic RI; its equivalent is the resource-based Committed Use Discount (next module). Always check the current terms for exchanges and refunds.

    🧮 Working the numbers (basic → intermediate)

    🧮 Example — payment options

    m-class instance, on-demand $0.10/hr × 8,760 h = $876/yr. A 1-yr Standard RI might cost:

    • No Upfront: $0.066/hr → $578 (34% off)
    • Partial Upfront: $290 up front + $0.033/hr → $579 (~34%)
    • All Upfront: $556 (~36% off)

    Paying earlier buys a few extra percent; compare against your cost of capital before choosing.

    INTERMEDIATE Instance size flexibility (normalization)

    With a Regional RI on Linux, one RI covers any size in the same family using normalization factors: small=1, medium=2, large=4, xlarge=8, 2xlarge=16.

    🧮 Example — size flexibility

    You own 1 × m5.2xlarge RI (16 units) but run 4 × m5.large (4×4 = 16 units). The RI covers all four, because 16 = 16.

    INTERMEDIATE Utilization & waste

    Utilization % = RI hours used ÷ RI hours purchased. Target 95%+ for stable fleets. Under-used RIs can be modified, exchanged (Convertible), or sold (Standard on the AWS marketplace, where eligible).

    🏆 Expert: managing an RI portfolio

    • Ladder purchases: buy monthly/quarterly slices so expiries are staggered — no cliff when 100% of RIs expire the same day.
    • Blend terms: 3-yr Standard on the rock-solid base, 1-yr or Convertible on the “probably stable” layer.
    • Migration-aware buying: check roadmaps (Graviton/ARM, containers, serverless) before committing to x86 families.
    • Chargeback of benefit: decide whether RI discounts are shared across accounts (organization-wide sharing) or kept by the buying account.
    • Expiry runbook: alert 90/60/30 days ahead; decide renew, convert or let lapse using the latest utilization trend.
    🧮 Example — portfolio break-even for a Convertible

    3-yr Convertible gives 54% off vs 3-yr Standard 60% off. The 6-point premium is the option value of changing family. If there is a meaningful chance you will need to change family, OS or tenancy during the term, Convertible can win; model it per workload.

    💡
    Many teams now buy Savings Plans first and use RIs only where capacity reservation or a service-specific reservation (RDS, ElastiCache, OpenSearch, Redshift) is required.

    Module 03 · Commitment: spend-based

    Savings Plans & Committed Use Discounts

    Modern commitments are expressed as a dollar-per-hour spend (or resource amount) rather than a specific server. Learn how AWS Savings Plans, Azure Savings Plans and GCP CUDs differ — and how to size and layer them.

    🧾 What is a Savings Plan?

    You commit to spend $X per hour for 1 or 3 years. Every hour, eligible usage is billed at the discounted rate until your $X is consumed; the excess is billed on-demand. Unused commitment is still billed.

    ProviderProductWhat it coversFlexibility
    AWSCompute Savings PlanEC2 any family/region/OS, Fargate, LambdaHighest
    AWSEC2 Instance Savings PlanOne instance family in one regionMedium (size/OS/AZ free)
    AWSSageMaker Savings PlanSageMaker usageService-specific
    AzureSavings Plan for ComputeVMs, App Service, Functions Premium, containersHigh (any region/size in scope)
    GCPSpend-based (Flexible) CUDCompute across machine families/regionsHigh, lower discount
    GCPResource-based CUDSpecific vCPU/RAM/GPU in a regionLow, higher discount
    🧮 Example — hourly commitment

    You commit $10/hr of Compute Savings Plan (discounted rate). Hour A usage worth $14 at the discounted rate → $10 covered + $4 on-demand. Hour B usage only $7 → you still pay $10, so $3 is wasted.

    📐 Choosing: RI vs Savings Plan vs CUD

    NeedPrefer
    Workloads will move across instance families, regions or to containers/serverlessCompute Savings Plan / Azure SP / Flex CUD
    Maximum discount on a fixed, long-lived instance shapeEC2 Instance SP / Standard RI / Resource CUD
    Need guaranteed capacity in an AZZonal RI / On-demand capacity reservation
    Database, cache, search servicesService-specific RIs / reservations
    Fault-tolerant batch jobsSpot / Preemptible (no commitment)
    ✅
    Usual application order: (1) Spot where possible, (2) instance-specific commitments (RI / Instance SP), (3) flexible Compute Savings Plan, (4) remaining usage at on-demand. AWS applies the highest-discount rules first automatically.

    🏗️ Expert: sizing and laddering commitments

    EXPERT Sizing the commitment

    1. Pull 30–90 days of hourly eligible spend at on-demand-equivalent rates.
    2. Find the stable floor — e.g. the 10th percentile or minimum of daily minimums.
    3. Commit to 70–85% of that floor, leaving a buffer for rightsizing and migrations.
    4. Re-evaluate monthly; add small tranches as the floor rises.
    🧮 Example — commitment sizing

    Hourly eligible usage ranged $38–$92/hr; P10 = $44/hr. Commit ≈ 80% × 44 = $35/hr. At an effective 28% discount, annual saving ≈ $35 ÷ 0.72 × 0.28 × 8,760 ≈ $119k, while the risk of unused commitment stays small.

    EXPERT KPIs & guardrails

    KPIHealthy target
    Commitment utilization≥ 95–98%
    Coverage of steady-state compute60–80%
    Effective Savings RateTrend up, compare vs benchmark
    Expiry cliff (% expiring in any 90 days)< 30%
    • Laddering: purchase in monthly tranches with 1-yr terms plus a 3-yr base.
    • Shared across the organization: centralize purchases in the management/payer account so unused commitments float to other teams.
    • Risk events: big migrations, M&A, product sunsets — freeze buying and stress-test.
    ⚠️
    Savings Plans/CUDs generally cannot be cancelled or resold. Always verify current return/refund rules in the provider docs before purchase.

    Module 04 · Enterprise pricing

    Rate Negotiation & Private Pricing

    Beyond public discounts, large customers negotiate an organization-wide agreement. Learn the vocabulary, the levers, the traps, and how to model a deal before you sign.

    🤝 What is a negotiated rate?

    A private pricing agreement gives a percentage discount on top of (or instead of) public prices in exchange for a spend commitment over 1–5 years.

    ProviderTypical agreementCore idea
    AWSEDP / Private Pricing Agreement (PPA)Commit total annual AWS spend → discount % on eligible services
    AzureMACC (Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitment) / EACommit total Azure consumption; Marketplace purchases may burn it down
    GCPCommit contract / Private offerAnnual spend commitment with discount and Marketplace burn-down
    📝
    Terms vary by region, reseller and year. Agreements are confidential; validate eligibility and exclusions (e.g. support, Marketplace, some services) with your provider or reseller.

    🧰 Negotiation levers & process

    INTERMEDIATE What you can negotiate

    • Discount tier by committed spend band (e.g. higher % at higher commitment).
    • Ramp schedule — lower commitment in year 1, rising later, instead of flat.
    • Shortfall terms — what happens if you miss the commitment (pay difference, roll-over, extend term).
    • Marketplace burn-down — can third-party software count toward commitment?
    • Credits for migration, training, POCs; support plan pricing.
    • Discount stacking — whether the negotiated % applies on top of Savings Plans/RIs or only to on-demand.

    INTERMEDIATE Process

    1. Build a 12–36 month forecast by service (with growth, migrations and optimization).
    2. Benchmark against peers/FinOps community and competing clouds.
    3. Define your BATNA (multi-cloud, workload moves, smaller commit).
    4. Start 6–9 months before renewal; align with the provider’s fiscal quarter-end.
    5. Model scenarios, then have Finance, Legal and Engineering approve.

    📊 Expert: modeling a deal

    🧮 Example — discount vs shortfall risk

    Option A: commit $10M/yr (billed dollars) at 9% off. Option B: commit $8M/yr at 6% off. Forecast billed spend: $9M.
    A: $9M < $10M, so you pay the $10M commitment. The usage would cost $9M ÷ 0.91 ≈ $9.89M at list → you pay more than list (≈ −$0.11M).
    B: $9M ≥ $8M, so you pay $9M for usage worth $9M ÷ 0.94 ≈ $9.57M at list → saving ≈ $0.57M.
    Lesson: a bigger headline % can be worse if you will not reach the commitment.

    • Optimization vs commitment tension: aggressive cost cutting reduces consumption — don’t commit to spend you plan to eliminate.
    • Interaction effects: compute the blended effective rate after SPs/RIs and the enterprise discount.
    • Governance: track commitment burn monthly (YTD spend ÷ pro-rated commitment) and escalate early if tracking below 90%.
    💡
    Negotiate flexibility as hard as price: a slightly lower discount with rollover and ramp is often worth more than 1–2 extra points.

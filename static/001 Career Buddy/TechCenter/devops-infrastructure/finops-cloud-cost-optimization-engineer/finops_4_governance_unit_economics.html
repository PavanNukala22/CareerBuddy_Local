    Module 01 · Governance

    Cost Allocation & Tagging Strategies

    You cannot optimize, charge back or compute unit costs for spend you cannot attribute. Learn how to label cloud cost to the right owner — with tags, account structure and shared-cost rules.

    🏷️ What is cost allocation?

    Cost allocation is the practice of assigning every dollar of cloud spend to a team, product, environment or customer. It is the foundation of accountability: no owner, no action.

    🏷️
    Tags / Labels
    Key-value metadata on resources: team=payments. (AWS & Azure: tags; GCP: labels.)
    🏢
    Account structure
    Separate AWS accounts / Azure subscriptions / GCP projects per team or env.
    🔀
    Shared cost rules
    Rules to split networking, support, platform clusters across consumers.
    📦
    Allocation tools
    Cost Categories, Cost Allocation Tags, Cost Management scopes, BigQuery billing export.

    BASIC Types of allocation

    TypeHow it worksProsCons
    Tag-basedCosts follow resource tagsGranular, flexibleNeeds discipline; some resources untaggable
    Account / project-basedEach team has its own account/projectClean, no tags neededMany accounts to manage
    Rule-based (categories)Rules map accounts/tags/services to groupsRe-map without touching resourcesRules must be maintained
    Proportional (shared costs)Split shared spend by usage ratioFair, incentivizes efficiencyNeeds usage data

    INTERMEDIATE Rules that make tags work

    • Fixed naming standard (lower-case, hyphens, one spelling: cost-center, not CostCentre).
    • Activate user-defined tags for billing (AWS Cost Allocation Tags) — tags only appear in cost data after activation and are not retroactive.
    • Enforce at creation: AWS tag policies/SCPs, Azure Policy (require tag / inherit tag), GCP org policy + labels in IaC.
    • Tag in IaC (Terraform default tags) so tags can’t be forgotten.
    • Report tag compliance % weekly; auto-ticket violators.
    ⚠️
    Never put secrets or personal data in tags; tags are visible in billing and APIs.

    🧠 Expert: shared & untagged cost

    EXPERT Allocating shared costs

    Three accepted methods:

    • Fixed: pre-agreed percentages (simple, predictable).
    • Even split: equal share per consumer (rarely fair).
    • Proportional: split by a usage driver — requests, vCPU-hours, data processed (fairest).
    🧮 Example — shared Kubernetes cluster

    Cluster cost $30,000. Namespace CPU requests: Search 50%, Payments 30%, Reco 20%. Allocation → Search $15,000; Payments $9,000; Reco $6,000. Add idle capacity as a separate line (e.g. 15% idle = $4,500 shown to the platform team, not hidden in tenants).

    EXPERT Handling untagged spend

    Create an “Unallocated” bucket and shrink it with targets (e.g. <10% → <5%). Backfill using account mapping, resource-name patterns, or tag inheritance from parent (resource group/stack) before leaving costs to central IT.

    💡
    KPI: allocation coverage = allocated spend ÷ total spend. Mature FinOps teams target 90–95%+ allocated.

    Module 02 · Governance

    Showback & Chargeback Models

    Once cost is attributed, you must communicate it. Showback informs; chargeback bills. Pick the right model, define how discounts and shared cost flow, and mature step by step.

    📣 What are showback and chargeback?

    👁️ Showback

    Teams see their cloud cost, but are not billed internally. Drives awareness and accountability with low friction.

    💸 Chargeback

    Cost is transferred to the team’s budget / P&L through internal billing. Drives ownership of real money.

    AspectShowbackChargeback
    Money moves?NoYes (journal entries)
    EffortLowHigh (finance process, disputes)
    Behavior changeModerateStrong
    Accuracy neededGoodAudit-level
    Best whenStarting FinOps, tagging immatureAllocation ≥ 90%, finance buy-in
    📝
    They are stages, not rivals: most organizations run Crawl: showback → Walk: partial chargeback → Run: full chargeback.

    🧮 Chargeback models (types)

    ModelFormulaUse when
    Direct / actualTeam pays exactly its tagged costDedicated accounts, good tagging
    ProportionalShare of a pool × usage driver %Shared platforms, Kubernetes, data lakes
    Fixed / flatPre-agreed monthly feePredictable internal services
    Tiered / rate cardPrice per unit (e.g. $/vCPU-hr) set by platform teamInternal cloud “as a product”
    Blended rateAverage discounted rate for all teamsSimplicity, avoid RI “lottery”
    🧮 Example — proportional chargeback

    Monthly invoice: Team A direct $40,000; Team B direct $25,000. Shared network/support pool $15,000, split by direct-spend ratio (A 61.5%, B 38.5%).
    A pays $40,000 + $9,231 = $49,231; B pays $25,000 + $5,769 = $30,769.

    INTERMEDIATE The commitment-discount question

    Who gets the benefit of a Savings Plan/RI? Options: (1) buyer-keeps (unfair), (2) actual benefit to the consuming team, (3) blended/amortized rate for everyone — the most common and fair for shared pools.

    🚀 Expert: operating the program

    • Use amortized & blended cost for allocation, not raw invoice cash, so upfront fees do not distort a single month.
    • Unit price transparency: publish an internal rate card (price per vCPU-hr, GB-month) so teams can forecast.
    • Budgets & alerts per team at 50/80/100% of forecast; escalate to finance on breach.
    • Dispute workflow: a 5-day window to challenge allocations before month-end close.
    • Incentive design: reward teams for reducing unit cost, not just total cost — growth should not be punished.
    • Pitfalls: charging for untagged costs without grace, hiding idle capacity, changing rules mid-quarter.
    💡
    Start chargeback with a pilot business unit, publish methodology, and freeze rules for at least a quarter to build trust.

    Module 03 · Business value

    Unit Economics & KPIs

    Total cloud spend rising is not bad if revenue and usage grow faster. Unit economics ties cost to business output — cost per transaction, customer or feature — so you can judge efficiency, not just size.

    🎯 What is unit economics?

    Unit cost = allocated cloud cost ÷ business metric (the driver). It answers: “What does it cost us to serve one more order / user / API call?”

    🧾
    Cost per transaction
    Cloud cost ÷ orders, payments or API calls.
    👤
    Cost per customer / tenant
    Cloud cost ÷ active customers (SaaS).
    🎬
    Cost per feature / stream
    Cost of one product capability, minute streamed, GB processed.
    📉
    Cloud cost % of revenue
    Cloud cost ÷ revenue; ties to gross margin.
    🧮 Example — cost per transaction

    Checkout service cost last month = $42,000, transactions = 12,000,000.
    Unit cost = 42,000 ÷ 12,000,000 = $0.0035 per transaction (= $3.50 per 1,000).

    ✅
    Why it matters: if spend rises 20% but transactions rise 40%, unit cost falls ~14% — healthy growth, not a problem.

    📊 Core FinOps KPIs

    KPIFormulaTypical goal
    Cost per unit (CPU)Allocated cost ÷ driverFlat or declining

    Allocation coverageAllocated ÷ total cost≥ 90–95%

    BASIC How to build a unit metric (step by step)

    1. Pick a meaningful business driver (not CPU hours).
    2. Allocate cost to that product/service (tags, accounts, shared-cost rules).
    3. Join cost and driver data by time (daily/weekly).
    4. Divide, chart the trend, set a target, review monthly with product owners.

    🔬 Expert: marginal cost, margin and pitfalls

    EXPERT Average vs marginal cost

    Average unit cost includes fixed cost (databases, baseline clusters). Marginal cost is the cost of the next unit — usually lower at scale.

    🧮 Example — fixed vs variable

    Fixed platform = $20,000/mo; variable = $0.001 per transaction. At 10M tx: (20,000 + 10,000) ÷ 10M = $0.0030. At 20M tx: (20,000 + 20,000) ÷ 20M = $0.0020. The marginal cost stays $0.001 — scale improves unit cost.

    EXPERT Unit margin per tenant

    Tenant revenue $5,000/mo, allocated cloud cost $1,200 → cloud margin 76%. Identify tenants with <50% margin for repricing or optimization.

    • Targets: set a “unit cost budget” per release; use anomaly detection on unit cost, not just total cost.
    • Include all costs: amortized commitments, data transfer, shared platform, support.
    • Avoid vanity drivers that don’t correlate with cost (e.g. page views for a batch workload).
    • Normalize for price changes (rate vs usage variance) when explaining movement.
    ⚠️
    Unit economics depends on trustworthy allocation. If 30% of cost is unallocated, your unit costs are understated.

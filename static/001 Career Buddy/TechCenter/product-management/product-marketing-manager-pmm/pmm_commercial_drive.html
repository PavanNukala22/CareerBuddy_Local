    Module 01

    Commercial Drive for Growth Product Managers

    A tight focus on customer acquisition cost (CAC), lifetime value (LTV), activation velocity and payback periods: the unit economics that decide whether growth is profitable and fundable.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    Commercial Drive turns a Growth PM from a metrics optimizer into a business owner. Every test, channel and onboarding change is tied to CAC, LTV, activation and payback so growth is both fast and profitable.

    💵
    CAC
    What it costs to win a customer.
    📈
    LTV
    What a customer is worth.
    ⚡
    Activation velocity
    How fast users reach value.
    ⏱️
    Payback
    How fast cash returns.
    💡
    Growth that loses money on every customer is not growth. Commercial Drive means every initiative is judged by unit economics.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Compute blended, paid and fully loaded CAC by channel.
    2. Calculate LTV with margin and churn, and read cohort LTV.
    3. Use LTV:CAC and payback period to judge channels.
    4. Measure and improve activation velocity with financial impact.
    5. Allocate budget using marginal, not average, returns.
    6. Communicate unit economics to finance and leadership.
    7. Avoid pitfalls: vanity CAC, optimistic LTV and ignoring cash.

    Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

    Blended, paid and fully loaded CAC, channel-level CAC, attribution caveats, trends and benchmarks.

    💵 Basic: Computing CAC

    textCAC = Sales and marketing spend / New customers acquired

Quarter: S&M spend $120,000; new customers 800
Blended CAC = 120,000 / 800 = $150

Paid-only CAC = paid media spend / customers from paid channels
Fully loaded CAC = media + salaries + tools + agency + overhead for S&M

    Module 03

    Lifetime Value (LTV) and LTV:CAC

    LTV formulas, margin and churn, cohort-based LTV, discounting, segment LTV and the LTV:CAC ratio.

    📈 LTV Formulas

    textLTV = ARPA x Gross margin / Churn rate (simple, steady churn)

ARPA $50/month; gross margin 80%; monthly churn 4%
  Monthly gross profit per customer = 50 x 0.80 = $40
  Expected lifetime = 1 / 0.04 = 25 months
  LTV = 40 / 0.04 = $1,000

LTV:CAC = 1,000 / 150 = 6.7 : 1
    MethodProsCons
    Simple formula (ARPA x margin / churn)Quick, intuitiveAssumes constant churn and no expansion
    Cohort-based cumulative marginUses real behaviorNeeds history; truncation
    Predictive (probabilistic) LTVEarly estimatesModel risk
    Discounted LTVAccounts for time valueNeeds discount rate
    Figure 3.1 · LTV and LTV:CAC

    ⚖️ Intermediate: LTV by Channel and Segment

    ChannelCACLTVLTV:CACVerdict
    Paid search$250$9003.6Healthy; scale carefully
    Paid social$300$6002.0Below 3:1; optimize
    Referral$50$1,30026Scale (limited capacity)
    Content / SEO$100$1,00010Invest; slow to build
    💡
    LTV:CAC of 3:1 is a common benchmark. Much higher can mean under-investing; below 1:1 is losing money.
    Figure 3.2 · Levers to raise LTV

    🏆 Advanced: Cohort LTV and Caution

    PracticeReason
    Use cohort LTV for decisionsReal behavior over time
    Cap the lifetime (e.g., 3 to 5 years)Avoid fantasy tails
    Use gross margin not revenueProfit-based
    Segment LTVAverages hide differences
    Early LTV proxiesPredict from first weeks of behavior
    Discount future cashTime value of money
    textCAUTION: churn sensitivity
Same ARPA $50 and margin 80%:
  Churn 4%  -> LTV $1,000
  Churn 6%  -> LTV $667
  Churn 2%  -> LTV $2,000
Small churn changes move LTV a lot, so measure churn carefully.

    Module 04

    Payback Periods and Unit Economics

    CAC payback, cash payback, contribution margin, annual vs monthly plans, and growth funding implications.

    ⏱️ Payback Basics

    textCAC payback (months) = CAC / (ARPA x Gross margin)

CAC $150; ARPA $50; margin 80% -> monthly gross profit $40
Payback = 150 / 40 = 3.75 months

Paid social: CAC $300 / $40 = 7.5 months
If churn is 4% per month, some customers leave before payback: cohort payback is slightly longer.
    Payback band (SaaS-style)Interpretation
    Under 6 monthsExcellent; aggressive scaling possible
    6 to 12 monthsGood for many B2B SaaS
    12 to 18 monthsAcceptable with strong retention and funding
    Over 18 monthsRisky; capital intensive
    Figure 4.1 · Payback timeline
    ⏱️
    Payback
    Months to recover CAC.
    💰
    Cash
    Annual prepay speeds it.
    📊
    Margin
    Profit not revenue.
    🚀
    Reinvest
    Faster cycles compound.

    💳 Intermediate: Cash and Plan Structure

    StructureEffect on payback
    Annual prepayCash collected upfront; payback can be immediate
    Monthly billingGradual recovery
    DiscountsLower early margin; longer payback
    Free trial costsRaise CAC through unconverted costs
    Expansion revenueShortens effective payback
    textANNUAL PREPAY EXAMPLE
CAC $150. Annual plan $480 collected upfront, gross margin 80% -> $384 gross profit upfront
Cash payback is immediate (cash positive on day 1).
Monthly plan: 3.75 months to recover.
Strategy: incentivize annual plans if retention risk is acceptable.

    Module 06

    Budget Allocation and Marginal Returns

    Average vs marginal CAC, saturation, portfolio allocation, scenario modeling and guardrails.

    🎯 Marginal CAC

    textPAID SEARCH SATURATION
Spend $50,000 -> 200 customers (average CAC $250)
Spend $70,000 -> 240 customers (average CAC $291.67)
Marginal CAC = extra spend / extra customers = 20,000 / 40 = $500

If LTV is $900 and the target LTV:CAC is 3:1, max CAC = $300.
The marginal $500 fails the target -> do not raise search spend; shift to referral or content.
    RuleWhy
    Allocate by marginal CAC, not averageLast dollar matters
    Set max CAC from LTV and paybackGuardrail
    Cap spend in saturating channelsDiminishing returns
    Keep an experiment budgetFind new channels
    Reforecast monthlyPrices and conversion shift

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked in Growth Product Manager interviews about unit economics, grouped by level. Draft your answer, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.How do you calculate CAC?
    Sales and marketing spend divided by new customers; report blended, paid and fully loaded, and by channel, adjusting for lag and attribution.
    IQ2.How do you calculate LTV?
    ARPA x gross margin / churn for a quick estimate; use cohort-based LTV for decisions, cap horizon, segment and discount cash flows.
    IQ3.What is a good LTV:CAC and payback?
    About 3:1 or higher and payback under 12 months for many SaaS businesses; context such as funding and retention matters.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.Why use marginal rather than average CAC?
    The next dollar often buys customers at a higher cost than the average; allocation should follow marginal returns.
    IQ5.How do you improve CAC?
    Raise conversion and activation, improve targeting and creative, shift mix toward efficient channels and shorten sales cycles.
    IQ6.How does activation velocity affect economics?
    Faster and higher activation converts more signups at the same spend, lowering CAC and shortening payback.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.A channel has low CAC but poor retention. What do you do?
    Compare LTV and payback by cohort, diagnose targeting and onboarding fit, adjust and cap spend until economics work.
    IQ8.How do you explain unit economics to finance?
    Share definitions, reconcile to the ledger, show cohort curves and ranges, and state assumptions.
    IQ9.How do annual plans affect payback?
    Upfront cash shortens cash payback, with retention and discount trade-offs to evaluate.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you decide spend ceilings?
    Set max CAC from LTV and the target ratio plus payback limits, then check marginal CAC against it.
    IQ11.How sensitive is LTV to churn?
    Very: with constant margin, LTV is inversely proportional to churn, so small churn changes move LTV substantially.
    IQ12.Tell me about a time you used unit economics to change a decision.
    Describe the metrics, analysis, trade-off, decision and result in dollars.

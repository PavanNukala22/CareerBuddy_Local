    Module 01

    Portfolio Strategy for Directors of Product Management

    Multi-product portfolio allocation, long-term resource budgeting and aligning product vision to company enterprise strategy: deciding where the company places its biggest product bets.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    Portfolio strategy lifts a Director above single-product thinking. You allocate people and money across products, tie them to enterprise strategy, and make the hard calls to invest, harvest or retire.

    🗂️
    Portfolio
    See every product as an investment.
    💰
    Budgeting
    Capacity and long-term funding.
    🧭
    Strategy
    Vision tied to enterprise goals.
    ⚖️
    Trade-offs
    Invest, harvest, sunset.
    💡
    A Director's core job is allocation: where do scarce people and dollars create the most enterprise value?

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Classify products and balance the portfolio across growth stages.
    2. Allocate resources across products with transparent criteria.
    3. Build multi-year product budgets and capacity plans.
    4. Cascade enterprise strategy into product vision, OKRs and roadmaps.
    5. Evaluate investments with NPV, payback and strategic fit.
    6. Decide when to invest, maintain, harvest or sunset a product.
    7. Run portfolio reviews and communicate trade-offs to executives.

    💰 Capacity and Headcount Allocation

    textCAPACITY MODEL: 100 product engineers
Effective capacity = 100 x (1 - 15% leave/overhead/attrition) = 85 engineer-equivalents
Allocation:
  Product A (maintain, 25%): 21
  Product B (invest, 30%): 26
  Product C (bet, 15%): 13
  Product E (platform, 20%): 17
  Product D (harvest, 5%): 4
  Reserve / incidents (5%): 4  (sums 85 after rounding: 21+26+13+17+4+4 = 85)
    PrinciplePractice
    Allocate by strategy, not historyLast year is not the baseline for growth bets
    Protect a reserve5% for incidents and urgent opportunities
    Stable teamsAvoid constant reshuffling
    Explicit trade-offsShow what each increase costs elsewhere
    Review quarterlyRebalance with evidence

    Module 05

    Investment Decisions: NPV, Payback and Kill/Sunset

    Business cases, NPV and payback, stage-gated investments, build/buy/partner, harvest and sunset decisions.

    📈 Investment Case Math

    textNPV EXAMPLE (discount rate 10%)
Invest $2.0M at year 0. Cash flows: Y1 $0.8M, Y2 $1.0M, Y3 $1.2M
PV Y1 = 0.8 / 1.10 = 0.727
PV Y2 = 1.0 / 1.21 = 0.826
PV Y3 = 1.2 / 1.331 = 0.902
Sum PV = 2.455 -> NPV = 2.455 - 2.000 = $0.455M (positive: value-creating)
Payback: cumulative 0.8, 1.8, then 0.2 of Y3 needed (0.2 / 1.2 = 0.17) -> 2.17 years
    MetricFormulaUse
    NPVSum of discounted cash flows minus investmentValue of the investment
    PaybackTime to recover investmentLiquidity and risk
    IRRDiscount rate where NPV = 0Compare projects
    ROINet return / investmentSimple comparison
    Strategic valueOption value, defense, platformNot captured in NPV

    Figure 5.2 · Layers of investment decisions

    🔀 Build, Buy or Partner

    OptionBest whenRisks
    BuildCore differentiator; unique needTime, talent, cost
    Buy (acquire)Speed to market; capability or customersIntegration, price, culture
    PartnerNon-core; ecosystem valueDependence, margin share
    LicenseCommodity capabilityLock-in
    textBUILD VS PARTNER (3-year view)
Build: $3.0M cost, 14 months to launch, full margin
Partner: $0.6M/year ($1.8M over 3 years), 3 months to launch, 80% margin retention
If 11 months earlier launch yields $1.1M extra gross profit, partner may win early; revisit build when volume justifies it.

    🧯 Harvest and Sunset Decisions

    OptionWhenActions
    InvestHigh growth, strong positionIncrease funding
    MaintainStable, profitableHold investment; optimize
    HarvestDeclining but profitableCut investment; maximize cash
    SunsetDeclining, unprofitable or off-strategyMigrate customers; retire; reassign staff
    textSUNSET ANALYSIS: Product D
Revenue $4.0M; direct costs $3.4M -> contribution $0.6M
Team: 12 engineers (~$2.4M loaded cost already inside direct costs); reassigning them to Product B could add an estimated $5M revenue.
Migration: 60% of D customers (est.) move to A or B (retain ~$2.4M). Sunset timeline 12 months, with notice and migration support.
Decision: sunset; net benefit = redeployed value minus lost contribution and migration cost.

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked in Director of Product interviews about portfolio strategy, grouped by level. Draft your answer, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.How do you allocate resources across products?
    By strategy and evidence using criteria, horizons and capacity, with a reserve and quarterly rebalancing.
    IQ2.How do you balance the portfolio?
    Use Three Horizons and growth-share to balance core, adjacent and transformational work and manage concentration.
    IQ3.How do you align product vision with enterprise strategy?
    Cascade themes to product vision and OKRs with traceability and regular review.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you evaluate an investment?
    NPV, payback, sensitivity and strategic fit, with stage gates.
    IQ5.How do you decide to sunset a product?
    Assess growth, margin, strategy fit and opportunity cost, then plan migration and communication.
    IQ6.Build, buy or partner?
    Build core differentiators, buy for speed or capability, partner for non-core needs.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you budget for the long term?
    Multi-year plans by horizon with scenarios and stage-gated funding.
    IQ8.How do you handle a CEO pet project?
    Reframe as a staged bet with evidence milestones and capped investment.
    IQ9.How do you communicate cuts?
    Share criteria, brief affected leaders first, use decision records and offer a path forward.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.What metrics do you track at portfolio level?
    Financial, customer, strategic, delivery, innovation and risk metrics.
    IQ11.How do you manage concentration risk?
    Monitor product and customer concentration and fund diversification.
    IQ12.Tell me about a tough portfolio decision.
    Describe options, analysis, decision, communication and result.

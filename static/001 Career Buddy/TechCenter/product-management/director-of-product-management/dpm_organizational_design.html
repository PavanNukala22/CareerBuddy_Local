    Module 01

    Organizational Design for Directors of Product Management

    Structuring product pods and tribes, establishing company-wide discovery and delivery standards, and managing organizational risk as the product organization scales.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    Organizational design turns strategy into structure. The right pods, clear ownership and light but real standards let hundreds of people move as one product organization.

    🧩
    Pods and tribes
    Structure for autonomy.
    📐
    Standards
    Discovery and delivery.
    ⚠️
    Risk management
    Org and delivery risk.
    🔄
    Evolution
    Reorgs done well.
    💡
    Organizational design is the practice of shaping teams, ownership and standards so the right work gets done with minimal friction.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Choose and design team topologies: pods, squads, tribes and platform teams.
    2. Define clear ownership, boundaries and interfaces between teams.
    3. Establish company-wide discovery and delivery standards that teams actually adopt.
    4. Plan and execute reorganizations with minimal disruption.
    5. Identify and manage organizational and delivery risk.
    6. Measure organizational health and effectiveness.
    7. Scale the product organization from tens to hundreds of people.

Does not own: pricing engine (Pricing pod), identity (Platform)

    📏 Intermediate: Ownership and Span of Control

    PrinciplePractice
    Outcome ownershipEach pod owns a measurable customer or business outcome
    Clear boundariesOne owner per component, journey or metric
    Minimize dependenciesAlign pods to value streams; reduce handoffs

Number of pods: 60 people / ~8 per pod = 7.5 -> plan 7 to 8 pods

    When to reorganize, designing the new structure, change communication, transition planning, scaling stages and avoiding reorg fatigue.

    🔄 When and How to Reorganize

    TriggerExample
    Strategy shiftNew market or business priority
    GrowthTeams too large; coordination breakdown
    Dependency overloadMost work needs 3+ teams
    Ownership gapsAreas with no owner
    Performance issuesPersistent delivery or quality problems
    AcquisitionMerging teams and products
    StepPractice
    1 DiagnoseIdentify the real problem; ask if structure is the cause
    2 Design principlesState goals and constraints
    3 OptionsEvaluate 2 to 3 structures
    4 DecideLeadership alignment; consult HR and Legal
    5 CommunicateClear why, what changes, what stays
    6 TransitionPlan handovers; protect delivery
    7 ReviewMeasure at 90 days; adjust

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked in Director of Product interviews about organizational design, grouped by level. Draft your answer, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.What is a pod?
    A small cross-functional team of PM, design and engineering owning a measurable outcome.
    IQ2.What is the role of a tribe?
    Group of related pods sharing a domain, providing coordination and shared priorities.
    IQ3.What is Conway's Law?
    Systems mirror the communication structure of the organization, so design teams to match the desired architecture.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you size teams and spans?
    Pods of 5 to 9; managers with 5 to 8 reports, adjusted for seniority and complexity.
    IQ5.How do you decide on a platform team?
    When many teams need the same capability; provide self-service, clear intake and SLAs.
    IQ6.How do you establish discovery standards?
    Customer contact cadence, opportunity trees, assumption tests and a discovery gate, co-created and kept light.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you make standards stick?
    Co-create, keep few, provide templates and tools, coach, and measure adoption.
    IQ8.When do you reorganize?
    When structure is the real problem: strategy shift, dependency overload, ownership gaps; not as a reflex.
    IQ9.How do you communicate a reorg?
    Why, what changes, what stays, timing, support and a 90-day review.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you manage org risk?
    Risk register with scoring, owners, mitigations and review cadence.
    IQ11.How do you measure org health?
    Cycle time, predictability, dependencies, eNPS, attrition and standards adoption.
    IQ12.Tell me about a structure you changed.
    Describe the problem, options, decision, communication and measured results.

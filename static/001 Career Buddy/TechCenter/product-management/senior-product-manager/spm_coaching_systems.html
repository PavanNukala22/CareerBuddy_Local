    Module 01

    Coaching & Systems for Senior Product Managers

    How senior PMs multiply their impact: mentoring junior PMs and improving the organisation's product discovery and delivery frameworks.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    Coaching and systems is how a Senior Product Manager (Senior PM) scales. You grow junior PMs and improve how the whole organisation discovers problems and delivers solutions, so quality does not depend on a few heroes.

    🧑‍🏫
    Mentoring
    Grow junior PMs through coaching, feedback and stretch work.
    🔍
    Discovery systems
    Make customer learning a team habit with tools and standards.
    📦
    Delivery systems
    Improve flow, quality and predictability across teams.
    🔄
    Change leadership
    Introduce better ways of working that stick.
    💡
    A senior PM's impact is measured by how much better the people and systems around them become, not only by their own output.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Coach junior PMs using questions, feedback and deliberate practice.
    2. Set growth plans with clear skills, stretch assignments and checkpoints.
    3. Give feedback that is specific, timely and actionable.
    4. Assess and improve the organisation's discovery practices.
    5. Assess and improve delivery flow, quality and predictability.
    6. Design a product operating model with lightweight standards and rituals.
    7. Lead change and measure whether the system is actually improving.

    Real-World Scenarios & Troubleshooting

    Case studies: a junior PM who freezes, a team resisting new standards, a hero culture and a mentor who does too much.

    😰 Case Study: The Junior PM Who Freezes

    A junior PM avoids decisions and escalates everything.

    Figure 8.1 · Building decision confidence
    ✅
    Outcome: after weekly "recommend then decide" practice, the PM began making reversible decisions alone and escalated only irreversible ones.

    🧱 Troubleshooting: A Team Resists New Standards

    1. Ask what the standard would cost them and what concern they have.
    2. Show evidence of benefit; start with the least burdensome piece.
    3. Pilot with volunteers; publish results.
    4. Adapt the standard; keep the non-negotiables small.
    Figure 8.2 · Responding to resistance

    🦸 Troubleshooting: A Hero Culture

    SymptomCauseFix
    Everything escalates to one personUnclear decision rightsDACI and delegation
    Late-night rescues celebratedReward for firefightingRecognise prevention
    Knowledge in few headsNo documentationPlaybooks, pairing, repository
    BurnoutOverloadWIP limits, capacity planning

    🎒 Trade-off Scenario: Mentor Time vs Own Delivery

    Mentoring takes 3 hours a week while your roadmap is full. Make the cost visible to your manager, agree it as part of your role, group coaching into a weekly small-group session, and use review of their work instead of doing it. Track mentees' outcomes as part of your impact.

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked in Senior PM interviews about coaching and systems, grouped by level. Draft your answer, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.How do you mentor a junior PM?
    Agree goals, hold regular 1:1s focused on their agenda, coach with questions, give specific feedback, assign stretch work with support, and review progress against evidence.
    IQ2.What is the difference between coaching and mentoring?
    Coaching asks questions to help someone find their own answers; mentoring shares experience and advice. I start with coaching and mentor when they need context or are stuck.
    IQ3.How do you give feedback?
    Promptly, privately and specifically using Situation–Behaviour–Impact, then ask for their view and agree a next step. I also give positive feedback so strengths are reinforced.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you assess discovery in a team?
    Look for weekly customer contact, problem framing, assumption tests, evidence in decisions and shared insights. Use a scorecard with evidence and compare with outcomes such as launch success.
    IQ5.How would you improve a team's delivery?
    Measure cycle time, throughput and quality, find the bottleneck, change one thing at a time (smaller stories, DoR/DoD, WIP limits), run for a few sprints, compare and standardise what works.
    IQ6.How do you handle a junior PM who makes a big mistake?
    Stay calm, protect the customer and team, help fix the issue, run a blameless review to learn, and rebuild confidence with support and a smaller next challenge.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.Tell me about a time you improved a process.
    Describe the problem and evidence, how you involved the team, the pilot, the metrics, the result and how you scaled it. Show listening and measurement.
    IQ8.How do you introduce a new framework without bureaucracy?
    Start from a real pain, make the minimum standard, co-design with teams, pilot, measure value and drop what does not help.
    IQ9.How do you develop other mentors?
    Share coaching models, pair them in 1:1s, review their sessions, create a mentoring circle and recognise mentoring in performance conversations.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you balance coaching with your own delivery?
    Make mentoring part of my role with my manager, schedule it, use group sessions and reviews, delegate appropriately and measure mentees' growth as part of my impact.
    IQ11.What does a healthy product operating model look like?
    Clear roles and decision rights, goals linked to strategy, regular planning and review rituals, lightweight standards for discovery and delivery, and metrics of system health.
    IQ12.How do you know a system improvement worked?
    Track adoption and outcome metrics against a baseline, gather team feedback, check for gaming or side effects, and decide to scale, adapt or stop.

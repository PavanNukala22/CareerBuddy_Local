    Module 01

    Stakeholder Management for Senior Product Managers

    How senior PMs influence executives, resolve conflicts between business units and run customer advisory boards to steer strategy.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    Stakeholder management at senior level is a core product skill. A Senior Product Manager (Senior PM) must win executive sponsorship, reconcile business units with opposing goals and keep customers close through advisory boards — all without direct authority.

    🏛️
    Executive influencing
    Win support with evidence, framing and trust at the top.
    ⚔️
    Conflict resolution
    Resolve competing business-unit demands with clear decisions.
    🤝
    Customer advisory boards
    Create a trusted customer forum that informs strategy.
    🗣️
    Executive communication
    Short, decision-ready narratives.
    💡
    Senior influence is earned trust plus clear framing: executives back the PM who understands their goals, brings evidence and offers decisions, not problems.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Map executive stakeholders, their goals, constraints and decision styles.
    2. Frame proposals in terms of executive priorities and risks.
    3. Resolve conflicts across business units using interests, criteria and decision rights.
    4. Design, launch and run a customer advisory board (CAB).
    5. Write and present executive narratives and decision memos.
    6. Negotiate and escalate effectively, and keep relationships intact.
    7. Build an operating rhythm that keeps stakeholders aligned.

    🧭 Intermediate: Frame, Evidence, Ask

    Figure 2.1 · Executive pitch structure
    textDECISION MEMO

    Module 04

    Customer Advisory Boards

    Designing and running a customer advisory board: purpose, membership, agenda, confidentiality, feedback loops and measuring value.

    🤝 What a CAB Is For

    A Customer Advisory Board (CAB) is a small group of strategic customers who meet regularly to advise on strategy, roadmap and market trends. It is not a support channel or a sales event.

    PurposeExample
    Strategic inputTest the 12-month direction with leaders
    Early validationPreview concepts and pricing
    Peer learningCustomers share best practice
    AdvocacyReference stories and co-innovation
    Risk signalsHear about market shifts and competitors early
    Figure 4.1 · Running a CAB

    📋 Design: Charter, Members and Agenda

    ElementGuidance
    CharterPurpose, scope, term (1–2 years), confidentiality, what is not covered
    MembersStrategic, diverse by segment, region and size; decision-level seniority
    Size10–15 customers plus your leadership
    CadenceQuarterly or semi-annual; one in-person per year
    AgendaStrategy preview, customer talks, roadmap discussion, open forum
    PreparationPre-read and pre-interviews to focus the day
    textCAB MEETING AGENDA (Half day)
09:00 Welcome and updates since last meeting (what we did with your feedback)
09:30 Market trends: customer leaders share (3 × 15 min)
10:30 Strategy preview: three bets we are considering
11:30 Breakouts: reactions and risks by theme
12:30 Readout and priorities vote
13:00 Next steps and commitments
    Figure 4.2 · CAB structure

    🔁 Advanced: Closing the Loop and Avoiding Pitfalls

    ✅ Best practices

    "You said, we did" updates; clear purpose; mix of segments; honest about what you will not build; follow-up in two weeks; measure value for members and company.

    ❌ Common mistakes

    Using it as a sales pitch; only friendly customers; promising features; no follow-up; dominated by one loud member; no exit for inactive members.

    Real-World Scenarios & Troubleshooting

    Case studies: a sponsor loses interest, two VPs fight over one roadmap slot, a CAB member dominates, and bad news for the board.

    😶 Case Study: The Sponsor Loses Interest

    The executive sponsor stops attending reviews and funding is at risk.

    Figure 8.1 · Re-engaging a sponsor
    ✅
    Outcome: short monthly updates in the sponsor's metrics, a quick win in 4 weeks and a second sponsor in finance restored funding.

    ⚔️ Troubleshooting: Two VPs, One Roadmap Slot

    1. Meet each VP; capture interests and metrics.
    2. Build a shared scorecard with agreed criteria.
    3. Score both requests; show capacity.
    4. Offer options (swap, stage, partial).
    5. Decide with the decision owner; record.
    Figure 8.2 · Resolving a two-VP conflict

    🎙️ Troubleshooting: A CAB Member Dominates

    SituationResponse
    One member talks too muchUse structured rounds and time limits; invite others by name
    A member pushes a custom requestAcknowledge; separate account issues; take offline
    Low engagementPre-interview; adjust agenda; refresh membership
    Feedback conflictsExplore by segment; use data to weigh

    📉 Trade-off Scenario: Bad News for the Board

    A flagship launch will miss its date. Tell the CEO first, then the board with the headline, impact, cause, options and recommended plan. Provide a revised date range, mitigation and the next update date; avoid surprises and blame.

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked in Senior PM interviews about stakeholder management, grouped by level. Draft your answer, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.How do you influence executives?
    Understand their goals and constraints, frame proposals in their terms with evidence, pre-wire, bring options and a recommendation, and follow through on commitments to build trust.
    IQ2.What is pre-wiring?
    Talking to key stakeholders individually before a decision meeting to hear concerns, adjust the proposal and avoid surprises, so the meeting confirms a decision rather than starts a debate.
    IQ3.What is a decision memo?
    A short document asking for a decision: headline ask, why now, evidence, options with trade-offs, recommendation, risks and next steps. It respects executives' time.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you resolve conflict between two business units?
    Meet each separately to learn interests, agree on a shared goal and criteria, offer options, decide with the accountable owner using decision rights, and record and communicate the decision.
    IQ5.How do you handle an executive who dismisses your proposal?
    Seek to understand the objection, acknowledge it, show evidence and a lower-risk option such as a pilot, and follow up 1:1 rather than arguing in public.
    IQ6.What is a customer advisory board and why run one?
    A forum of strategic customers who advise on direction. It validates strategy, surfaces market trends, builds loyalty and creates advocates — if run with a clear charter and follow-up.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you decide who joins a CAB?
    Select strategic, diverse customers by segment, size and region, with decision-level seniority and willingness to contribute, plus a mix of supporters and constructive critics.
    IQ8.Tell me about a time you changed an executive's mind.
    Describe the context and objection, how you learned their interests, the evidence and options you brought, how you pre-wired, the result and what you learned.
    IQ9.How do you say no to a senior leader?
    Acknowledge the need, explain criteria and trade-offs with data, offer alternatives or a date to revisit, and follow up in writing, keeping the relationship intact.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you deliver bad news to executives?
    Tell them early, start with the headline, share impact, cause, options and recommendation, and give a clear date for the next update.
    IQ11.How do you manage conflicting feedback from customers on a CAB?
    Segment the feedback, look for patterns and underlying jobs, weigh with data and strategy, explain decisions transparently and avoid promising features.
    IQ12.How do you build an operating rhythm for alignment?
    Define forums with purpose and cadence (weekly review, BU sync, exec update, quarterly strategy review), assign decision rights and keep a decision log, so alignment is routine.

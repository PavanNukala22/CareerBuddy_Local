    Module 01

    Business Acumen for Senior Product Managers

    How senior PMs think like owners: reading a P&L, modelling unit economics, setting prices and building business cases that win investment.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    Business acumen lets a Senior Product Manager (Senior PM) judge ideas by their economic effect. You must read a P&L, model unit economics, price confidently and make business cases that earn investment — and then be accountable for results.

    📒
    P&L understanding
    Know how revenue, costs and margin flow to profit.
    🧮
    Unit economics
    CAC, LTV, payback and contribution margin per customer.
    🏷️
    Pricing strategy
    Price to capture value, segment customers and protect margin.
    📈
    Business cases
    Model investment, benefit, risk and return for decisions.
    💡
    Business acumen means every product decision is also a financial decision: what it costs, who pays, how much, and what return it earns.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Read a P&L and explain how product decisions affect each line.
    2. Calculate unit economics: CAC, LTV, LTV/CAC, payback and contribution margin.
    3. Design pricing and packaging using value, competition and segments.
    4. Build a business case with assumptions, scenarios, NPV and payback.
    5. Use sensitivity and scenario analysis to show risk.
    6. Connect product metrics to financial outcomes and budgets.
    7. Present financial reasoning to executives and finance partners.

    Module 03

    Unit Economics

    CAC, LTV, contribution margin and payback: modelling the economics of one customer and finding the levers that make growth profitable.

    🧮 Basic: The Key Metrics

    MetricFormulaExample
    ARPAAverage revenue per account per month$100
    Gross margin(ARPA − cost to serve) ÷ ARPA80%
    Contribution per monthARPA × gross margin$80
    Monthly churnCustomers lost ÷ start-of-month customers4%
    Lifetime1 ÷ churn25 months
    LTVContribution ÷ churn$80 ÷ 0.04 = $2,000
    CACSales & marketing spend ÷ new customers$600
    LTV/CACLTV ÷ CAC3.3
    PaybackCAC ÷ monthly contribution$600 ÷ $80 = 7.5 months
    Figure 3.1 · Unit economics of a subscription customer

    🎚️ Intermediate: Sensitivity — Which Lever Matters?

    ChangeNew LTVNew LTV/CACEffect
    Churn 4% → 3%$80 ÷ 0.03 = $2,6674.4Large gain
    ARPA $100 → $110 (same cost)$88 ÷ 0.04 = $2,2003.7Moderate gain
    CAC $600 → $500$2,0004.0Moderate gain
    Gross margin 80% → 85%$85 ÷ 0.04 = $2,1253.5Small gain
    Figure 3.2 · Levers on unit economics
    📌
    Churn is usually the highest-leverage input because it affects lifetime. Retention work often beats acquisition work.

    🏆 Advanced: Cohorts, Channels and Benchmarks

    TopicPractice
    By channelCompute CAC and LTV per channel; paid users often churn faster
    By segmentEnterprise vs SMB have different economics
    Cohort LTVUse actual cohort retention rather than a single churn rate
    Blended vs paid CACReport both; avoid hiding paid inefficiency
    BenchmarksLTV/CAC ≈ 3 and payback < 12 months are common guides

    Business Case Modeling

    Building a business case: assumptions, costs and benefits, NPV, ROI, payback, scenarios, sensitivity and presenting the decision.

    📈 The Structure of a Business Case

    Figure 5.1 · Business case build
    SectionContent
    SummaryDecision requested, amount, return, risk
    Problem / opportunityCustomer and business evidence
    OptionsRecommended option vs alternatives, including do nothing
    FinancialsCosts, benefits, timeline
    Risks & mitigationsWhat could go wrong
    Plan & gatesMilestones and decision points

    🧮 A Worked Example

    textINVESTMENT: $600k (Year 0)
Benefits: Y1 $200k, Y2 $400k, Y3 $500k   Discount rate 10%

PV Y1 = 200 / 1.10   = 181.8
PV Y2 = 400 / 1.21   = 330.6
PV Y3 = 500 / 1.331  = 375.7
Sum PV = 888.1
NPV = 888.1 − 600 = +$288k
ROI (undiscounted) = (1,100 − 600) / 600 = 83%
Payback: cumulative 200, 600 → recovered at end of Year 2
    Figure 5.2 · Layers of the example case

    🎚️ Advanced: Scenarios and Sensitivity

    ScenarioAssumption changeNPV
    BaseAs above+$288k
    DownsideBenefits 30% lowerAbout +$22k (888 × 0.7 − 600)
    SevereBenefits 40% lower, 3 months lateNegative — review gates
    UpsideBenefits 20% higherAbout +$466k
    Figure 5.3 · What makes a business case credible
    ⚠️
    Never hide the assumption that drives the answer. Name it and show how the case changes if it is wrong.

    Real-World Scenarios & Troubleshooting

    Case studies: a growth plan with bad payback, a price increase backlash, a CFO challenging the case and cutting costs without hurting the product.

    💸 Case Study: Growth With Bad Payback

    Paid acquisition brings 40% more signups, but payback has stretched from 8 to 19 months.

    Figure 8.1 · Fixing unit economics
    ✅
    Outcome: two paid channels cut, onboarding improved for the rest; blended payback returned to 10 months while signups fell only 12%.

    📣 Troubleshooting: Price Increase Backlash

    1. Quantify actual churn and support impact versus forecast.
    2. Segment: who is reacting (plan, tenure, region)?
    3. Options: grandfather, phase, add value, adjust tiers.
    4. Communicate clearly and transparently.
    Figure 8.2 · Handling a price backlash

    🧐 Troubleshooting: The CFO Challenges Your Case

    ChallengeResponse
    "Where does this benefit come from?"Show the driver tree and source of each assumption
    "What if you are wrong?"Show downside scenario and gates
    "Why not do nothing?"Show baseline cost of inaction
    "Which cost is missing?"Add run costs, support, opportunity cost

    ✂️ Trade-off Scenario: Cut Costs Without Hurting the Product

    Find cost to serve by feature and customer; cut waste (unused infrastructure, low-value features, vendor overlap) before cutting capabilities that drive retention; protect the core journey; stage reductions and track churn and satisfaction as guardrails.

    Figure 10.1 · Lab workflow

    🌱 Beginner Tasks

    Task 1BeginnerCompute margins
    Revenue $8M, COGS $2M, R&D $2.5M, S&M $2M, G&A $0.7M. Compute gross margin and operating income.
    Gross profit first.
    Gross profit = 6.0M (75%). Opex = 5.2M. Operating income = 0.8M (10%).
    Task 2BeginnerCompute LTV and payback
    ARPA $50, gross margin 70%, monthly churn 5%, CAC $200. Compute LTV, LTV/CAC and payback.
    Contribution = ARPA × margin.
    Contribution $35/month. LTV = 35 ÷ 0.05 = $700. LTV/CAC = 3.5. Payback = 200 ÷ 35 ≈ 5.7 months.

    🛠️ Intermediate Tasks

    Task 3IntermediatePick the lever
    With the same inputs, compare effect on LTV of cutting churn to 4% versus raising ARPA to $55 (margin unchanged).
    Recompute LTV for each.
    Churn 4%: 35 ÷ 0.04 = $875 (+25%). ARPA $55: contribution 38.5 ÷ 0.05 = $770 (+10%). Churn reduction is the bigger lever here.
    Task 4IntermediateDesign pricing tiers
    Design three tiers for a project-management tool with a value metric and one fence.
    Match segments.
    Free (1 project), Team ($12 per seat; unlimited projects), Business ($24 per seat; SSO, audit logs, priority support). Value metric: seats. Fence: SSO and audit logs for enterprise buyers.

    🚀 Advanced Tasks

    Task 5AdvancedEvaluate a price increase
    Raise price from $40 to $44. You expect 6% fewer customers from 2,000. Compute revenue change and what to monitor.
    Compare monthly revenue.
    Before: 40×2,000 = $80,000. After: 44×1,880 = $82,720 (+3.4%). Monitor churn after 3 months, support tickets, conversion for new customers, segment-level reaction and margin.
    Task 6AdvancedCompute NPV
    Invest $400k. Benefits: Y1 $150k, Y2 $250k, Y3 $300k. Discount 10%. Compute NPV and payback.
    Discount each year.
    PV = 150/1.1 + 250/1.21 + 300/1.331 = 136.4 + 206.6 + 225.4 = 568.4. NPV = +168.4k. Cumulative: 150, 400 → payback at end of year 2.

    🏆 Expert Tasks

    Task 7ExpertStress-test the case
    For the case above, benefits come 30% lower. What happens and what do you recommend?
    Scale PV.
    PV = 568.4 × 0.7 = 397.9; NPV ≈ −2k, about break-even. Recommend a staged investment with a gate after year 1 benefits and cost controls, or improve benefit certainty with a pilot.
    Task 8ExpertDefend to the CFO
    The CFO says your $1M feature may not move revenue. Write your answer in 5 lines.
    Driver, evidence, scenario, gate.
    Driver: conversion +1.5 points on 200k visitors/month × $60 AOV. Evidence: prototype test and pilot lift of 1.2 points. Downside: +0.8 points still gives positive NPV. Gate: release to 20% and review after 6 weeks. Ask: approve $300k stage 1.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.What is a P&L and how does product affect it?
    A statement of revenue, costs and profit over a period. Product affects revenue through acquisition, conversion, retention and pricing; COGS through cost to serve; and opex through R&D and go-to-market efficiency.
    IQ2.What are CAC and LTV?
    CAC is the cost to acquire a customer; LTV is the margin a customer generates over their lifetime. LTV/CAC around 3 and payback under about 12 months are common guides for healthy growth.
    IQ3.What is gross margin?
    Revenue minus the direct cost of delivering the product, as a share of revenue. It shows the profitability of the product before operating costs.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How would you price a new product?
    Understand customers and value, choose a value metric, research willingness to pay, check competitor and cost floors, design tiers and fences, and test with guardrails.
    IQ5.How do you decide between raising price and acquiring more customers?
    Model both with unit economics: price effect on conversion and churn versus cost of acquisition. Test with experiments and choose the one with better margin-adjusted return and strategic fit.
    IQ6.What is in a good business case?
    Problem and options including do nothing, sourced assumptions, costs and benefits over time, NPV or payback, scenarios and sensitivity, risks, gates and an owner who tracks results.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.What is NPV?
    The present value of future cash flows minus the initial investment, using a discount rate. A positive NPV means the investment earns more than the required return.
    IQ8.Tell me about a time you used financial analysis to change a decision.
    Describe the decision, the model, the key assumption, what the numbers showed, how you presented it and the outcome. Highlight the trade-offs and what you learned.
    IQ9.How do you handle a CFO challenging your numbers?
    Show the driver tree and sources, run sensitivity and downside scenarios, invite specific challenges, adjust and agree on gates and tracking. Treat finance as a partner.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you evaluate a build versus buy decision?
    Compare total cost of ownership over 3 years, time to value, strategic differentiation, risk and lock-in. Build core differentiators and buy or partner for commodities.
    IQ11.How do you grow revenue without growing cost proportionally?
    Improve retention and expansion, raise conversion, optimise pricing and packaging, automate cost to serve and focus acquisition on efficient channels.
    IQ12.How do you take ownership of a product P&L?
    Get contribution margin by product, agree drivers with finance, link roadmap bets to P&L lines, review variance monthly, and make visible trade-offs between growth, margin and cash.

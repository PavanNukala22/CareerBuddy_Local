    Module 01

    Leadership & Stakeholder Alignment for Product Managers

    How PMs lead without authority: stakeholder alignment, cross-functional trade-offs, roadmap communication and running Agile/Scrum rituals.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    Leadership and stakeholder alignment is how a Product Manager (PM) gets results through people they do not manage. You must align engineers, designers, sales, support and executives around goals, make trade-offs visible and keep the team's rituals healthy.

    🗺️
    Stakeholder mapping
    Know who decides, who influences and who must be informed.
    ⚖️
    Trade-offs
    Resolve conflicts between engineering, design, sales and support.
    🛣️
    Roadmap communication
    Share direction without over-promising.
    🔁
    Agile rituals
    Lead planning, stand-ups, reviews and retros that work.
    💡
    Leadership for a PM is clarity, trust and decisions: make the goal clear, build trust through consistency and decide visibly.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Map stakeholders by influence and interest and plan engagement.
    2. Run a trade-off conversation and reach a documented decision.
    3. Build and communicate an outcome-based roadmap to different audiences.
    4. Lead Scrum events: refinement, planning, stand-up, review, retrospective.
    5. Influence without authority, manage conflict and say no constructively.
    6. Make and record decisions, and align teams around a vision.
    7. Handle escalations, bad news and change with credibility.

    Module 03

    Managing Cross-Functional Trade-offs

    Handling competing demands from engineering, design, sales, support and finance: framing options, criteria, negotiation and documenting decisions.

    ⚖️ Basic: Why Trade-offs Are the Job

    Time, scope, quality, cost and risk cannot all be maximised. The PM's job is to make the trade-off visible and help the right people decide.

    ⏱️
    Time
    How fast can we ship?
    📦
    Scope
    What will be included?
    💎
    Quality
    How polished and reliable?
    ⚠️
    Risk
    What could go wrong?

    🧮 Intermediate: A Method for Trade-off Decisions

    Figure 3.1 · Trade-off decision method
    OptionBenefitCostRisk
    A: Ship on date with core onlyMeets eventFewer featuresLow
    B: Add reports, move date 3 weeksFull valueDelayMedium
    C: Ship core, reports in 2 weeksDate met and value soonTwo releasesLow

    🗣️ Intermediate: Typical Conflicts

    ConflictUnderlying interestsApproach
    Sales wants a custom feature; engineering wants focusRevenue vs roadmapEstimate revenue at risk; compare with priority score
    Design wants polish; engineering wants speedQuality vs timeDefine must-be-right vs may-be-rough
    Support wants bug fixes; PM wants featuresCustomer pain vs growthUse ticket volume and cost of delay
    Legal blocks a launchRisk vs speedUnderstand the rule; find a compliant option
    Figure 3.2 · Elements of a trade-off conversation

    🏆 Advanced: Saying No and Documenting Decisions

    textDECISION RECORD
Decision: Defer custom export for Client X to v1.1 (Nov 20).
Context: Sprint goal is checkout conversion; export requires 4 days.
Options: (1) swap checkout polish, (2) defer, (3) manual export now.
Chosen: (3) manual export this week, (2) for automation.
Trade-off: Sales gets the data on time; automation waits.
Owner: PM. Review date: Nov 15. Informed: Sales, Eng, Support

    ✅ Best practices

    Acknowledge the need; explain the criteria; offer an alternative or date; keep the door open; record the decision.

    ❌ Common mistakes

    Flat refusal; no reason; promising and then slipping; deciding in private; forgetting to inform.

    Module 04

    Roadmap Communication

    Building outcome-based roadmaps, choosing formats, tailoring to audiences, communicating changes and avoiding over-promising.

    🛣️ Basic: What a Roadmap Is

    A roadmap communicates the direction and priorities of the product over time. It is a plan of problems and outcomes, not a guaranteed list of features and dates.

    ✅ Good roadmaps

    Outcome-based themes; clear priorities; confidence levels; regularly updated; linked to strategy.

    ❌ Weak roadmaps

    Long feature lists; fixed dates months ahead; one version for all audiences; never updated; sales promises based on it.

    📐 Intermediate: Roadmap Formats

    FormatDescriptionBest for
    Now–Next–LaterThree horizons, no datesMost teams; reduces date pressure
    Theme-basedGoals grouped by themeStrategy conversations
    TimelineDated bars (Gantt)Contracts, launches with fixed dates
    Outcome roadmapOutcomes with metricsExecutive alignment
    Figure 4.1 · A Now–Next–Later roadmap

    🎤 Intermediate: Tailoring to Audiences

    AudienceThey wantShow
    ExecutivesOutcomes, risk, investmentThemes, metrics, big bets
    EngineeringContext and sequencingProblems, dependencies, tech needs
    SalesWhat is coming for customersThemes and timeframes (no promises)
    CustomersDirection and benefitsPublic highlights, no dates
    SupportChanges to prepare forRelease notes, impact on tickets

    😠 Case Study: A Stakeholder Who Feels Ignored

    The sales director complains in a leadership meeting that product ignores customers.

    Figure 8.1 · Repairing a stakeholder relationship
    ✅
    Outcome: monthly customer-request review started; sales sees scoring and status; escalations dropped.

    ⚔️ Troubleshooting: Engineering and Design Are in Conflict

    1. Meet each privately; understand concerns.
    2. Bring both together around the user goal.
    3. Generate options; test with a prototype or data.
    4. Decide, record and review after launch.
    Figure 8.2 · Resolving team conflict

    🛣️ Troubleshooting: Roadmap Under Attack

    ChallengeResponse
    "Why is my item not on the roadmap?"Show criteria and where it stands; give a path to re-score
    "Commit a date"Offer confidence ranges and what could change them
    "Add more"Show capacity and trade-off: what moves out
    "Roadmap changed again"Explain why, impact, and update log

    🔄 Trade-off Scenario: Retro Actions Never Happen

    Reduce actions to one or two, name owners, put them on the board as stories, review at the start of the next retro and celebrate completed actions. If the same problem repeats, escalate the systemic cause (capacity, dependencies).

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked in PM interviews about leadership and stakeholder alignment, grouped by level. Draft your answer, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.How can a PM lead without authority?
    By building trust, using evidence, linking asks to shared goals, making decisions transparent, helping others succeed and being clear about outcomes and next steps.
    IQ2.What is a RACI chart?
    A matrix clarifying who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed for tasks or decisions. It prevents confusion about ownership and ensures the right people are involved.
    IQ3.What is your role in Scrum events?
    In refinement I explain the why; in planning I propose the sprint goal and priorities; in stand-ups I listen and unblock; in review I frame outcomes; in retros I participate as an equal.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you prioritise competing requests from stakeholders?
    Clarify the problem and outcome behind each, score with shared criteria and data, show trade-offs and capacity, decide with the right owner, and communicate the reasoning and path forward.
    IQ5.How do you communicate a roadmap?
    Use an outcome-based format such as Now–Next–Later, tailor to each audience, show confidence levels, avoid fixed dates far ahead, and update regularly with the reasons for changes.
    IQ6.How do you say no to a stakeholder?
    Acknowledge the need, explain the criteria and trade-offs, offer an alternative or a date to revisit, and keep the relationship strong by following up.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.Tell me about a time you resolved a conflict between teams.
    Describe the situation, interests, how you listened separately, aligned on the shared goal, offered options, decided with the owner and recorded it, and the result and lesson.
    IQ8.How do you handle an executive who changes priorities frequently?
    Seek the underlying reason, show the cost of change on the roadmap, agree on a decision cadence and criteria, make trade-offs visible and keep a change log.
    IQ9.How do you run an effective retrospective?
    Set a safe tone, gather data, find system causes, choose 1–3 actions with owners, and review them at the next retro. Rotate formats and avoid blame.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you build trust with a new engineering team?
    Listen first, learn the system and constraints, share context and the why, respect focus time, keep promises, shield them from churn and give credit publicly.
    IQ11.How do you lead a team through a major change in direction?
    Explain why with data, listen to concerns, involve the team in the plan, clarify what stays and what changes, support with time and resources, show early wins and communicate progress.
    IQ12.How would you align five VPs with conflicting goals around one launch?
    Map their goals, pre-wire each, find the shared outcome, present options with trade-offs, agree decision rights (RACI) and a cadence, and document commitments.

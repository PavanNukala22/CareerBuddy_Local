    Module 01

    Process Optimization for Product Operations

    Standardizing product discovery and delivery frameworks and managing the product tool stack (Productboard, Jira, Gong) so product teams move faster with less friction.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    Process optimization is the core of Product Ops. You standardize what should be common, leave room where teams differ, manage the tools well and prove the value in time saved and quality gained.

    📐
    Frameworks
    One shared way to discover and deliver.
    🧰
    Tooling
    Productboard, Jira, Gong managed well.
    🔁
    Workflows
    Templates, rituals, handoffs.
    📈
    Adoption
    Change management that sticks.
    💡
    Product Ops removes friction. Standards exist to save PM time, not to add bureaucracy.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Standardize discovery and delivery frameworks with light templates.
    2. Design Jira workflows, fields and boards that teams actually use.
    3. Configure Productboard for insights, prioritization and roadmaps.
    4. Use Gong call intelligence as a customer evidence source.
    5. Drive adoption through change management and training.
    6. Govern tools: access, cost, integrations and data quality.
    7. Measure and continuously improve the product process.

    📝 Intermediate: Templates and Stage Gates

    textLEAN OPPORTUNITY BRIEF (1 page)
Problem: who has it, how often, how painful
Evidence: 3+ sources (interviews, support tickets, Gong calls, usage data)
Size: users affected x frequency; revenue or cost impact
Hypothesis and success metric
Risks and open questions
Recommendation: pursue / park / drop
    GateQuestionPass criteria
    Opportunity -> DiscoveryIs the problem real and big?Evidence from 3+ sources; sized
    Discovery -> DefinitionIs the solution valuable and feasible?Prototype tested; eng estimate

    Delivery -> LaunchIs it ready to release?Done criteria, launch checklist complete

    ⚖️ Intermediate: Standardize vs Flex

    StandardizeAllow variation
    Terminology and status namesMeeting cadence details
    Core artifacts and success metricsResearch methods chosen per problem
    Launch checklistTeam rituals beyond the minimum
    Roadmap format for leadershipBacklog grooming style
    Tool configuration and data fieldsLocal boards and views
    Figure 2.3 · Right level of standard

    🏆 Advanced: Framework Design Process

    StepActivity
    1 ListenInterview PMs, engineers, designers about friction
    2 MapMap current process; find waits and rework
    3 DesignDraft the minimum standard
    4 PilotTest with 1 to 2 teams for 4 to 6 weeks
    5 RefineAdjust from feedback and metrics
    6 ScaleRoll out with training and support
    textIMPACT EXAMPLE
10 PMs x 4 PRDs/month = 40 PRDs. Template and examples cut writing time from 6h to 4h.
Saved: 40 x 2h = 80 hours/month, about 0.5 FTE (at 160 h/FTE).

    Figure 3.2 · Product stack

    ⚙️ Intermediate: Jira Workflow Design

    ProblemBetter design
    14 custom statuses; nobody knows meaningSix statuses: Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, Done, Released
    Many custom fields, mostly emptyOnly fields used in decisions or reports
    Every team different issue typesShared types: Epic, Story, Bug, Task, Spike
    No link to roadmapEpics linked to Productboard features
    Weak permissionsRole-based; admin limited
    textSTANDARD WORKFLOW
Backlog -> Ready (meets definition of ready) -> In Progress -> In Review -> Done (meets definition of done) -> Released
Required fields: Epic link, Estimate, Owner, Outcome label
Definition of ready: clear acceptance criteria, design attached, dependencies known
Definition of done: tested, reviewed, documented, feature-flag plan, release note drafted

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked in Product Operations interviews about process optimization, grouped by level. Draft your answer, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.What does Product Ops do?
    Enables product teams through standard processes, tools, insights, launch readiness and metrics so PMs spend time on product decisions.
    IQ2.How do you standardize without killing autonomy?
    Standardize the core (terms, artifacts, checks) and allow flex elsewhere; co-design with teams and pilot.
    IQ3.How do you design a discovery framework?
    Lifecycle with light gates, templates, evidence requirements and owners; pilot and refine.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How would you configure Jira for product teams?
    Few statuses, shared issue types, required fields tied to decisions, definitions of ready and done, links to roadmap.
    IQ5.How do you use Productboard?
    Centralize insights linked to features, score priorities, build audience roadmap views and integrate with Jira.
    IQ6.How do you use Gong for product?
    Trackers for topics, curated libraries and clips, insights pushed to Productboard, with privacy controls.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you measure the value of Product Ops?
    Time saved, cycle time, rework, adoption, tool utilization and satisfaction against a baseline.
    IQ8.How do you drive adoption?
    Solve real pain, co-design, champions, training, office hours and make the right way easy.
    IQ9.A team resists a new process. What do you do?
    Listen, find the real friction, adapt, show benefit, and pilot changes.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you govern tools?
    Role-based access, seat audits, documented integrations, data quality rules, privacy review and change control.
    IQ11.How does your approach change at scale?
    Move to a federated model with core standards and local ops partners.
    IQ12.Tell me about a process you improved.
    Describe baseline, change, adoption and measured results.

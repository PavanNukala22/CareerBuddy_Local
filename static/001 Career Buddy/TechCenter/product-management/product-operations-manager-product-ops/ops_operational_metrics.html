    Module 01

    Operational Metrics for Product Operations

    Measuring product team efficiency, velocity, sprint health and delivery reliability, then using those measures to improve flow without turning metrics into weapons.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    Operational metrics give product organizations an honest view of how work flows. Product Ops builds the measures, the dashboards and the review habits that turn data into steady improvement.

    ⚡
    Velocity
    Throughput and predictability.
    🩺
    Sprint health
    Commitment, scope, carryover.
    🚚
    Reliability
    Delivery and quality outcomes.
    📊
    Dashboards
    Metrics that drive improvement.
    💡
    Operational metrics describe the health of the system, not the performance of individuals. Use them to find bottlenecks, not to rank people.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Choose meaningful metrics for efficiency and flow.
    2. Measure velocity, throughput, cycle time and lead time correctly.
    3. Assess sprint health: commitment reliability, scope change, carryover.
    4. Track delivery reliability with DORA, predictability and quality metrics.
    5. Build team and org dashboards from Jira and other tools.
    6. Avoid metric gaming and misuse.
    7. Turn metrics into improvement actions and reviews.

    Module 02

    Velocity, Throughput and Flow Metrics

    Story point velocity, throughput, cycle time, lead time, WIP, Little's Law, percentiles and flow efficiency.

    ⚡ Basic: Velocity and Throughput

    textVELOCITY (story points completed per sprint)
Sprints 1-6: 34, 38, 30, 36, 32, 40 -> total 210
Average velocity = 210 / 6 = 35 points
Use the average (or median) for planning, not as a target.

THROUGHPUT (items completed per sprint)
Sprint items: 17, 19, 16, 18, 20, 18 -> average 18 items
    MetricUnitBest useCaution
    VelocityStory points / sprintTeam capacity planningTeam-relative; not comparable across teams
    ThroughputItems / periodForecasting, comparisons over timeItems vary in size
    Cycle timeDays per itemSpeed, predictabilityUse percentiles
    Lead timeDays from requestCustomer responsivenessIncludes queue time
    WIPItems in progressFlow controlLimit it
    Figure 2.1 · Lead time vs cycle time

    📈 Intermediate: Cycle Time and Percentiles

    textCYCLE TIME (days) for 20 items: median 4.2; 85th percentile 9; 95th percentile 14
Interpretation: half of items finish within 4.2 days; 85% within 9 days.
Forecast statement: "85% of items finish within 9 days of starting."
Average hides skew: a few long items pull the mean up.
    ChartWhat it shows
    Cycle time scatterplotEach item with percentile lines; outliers
    Cumulative flow diagramBottlenecks where bands widen
    Throughput run chartTrend and variability
    Aging WIPItems stuck beyond typical cycle time
    Figure 2.2 · From timestamps to forecasts

    🔬 Intermediate: Little's Law and Flow Efficiency

    textLITTLE'S LAW: Average WIP = Throughput x Average cycle time
Throughput 2 items/day; average cycle time 5 days -> WIP = 10 items
To shorten cycle time at the same throughput, reduce WIP: WIP 6 -> cycle time = 6 / 2 = 3 days.

FLOW EFFICIENCY = touch time / total cycle time
Touch time 2 days; cycle time 8 days -> 25%. Most of the time is waiting.
    Figure 2.3 · Improving flow

    🏆 Advanced: Probabilistic Forecasting

    TechniqueUse
    Monte Carlo simulationForecast completion date from historical throughput
    Cycle time percentilesSet service level expectations (SLEs)
    Throughput variabilityUnderstand range, not a single number
    Scope growth ratesAccount for added work

    textSPRINT 14 REVIEW
Committed 40 points; completed 34 -> commitment reliability 34/40 = 85%
Added mid-sprint 6 points -> scope change 6/40 = 15% (above 10% target)
Carryover: 6 points -> 6/40 = 15%
Sprint goal: met. Blocked: 3 items, average 2 days (waiting on design review).
Action: add a design review slot twice weekly; stricter intake rules.

    Delivery Reliability: DORA, Predictability and Quality

    DORA metrics, roadmap predictability, escaped defects, on-time delivery, SLEs and quality gates.

    🚚 DORA and Reliability Metrics

    DORA metricMeaningExample
    Deployment frequencyHow often code is deployed to production60 deploys/month
    Lead time for changesCommit to production1.5 days median
    Change failure rateDeployments causing failure / total3 of 60 = 5%
    Time to restore (MTTR)Time to recover from failure2.5 hours
    Product reliability metricFormulaExample
    Roadmap predictabilityItems delivered in quarter / planned14 of 20 = 70%
    Escaped defect rateBugs found in production / items shipped9 / 120 = 7.5%
    On-time deliveryDelivered by committed date / total85%
    Rework rateReopened or redone items / completed8%

    🎯 Intermediate: Predictability and Commitments

    textQUARTERLY PREDICTABILITY
Planned roadmap items: 20 | Delivered as planned: 14 | Delivered late: 3 | Dropped or deferred: 3
Predictability = 14 / 20 = 70% (goal 80%)
Causes: unplanned work (interrupts) 40%, dependency delays 35%, scope growth 25%
Actions: reserve 15% capacity for unplanned work; dependency review at planning; commit only to P0 and P1 items.
    PracticeBenefit
    Commit to fewer, clearer itemsHigher reliability
    Reserve capacity bufferAbsorb interrupts
    Use ranges and confidenceHonest forecasts
    Track unplanned workVisible cost of interrupts

New features 60% | Technical debt 20% | Bugs and support 15% | Other 5%
Goal for this team: features 55%, tech debt 25%, bugs 15%, other 5%
Rising bug time (10% -> 15% over 3 quarters) signals quality investment needed.

    Capacity useHow much time goes to roadmap vs unplanned?

    ⚠️ Healthy Metric Practice

    MisuseResultBetter
    Velocity as performance targetInflated estimatesUse for planning only
    Ranking engineers by commitsGaming, poor collaborationTeam-level flow metrics
    Single metric focusQuality dropsBalanced set: speed, quality, predictability
    Using metrics to blameHiding problemsUse to find system causes
    Comparing teams by pointsMeaninglessCompare each team to its own trend
    ⚠️
    Goodhart's Law: when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. Pair every speed metric with a quality metric.

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked in Product Operations interviews about operational metrics, grouped by level. Draft your answer, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.Which metrics do you use for product team efficiency?
    Flow metrics (cycle time, throughput, WIP), sprint health, DORA and predictability, paired with quality and outcome measures.
    IQ2.What is velocity and how should it be used?
    Story points completed per sprint; use for team planning only, not as a target or cross-team comparison.
    IQ3.Why use percentiles for cycle time?
    Distributions are skewed; percentiles give forecasts like 85% within 9 days, unlike averages.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.Explain Little's Law.
    Average WIP equals throughput times cycle time; lowering WIP shortens cycle time at constant throughput.
    IQ5.How do you assess sprint health?
    Commitment reliability, scope change, carryover, sprint goal success, blockers and estimation trends.
    IQ6.What are the DORA metrics?
    Deployment frequency, lead time for changes, change failure rate and time to restore service.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you improve delivery predictability?
    Commit to fewer clear items, reserve buffer for unplanned work, manage dependencies and use ranges.
    IQ8.How do you avoid metric gaming?
    Use metrics for learning, pair speed with quality, share definitions, avoid individual targets and review with teams.
    IQ9.How do you design dashboards?
    Audience-specific, few metrics, trends, documented definitions, automated data.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you fix unreliable Jira data?
    Audit, standardize workflow, automate transitions, recompute and publish definitions.
    IQ11.How do you run process experiments?
    Change one thing, define the metric, run a few sprints, compare, and keep or drop.
    IQ12.Tell me about a metric that led to a real improvement.
    Describe the metric, insight, action and measured result.

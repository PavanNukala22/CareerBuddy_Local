    Module 01

    Engineering Empathy for Technical Product Managers

    How a TPM evaluates technical trade-offs with architects and judges third-party developer tools and SDKs, with respect for engineering reality and clear product reasoning.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    Engineering empathy is how a Technical Product Manager earns trust and makes better decisions. It combines curiosity about constraints, rigorous trade-off analysis and fair evaluation of tools so that technical choices serve product outcomes.

    🤝
    Partnership
    Work with engineers as co-owners of outcomes.
    ⚖️
    Trade-offs
    Weigh speed, quality, cost and risk openly.
    🧰
    Tool evaluation
    Assess SDKs, libraries and vendors rigorously.
    📝
    Decision records
    Capture reasoning so choices survive time.
    💡
    Empathy is not agreement. It means understanding the constraint behind the opinion before you decide.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Run constructive trade-off discussions with architects and tech leads.
    2. Use decision frameworks: weighted scoring, reversibility, cost of delay.
    3. Write architecture decision records (ADRs) and options papers.
    4. Evaluate third-party tools, libraries and SDKs with a structured scorecard.
    5. Assess build vs buy vs open source with total cost of ownership.
    6. Review vendor risk: security, licensing, lock-in, support and roadmap.
    7. Handle disagreement and escalation without damaging trust.

    Module 03

    Technical Trade-off Frameworks

    Structured ways to compare options: weighted scoring, reversibility, cost of delay, risk matrices and decision records.

    ⚖️ Basic: The Trade-off Triangle and Options Papers

    Figure 3.1 · From goals to decision
    LeverGainGive up
    Faster deliveryEarlier valueScope or quality
    Higher qualityFewer incidentsTime
    Lower costBudget savedCapability or speed
    More flexibilityFuture optionsSimplicity today

    🧮 Intermediate: Weighted Scoring

    textDECISION: Message queue for order events
Criteria (weight): Reliability 30 | Team skill 25 | Cost 20 | Ecosystem 15 | Ops effort 10
                 Reliab  Skill  Cost  Ecosys  Ops   Total
Option A (Kafka)    9      5      5     9      4    = 9*.30+5*.25+5*.20+9*.15+4*.10 = 6.70
Option B (SQS)      8      8      8     6      9    = 8*.30+8*.25+8*.20+6*.15+9*.10 = 7.80
Option C (RabbitMQ) 7      7      7     7      6    = 7*.30+7*.25+7*.20+7*.15+6*.10 = 6.90
Result: B wins; sensitivity: A wins if reliability weight rises above 50.
    TipWhy
    Set weights before scoringAvoid bias toward a favourite
    Score with evidencePoC data, benchmarks, references
    Run sensitivity checksSee if the winner is robust
    Treat total as a guideDiscuss gaps, not just the number

    🚪 Intermediate: Reversibility and Risk

    Two-way doorUI library, internal tool, feature flagDecide fast; learn; change if wrong
    One-way doorPrimary database, cloud provider, public API contractAnalyze deeply; PoC; involve leaders

    🏆 Advanced: Architecture Decision Records

    textADR-014: Use SQS for order events
Status: Accepted (2026-09-12)
Context: Order events must reach three services reliably; team has no streaming experience; budget limited.
Options: Kafka, SQS, RabbitMQ (see scoring).
Decision: SQS with a thin publisher interface.
Consequences: + low ops, + fast start; - limited replay, - vendor lock-in mitigated by interface.

    🧱 Debt, Refactor or Rewrite?

    OptionUse whenRisk
    Live with itLow interest, area rarely changesSilent growth

    🔄 Case Study: The Popular SDK That Backfired

    A team adopts a trending SDK; six months later maintainers stop releasing and security issues pile up.

    Figure 8.1 · Recovering from a bad dependency
    ✅

    ⚔️ Case Study: Architect vs Team Standoff

    1. Write both positions and their underlying goals.
    2. Agree evaluation criteria and risks.
    3. Run a one-week spike on the contested point.
    4. Decision owner decides; record the ADR; commit.

    💸 Troubleshooting: Vendor Raises Prices 40%

    StepAction

    2Quantify exit cost and alternatives

    ⚠️ Scenario: Risky Open-Source Dependency

    A critical library has a single maintainer and a known CVE. Assess exposure, patch or pin, consider sponsoring or forking, add a wrapper, and set a policy for minimum maintainer health before adoption.

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked in Technical Product Manager interviews about engineering empathy, grouped by level. Draft your answer, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.How do you build trust with engineers?
    Listen to constraints, ask open questions, share business context, protect focus time, keep promises, and give credit. Decisions are explained and recorded.
    IQ2.How do you evaluate a technical trade-off?
    Define goals, agree criteria and weights, gather evidence with spikes or benchmarks, compare options on cost, risk and reversibility, then decide and record in an ADR.
    IQ3.What is an ADR and why use it?
    An architecture decision record captures context, options, decision, consequences and review trigger so teams remember why a choice was made.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you evaluate a third-party SDK?
    Write requirements, build a short list, screen on must-haves, run a PoC in a real scenario, score fit, DX, reliability, security, licence, performance and exit cost.
    IQ5.Build, buy or open source?
    Build core differentiators, buy commodity capabilities, use open source when a standard need meets strong team skill. Compare three-year TCO and lock-in.
    IQ6.What is vendor lock-in and how do you reduce it?
    High cost of switching. Reduce with standards, abstraction layers, data export rights and a tested exit plan.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you handle disagreement with an architect?
    Clarify underlying goals, agree criteria, test with a spike, let the decision owner decide, record the decision and commit.
    IQ8.How do you discuss technical debt with business leaders?
    Express it in impact: slower delivery, incident risk and cost, then offer options with a capacity proposal.
    IQ9.How do you decide between refactor and rewrite?
    Use evidence of cost, prefer incremental or strangler approaches, and only rewrite for dead-end technology with clear scope and safeguards.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you assess open-source risk?
    Check maintainers, release cadence, security history, licence and dependency tree; plan updates and wrappers.
    IQ11.How do you communicate a decision to executives?
    One-page paper: problem, goal, options with cost and risk, recommendation, and decision date.
    IQ12.Tell me about a technical decision you influenced.
    Describe context, options, evidence, how you aligned people, the outcome and what you would change.

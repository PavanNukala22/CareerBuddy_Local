    Module 01

    Tactical Prioritization for Product Owners

    How a Product Owner balances technical debt, bug fixes and feature velocity inside short iterations: capacity allocation, ordering rules, trade-offs and transparent decisions.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    Tactical prioritization is the sprint-to-sprint discipline of deciding what the team does next with a fixed capacity. A Product Owner weighs features that create value, bugs that destroy trust and technical debt that taxes every future change.

    ⚖️
    Balance
    Split capacity across features, bugs and debt.
    📊
    Evidence
    Use data on impact, cost and risk.
    🔁
    Short cycles
    Re-decide every sprint with fresh information.
    🗣️
    Transparency
    Explain and record every trade-off.
    💡
    Prioritization is not picking favourites. It is choosing what not to do now and being able to explain why.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Explain why feature, bug and tech debt work compete for one capacity pool.
    2. Classify bugs by severity and priority and set response rules.
    3. Quantify technical debt by cost of delay, interest and risk.
    4. Use scoring models (WSJF, RICE, cost of delay) at sprint level.
    5. Allocate capacity with explicit policies and adjust with data.
    6. Handle urgent interrupts without destroying the sprint.
    7. Communicate trade-offs to stakeholders and developers.

    Scoring Models: WSJF, RICE & Cost of Delay

    Ranking features, bugs and debt on a common scale with Weighted Shortest Job First, RICE and cost-of-delay thinking.

    🔢 WSJF: Weighted Shortest Job First

    textWSJF = Cost of Delay / Job Size
Cost of Delay = User/Business Value + Time Criticality + Risk Reduction/Opportunity

Item A  CoD 8+5+3 = 16   size 8   WSJF 2.0
Item B  CoD 5+8+2 = 15   size 3   WSJF 5.0   <- first
Item C  CoD 3+2+8 = 13   size 5   WSJF 2.6

    📊 RICE and Other Models

    ModelFormulaBest for
    RICEReach × Impact × Confidence / EffortFeature choice
    WSJFCost of delay / sizeMixed backlog with urgency and debt
    MoSCoWMust, Should, Could, Won'tFixed-scope releases
    Value vs effort2×2 matrixQuick workshop
    KanoBasic, performance, delightFeature value types
    Figure 4.2 · From collection to decision

    ⚠️ Using Scores Wisely

    ✅ Scores as aid

    Scores start the conversation; assumptions are visible; compare similar-size items; recalculate each sprint; combine with sprint goal.

    ❌ Scores as dictator

    Treat the number as truth; game the inputs; mix epics with tasks; ignore dependencies and strategy.

    💡
    Always ask: "What would change this ranking?" That reveals the weak assumptions.

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked in Product Owner interviews about tactical prioritization, grouped by level. Draft your answer, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.How do you balance features, bugs and tech debt?
    Use one visible capacity pool with a written policy (for example 60/20/20 plus a buffer), score items on a common scale such as WSJF, and review the split each sprint with data like cycle time and defects.
    IQ2.How do you triage bugs?
    Reproduce, classify severity, check reach and workaround, then decide priority as Product Owner. Critical issues are expedited; others follow response targets and a bug budget.
    IQ3.What is the difference between severity and priority?
    Severity is technical damage; priority is business urgency to fix. QA assigns severity, and the PO sets priority.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you justify technical debt work?
    Express it in business terms: hours lost per sprint, incidents, security risk and feature delay, then score it next to features.
    IQ5.What is WSJF?
    Weighted Shortest Job First: cost of delay (value + time criticality + risk reduction) divided by job size. Highest score goes first.
    IQ6.How do you handle urgent requests during a sprint?
    Check against the expedite definition, swap equal-size work out, record the reason, inform stakeholders and review root causes.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you say no to stakeholders?
    Acknowledge the need, show the trade-off and options, give a date, and record the decision; never a flat no without context.
    IQ8.How do you use velocity?
    As a planning input based on a rolling average, never as a target or a performance measure.
    IQ9.What if the team constantly misses the sprint goal?
    Check unplanned work, overcommitment and unclear stories; reduce commitment, add buffer, improve refinement and review in the retrospective.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you decide between two high-value features?
    Score with agreed criteria, check dependencies and cost of delay, consider capacity and strategy, and make a transparent call, with a sponsor if tied.
    IQ11.How do you make prioritization transparent?
    Publish the sprint goal, a trade-off note, capacity split and metrics, and keep the backlog order visible with reasons.
    IQ12.Tell me about a hard trade-off you made.
    Describe the options, the data used, the decision and who was affected, how you communicated it, and the result and what you learned.

    Module 01

    Agile/Scrum Expertise for Product Owners

    How a Product Owner runs the backlog and the Scrum cycle: refinement, story grooming, sprint planning, acceptance testing and release definition.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    A Product Owner (PO) maximises the value delivered by a Scrum team. You own the Product Backlog, keep it ordered and understandable, clarify requirements with developers, accept finished work and decide what is released.

    📚
    Backlog refinement
    Keep the backlog ordered, clear and ready.
    ✂️
    Story grooming
    Write, split and size stories the team can finish.
    🗓️
    Sprint planning
    Agree a sprint goal and a realistic commitment.
    ✅
    Acceptance and release
    Verify value and define what ships when.
    💡
    The Product Owner is the single voice of value for the team: one ordered backlog, clear acceptance and decisions made quickly.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Explain the Product Owner accountabilities in Scrum.
    2. Run backlog refinement and keep the top of the backlog ready.
    3. Write, split and groom user stories with acceptance criteria.
    4. Lead sprint planning with a clear sprint goal and capacity-based scope.
    5. Perform acceptance testing and make accept/reject decisions.
    6. Define releases: scope, readiness, increments and release notes.
    7. Use Scrum metrics to improve predictability without gaming them.

    Module 04

    Acceptance Testing

    Verifying that work delivers the intended value: acceptance criteria, UAT, accept/reject decisions, exploratory checks and regression.

    ✅ Basic: What Acceptance Testing Is

    Acceptance testing confirms that a finished story meets its acceptance criteria and delivers the intended value. The PO is accountable for accepting; testers and developers help verify.

    Figure 4.1 · Acceptance flow

    🧪 Intermediate: How to Accept a Story

    CheckQuestion
    CriteriaDoes every scenario pass, including negative ones?
    ValueDoes it solve the user problem as intended?
    QualityIs the Definition of Done met (tests, accessibility, analytics)?
    RegressionDid it break related features?
    ExperienceIs it usable on target devices?
    textACCEPTANCE NOTE
Story: Guest checkout (ORD-142)
Result: Accepted with 1 follow-up
Scenarios: 5 of 5 passed (valid, invalid email, declined card, timeout, confirmation)
Follow-up: Confirmation email arrives after 90s on slow network (ORD-155, P2)
Evidence: Test run link; screen recording
    📋
    Criteria pass
    All scenarios verified.
    💎
    Value confirmed
    Solves the user problem.
    ✔️
    DoD met
    Tests, accessibility, analytics.
    📝
    Notes recorded
    Evidence and follow-ups.

    🔎 Advanced: Reject, Partially Accept and UAT

    SituationDecision
    Criteria failReject; return with failing scenarios
    Minor cosmetic issueAccept; log follow-up ticket
    Missing acceptance criterion discoveredAccept what was agreed; add new story
    Value not achievedDiscuss; may need new story; learn
    Requires business sign-offPlan UAT with business users in advance

    Real-World Scenarios & Troubleshooting

    Case studies: a bloated backlog, constant mid-sprint changes, rejected stories and a release at risk.

    🗂️ Case Study: The 600-Item Backlog

    Nobody trusts the backlog; stakeholders add items daily.

    Figure 8.1 · Cleaning a backlog
    ✅
    Outcome: 600 items reduced to 90 active; top 20 ready; intake form with weekly triage; stakeholders trust the list.

    🔀 Troubleshooting: Mid-Sprint Changes

    1. Clarify the need and urgency.
    2. Check impact on the sprint goal with developers.
    3. Options: swap, defer, or (if goal obsolete) cancel the sprint.
    4. Decide; update the sprint backlog; inform stakeholders.
    Figure 8.2 · Handling mid-sprint requests

    ❌ Troubleshooting: Stories Keep Getting Rejected

    CauseFix
    Criteria unclear or incompleteCo-write criteria in refinement with the tester
    PO changes mind lateRecord decisions; limit changes mid-story
    Misunderstood valueUse examples and mock-ups; short demos before completion
    Quality issuesStrengthen Definition of Done

    ⏰ Trade-off Scenario: Release at Risk

    Two sprints before a fixed launch, scope is behind. Re-order by value and risk, cut to the Must set, split larger stories, ask stakeholders for decisions on what moves, and agree a daily check on blockers. Announce the revised scope early.

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked in Product Owner interviews about Agile and Scrum, grouped by level. Draft your answer, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.What does a Product Owner do?
    Maximises the value of the product by owning the Product Backlog: ordering it, making items clear, ensuring the team understands them, accepting finished work and deciding on releases.
    IQ2.What is backlog refinement?
    Ongoing work to make items clear, sized and ready: clarifying, splitting, adding acceptance criteria. Roughly 5–10% of sprint time, so the top of the backlog is always ready for planning.
    IQ3.What makes a good user story?
    Follows INVEST: independent, negotiable, valuable, estimable, small and testable, with a user, action and benefit, plus clear acceptance criteria.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you run sprint planning?
    Propose a sprint goal, review ready items and capacity, let developers select what they can complete, help them plan the work, and agree the goal and scope as a team.
    IQ5.How do you decide whether to accept a story?
    Check all acceptance scenarios and the Definition of Done, confirm it delivers the intended value, check regression, then accept or reject with clear notes and evidence.
    IQ6.What is the difference between acceptance criteria and Definition of Done?
    Acceptance criteria are specific to a story. Definition of Done is the quality standard for every story, such as tests, review and accessibility.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you handle a mid-sprint request from a stakeholder?
    Clarify need and urgency, assess impact on the sprint goal with developers, offer options such as swap or defer, decide and communicate. Never silently add scope.
    IQ8.How do you define a release?
    Set a release goal and metric, define MoSCoW scope, readiness criteria, rollout and rollback plans, and communication to users, support and sales.
    IQ9.How do you say no to stakeholders?
    Use the ordered backlog and capacity, explain trade-offs and value, offer alternatives or dates, and keep the conversation transparent.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you handle a team that keeps missing sprint goals?
    Analyse carry-over, scope change and readiness; improve refinement and DoR, reduce commitment, split stories and protect the goal; review in retros.
    IQ11.What metrics do you use as a PO?
    Sprint goal success, cycle time, velocity trends for forecasting, burn-up for releases, escape rate for quality, and outcome metrics linked to goals. Never use them to rank people.
    IQ12.How do you work with the Scrum Master and developers?
    PO focuses on value and what; developers on how and capacity; Scrum Master on process and impediments. I clarify early, respect self-management and collaborate daily.

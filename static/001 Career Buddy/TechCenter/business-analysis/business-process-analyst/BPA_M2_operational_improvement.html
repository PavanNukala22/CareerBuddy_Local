    Improvement Foundations

    What operational improvement is, where Lean and Six Sigma came from, and the PDCA mindset behind every improvement.

    🚀 What is Operational Improvement?

    Operational improvement is the disciplined effort to raise the speed, quality, cost and consistency of how work is done, using data and structured methods instead of opinion.

    ApproachIdeaScale
    KaizenSmall, continuous, employee-led improvementsIncremental
    KaikakuRadical redesign of a processBreakthrough
    LeanMaximise customer value, minimise wasteFlow and speed
    Six SigmaReduce variation and defects with statisticsQuality and consistency
    Lean Six SigmaLean speed + Six Sigma controlBoth
    TOCFind and exploit the single constraintThroughput

    PDCA - the engine of improvement

    PlanDoCheckAct

    Popularised by W. Edwards Deming: Plan a change and a prediction, Do it small, Check the result against the prediction, Act to standardize or adjust. DMAIC is PDCA with statistical rigour.

    💡
    Improvement is a loop, not a project. Without the *Act* step, gains decay back to the old way within months.

    🎧 Voice of Customer and CTQ

    Improvement starts with what the customer values. VOC (Voice of the Customer) is translated into CTQs (Critical-to-Quality characteristics) that are measurable.

    VOC statementNeedCTQ (measurable)
    "Takes forever to get my refund"Fast refundRefund issued within 3 working days, 95% of cases
    "Statement was wrong again"AccuracyError rate under 0.5% per 1,000 statements
    "Nobody calls me back"ResponsivenessFirst response within 4 business hours
    📌
    A CTQ always has a metric, a target and a specification limit. If you cannot measure it, you cannot improve it.

    💰 Cost of Poor Quality and Core KPIs

    • COPQ: cost of failure (rework, scrap, complaints) plus appraisal (inspection). Often 10-30% of revenue in unmanaged operations.
    • First-pass yield (FPY): units right the first time / units started.
    • Rolled throughput yield (RTY): multiplication of FPY across steps; 3 steps at 95% give 0.95 x 0.95 x 0.95 = 85.7%.
    • On-time delivery, cycle time, cost per transaction, backlog age.

    Lean & Waste Reduction

    The eight wastes, lean principles, 5S, pull systems and the tools that remove waste from services and operations.

    🗑️ The Eight Wastes (DOWNTIME)

    WasteMeaningOffice / service example
    DefectsWork that must be redoneWrong data entered; invoice errors
    OverproductionMore than demanded or too earlyReports nobody reads
    WaitingIdle time between stepsApproval queue of 3 days
    Non-utilised talentSkills not usedExperts doing data entry
    TransportationUnneeded movement of work/materialFiles passed between 6 teams
    InventoryExcess WIP or stock500 open tickets
    MotionExtra human movement/clicksRe-keying across 4 screens
    Extra-processingWork customer will not pay forTriple approval for 50 USD
    💡
    Memorise DOWNTIME. In service processes, Waiting, Defects and Extra-processing are usually the top three.

    🧭 Five Lean Principles and Core Tools

    1. Specify value from the customer's view.
    2. Map the value stream and remove non-value steps.
    3. Create flow - no stops and queues.
    4. Establish pull - produce only on demand.
    5. Pursue perfection - repeat continuously.
    ToolWhat it does
    5SSort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain - workplace order
    KanbanVisual signal that limits WIP and triggers pull
    Poka-yokeMistake-proofing so errors cannot occur (mandatory fields, drop-down lists)
    SMEDCut changeover/setup time (single-minute exchange of dies)
    Standard workBest-known sequence, time and WIP for a task
    Visual managementBoards and dashboards that show status at a glance
    A3 reportOne-page problem-solving story on A3 paper
    HeijunkaLevel the mix and volume of work

    🛠️ Example: Waste Reduction in an Approval Process

    before vs afterBefore  7 approvals, avg lead time 9 days, 18% returned for missing data
  Waste found: Waiting (queues), Defects (missing data), Extra-processing (low-value approvals)
Changes Mandatory fields (poka-yoke) | auto-approve under 500 USD | daily 15-min huddle on board
After   2 approvals, avg lead time 2.5 days, 3% returned
  Gain: lead time -72%, rework -83%
    ⚠️
    Do not remove required control steps (regulatory, anti-fraud) as "waste". They are *Business Non-Value-Added* - minimise, never silently delete.

    Six Sigma & DMAIC

    Sigma levels, belts, the five DMAIC phases with their tools and deliverables, and a worked project.

    📈 Six Sigma Basics

    Six Sigma reduces variation so output stays within customer specification limits. A sigma level tells how many standard deviations fit between the process mean and the nearest limit.

    Sigma levelDPMO (with 1.5 shift)Yield
    2308,53769.1%
    366,80793.3%
    46,21099.38%
    523399.977%
    63.499.99966%
    formulasDPU  = defects / units
DPMO = defects / (units x opportunities per unit) x 1,000,000
Example: 25 defects, 500 invoices, 5 opportunities each
DPMO = 25 / (500 x 5) x 1,000,000 = 10,000   (about 3.8 sigma)

    ChampionSenior sponsor; removes barriers
    Master Black BeltCoaches belts, sets methodology
    Black BeltLeads complex projects full-time
    Green BeltLeads smaller projects part-time
    Yellow BeltTeam member with basic knowledge

    🧪 DMAIC Phase by Phase

    PhaseGoalKey toolsDeliverable
    DefineAgree problem, scope, customer needProject charter, SIPOC, VOC/CTQ tree, stakeholder mapApproved charter
    MeasureBaseline performance with trusted dataData collection plan, MSA / Gage R&R, process map, sigma/DPMOBaseline metric
    AnalyzeProve the root causesFishbone, 5 Whys, Pareto, hypothesis tests, regression, FMEAVerified root causes
    ImproveDesign and pilot solutionsBrainstorming, impact-effort matrix, pilot, FMEA update, poka-yokePiloted solution
    ControlHold the gainsControl charts, control plan, SOPs, dashboards, trainingHandover to owner
    💡
    Never jump to Improve without proving causes in Analyze. Solutions chosen on gut feeling fail more often than they work.

    📂 Project Charter Example

    charterProblem   Customer onboarding takes 14 days on average; target is 5 (VOC: 31% cancel before day 10)
Goal      Reduce median lead time from 14 to 5 days by Q4, with no rise in error rate
Scope     Application received to account active (excludes sales)
Metric    Lead time (days), FPY; secondary: cost per onboarding
Team      Sponsor: COO | Lead: Green Belt | SMEs: Ops, Risk, IT
Benefit   Fewer cancellations, estimated 480,000 USD per year
    📌
    A good problem statement states what, where, how big, since when - and never contains a cause or a solution.

    Root-Cause Analysis

    Ishikawa diagrams, 5 Whys, Pareto, FMEA and 8D - finding and proving why problems really occur.

    🐟 Ishikawa (Fishbone) Diagram

    The Ishikawa (cause-and-effect or fishbone) diagram, created by Kaoru Ishikawa, groups possible causes under categories that branch off the problem 'head'.

    SettingCategories
    Manufacturing (6M)Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, Mother Nature (environment)
    Service (8P)Product, Price, Place, Promotion, People, Process, Physical evidence, Productivity
    Admin / office (4S)Surroundings, Suppliers, Systems, Skills
    exampleProblem: 22% of loan files returned for missing documents
  People     > new staff untrained on checklist
  Method     > no mandatory-field check at intake
  Systems    > upload accepts blank files
  Materials  > customer form has unclear labels
  Measurement> returns not tracked by reason
    ⚠️
    A fishbone lists possible causes. It is a hypothesis map and must be validated with data.

    ❓ 5 Whys

    Ask 'why?' repeatedly (about five times, not rigidly) until you reach a cause you can fix and prevent.

    exampleProblem: Customer refund delayed
Why 1: Refund request was not processed          > it sat in an unassigned queue
Why 2: Why unassigned?                           > auto-routing rule failed
Why 3: Why did the rule fail?                    > new product code was not in the table
Why 4: Why not in the table?                     > product launch checklist lacks a systems step
Why 5: Why no step?                              > checklist has no owner
Root cause: no ownership of the launch checklist  >  Action: assign owner and add step
    💡
    Stop when the answer is a process or system cause you control. If the answer is "human error", ask why the process allowed it.

    🔬 Pareto, FMEA, 8D and Is/Is-Not

    • Pareto chart: bars of causes sorted by frequency with cumulative line; typically ~80% of effects come from ~20% of causes.
    ToolUse
    FMEARate Severity, Occurrence, Detection (1-10); RPN = S x O x D; act on highest risk first
    Is / Is-NotCompare where/when/what the problem occurs versus where it does not
    8DEight-discipline team method: team, describe, contain, root cause, corrective action, verify, prevent, congratulate
    Fault treeTop-down logic (AND/OR) from failure to causes
    Scatter / regressionTest whether a suspected cause moves with the effect
    FMEA rowFailure mode: wrong bank code keyed      Severity 8 | Occurrence 5 | Detection 6
RPN = 8 x 5 x 6 = 240  >  add validation list (poka-yoke): Occurrence 5>1, Detection 6>2  >  new RPN = 16

    Flow, Constraints & Quantifying Waste

    Flow metrics, Little's Law, bottleneck thinking and turning waste into money and a prioritised improvement backlog.

    ⏱️ Flow Metrics and Little's Law

    Little's LawWIP = Throughput x Cycle time        (lead time = WIP / throughput)
Example: 300 open cases, 60 closed per day
Average lead time = 300 / 60 = 5 days
Cut WIP to 150 with the same throughput  >  lead time = 2.5 days
    • Reducing WIP is the fastest way to cut lead time without extra capacity.
    • Utilisation near 100% makes queues explode; planning 80-85% leaves room for variation.
    • Cycle time vs lead time: cycle time is work time per unit; lead time includes waiting.

    🔒 Theory of Constraints (TOC)

    1. Identify the constraint (the step limiting throughput).
    2. Exploit it - keep it busy with good work only.
    3. Subordinate everything else to its pace.
    4. Elevate it - add capacity if still needed.
    5. Repeat - the constraint will move.
    📌
    An hour lost at the bottleneck is an hour lost for the whole system; an hour saved elsewhere is a mirage.

    💡 Quantify and Prioritise

    Benefit typeExampleCalculation
    Hard savingOvertime removedHours x loaded rate
    Cost avoidancePenalty fees avoidedExpected fines avoided
    Capacity releaseFTE time freedMinutes saved x volume / working minutes
    RevenueFewer cancellationsRetained customers x margin
    capacity example4 min saved x 120,000 cases/yr = 480,000 min = 8,000 h
FTE released = 8,000 / 1,600 productive hours = 5 FTE  (redeploy; it is not a headcount cut by default)
    • Impact-effort matrix: do high-impact/low-effort 'quick wins' first.
    • Track benefits against baseline for 6-12 months; finance signs off 'hard' savings.

    Expert: Statistics, Control & Sustainment

    Control charts, capability, hypothesis tests, DFSS, change management and interview-ready summaries.

    📉 Control Charts and Process Capability

    ChartData type
    I-MRIndividual continuous values
    X-bar & R/SSubgroup continuous data
    p / npProportion / count of defective units
    c / uCount of defects per unit
    • Control limits are mean ± 3 sigma and come from the process, not from customer specification.
    • Special-cause signals: a point outside limits, 8 consecutive points on one side of the mean, 6 points trending, 2 of 3 beyond 2 sigma.
    • Common cause variation is inherent; special cause is assignable and should be investigated.
    capabilityCp  = (USL - LSL) / (6 x sigma)                 potential
Cpk = min(USL - mean, mean - LSL) / (3 x sigma)  actual, accounts for centring
Rule of thumb: Cpk >= 1.33 capable, >= 1.67 for critical features
    ⚠️
    Specification limits are the customer's requirement; control limits describe what the process does. Mixing them up is a classic error.

    🧮 Choosing a Hypothesis Test

    QuestionDataTest
    Is the mean different from a target?Continuous, 1 sample1-sample t
    Do two groups differ in mean?Continuous, 2 groups2-sample t
    Do 3+ groups differ in mean?Continuous, 3+ groupsANOVA
    Do proportions differ?DiscreteChi-square / 2-proportion
    Are two variables related?ContinuousCorrelation / regression

    If the p-value < 0.05 (alpha), reject the null hypothesis (no difference). Always pair statistical significance with practical significance: is the gain big enough to matter?

    🏁 DFSS, Change and Interview Points

    • DMADV (Define, Measure, Analyze, Design, Verify) is used when designing a new process; DMAIC improves an existing one.
    • Hold gains with a control plan: metric, owner, frequency, reaction plan, linked SOP.
    • Adoption model ADKAR: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement.
    PointDetail

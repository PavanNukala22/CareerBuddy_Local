    Process Fundamentals

    What a process is, how processes are layered, and why standardizing them matters — the base for every BPA task.

    🔁 What is a Business Process?

    A business process is a set of related, repeatable activities that turns inputs into an output of value for a customer, started by a trigger, run by defined roles, and measurable by outcome.

    TypePurposeExamples
    Core (primary)Directly creates customer valueOrder-to-Cash, Claims Handling, Loan Origination
    SupportKeeps the core runningRecruit-to-Hire, Procure-to-Pay, IT Service Desk
    ManagementDirects and controls the businessStrategic planning, Budgeting, Risk & Compliance

    What is what

    TermMeaningLevel of detail
    PolicyRule of intent set by management ("all vendors must be vetted")Why / must
    StandardMeasurable requirement that satisfies a policyWhat
    ProcessEnd-to-end flow of activities toward an outcomeWhat happens, in what order
    Procedure / SOPDocumented steps and responsibilities for a processWho does what, how
    Work instructionTask-level detail for one role or system screenClick-level how
    GuidelineRecommended, non-mandatory practiceAdvice
    💡
    Remember the cascade: Policy → Standard → Process → SOP → Work Instruction. Each layer adds detail and removes freedom.

    🧩 Process Hierarchy & SIPOC

    Processes are decomposed in levels so the right audience sees the right detail.

    LevelNameTypical artefact
    L0Value chain / process areaEnterprise process map
    L1Process groupOrder-to-Cash
    L2ProcessInvoice Management
    L3Sub-process (activity flow)BPMN diagram
    L4Task / work instructionSOP step, screen guide

    SIPOC (Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, Customers) is a one-page, high-level view used to set scope before any detailed modelling.

    sipocSupplier:  Vendor, Warehouse
Input:     Purchase order, goods receipt note, vendor invoice
Process:   Receive invoice > 3-way match > approve > pay
Output:    Payment, remittance advice, ledger entry
Customer:  Vendor, Finance Controller, Auditor
    📌
    Define the start trigger and end state first. Most scoping disputes disappear once both are written down.

    📏 Why Standardize? (and When Not To)

    Benefits

    • Consistent quality and customer experience
    • Faster onboarding and cross-cover
    • Audit and compliance evidence
    • Baseline for measuring and improving
    • Prerequisite for automation

    Risks of over-standardizing

    • Kills useful local variation
    • Rigid rules for judgement-heavy work
    • Documents nobody follows
    • Slows innovation and exception handling

    Rule of thumb: standardize the routine, enable the exception. Stable, high-volume, rule-based work is a strong candidate; creative or highly variable work needs guidelines, not scripts.

    IdentifyDiscoverAnalyzeRedesignImplementMonitor

    The pills above are the classic BPM lifecycle; standardization sits in *Redesign* and *Implement*, and conformance checking in *Monitor*.

    BPMN 2.0 Essentials

    Reading and drawing standard BPMN: events, tasks, gateways, flows, pools and lanes.

    📐 BPMN 2.0 Core Elements

    BPMN 2.0 (OMG, ISO/IEC 19510) is the common visual language for business processes. Every diagram is built from four families of flow objects plus connectors and swimlanes.

    ElementShapeUse
    EventCircleSomething that happens: start (thin), intermediate (double), end (thick)
    ActivityRounded rectangleWork done: task, sub-process, call activity
    GatewayDiamondControls divergence/convergence of flow
    Sequence flowSolid arrowOrder of flow objects inside one pool
    Message flowDashed arrow, open headCommunication between different pools
    AssociationDotted lineLinks text/artifacts/data to elements
    Pool / LaneContainerParticipant (pool) and role/department (lane)
    Data object / storePage / cylinderInformation used or produced

    Task types

    UserManualServiceScriptBusiness RuleSendReceive
    💡
    Label tasks verb + noun ("Approve invoice"), events with a state or trigger ("Invoice received"), and gateways as questions ("Amount > 10k?").

    🔀 Gateways You Must Know

    GatewayMarkerSemantics
    Exclusive (XOR)X or emptyExactly one outgoing path; first true condition wins
    Parallel (AND)+All paths fire; join waits for all
    Inclusive (OR)OOne or more paths whose conditions are true; join waits for the active ones
    Event-basedPentagon in circleWaits; the first event that occurs decides the path
    example# Invoice approval (one pool, two lanes)
(Start) Invoice received
  [AP Clerk]  Register invoice  >  Match to PO
  <XOR> Match OK?
     yes > [AP Clerk] Schedule payment > (End) Invoice paid
     no  > [Buyer]    Resolve discrepancy > back to Match to PO
    ⚠️
    Never join an XOR split with a parallel join: the join waits forever for a token that never arrives (deadlock).

    🏊 Pools, Lanes and Message Flow

    A pool is one participant (a company, system or customer). Lanes subdivide a pool by role. Sequence flow may cross lanes but never pool boundaries; across pools only message flow is allowed.

    • Collaboration diagram: two or more pools exchanging messages.
    • Black-box pool: the customer is shown as an empty pool because its internals are unknown.
    • Keep the main path left-to-right and avoid crossing lines.

    Advanced BPMN 2.0

    Event types, boundary events, sub-processes, compensation, multi-instance and modelling style rules.

    ⚡ Event Types and Positions

    TriggerStartIntermediate catchIntermediate throwBoundaryEnd
    NoneYes-Yes (milestone)-Yes
    MessageYesYesYesYesYes
    TimerYesYes-Yes-
    ErrorEvent sub-process--Yes (interrupting)Yes
    SignalYesYesYesYesYes
    EscalationEvent sub-process-YesYesYes
    ConditionalYesYes-Yes-
    Terminate----Yes

    Interrupting vs non-interrupting

    Interrupting boundary (solid ring)

    • Cancels the host activity when triggered
    • Use: timeout, error, cancellation
    • Example: payment timer 30 days then cancel order

    Non-interrupting (dashed ring)

    • Host activity keeps running
    • Use: reminders, escalation, notifications
    • Example: send reminder after 3 days, task continues
    📌
    A signal is broadcast to any listener; a message targets a specific receiver. Use signals for "market closed" style announcements.

    📦 Sub-processes, Reuse and Special Constructs

    ConstructMarkerWhen to use
    Embedded sub-process+Hide detail; shares data with parent
    Call activityThick borderReuse a global, independently owned process
    Event sub-processDotted borderHandle events anywhere inside the parent scope
    TransactionDouble borderAll-or-nothing group with cancel/compensation
    Ad-hoc sub-processTilde ~Tasks with no fixed order
    Multi-instanceThree barsRun for each item (parallel ||| or sequential =)
    LoopCircular arrowRepeat until condition

    Compensation undoes already-completed work (for example, *Refund payment* compensates *Charge card*). It is not a rollback; it is a business-defined reversal.

    pattern# Order to delivery with reminders
Task: Pay invoice
  Boundary timer (non-interrupting, 3 days) > Send reminder
  Boundary timer (interrupting, 30 days)     > Cancel order > (End: Order cancelled)
Multi-instance (parallel) over each order line: Reserve stock

    ✍️ Modelling Style and Conformance

    • One start event per process level; every path ends in an explicit end event named for the end state.
    • Split and merge gateways in matched pairs; label every XOR outgoing flow.
    • Do not use gateways as tasks and do not put conditions on flows leaving a parallel gateway.
    • Keep diagrams to ~7-12 shapes per page; push detail into sub-processes.
    Conformance levelPurpose
    DescriptiveSimple business documentation
    AnalyticFull event/gateway semantics for analysis and simulation
    Common executableEngine-ready with data mappings and technical attributes
    💡
    Design for the audience: executives need L1-L2, analysts L3, engineers need executable BPMN with service tasks and data.

    Value Stream Mapping

    Mapping material and information flow, computing lead time and flow efficiency, and designing a leaner future state.

    🗺️ What is VSM?

    Value Stream Mapping draws every step, queue and information signal from customer request to delivery, separating value-added (VA) from non-value-added (NVA) time. It originated in Toyota's lean system and was popularised by Rother and Shook in *Learning to See*.

    MetricMeaningFormula / note
    Cycle time (C/T)Time to complete one unit at a stepMeasured by observation
    Process time (P/T)Hands-on value-adding timeSum across steps
    Wait / queue timeTime work sits idleUsually the biggest bucket
    Lead time (L/T)Request to delivery, end to endSum of P/T + wait
    Takt timePace needed to meet demandAvailable time / customer demand
    Process cycle efficiencyShare of lead time that adds valuePCE = VA time / lead time
    %C&AComplete and accurate: share of work passed on without reworkFrom downstream customer
    Changeover timeTime to switch work typeReduces batch sizes when cut
    💡
    Typical service-process PCE is under 10%. Any value above 25% already signals a lean process.

    🛠️ How to Build a Current-State Map

    1. Pick one product/service family and define start and end.
    2. Walk the process (gemba) backwards from the customer; do not rely on documents.
    3. Capture per step: C/T, %C&A, staff, systems, batch size, queue before it.
    4. Draw the information flow (orders, schedules, forecasts) above the material flow.
    5. Add a timeline ladder at the bottom: waits on the upper rung, process time on the lower.
    worked exampleTakt = 28,800 s available / 120 requests = 240 s per request
Steps (P/T):  Intake 5 min | Review 20 min | Approve 10 min | Issue 5 min = 40 min
Waits:        Intake-Review 1 day, Review-Approve 2 days, Approve-Issue 4 h
Lead time ~= 3.5 days (in working hours ~ 28 h + 40 min)
PCE = 40 min / (28 h x 60 + 40) ~= 2.3%  >  97% of the time is waiting

    🎯 Future State and VSM vs BPMN

    • Eliminate or combine steps that exist only to hand off work.
    • Introduce flow and pull: limit work-in-progress, release work at takt.
    • Level the load; cut batch sizes; fix quality at source to lift %C&A.
    • Add a kaizen burst icon wherever the plan needs an improvement event.

    VSM

    • Focus: time, queues, flow efficiency
    • Shows information and material flow
    • Best for waste and lead-time reduction

    BPMN

    • Focus: logic, roles, exceptions
    • Shows decisions and messages
    • Best for standardization and automation
    📌
    Use both: VSM to find where time is lost, BPMN to define the new standard flow.

    SOP Design

    Structure, write, test, approve and maintain Standard Operating Procedures that people actually follow.

    📘 Anatomy of a Good SOP

    SectionContent
    HeaderTitle, ID, version, owner, effective date, approver
    Purpose & scopeWhy it exists; what is in and out
    Roles (RACI)Who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed
    DefinitionsTerms and abbreviations
    PrerequisitesAccess, tools, inputs, training
    ProcedureNumbered steps with decision points
    ExceptionsWhat to do when it does not fit; escalation path
    Controls & KPIsChecks, quality criteria, SLAs
    References & recordsRelated documents, forms, retention
    Revision historyWhat changed, when, why
    SOP formatBest for
    Numbered stepsShort linear tasks
    Hierarchical stepsComplex tasks with sub-steps
    Flowchart / BPMNMany decisions and handoffs
    ChecklistInspections, pre-flight, quality checks
    PlaybookVariable, judgement-based work

    ✏️ Writing Rules and Example

    • One action per step, imperative verb first (Select, Verify).
    • Name the role and the system in every step.
    • Use measurable terms: within 2 working days, not promptly.
    • State decision rules explicitly; put warnings before the step they affect.
    • Keep it short; link to work instructions for click-level detail.
    SOP excerptSOP-AP-014 v2.1  Process vendor invoice            Owner: AP Manager
4.1 [AP Clerk] Register invoice in ERP within 1 working day of receipt.
4.2 [AP Clerk] Run 3-way match (PO, goods receipt, invoice).
4.3 If variance <= 2% or 100 USD, then approve automatically; else route to Buyer.
4.4 [AP Supervisor] Release payment run on Tuesday and Friday.
Control: 100% of invoices above 10,000 USD get second approval.
    ⚠️
    If steps need more than one page of prose, switch to a flowchart plus a checklist. Long paragraphs are skipped.

    🔄 SOP Lifecycle and Change Control

    DraftSME reviewWalk-through / pilotApproveTrainPublishAuditReview & retire
    • Test the SOP by having a new employee perform it unaided; every question they ask is a defect.
    • Version control: one source of truth, controlled copies, obsolete versions withdrawn.
    • Review on a fixed cycle (often 12 months) and on any trigger: incident, audit finding, system change.
    • Measure: adherence rate, error rate, time-to-competence of new hires.
    ✅
    A good SOP lets a trained stranger deliver the same result as the expert, without asking the expert.

    Governance, DMN & Harmonization

    Expert view: process architecture, ownership, standard-with-variants, decision modelling and measuring conformance.

    🏛️ Process Governance and Architecture

    • Process owner: accountable end to end for performance, design and compliance of one process.
    • Process council / CoE: sets modelling standards, repository rules and prioritises changes.
    • Process architecture: the L0-L4 map plus ownership, KPIs, risks, systems and regulations mapped to each process.
    • Global process template with controlled local variants (typical target: 80% common, 20% local).
    Maturity levelCharacteristic
    1 InitialAd hoc, hero-dependent
    2 ManagedDocumented locally
    3 DefinedStandard, owned and measured
    4 Quantitatively managedData-driven control
    5 OptimizingContinuous, automated improvement
    💡
    Frameworks to know: CMMI, APQC PCF (process classification), Hammer PEMM, ISO 9001 process approach.

    📊 DMN Decision Tables and CMMN

    DMN (Decision Model and Notation) separates *decision logic* from the process so rules change without redrawing the flow. CMMN models knowledge-work cases where the order of tasks is not fixed.

    DMN decision table - hit policy U (unique)Customer tier | Order value  | Discount
Gold          | >= 1000      | 15%
Gold          | < 1000       | 10%
Silver        | any          | 5%
Other         | any          | 0%
    Hit policyMeaning
    Unique (U)Exactly one rule may match
    First (F)First matching rule wins (ordered)
    Any (A)Several may match but must give same result
    Collect (C)All matches returned, optional sum/min/max
    📌
    Business Rule Tasks in BPMN call DMN tables, so policy owners can update thresholds without developers.

    🎤 Measuring Conformance and Interview Points

    PointDetail
    What it isChecking that actual execution follows the approved standard process.
    Why it is usedStandards only create value when followed; deviations signal training gaps, bad design or workarounds.
    How it worksCompare event logs or audits to the model: conformance rate, number of variants, rework loops, SLA breaches.
    How to use itSet a target (e.g. 90% happy-path), analyse top deviations, fix the standard if the deviation is smarter.
    Common mistakesStandardizing before understanding variation; documenting the ideal not the real; no owner; no review cycle.
    Real-world usageA shared-service centre cut invoice variants from 140 to 12 and halved lead time after harmonizing five country processes.
    Interview pointsExplain SOP vs work instruction; XOR vs OR; interrupting vs non-interrupting events; how you decide what not to standardize.

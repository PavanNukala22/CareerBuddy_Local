    Decomposition & Workstreams

    Breaking multi-year initiatives into deliverable, owned workstreams.

    🧱 Decomposing a program

    Slice by outcomes and capabilities, not by layers. Check coverage (100% rule), ownership and minimal coupling.

    1. Define outcomes and benefits
2. Map capabilities / value streams
3. Form workstreams with one accountable owner
4. Slice into epics and increments (vertical)
5. Define interface contracts
6. Baseline and trace benefits to workstreams
    💡
    Cycles in the dependency map mean re-slice or add an interface contract.

    Decomposition Methods

    From strategic outcome to workstreams, epics and vertical slices.

    🧱 Outcome → capability → workstream

    Start with benefits, map the capabilities that create them, then form workstreams with one accountable owner and minimal coupling. Check the 100% rule: the structure covers all scope and nothing outside it.

    TechniqueUse forWatch for
    WBS (deliverable-based)Complete scope coverageActivity lists disguised as deliverables
    Capability mapStable business ownershipToo abstract to schedule
    Story mapJourney-based slicingHuge maps with no release cut
    Event stormingComplex domain boundariesNeeds skilled facilitation

    🍰 Slice vertically, not horizontally

    A horizontal slice (all databases, then all APIs) delivers no usable value until the end. A vertical slice delivers a thin end-to-end journey.

    Example: digital onboarding for a bank
Slice 1: identity check + account opening for one product
Slice 2: add second product and document upload
Slice 3: add risk scoring and manual review queue
Each slice is releasable and measurable

    Value Stream Mapping in Depth

    Measure the flow, find the constraint and size the improvement.

    ⏱️ VSM step by step

    Map from trigger to value delivered.

    1. Define start trigger and end value event
2. Walk the real flow (gemba), not the documented one
3. Record process time, wait time, %C&A per step
4. Compute lead time and flow efficiency
5. Identify the constraint and handoff waste
6. Design the future state and quantify benefit
    💡
    Flow efficiency = process time ÷ lead time. Typical knowledge-work flows are often in the 5–15% range, so wait time is the main lever.

    🧮 Worked example: loan approval

    Process time 4 days, lead time 32 days: flow efficiency 12.5%. Removing a weekly approval board (average wait 5 days) and automating document checks (wait 8 days) cuts lead time to 19 days.

    Application intake0.5270%
    Document check1860%
    Credit decision1.5585%
    Approval board0.5595%
    Funds release0.50.598%

    Dependencies, Release Trains & Forecasting

    Sequencing, integrating and forecasting a multi-year program.

    🔗 Managing dependencies

    Classify dependencies, then reduce them by re-slicing, ownership change or interface contracts.

    TypeExampleResponse
    Finish-to-startData migration before reportingSequence, protect float
    Shared resourceOne architect for two streamsCentral capacity decision
    ExternalVendor API or regulation dateBuffer, early integration
    CyclicA needs B needs ARe-slice or add contract

    🚆 Release train cadence

    An ART (Agile Release Train) plans, builds and demos on a fixed cadence. PI Planning typically runs two days: business context, team breakouts, dependency mapping, risk ROAM, confidence vote.

    💡
    ROAM: Resolved, Owned, Accepted, Mitigated. A low confidence vote (below about 3 of 5) triggers re-planning.

    📉 Forecasting and benefits tracking

    Forecast with historical throughput and Monte Carlo ranges (for example "85% likely by week 22"), not single dates. Track benefits after release against baseline measures and map each workstream to at least one benefit.

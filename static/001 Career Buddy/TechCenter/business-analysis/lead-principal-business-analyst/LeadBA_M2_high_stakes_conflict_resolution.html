    Negotiation Frameworks

    Principled negotiation, BATNA/ZOPA and conflict modes for executive-level disputes.

    🤝 Principled negotiation

    Fisher & Ury’s four rules: separate people from the problem; focus on interests, not positions; generate options for mutual gain; use objective criteria.

    TermMeaning
    PositionWhat someone demands
    InterestWhy they want it
    BATNABest alternative if no deal
    ZOPAOverlap of acceptable outcomes
    💡
    Scenario: CFO wants Q1 delivery, CTO wants a refactor first — both want low risk and predictable growth.

    ⚔️ Thomas-Kilmann modes

    Choose a mode by stakes and time.

    ModeUse when
    CompetingEmergency, vital principle
    CollaboratingBoth interests matter, time exists
    CompromisingModerate stakes, time pressure
    AvoidingTrivial or cooling-off
    AccommodatingPreserving goodwill, low stakes

    Prioritisation Conflicts

    Turning executive vs engineering disagreements into transparent trade-offs.

    ⚖️ Resolution steps

    A repeatable path for priority clashes.

    1. Clarify the decision and its owner (RACI)
2. Surface interests behind each position
3. Agree criteria before scoring options
4. Quantify with Cost of Delay / WSJF, risk, capacity
5. Present 2–3 options with a recommendation
6. Decide, log (ADR / decision record), communicate
    💡
    Translate tech debt into risk, incident cost and delay for the C-suite.

    Pick the lightest model that fits the decision.

    ModelBest for
    Fist-of-fiveFast read of agreement

    DelphiDistributed experts, anonymity

    Diagnosing the Conflict

    Classify the dispute, map stakeholders and surface interests before proposing solutions.

    🔍 Types of conflict

    Treat each type differently: data resolves task conflict, structure resolves process/role conflict, and mediation addresses relationship conflict.

    TypeSignalBest tool
    Task / factualDisagreement on scope, dates, riskData, options pack
    Process / roleUnclear who decidesDecision-rights matrix
    Values / prioritiesDifferent success definitionsInterest mapping, shared criteria
    RelationshipPersonal frictionMediation, working agreements

    🗺️ Interest-mapping steps

    Before any negotiation, map positions to interests.

    1. List each party and their stated position
2. Ask "What would that achieve?" and "What worries you?"
3. Record interests (risk, cost, speed, reputation, compliance)
4. Mark shared and compatible interests
5. Identify each party’s BATNA and the likely ZOPA
6. Draft options that serve several interests
    💡
    Example: CFO "must ship in Q1" (interest: revenue predictability) and CTO "refactor first" (interest: outage risk). A phased release with a hardened core serves both.

    Executives vs Engineering

    Translating technical risk into business terms and using economics to prioritise.

    💱 Translating tech debt for the C-suite

    Replace architecture jargon with exposure, cost and delay.

    Engineering saysExecutive-ready framing
    "The platform is brittle"Three Sev-1 incidents last quarter cost about $240k in lost orders
    "We need to refactor"12-week investment cuts change lead time from 6 weeks to 2
    "Unsupported library"Support ends in Q3; compliance finding likely

    🧮 Cost of Delay and WSJF worked example

    WSJF = Cost of Delay ÷ Job size. Cost of Delay combines user/business value, time criticality and risk reduction.

    ItemCoD scoreSizeWSJF
    Promo feature2182.6
    Platform hardening34132.6
    Compliance fix4058.0
    💡
    Result: the compliance fix goes first, then the two tie, and the tie is broken by dependency (hardening unblocks promo). Agree the scoring criteria before scoring.

    📦 The options pack

    Present two or three options with scope, time, cost, risk and recommendation so the decision-owner chooses between trade-offs, not between people.

    Negotiation, Consensus & Escalation

    Running the conversation, recording the decision and escalating well.

    🎙️ Facilitating a high-stakes meeting

    Neutral structure protects the quieter voices.

    1. State the decision, owner and deadline
2. Agree ground rules and timeboxes
3. Each party restates the other’s interests (active listening)
4. Silent option generation, then group clustering
5. Score options against agreed criteria
6. Test consent (fist-of-five), record concerns and review date

    📈 Escalation ladder and decision record

    Escalate only after structured attempts; arrive with facts, options and a recommendation.

    LevelActionEvidence
    1Direct conversationShared problem statement
    2Facilitated sessionInterest map, options
    3Decision-owner briefImpact, options, recommendation, date
    4Executive forumSame pack plus attempts made
    💡
    Log every outcome in an ADR/decision record (context, options, decision, rationale, owner, date). It stops re-litigation and supports audit.

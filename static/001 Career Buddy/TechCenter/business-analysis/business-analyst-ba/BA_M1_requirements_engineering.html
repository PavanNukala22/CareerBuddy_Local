    Course Overview

    Requirements Engineering for Business Analysts: from first conversation to a verified, traceable specification.

    Course Overview

    This course teaches you to elicit, specify, analyse, prioritise, verify and validate requirements to BABOK and ISO 29148 standards. You will learn to turn vague stakeholder wishes into atomic, testable requirements, choose the right document (BRD, FRD, user stories, use cases), quantify non-functional requirements, and run gap analysis and SWOT on current versus target state.

    Duration: approx. 12-14 hours plus a 60-minute assessment. Level: intermediate to advanced.

    Learning Objectives

    1. Select and combine elicitation techniques and avoid proxy-requirement traps.
    2. Write atomic, unambiguous, testable requirements and acceptance criteria in Given/When/Then.
    3. Distinguish BRD from FRD and classify business, stakeholder, solution and transition requirements.
    4. Quantify NFRs using quality-attribute scenarios and measurable targets.
    5. Apply verification versus validation, requirement smell detection and peer inspection.
    6. Perform gap analysis and SWOT and prioritise with MoSCoW, Kano and weighted scoring.

    Prerequisites

    • Basic understanding of the software or business-change lifecycle (Waterfall and Agile).
    • Comfort with stakeholder communication and note-taking.
    • Familiarity with spreadsheets and simple diagrams.
    • No programming knowledge required.

    🎤 Elicitation Techniques

    Elicitation is drawing out needs, not taking orders. Combine several techniques; no single one finds everything.

    TechniqueBest forWatch out for
    InterviewsDeep, individual context; sensitive topicsInterviewer bias; one person's view
    WorkshopsAligning many stakeholders, resolving conflicts fastLoud voices dominate; needs a facilitator
    Observation (job shadowing)Real vs. documented process; tacit knowledgeObserver effect — people behave differently
    Document analysisExisting policies, reports, legacy specsDocs are often outdated
    Surveys / prototypesMany users; validating UI ideas earlyShallow answers; leading questions
    💡
    Ask why five times. “We need a report” usually hides a decision the user can't make today.

    📝 User Stories & Acceptance Criteria

    A user story captures who, what, and why. Quality is checked with INVEST: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable.

    storyAs a returning customer
I want to reorder a past purchase in one click
So that I save time on repeat orders.

# Acceptance criteria (Given / When / Then)
Given I am logged in and have order history
When I click "Reorder" on a past order
Then all in-stock items are added to my cart
And out-of-stock items are flagged with a message
    ⚠️
    Avoid solution-baked stories (“I want a dropdown…”). Describe the need; let design pick the control.

    BRD vs FRD

    📄 BRD vs FRD

    BRD — Business Requirements

    • Audience: sponsors, business owners
    • Answers why & what at business level
    • Objectives, scope, KPIs, constraints

    FRD — Functional Requirements

    • Audience: designers, developers, testers
    • Answers how the system behaves
    • Functions, rules, data, interfaces, NFRs
    requirementBR-012 Reduce invoice processing time from 5 days to 2 days by Q4.
  FR-012.1 System shall auto-match invoices to POs within 60 seconds.
  FR-012.2 Mismatches > 2% shall route to an AP supervisor queue.
    📌
    Good requirements are unambiguous, atomic, testable, and traceable. Replace “fast”, “user-friendly”, “etc.” with measurable terms.

    🧭 The BABOK Framework (IIBA)

    The IIBA BABOK Guide v3 is the global standard of practice. It organizes the work into six knowledge areas; real projects run tasks from several of them at once.

    Knowledge areaTypical tasks
    Business Analysis Planning & MonitoringPlan BA approach, stakeholder engagement, governance, information management
    Elicitation & CollaborationPrepare for, conduct, and confirm elicitation; communicate BA information
    Requirements Life Cycle MgmtTrace, maintain, prioritize, assess changes to, and approve requirements
    Strategy AnalysisAnalyze current state, define future state, assess risks, define change strategy
    Requirements Analysis & Design DefinitionSpecify and model, verify, validate requirements; define requirements architecture
    Solution EvaluationMeasure and analyze solution performance, recommend actions to increase value

    Business Analysis Core Concept Model (BACCM)

    ChangeNeedSolutionStakeholderValueContext

    BABOK defines a requirement as a usable representation of a need. Every technique you use should connect back to one of these six concepts.

    Verification vs Validation

    ✅ Verification vs Validation

    Verification

    “Did we write the requirement correctly?” Check for clarity, completeness, consistency, testability, and conformance to templates.

    Validation

    “Are these the right requirements?” Confirm they deliver stakeholder value and trace to business objectives.

    • Verification techniques: peer review, inspection, checklists
    • Validation techniques: walkthroughs, prototypes, UAT, acceptance sign-off

    🔍 Quality Characteristics of a Requirement

    CharacteristicBad exampleBetter
    UnambiguousThe system should be user-friendlyNew users complete checkout in under 3 minutes without help
    AtomicValidate and save and emailThree separate requirements
    TestableHandle many usersSupport 5,000 concurrent users
    ConsistentReport monthly / report weeklyOne agreed frequency
    FeasibleZero downtime ever99.9% availability

    🎯 Gap Analysis & SWOT

    Gap analysis compares current state to future state across people, process, technology, and data. SWOT frames internal strengths/weaknesses against external opportunities/threats.

    CapabilityCurrentTargetGap / action
    Invoice matchingManual, 5 daysAutomated, 2 daysBuild matching service; retrain AP team
    ReportingExcel monthlyLive dashboardData feed + BI licence

    🗺️ Advanced Elicitation: Story Mapping, Event Storming & Bias

    For complex domains, move beyond interviews. User story mapping arranges the user journey horizontally (activities → tasks) and slices releases vertically, so scope is a walking skeleton, not a flat backlog. Event storming has domain experts place orange sticky notes of past-tense domain events (OrderPlaced, PaymentFailed) on a timeline, then expose commands, actors, policies and hotspots.

    Bias / trapSymptomCountermeasure
    AnchoringEvery later answer revolves around the first ideaElicit independently first, then compare
    Solution jumpingStakeholder dictates screensAsk 'what problem does that solve?'
    Curse of knowledgeExperts skip 'obvious' stepsAsk a novice to walk through; observe
    Confirmation biasBA only records supporting factsSeek disconfirming cases and edge cases
    Proxy requirementsManager speaks for usersInterview or observe actual users
    💡
    Triangulate: confirm every critical requirement with at least two independent sources (e.g., a stakeholder statement plus data or observation).

    🧪 Specification by Example, Gherkin & Requirement Smells

    gherkinFeature: Order approval
  Scenario Outline: Approval depends on total and customer tier
    Given a <tier> customer places an order of <total>
    Then the order status is <status>
    Examples:
      | tier     | total  | status           |
      | Gold     | 600    | APPROVED         |
      | Standard | 500    | APPROVED         |
      | Standard | 500.01 | PENDING_APPROVAL |

    Concrete examples remove ambiguity and become executable acceptance tests. Watch for requirement smells:

    vague adjectives (fast, easy)passive voice (hides the actor)'and/or' compound'etc.' / 'such as'comparatives without baselineunbounded listsescape clauses (if possible)
    📌
    Apply quality attributes from ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148: necessary, appropriate, unambiguous, complete, singular, feasible, verifiable, correct, conforming.

    📐 Quantifying NFRs with Quality-Attribute Scenarios

    Write each NFR as a scenario: source → stimulus → environment → response → measure.

    scenarioSource: 2,000 concurrent shoppers   Stimulus: search request
Environment: peak sale hour   Response: results rendered
Measure: p95 latency < 2 s, error rate < 0.1%
    AttributeMeasurable form
    Availability99.9% monthly (about 43 min downtime), RTO 1 h, RPO 15 min
    SecurityMFA for admin roles; audit log retained 7 years
    Usability80% of new users complete task unaided in 3 min
    ScalabilityHandle 3× load with < 10% latency increase

    🎤 Elicitation: Interviews, Workshops and Observation

    PointDetail
    What it isStructured ways to draw requirements out of people and systems: interviews, facilitated workshops, observation (job shadowing), document analysis, surveys and prototyping.
    Why it is usedStakeholders rarely state requirements; they state symptoms and solutions. Technique choice controls coverage, bias and cost of discovery.
    How it worksInterviews give depth on one person; workshops give breadth and fast conflict resolution; observation reveals work as done rather than as described; documents show rules and history; surveys give scale. Triangulating two or more techniques exposes contradictions.
    How to use itPick by question: unknown domain → interviews and observation; conflicting needs → workshop; many users → survey; unclear process → shadowing. Prepare a guide, record decisions, play back notes within 24 hours for confirmation.
    Common mistakesAsking leading questions; interviewing only senior people; trusting what people say over what they do; skipping confirmation; running workshops without an objective, agenda or decision owner.
    Real-world usageA BA shadows three claims handlers for a day, finds a 5-step workaround nobody mentioned, then runs a 90-minute workshop to agree the future process.
    Interview pointsName two techniques you would combine and why; explain confirmation bias in elicitation; describe how you prepare, run and follow up a workshop.

    🧾 User Stories, Acceptance Criteria and Gherkin

    PointDetail
    What it isA user story states a need as As a role, I want a capability, so that a benefit; acceptance criteria and Gherkin scenarios make it testable.
    Why it is usedStories keep requirements small and value-focused; acceptance criteria remove ambiguity so developers, testers and the business share one definition of done for the story.
    How it worksINVEST checks story quality. Gherkin expresses criteria as Given context, When action, Then outcome. Scenario outlines with examples tables cover many data combinations without prose.
    How to use itWrite the benefit first, split by value slice (not by technical layer), add 3 to 7 scenarios including one negative and one boundary case, and review them in a three-amigos session (BA, developer, tester).
    Common mistakesTechnical tasks disguised as stories; vague words such as fast or user-friendly; only happy-path criteria; solution-prescriptive wording; stories too large to finish in a sprint.
    Real-world usageA checkout story gets scenarios for valid card, declined card, expired card and a 3-second response boundary; automation turns them into regression tests.
    Interview pointsRecite INVEST, convert a vague requirement into measurable Gherkin on the spot, and explain the difference between acceptance criteria and Definition of Done.

    Testing & Quality

    • ISO 29148 characteristics: necessary, unambiguous, singular, feasible, verifiable, complete, consistent, traceable.
    • Requirement smells: vague adjectives, passive voice, escape clauses (etc., as appropriate), compound statements.
    • Peer inspection: checklist review with author, reviewer and moderator roles.
    • Traceability coverage: every requirement links to a source, a design item and a test.
    📌
    Verification asks: did we write the requirements right? Validation asks: did we write the right requirements?

    Best Practices

    • Elicit from multiple sources and triangulate critical requirements.
    • State the need, not the solution (avoid naming widgets or technologies).
    • Make each requirement atomic with a unique ID and an owner.
    • Agree acceptance criteria before development starts.
    • Baseline requirements and manage every change through impact analysis.
    • Keep a glossary and data dictionary to prevent ambiguity.

    Troubleshooting & Interview Prep

    Fix common problems, prepare for interviews and consolidate learning.

    Common Errors & Troubleshooting

    SymptomLikely causeFix
    Developers keep asking what a requirement meansAmbiguous or non-atomic wordingRewrite to ISO 29148; add acceptance criteria and examples
    Scope creep after sign-offNo baseline or change controlBaseline, impact analysis, formal change requests
    Stakeholders disagree on prioritiesNo shared criteriaUse MoSCoW or weighted scoring in a facilitated workshop
    Delivered feature unusedRequirement from a proxy, not real usersObserve and interview actual users; validate with prototypes
    Tests miss requirementsNo traceabilityMaintain RTM and check coverage per release

    Interview Preparation

    • Q: Stakeholders ask for a faster system. What do you do? A: ask what outcome faster enables and define a measurable target.
    • Q: BRD versus FRD? A: BRD states business objectives and scope; FRD specifies detailed system behaviour.
    • Q: Verification versus validation? A: right requirements written correctly versus right requirements delivering value.
    • Q: How do you prioritise? A: MoSCoW, Kano and weighted scoring, agreed with stakeholders.
    • Behavioural: prepare a STAR story about resolving conflicting stakeholder requirements.

    Course Summary

    • Requirements are usable representations of needs; elicit broadly and triangulate.
    • Good requirements are atomic, unambiguous, testable and traceable.
    • NFRs need measurable quality-attribute scenarios.
    • Verify against standards, validate with stakeholders, and manage change from a baseline.

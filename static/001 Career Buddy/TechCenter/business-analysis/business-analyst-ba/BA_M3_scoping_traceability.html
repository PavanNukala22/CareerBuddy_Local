    Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM)

    Scope baselines, change control, program-level dependencies, and bidirectional traceability with coverage metrics.

    🔗 Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM)

    The RTM links each requirement forward to design, build, and test — and backward to the business objective.

    Business NeedReq IDDesignTest CaseStatus
    BR-012 Faster invoicingFR-012.1Match serviceTC-044Passed
    BR-012 Faster invoicingFR-012.2Routing rulesTC-045In test
    BR-019 Audit complianceFR-019.1Audit logTC-051Not started
    ✅
    An untraced requirement is gold-plating; an untested one is a risk. The RTM exposes both.

    📏 Scope Baseline & Change Control

    • In scope / out of scope lists, plus assumptions, constraints, dependencies.
    • Baseline approved scope; every change goes through a change request.
    • Change request: description, reason, impact on time/cost/risk, decision.
    ⚠️
    Scope creep rarely arrives as one big ask — it arrives as “small tweaks”. Log each one.

    Requirements Life Cycle Management

    Scope baselines, change control, program-level dependencies, and bidirectional traceability with coverage metrics.

    🔄 Requirements Life Cycle Management

    BABOK Requirements Life Cycle Management covers five tasks: trace, maintain, prioritize, assess changes, approve. Typical requirement states: Draft → Reviewed → Approved → Implemented → Verified → Retired.

    💡
    Maintain attributes per requirement: ID, source, owner, priority, status, version, risk, and rationale.

    💥 Impact Analysis

    QuestionLook at
    Which other requirements depend on this one?RTM, dependency map
    Which screens, interfaces, and data change?Design docs, ERD
    Which tests must be re-run?Test case links
    Effort, cost, and schedule effect?Estimates, plan
    Who must retrain or sign off?Stakeholder register
    ⚠️
    A change request without an impact analysis is just a wish. Always present options: accept, defer, reject, or trade for equivalent scope.

    Program-level Scope: WBS, Dependencies & Critical Path

    Scope baselines, change control, program-level dependencies, and bidirectional traceability with coverage metrics.

    🧩 Program-level Scope: WBS, Dependencies & Critical Path

    A Work Breakdown Structure decomposes scope to deliverables (100% rule: the WBS includes 100% of the scope — no more, no less). Dependencies come in four types: FS (finish-to-start), SS, FF, SF — plus internal vs external and hard vs soft.

    TechniquePurpose
    Critical pathLongest dependent chain; zero float; sets minimum duration
    Dependency matrixSpot cross-team coupling early
    Assumption / risk logTrack what must be true; owners and expiry dates
    Scope statement exclusionsExplicit 'not in scope' prevents silent expansion
    ⚠️
    Scope creep vs scope change: creep is uncontrolled. A well-managed approved change is healthy — measure the percentage of changes that went through change control.

    📈 Advanced RTM: Bidirectional Coverage & Metrics

    DirectionQuestion answered
    Forward (need → test)Is every need designed, built, and tested?
    Backward (test → need)Does every test and feature trace to an approved need (no gold-plating)?
    HorizontalWhich requirements affect each other?
    Excel coverage checks// Requirements with no test case
=COUNTIFS(RTM[TestID],"")
// Test coverage %
=1 - COUNTIFS(RTM[TestID],"")/COUNTA(RTM[ReqID])
// Lookup status for a requirement
=XLOOKUP(A2, Tests[ReqID], Tests[Result], "No test")

    Baseline the RTM at each approved release; report coverage %, orphan tests, and volatility (changes per requirement per sprint).

    🔗 Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM)

    PointDetail
    What it isA structured table or live query linking each business objective to requirements, design or stories, tests and status, in both directions.
    Why it is usedIt proves coverage, supports impact analysis, finds orphans and gold plating, and gives audit evidence.
    How it worksForward tracing follows objective → requirement → build → test. Backward tracing follows test → requirement → objective. Coverage and volatility metrics are calculated from the links.
    How to use itAssign unique IDs; link at creation, not before audit; maintain in Jira link types or a controlled sheet; review untested and orphan items in every refinement.
    Common mistakesBuilding it at the end; tracing too deep for low-risk work; no ownership; duplicate IDs; link updates lost during rework.
    Real-world usageIn a regulated release, a BA answers "show me how REQ-21 was tested" by filtering one live table in minutes.
    Interview pointsExplain forward versus backward tracing; calculate coverage and volatility; say how you keep the RTM current.

    🛡️ Change Control and the CCB

    PointDetail
    What it isA governed process where changes after baseline are raised, analysed, decided and recorded by a Change Control Board.
    Why it is usedIt keeps scope, cost and schedule intentional and prevents creep while still allowing valuable change.
    How it worksA change request records the need, rationale, impact on scope, schedule, cost, risk and tests; the CCB approves, rejects, defers or requests information; approved changes update the baseline and RTM.
    How to use itUse a CR template, require impact analysis before review, define an expedited path for emergencies, and publish decisions with rationale.
    Common mistakesDecisions without impact data; no named decider; changes accepted informally; baseline not updated after approval.
    Real-world usageA late regulatory field is analysed at +6 days and risk of non-compliance if rejected; CCB approves with a moved date.
    Interview pointsDifferentiate creep from approved change; list CR fields; describe emergency change handling.

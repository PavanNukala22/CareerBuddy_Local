    👥 Personas & Expectation Management

    Map stakeholders on a Power / Interest grid: Manage closely (high/high), Keep satisfied (high power), Keep informed (high interest), Monitor.

    🧑‍💼
    Personas
    Fictional users with goals, pains, context — keep design user-centred.
    🗣️
    Facilitation
    Agenda, timebox, parking lot, capture decisions and actions.
    🤝
    Expectations
    Say what is not included; confirm in writing after meetings.

    🧑‍🏫 Facilitating a Requirements Workshop

    PhaseActions
    PrepareClear objective, right people, agenda, pre-reads, scribe
    OpenState goal, ground rules, timebox, parking lot
    RunRound-robin input, visual models, test ideas against objectives
    CloseSummarize decisions, owners, open issues; send notes within 24 h
    💡
    Quiet attendees often hold the key facts. Use silent brainstorming (sticky notes) before discussion.

    Conflict, Communication & Sign-off

    ⚔️ Conflict, Communication & Sign-off

    • Conflicting requirements: restate each need, find the shared goal, escalate the trade-off with options and impacts.
    • RACI clarifies who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed.
    • Written confirmation after every key decision protects everyone.

Owner: Priya (Product)   Impact: Sales target for Q3 re-forecast.
Open: Confirm payment-gateway capacity by Fri — Raj.

    🎯 Advanced Stakeholder Analysis: Salience, Influence & Resistance

    The Salience model scores stakeholders on power, legitimacy, urgency. Definitive stakeholders have all three and need immediate engagement.

    Resistance patternTypical causeResponse
    Passive (no-shows)Low perceived value, overloadShow WIIFM; shorten asks
    Active oppositionLoss of status, control, or jobs1-to-1 listening; involve in design
    Hidden agendaCompeting prioritiesMake trade-offs explicit with sponsor
    Change fatigueToo many initiativesSequence and bundle changes
    💡
    Create an influence map (who influences whom). The informal network often matters more than the org chart.

    Negotiation & Executive Communication

    Personas, workshops, conflict, resistance, negotiation, and executive-level communication.

    📣 Negotiation & Executive Communication

    • BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front): put the decision and ask in the first sentence.
    • Pyramid principle: answer first, then 3 supporting arguments, then evidence.
    • SBAR: Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation — ideal for escalations.
    • Negotiate interests, not positions; expand options; use objective criteria (cost, risk, benefit).
    escalationSituation: Payment API change slips Release 2 by 3 weeks.
Background: Vendor deprecated v1; v2 certification takes 15 days.
Assessment: Missing the date costs ~$40k/week in manual processing.
Recommendation: Approve contractor budget ($18k) to parallelise testing.

    🎙️ Interview Mastery: Funnel, Laddering & Active Listening

    • Funnel: start open (walk me through your day), narrow to specifics, finish with closed confirmation.
    • Laddering: ask why it matters, repeatedly, to move from feature to goal to value.
    • Active listening: paraphrase, summarise, use silence; note emotion words as risk signals.
    • Avoid leading questions; prefer open ones such as How do you track this today?
    💡
    Share a one-page summary within 24 hours and ask the interviewee to correct it; corrections surface hidden assumptions.

    Managing Up & Across: RAG Reporting and RACI vs RAPID

    🚦 Managing Up & Across: RAG Reporting and RACI vs RAPID

    ToolUse
    RAG statusGreen on track, Amber at risk (with recovery plan), Red off track (needs decision)
    RACIRole clarity in delivery work
    RAPIDDecision rights: Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, Decide
    Decision logWho decided what, when, and why
    ⚠️
    Report amber early. Watermelon reporting (green outside, red inside) destroys trust faster than bad news.

    🧭 Stakeholder Analysis, RACI and RAPID

    PointDetail
    What it isMethods to identify stakeholders, rate power, interest, legitimacy and urgency, and assign roles with RACI or decision rights with RAPID.
    Why it is usedEngagement effort is limited. Analysis targets it where influence and impact are highest and prevents surprise resistance.
    How it worksPower/interest sorts into manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed and monitor. Salience adds legitimacy and urgency. RACI assigns one Accountable per task; RAPID names who recommends, agrees, performs, gives input and decides.
    How to use itBuild a stakeholder register, review it each sprint, test the map with the sponsor, agree RACI at kickoff, and use RAPID for contested decisions.
    Common mistakesTreating the org chart as the influence map; multiple Accountables; one-time analysis; ignoring resistant stakeholders.
    Real-world usageA BA finds the real approver of a data policy is a senior analyst, not the sponsor, and engages them early.
    Interview pointsExplain salience classes; compare RACI and RAPID; give an example of changing strategy as influence shifted.

    🗣️ Workshop Facilitation and Negotiation

    PointDetail
    What it isThe practice of running structured group sessions and resolving competing demands through interest-based negotiation.
    Why it is usedWorkshops compress elicitation and decision making; negotiation resolves conflicts that would otherwise stall scope.
    How it worksFacilitation uses objective, agenda, ground rules, silent writing, time-boxing and a parking lot. Negotiation moves from positions to interests, uses BATNA and ZOPA and trades options.
    How to use itDefine the decision to be made, invite the right people, assign scribe and timekeeper, use structured turns, record decisions and owners, circulate minutes within 24 hours.
    Common mistakesNo clear objective; dominant voices; unprepared attendees; conceding too quickly; hiding impact of trade-offs.
    Real-world usageTwo directors want contradictory reports; the BA discovers one needs speed, the other accuracy, and designs a two-tier report.
    Interview pointsDescribe a difficult workshop; explain BATNA, ZOPA and interests; show a tactic for a dominant participant.

    Demand–Supply Fundamentals

    Treat BA capacity as a managed portfolio: forecast demand by skill and period, size supply honestly, and expose the gap before programs feel it.

    📊 The capacity equation

    Capacity planning matches supply (available BA hours by skill) to demand (BA effort that approved and probable work will consume) over a rolling horizon. Plan in skills × time, never in heads: 20 BAs is not 20 BAs of capacity.

    capacity modelGross hours     = working days x 8 x headcount
Net availability = Gross - holidays - PTO - training - practice time
Productive cap.  = Net availability x target utilization (80-85%)
Demand          = SUM(initiative BA effort x phase curve x confidence)
Gap             = Demand - Productive capacity   # per skill, per month
    💡
    Always report the gap in FTE-months as well as hours. Executives fund heads, not hours.

    Capacity Mathematics

    Move beyond headcount: availability, utilization ceilings, context-switching tax and queueing effects decide whether a plan is actually deliverable.

    🧮 Worked example: one month

    12 BAs, 21 working daysGross per BA      = 21 x 8                    = 168 h
Holiday 8 + PTO 12 + training 8               = -28 h
Net per BA        = 168 - 28                  = 140 h
Team net          = 140 x 12                  = 1,680 h
Productive @85%   = 1,680 x 0.85              = 1,428 h
Demand            = 1,620 h
Gap               = 1,620 - 1,428             = 192 h  (1.37 FTE-months)
    ⚠️
    Never plan at 100%. Zero slack means every illness, escalation or scope change becomes a missed date.

    📉 Utilization, queues and context switching

    Wait time in a shared-resource system grows non-linearly as utilization approaches 100%. Using the simple queueing ratio ρ/(1-ρ) as a relative delay index:

    Utilization (ρ)Relative delay indexImplication
    70%2.3Healthy responsiveness
    80%4.0Acceptable ceiling for knowledge work
    90%9.0Requests queue; escalations rise
    95%19.0Effectively unresponsive

    Context switching adds a hidden tax. A common heuristic (Weinberg) loses roughly 20% per extra concurrent project, so a BA split across four programs delivers little more than a BA on two.

    📌
    Agile teams: use trailing velocity (average of last 3 sprints) × availability ratio, e.g. velocities 30/34/38 → 34; with 1 of 5 members out → 34 × 0.8 ≈ 27 points.

    Roadmap Pipeline & Demand Forecasting

    Convert enterprise roadmaps and stage-gate pipelines into a confidence-weighted, phase-shaped BA demand curve.

    🗺️ From roadmap to weighted demand

    BA demand is front-loaded: analysis precedes build. A project that starts developing in Q3 needs BAs in Q2. Weight each initiative by the probability it proceeds.

    StageProbabilityTreatment
    Idea / intake10%Placeholder hours, T-shirt sized
    Funded, not started50%Estimate by role and skill
    Approved, scheduled90%Named plan, phase curve
    In flight100%Actuals plus remaining effort
    weighted pipelineA  600 h x 100%  =   600
B  900 h x  90%  =   810
C 1,200 h x 50%  =   600
D  800 h x  10%  =    80
Weighted demand   = 2,090 h
    typical BA phase curveDiscovery & analysis  45%
Design support        25%
Build clarification   15%
Test / UAT support    10%
Deploy & adoption      5%   # curve differs by delivery approach

    🔭 Rolling-wave horizons & forecast accuracy

    HorizonGranularityConfidenceAction
    0–3 monthsNamed BA, % per weekHighCommit staffing, lock allocations
    3–6 monthsRole/skill, hours per monthMediumStart hiring or contractor sourcing
    6–12 monthsSkill, FTE per quarterDirectionalScenario plans, budget input

    Track forecast error each month: |Actual - Forecast| / Actual, plus bias (consistent under- or over-forecasting). Example: forecast 1,000 h, actual 1,250 h → 20% error against actual (25% against forecast) and a clear under-forecast bias. Recalibrate effort ratios (e.g. BA:Dev 1:5 to 1:8) from actuals, not opinion.

    💡
    Pipeline coverage ratio = productive capacity ÷ weighted demand. Below 1.0 you are structurally short for that window.

    🧩 Allocation models

    ModelHow it worksStrengthRisk
    DedicatedBA 100% on one programDeep context, accountabilityIdle time, single point of failure
    Pooled / sharedDemand-driven assignment per requestHigh utilization, flexibleContext loss, queueing
    Hybrid (recommended)Dedicated on tier-1 programs; pooled for small work; T-shaped cross-trainingBalance of depth and flexibilityNeeds active rebalancing
    allocation rules of thumbMax 2 concurrent programs per BA
Min allocation slice 20%   # smaller slices cost more in switching than they deliver
Reserve 10-15% for practice work (mentoring, standards, hiring)

    Closing Capacity Gaps: Buy, Build, Borrow, Defer

    Pick the cheapest lever that meets the timeline and the risk profile, and test it against scenarios.

    ⚖️ Lever selection

    LeverUse whenWatch-outs
    Buy (hire FTE)Need persists 12+ monthsSlow; ramp-up; bench risk if demand drops
    Rent (contractor)Time-boxed spike, defined endPremium rate; knowledge walks out
    Borrow (loan/rotation)Short bridgeStarves lending team
    Build (upskill/T-shape)Skill mismatch, not volume gapTakes 3–6 months
    Defer / descopeGap larger than affordableNeeds portfolio decision
    contractor vs FTE break-evenFTE fully loaded cost     = $132,000 / yr
Contractor rate           = $95 / h
Break-even hours          = 132,000 / 95  = ~1,390 h
Need < ~1,390 h/yr  -> contractor cheaper (before recruiting + ramp)
Need > ~1,390 h/yr  -> hire
    💡
    Lead-time logic: if hire (10 wks) + ramp (8 wks) = 18 weeks and demand spikes in 12, bridge with a contractor while the requisition runs.

    🎲 Scenario planning

    ScenarioAssumptionResponse
    BaseApproved roadmap, 12% attritionFunded plan
    UpsideTwo Tier-2 initiatives acceleratePre-approved contractor pool
    DownsideBudget cut 15%, hiring freezeDefer Tier-3, release contractors first

    Release contractors before reducing permanent staff, and keep expected attrition (e.g. 15% of 20 BAs ≈ 3 leavers) in the supply line.

    Make capacity management a repeatable operating rhythm with a single intake, shared metrics and clear decision rights.

    📅 Cadence & decision rights

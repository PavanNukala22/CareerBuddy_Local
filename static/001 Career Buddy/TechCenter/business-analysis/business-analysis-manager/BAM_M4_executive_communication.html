    Executive Communication Principles

    Executives decide in minutes. Lead with the answer, quantify the impact, and make a clear ask.

    🔺 Answer-first structure

    Use the Pyramid Principle / BLUF (bottom line up front): conclusion first, then 2–3 supporting arguments, then evidence. Use SCQA (Situation, Complication, Question, Answer) to frame context quickly.

    one-slide executive summaryHeadline  BA defect leakage fell 22% -> 9%; on track for Q4 target of 8%
Why       peer-review gate + earlier UAT scripts
Risk      2 regulatory programs under-staffed from Nov (gap: 1.4 FTE)
Ask       approve 1 contractor BA for 6 months ($86k) by Oct 31
    💡
    Every executive message answers: So what? How much? By when? What do you need from me?

    🎯 Tailor to the audience

    AudienceCares aboutLead with
    CEO / SponsorStrategy, revenue, riskOutcome and decision needed
    CFOCost, ROI, predictabilityVariance, payback, forecast confidence
    CIO / CTODelivery risk, architecture, talentThroughput, quality, dependencies
    Business VPTheir capability and timelineImpact on their goals and dates

    Departmental KPI Framework

    Measure outcomes and quality with a small, balanced scorecard of leading and lagging indicators.

    📐 Core KPIs and formulas

    KPIFormulaTypeNotes
    Delivery velocityStory points or ready requirements delivered ÷ periodLaggingTrend within a team; never compare teams
    Requirement defect rateRequirement-caused defects ÷ requirements × 100LaggingNormalize for scope
    Defect escape rateRequirement defects found in production ÷ total requirement defectsLaggingQuality of validation
    Requirements volatilityChanges after baseline ÷ baselined requirementsLeadingSignals instability
    Ready-backlog coverageSprints of ready stories availableLeadingPrevents delivery starvation
    Requirement cycle timeIdea accepted → ready for buildLeadingFinds bottlenecks
    Stakeholder satisfactionPulse NPS / CSATLeadingInfluence and clarity
    quarter calculationBaselined requirements = 320;  changed post-baseline = 38
Volatility   = 38 / 320 = 11.9%
Requirement defects: UAT 14 + Production 4 = 18
Defect rate  = 18 / 320 x 100 = 5.6 per 100
Escape rate  = 4 / 18 = 22%

    ⚠️ Metric design pitfalls

    • Goodhart's law: targeting 'documents written' produces volume, not value.
    • Use 5–7 KPIs per tier; more than that dilutes attention.
    • Maintain a metric dictionary: definition, formula, source, owner, refresh. One source of truth.
    • Normalize (per 100 requirements, per story point) so scope changes don't distort trend.
    • Velocity is relative to a team's own scale; use it for forecasting, never for ranking.
    ⚠️
    Correlation is not attribution: say 'associated with' unless you have a control or before/after design.

    Dashboards, Reporting Cadence & Storytelling

    A good dashboard shows status, trend, target and a narrative; the narrative drives the decision.

    🚦 RAG design and cadence

    StatusRule (example)Expected behavior
    GreenWithin 5% of target, trend stableReport briefly
    Amber5–15% off or declining 2 periodsCause + recovery plan
    Red> 15% off or threat to a committed dateEscalate with options and ask
    CadenceAudienceContent
    WeeklyProgram leadsIntake, blockers, staffing
    MonthlyDepartment ops reviewKPIs, capacity, risks
    Quarterly (QBR)ExecutivesOutcomes, financials, roadmap, asks
    AnnualFinance / leadershipBudget, strategy, headcount

    📖 Narrative template

    what-why-so-what-askWhat changed   Requirement defect rate rose from 4.1 to 5.6 per 100
Why            rushed elicitation on Program Atlas (3 BAs vacated)
Impact         est. 410 rework hours, $38k
Action         peer-review gate restored; backfill hired by Nov 15
Ask            none this quarter; watch item
    💡
    Show trend, target and threshold, not a lone number. Avoid vanity metrics such as documents produced or meetings held.

    Budgeting the BA Practice

    Build a defensible cost model, choose a funding method and manage variance with finance-ready language.

    💵 Cost model worked example

    annual practice budgetPeople       18 FTE x $125,000                 = $2,250,000
Contractors  4 x 1,800 h x $90                 =   $648,000
Tools        licences & subscriptions          =    $60,000
Training     18 x $1,800                       =    $32,400
Subtotal                                        $2,990,400
Contingency  5%                                =   $149,520
Total                                           $3,139,920
    📌
    People are typically 60–80% of cost. Defend the headcount line with the demand–supply gap model (Module 2).

    🏦 Methods, funding and variance

    MethodHowUse when
    IncrementalLast year ± adjustmentsStable environment
    Zero-basedJustify every line from scratchCost-reduction or reorganization
    Driver-basedCost per unit × forecast volumeDemand-linked practices (recommended)
    Funding modelMeaning
    Central cost centerPractice funded centrally
    ShowbackReport consumption by business unit, no charge
    ChargebackBill business units for BA consumption
    variance: contractor spendBudget: 900 h x $80 = $72,000    Actual: 1,000 h x $88 = $88,000
Total variance  = $16,000
Price variance  = (88-80) x 1,000  = $8,000
Volume variance = (1,000-900) x 80 = $8,000
    ⚠️
    Capitalization (CapEx) versus expense depends on project phase and accounting policy; confirm with Finance. Planning-phase effort is commonly expensed.
    Module 4 · Part 7 of 13

    Value Case & ROI of Business Analysis

    Translate BA quality into money: rework avoided, delay removed, scope creep prevented.

    📈 ROI and payback

    business caseInvest: 2 senior BAs + tooling = $300,000
Benefit: rework reduced 15% of $4,000,000 = $600,000 / yr
ROI     = (600,000 - 300,000) / 300,000 = 100%
Payback = 300,000 / 600,000 x 12 = 6 months
Cost of delay: $40,000/week x 3 weeks earlier = $120,000
    💡
    Cost of poor requirements: defects found late cost ~10–100× more to fix. Use your own rework data to quantify it.

    🧾 Value levers & benefit tracking

    • Rework avoided (defects, change requests)
    • Time-to-market (cost of delay)
    • Compliance risk reduced
    • Scope creep contained
    • Benefit realization tracked 3–12 months after go-live, with named benefit owners
    ⚠️
    Be honest about attribution: show assumptions, ranges and what would falsify the claim.

    Influence, Escalation & Difficult Conversations

    Deliver bad news early, with options, and protect credibility with data you can defend.

    🧯 Bad news and escalation

    bad-news structure1 Headline  We will miss the 30 Nov date for Program Atlas
2 Cause     two BAs left; replacement ramp is 8 weeks
3 Options   (a) contractor now, hold date, +$86k  (b) slip to 15 Jan
4 Recommend option (a)
5 Decision  needed by Friday
    SituationResponse
    Exec challenges your dataAcknowledge, confirm definitions, agree owner and source, follow up
    Unfunded requestRoute through intake; show what drops
    Missed KPIOwn it, root cause, recovery plan, date to recheck
    Conflicting prioritiesEscalate with options, not complaints

    🤝 Influence habits

    • Pre-wire: brief key stakeholders 1:1 before the meeting; no surprises.
    • Say 'no' with trade-offs (scope, time, cost, quality) and options.
    • Escalate early along the defined path; never surprise a sponsor.
    • Keep a decision log so past agreements are visible.
    ✅
    Trust compounds: consistent definitions, honest variances and early warnings make later asks easier.

    Product Strategy Foundations

    Vision, strategy, goals, roadmap and backlog as one cascade; outcomes vs outputs; canvases; roles of PM, PO and BA.

    🪜 The strategy-to-backlog cascade

    LayerQuestion it answersExample (food-delivery app)Horizon
    VisionWhy do we exist? What change do we want?Everyone eats well, fast, affordably3–10 yrs
    StrategyHow will we win? Where to play, how to winBe the #1 choice for office lunches in 5 cities1–3 yrs
    Goals / OKRsWhat outcomes this quarter?O: Make lunch ordering effortless. KR: repeat orders 25% → 35%Quarter
    RoadmapWhat themes/bets are we pursuing and in what order?Now: reorder & scheduling · Next: group orders · Later: loyalty3–12 months
    BacklogWhat exactly do we build?Epics → stories with ACSprints
    📌
    A good strategy has a diagnosis (the challenge), a guiding policy (the approach) and coherent actions (Richard Rumelt). A list of goals or a roadmap alone is not a strategy.

    🎯 Outputs vs outcomes vs impact

    LevelDefinitionExample
    OutputWhat we deliverLaunched “scheduled orders” feature
    OutcomeChange in user behaviour30% of office users schedule at least one lunch/week
    ImpactBusiness/mission resultRetention +8%, revenue/user +12%
    💡
    A BA keeps asking: “What behaviour change would prove this worked?” If nobody can answer, the work is output theatre.

    North Star Metric & OKRs

    • North Star Metric (NSM): the one metric that best captures delivered customer value (e.g. “weekly nights booked”, “weekly active teams”).
    • OKR: Objective (qualitative, inspiring) + 3–5 Key Results (measurable outcomes, not tasks). Typical stretch scoring ≈ 0.6–0.7.
    • KPIs monitor health; OKRs drive change.

    🧾 Strategy canvases (what is what)

    CanvasBlocksUse
    Lean Canvas (Ash Maurya)Problem · Customer segments · Unique value proposition · Solution · Channels · Revenue streams · Cost structure · Key metrics · Unfair advantageEarly-stage hypotheses on one page
    Business Model CanvasKey partners · Key activities · Key resources · Value propositions · Customer relationships · Channels · Customer segments · Cost structure · Revenue streamsExisting/established business model
    Value Proposition CanvasCustomer: jobs, pains, gains ↔ Product: products & services, pain relievers, gain creatorsFit between product and customer
    Product Vision Board (Pichler)Vision · Target group · Needs · Product · Business goalsAligning stakeholders on direction
    ConceptMeaning
    Product-market fitProduct satisfies strong market demand (Sean Ellis test: ≥ 40% “very disappointed” without product)
    Product lifecycleIntroduction → Growth → Maturity → Decline; strategy changes by stage
    Three HorizonsH1 core (defend), H2 emerging (extend), H3 future (explore); common allocation ≈ 70/20/10

    🧑‍💼 PM vs PO vs BA

    RolePrimary focusTypical outputs
    Product ManagerMarket, strategy, business case, roadmapVision, roadmap, pricing, go-to-market
    Product Owner (Scrum)Maximise value of the product; own the Product BacklogOrdered backlog, Product Goal, acceptance
    Business Analyst / Product BAAnalyse needs, clarify requirements, model processes, evaluate solutionsDiscovery findings, process models, AC, impact analysis, benefit tracking
    ✅
    In smaller teams one person may do all three. In interviews describe how you contribute to strategic alignment: connecting every backlog item to an outcome and measuring it.

    Discovery & Discovery Sprints

    Dual-track agile, continuous discovery, Design Sprints, Jobs-to-be-Done and the four product risks — how to learn before you build.

    🔦 Why discovery? The four product risks

    Discovery reduces risk before delivery commits capacity; delivery data feeds back into discovery.
    Risk (Marty Cagan)QuestionTypical test
    ValueWill customers buy/use it?Interviews, fake door, landing page
    UsabilityCan they figure it out?Prototype usability tests
    FeasibilityCan we build it with our time, skills, tech?Engineering spike, architecture review
    Business viabilityDoes it work for the business (legal, finance, brand, sales)?Stakeholder review, unit economics
    📌
    A typical failure: building because stakeholders asked. Discovery tests problems and assumptions first; delivery builds only what survives.

    🧪 Discovery techniques

    TechniqueHowPitfall
    Customer interviews (The Mom Test)Ask about past behaviour and real problems, not opinions about your ideaLeading questions; praise is not validation
    Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)“When ___, I want to ___, so I can ___”; study the struggle and hiring/firing of productsConfusing jobs with features
    Opportunity Solution Tree (Teresa Torres)Outcome → opportunities (needs/pains) → solutions → assumption testsJumping to solutions
    Assumption mappingPlot assumptions by importance vs evidence; test the riskiest firstTesting safe assumptions
    Fake door / smoke testOffer a feature or landing page and measure clicks/signupsEthical: tell users after, avoid deception
    Concierge MVPDeliver the service manually for a few customersDoes not prove scalability
    Wizard of OzUI looks automated; humans work behind the scenesHidden operational cost
    Analytics & support-ticket miningQuantify the problem and its frequencyMisses non-users

    🏃 The Design Sprint (Google Ventures)

    DayActivityOutput
    Mon — MapLong-term goal, sprint questions, map the customer journey, pick a target momentTarget and expert interviews (HMW notes)
    Tue — SketchLightning demos, individual sketching (Crazy 8s, solution sketch)Competing solutions
    Wed — DecideArt museum, heat-map vote, Decider chooses, storyboardStoryboard (prototype plan)
    Thu — PrototypeBuild a realistic facade (Figma, Keynote)Testable prototype
    Fri — Test5 one-on-one user interviewsPatterns: advance, iterate, or kill

    Roles: Decider (final say), Facilitator, 5–7 team members including experts (product, design, engineering, marketing, BA). Why five users? About 5 users reveal most major usability patterns (Nielsen).

    Design SprintDiscovery sprint (in Scrum teams)
    Length5 days (or condensed 4 days)1–2 weeks within normal cadence
    PurposeAnswer a critical business question with a prototype and user testReduce uncertainty for upcoming epics (research, spikes, prototypes)
    TeamCross-functional, full-timePart of the Scrum Team; time-boxed discovery items in the backlog
    OutputValidated or invalidated hypothesisReady items, risk list, decisions
    ⚠️
    Not every problem needs a Design Sprint. If the main risk is technical (feasibility), run a spike; if the problem is already well understood, build the thin slice instead.

    🔁 Continuous discovery habits

    • Talk to customers every week (product trio: PM/PO, designer, engineer — BA often joins).
    • Interview story-based: “Tell me about the last time you…”.
    • Maintain an Opportunity Solution Tree tied to a single outcome.
    • Run multiple small assumption tests per week (cheap, fast).
    • Record decisions and evidence (Confluence/Productboard) so the backlog is traceable to learning.

    Minimum Viable Product (MVP) Scoping

    What an MVP is (and is not), types of MVP, how to scope with hypotheses and risk, and the skateboard-to-car metaphor.

    🛹 What an MVP really is

    Eric Ries: an MVP is the version of a new product that allows a team to collect the maximum amount of validated learning about customers with the least effort. It is an experiment, not version 1 with fewer features.

    TermPurposeAudience
    Proof of Concept (PoC)Prove technical feasibilityEngineers, architects
    PrototypeExplore design and usabilityUsers, stakeholders
    MVPTest a value hypothesis with real behaviourEarly adopters
    MLP (Minimum Lovable Product)Delight early users enough to adopt and recommendEarly adopters
    MMP (Minimum Marketable Product)Smallest release you can launch to marketMarket / sales
    PilotLimited rollout to evaluate operations and impactSelected customers/regions
    📌
    Kniberg’s metaphor: if the goal is a car, delivering wheels, then a chassis, then a body yields nothing usable until the end. Skateboard → scooter → bicycle → motorbike → car: every step is usable and teaches you something.

    🧰 Types of MVP

    TypeDescriptionExample
    Landing page / smoke testDescribe the product, measure signupsPre-sell a course; measure conversion
    Explainer videoVideo demonstrating the conceptDropbox demo video
    ConciergeManual service delivered personallyManually curate meal plans for 10 customers
    Wizard of OzLooks automated; humans behindChatbot answered by staff
    Single-feature MVPOnly the core jobPhoto sharing only
    Piecemeal MVPAssemble existing tools/servicesForms + spreadsheet + payment link
    Clickable prototypeInteractive designFigma flow for usability test

    🧮 MVP scoping process

    1. Frame the hypothesis: “We believe [customer] will [behaviour] because [reason]. We will know we are right when [metric ≥ threshold] within [time].”
    2. Identify riskiest assumption (value? usability? feasibility? viability?).
    3. Choose the smallest test for that assumption (landing page, concierge…).
    4. Define the core user journey and the thinnest slice (walking skeleton / story map first row).
    5. Decide success and kill criteria in advance; define data to collect.
    6. Time-box (2–8 weeks) and limit audience (early adopters).
    7. Plan the next step: persevere, pivot, or stop.
    hypothesisHypothesis (SplitPay MVP)
We believe flatmates who share rent and groceries (customer)
will settle shared bills within 3 days using a payment link (behaviour)
because chasing friends manually is awkward and slow (reason).
We will know we are right when >= 40% of the 20 pilot groups settle >= 80% of
shared bills within 3 days for 4 consecutive weeks (metric/threshold/time).
Kill criterion: < 15% of groups settle within 3 days after 4 weeks.
    Kano categoryEffect on satisfactionMVP implication
    Must-be (basic)Absent → dissatisfaction; present → neutralMust be in MVP to be usable (login, security)
    Performance (one-dimensional)More is better, linearInclude enough to be competitive
    Attractive (delighter)Unexpected; adds delightPick one to differentiate (MLP)
    IndifferentNo impactLeave out
    ReverseSome users dislike itAvoid / allow opt-out

    🚧 MVP pitfalls and metrics

    PitfallFix
    “Viable” ignored; product too thin to learn fromEnsure the core job is fully completable
    Feature-bloat MVP to please stakeholdersUse the hypothesis as a scope filter
    No measurement planInstrument before release; define metrics
    Vanity metricsUse cohort retention, activation, conversion to paid
    Pivot fearPre-agree decision rules
    AARRR (Pirate metrics)QuestionExample metric
    AcquisitionHow do users find us?Visitors → signups
    ActivationDo they have a great first experience?% completing first booking
    RetentionDo they come back?Week-4 retention
    RevenueDo they pay?Paid conversion, ARPU
    ReferralDo they tell others?Invites per user

    Prioritisation: MoSCoW, RICE & WSJF

    The three core feature prioritisation models, scoring scales, worked calculations and when each fits.

    🧱 MoSCoW

    CategoryMeaningTest
    Must haveNon-negotiable for this timebox; without it the release fails or is illegal/unsafe/unusable“What happens if it’s left out?” — if still viable, it’s not a Must
    Should haveImportant but a workaround existsPainful to omit
    Could haveDesirable, small impact if omittedFirst to drop
    Won’t have (this time)Explicitly agreed out of scope for this timeboxMay return later

    DSDM guidance: aim for Musts ≈ 60% of effort, Shoulds ≈ 20%, Coulds ≈ 20% so that Coulds act as contingency. Musts = “Minimum Usable SubseT”.

    ⚠️
    Failure modes: “Everything is Must”; no capacity constraint; “Won’t” treated as “never”; no re-evaluation per timebox.

    🎛️ RICE

    RICE score = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) ÷ Effort (Intercom).

    FactorDefinitionTypical scale
    ReachPeople/events affected per time periode.g. users per quarter
    ImpactEffect on each person toward the goal3 massive · 2 high · 1 medium · 0.5 low · 0.25 minimal
    ConfidenceCertainty in estimates100% high · 80% medium · 50% low
    EffortTotal person-months (or weeks) across teamPositive number
    FeatureReachImpactConfidenceEffort (p-m)RICE
    A: Saved cards4,000280%4(4000×2×0.8)/4 = 1,600
    B: Dark mode10,0000.5100%2(10000×0.5×1)/2 = 2,500
    C: Group orders1,500350%3(1500×3×0.5)/3 = 750
    💡
    Ranking B > A > C is a prompt for discussion, not a verdict: B scores high through reach but has low impact. Check against the objective and strategic bets before ordering.

    ⏱️ WSJF (Weighted Shortest Job First)

    WSJF = Cost of Delay ÷ Job Size, where CoD = User/Business Value + Time Criticality + Risk Reduction/Opportunity Enablement. Each component is scored relatively (modified Fibonacci 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20) against other items; job size is relative duration/effort.

    ItemBVTCRR/OECoDSizeWSJF
    1: Statement redesign13852683.25
    2: Regulatory report51382655.20
    3: AI assistant203225131.92

    Order: 2 → 1 → 3. The regulatory report wins thanks to time criticality and small size even though it has less user value.

    📌
    Relative scoring: pick the smallest item in each column, give it 1, score others relative to it. Absolute numbers are meaningless; the comparison matters.

    🧭 Choosing between models

    ModelBest whenStrengthWeakness
    MoSCoWFixed timebox/scope negotiationSimple, stakeholder-friendlyCoarse; no ordering within category
    RICEProduct teams with usage dataQuantitative; includes confidenceImpact/Reach estimates can be gamed
    WSJFScaled/enterprise, time-sensitive workAccounts for urgency and sizeRelative scoring requires calibration
    ICE (Impact × Confidence × Ease, 1–10)Rapid growth experimentsFastSubjective, no reach
    Value vs Effort matrixWorkshopsVisual; quick wins vs money pitsQualitative
    KanoUnderstanding delight vs basicsUser-drivenSurvey effort

    Advanced Prioritisation & Trade-offs

    Calibration, bias, dependencies, capacity allocation, weighted scoring, opportunity scoring and stakeholder negotiation.

    🧩 Other models (what is what)

    ModelHow it worksNotes
    Weighted scoringDefine criteria with weights (e.g. Revenue 40%, Risk 30%, Effort 30%); score each 1–5; Σ weight × scoreMake weights explicit and approved by stakeholders
    Value vs Effort (2×2)Quick wins (high value/low effort), Big bets, Fill-ins, Money pitsGreat for workshops
    Opportunity scoring (Ulwick)Opportunity = Importance + (Importance − Satisfaction)High importance + low satisfaction = under-served need
    Buy a FeatureStakeholders “buy” features with limited play moneyReveals true priorities; exposes trade-offs
    Cost of Delay / CD3Value lost per week ÷ durationTime-sensitive sequencing
    Story-map slicingOrder by user journey, thin end-to-endPreserves coherence
    Impact mappingGoal → Actors → Impacts → DeliverablesAligns deliverables to behavioural change
    weightedCriteria weights: Revenue 40%, Risk reduction 30%, Customer value 20%, Effort(inverse) 10%
Feature X scores (1-5): Revenue 4, Risk 2, Customer 5, Effort 3
Weighted = 0.4*4 + 0.3*2 + 0.2*5 + 0.1*3 = 1.6 + 0.6 + 1.0 + 0.3 = 3.5
Feature Y scores: Revenue 3, Risk 5, Customer 3, Effort 4
Weighted = 1.2 + 1.5 + 0.6 + 0.4 = 3.7  -> Y outranks X

    ⚖️ Calibration, bias and gaming

    ProblemSymptomRemedy
    InflationEveryone scores Impact = 3Anchor items; forced distribution; limit number of 3s
    HiPPO biasHighest-paid person’s opinion winsBlind scoring then discuss; evidence before opinion
    AnchoringFirst number shared biases othersSilent simultaneous estimates (poker)
    Effort optimismUnderestimated effort inflates scoresUse ranges; include QA/BA/UX effort
    Ignoring dependenciesHigh-score items blocked by othersSequence by dependency; bundle enablers
    Precision illusionScore 1,603 vs 1,600 treated as differentUse bands; run sensitivity analysis
    Confidence neglectTreating guesses as factsUse confidence factor; plan validation
    💡
    Sensitivity analysis: change each input ±20%; if the ranking flips, the decision is not robust — buy information (discovery) before committing.

    🍰 Capacity allocation and strategic bets

    AllocationShare (example)Purpose
    Core/optimise (H1)70%Improve current product
    Adjacent/expand (H2)20%New segments, features
    Transformational (H3)10%Experiments for the future
    Work classShare per sprintNote
    Strategic features/outcomes55–60%Linked to OKRs
    Technical enablers/debt15–20%Protect flow
    Defects & support10%Stability
    Experiments/learning10–15%Discovery
    📌
    Prioritisation is deciding what not to do. Capture rejected ideas with reasons (an “icebox” with decision log) to prevent repeated debate.

    🤝 Stakeholder negotiation

    • Map stakeholders: power/interest grid → Manage closely, Keep satisfied, Keep informed, Monitor.
    • Make criteria visible: agree the scoring model before scoring items.
    • Show trade-offs: “To add X (8 pts) we must remove Y or move date by 1 sprint.”
    • Use data: customer evidence, experiment results, cost of delay.
    • Record decisions and rationale (decision log/ADR style).
    • Revisit regularly: cadence-based re-prioritisation reviews tied to metrics.

    Roadmaps, Metrics & Experiments

    Outcome-based roadmaps, OKR alignment, A/B testing basics, HEART/AARRR, benefits realisation and strategy-to-backlog traceability.

    🛣️ Roadmap types

    TypeDescriptionBest for
    Now–Next–LaterBuckets by certainty, not datesProduct teams, high uncertainty
    Outcome-basedOrganised by goals/outcomes with problems to solveEmpowered teams; OKRs
    Theme-basedThemes (e.g. onboarding, trust) rather than featuresStakeholder communication
    Release / timelineDates and releasesRegulated, contractual commitments
    Portfolio / strategicMultiple products/bets and fundingExecutives
    ⚠️
    A roadmap is a statement of intent, not a promise. Show confidence levels (committed vs exploratory) to avoid it being read as a fixed schedule.

    🔗 OKR alignment and traceability

    traceabilityVision: Make lunch ordering effortless
 Objective (Q3): Make repeat ordering effortless for office workers
  KR1: Repeat-order rate 25% -> 35%
  KR2: Time to reorder 90 s -> 20 s
   Opportunity: "I forget what I ordered last time"          (from interviews, 9/12 users)
    Epic: One-click reorder + saved favourites                (hypothesis, metric = KR2)
     Story: Reorder last order from home screen              (AC in Gherkin, tagged KR2)
     Story: Save a meal as favourite
   Experiment: Fake door "Reorder" on 10% of users for 2 weeks

    Maintain bi-directional traceability: every epic points up to a key result; every KR lists epics/experiments contributing. Unlinked work is either maintenance (explicit) or a candidate to drop.

    🧬 Experimentation basics (A/B tests)

    ConceptMeaningBA caution
    HypothesisChange → expected effect on a metricOne primary metric
    Control/variantExisting vs changed experienceRandom assignment
    Minimum detectable effect (MDE)Smallest improvement worth detectingSmaller MDE → more users needed
    Statistical significanceProbability of seeing the result if there were no real effect (p < 0.05 common)Not the probability the variant is better
    Sample sizeComputed in advance from baseline, MDE, power (≈80%)Do not stop early when significant (peeking)
    Guardrail metricsMetrics that must not worsen (latency, refunds)Prevent harmful wins
    Sample ratio mismatchUnexpected split of traffic (e.g. 52/48 vs 50/50)Signals a bug in assignment
    ✅
    When traffic is too low for A/B testing, use qualitative tests, concierge MVPs, staged rollouts with feature flags and before/after comparison with caution.

    Expert Case & Playbook: SplitPay

    A complete example from strategic outcome to discovery, MVP scoping, prioritisation and measurement — plus an expert checklist.

    💸 Case: SplitPay (bill splitting for flatmates)

    PhaseWhat the BA doesResult
    StrategyClarify objective with leadershipObjective: become the default way young renters settle shared costs. KR: 30k weekly settled groups within 12 months
    Discovery12 interviews + 200-person survey + support data from a partner bank9/12 settle manually via chat; 4/12 have unpaid shares > 2 weeks
    OpportunitiesOpportunity tree(1) chasing friends is awkward; (2) tracking who owes what; (3) fear of linking bank account
    Riskiest assumptionAssumption mapValue: friends will pay via a link; Viability: low fees acceptable
    MVPConcierge + payment link; 20 pilot groups; 4 weeksSuccess ≥ 40% groups settle ≥ 80% bills within 3 days
    Prioritise nextRICE + dependenciesAuto-reminders, recurring bills, then bank linking
    Next-step candidateReachImpactConf.EffortRICE
    Auto-reminders to payers6,000280%1.5(6000×2×0.8)/1.5 = 6,400
    Recurring bills (rent)3,500380%3(3500×3×0.8)/3 = 2,800
    Bank account linking8,000150%5(8000×1×0.5)/5 = 800
    Receipt OCR splitting2,500150%4(2500×1×0.5)/4 = 312.5
    💡
    Decision: do reminders first (cheap, high RICE), and run a fake-door test for recurring bills because confidence is only 80% and effort is high. Bank linking waits for a trust-building experiment.

    📉 Result analysis and decision rules

    Result after 4 weeksInterpretationDecision
    48% of groups settle on time (target ≥ 40%)Value hypothesis supportedPersevere; scale pilot to 200 groups
    22% (between 15% and 40%)AmbiguousQualitative follow-up; run second test with modified flow
    9% (< 15%)Kill criterion metPivot (e.g. target couples/shared households) or stop
    ✅
    Pre-agreeing thresholds prevents post-hoc rationalisation. The BA documents the evidence, decision and next hypothesis in the decision log.

    🏁 Expert checklist

    • Every epic links to an outcome/KR and has a hypothesis plus metric.
    • Discovery targets the riskiest assumption first with the cheapest test.
    • MVP is defined by learning goals, not feature count; kill criteria exist.
    • Prioritisation criteria agreed up front; scores are calibrated; sensitivity checked; dependencies handled.
    • Roadmap shows confidence; decisions are logged; capacity is allocated across H1/H2/H3 and debt.
    • Benefits are measured post-release and fed back into discovery.

    🎤 Interview talking points

    QuestionStrong answer shape
    How do you prioritise a backlog?Link to outcomes; choose model by context (MoSCoW for timebox, RICE for product data, WSJF for time-critical/scaled); calibrate, sensitivity analysis, dependencies
    What makes a good MVP?Hypothesis + riskiest assumption + smallest test + success/kill criteria; examples of concierge/Wizard of Oz
    How do you align with strategy?Cascade vision → OKRs → roadmap → epics → stories; traceability and measurement
    Stakeholders insist everything is critical?Show capacity, trade-offs and cost of delay; use agreed criteria and decision log
    Feature shipped but no impact?Check instrumentation, adoption funnel, hypothesis; run user research; decide iterate/pivot/remove

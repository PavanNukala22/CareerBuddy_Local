    Jira: Issues, Workflows and JQL

    Designing Jira so the backlog stays queryable, flow is visible and Definition of Ready is enforced rather than hoped for.

    🧰 Issue model, workflows and boards

    PointDetail
    What it isWork tracker built on a hierarchy: Initiative → Epic → Story/Task/Bug → Sub-task (levels above Epic need Premium plans/Plans). Each issue type follows a workflow of statuses, transitions, conditions, validators and post-functions.
    Why it is usedOne system of record for scope, status and traceability from strategy to code, with reporting for velocity, flow and release readiness.
    How it worksBoards are filtered views of JQL; columns map to statuses; Scrum boards add sprints, Kanban boards add WIP limits and swimlanes. Automation rules (trigger, condition, action) remove manual toil.
    How to use itAdd a Ready for Sprint status gated by a validator (acceptance criteria and estimate present); keep one workflow per process, not per team; use components and labels sparingly; link PRs and builds via dev integrations.
    Common mistakesStatus sprawl (20+ states), epics used as buckets that never close, story points as hours, boards without a backing filter owner, editing shared workflows without impact analysis.
    Real-world usageA Product BA adds a Ready gate and cuts sprint spill-over by refusing unestimated stories into the sprint.
    Interview pointsEpic vs initiative; workflow validators; board filter vs board columns; how you migrate a workflow safely.

    🔎 JQL and reporting

    JQLproject = SHOP AND sprint in openSprints() AND statusCategory != Done ORDER BY priority DESC
issuetype = Story AND "Story point estimate" is EMPTY AND status = "Ready for Sprint"
issuetype = Epic AND statusCategory != Done AND updated <= -60d   # stale epics
status changed to "In Progress" before -10d AND statusCategory = "In Progress"  # aging WIP
    ReportReadsDecision it supports
    Burndown / BurnupRemaining or completed scope vs timeBurnup exposes scope creep; burndown hides it
    VelocityPoints completed per sprintForecast range, never a performance target
    Cumulative FlowItems per status over timeWidening band means a bottleneck
    Control chartCycle time per issueSet an 85th percentile service-level expectation
    ⚠️
    Never compare velocity across teams: points are relative to each team. Use cycle-time percentiles when a forecast must be defensible.

    Jira Align: Enterprise and SAFe Planning

    Connecting team backlogs to portfolio strategy, PI planning and cross-team dependencies.

    🏢 Jira Align in a SAFe organisation

    PointDetail
    What it isEnterprise agility platform that sits above team tools (Jira and others): Portfolio → Program (ART) → Team with Themes, Epics, Capabilities, Features and Stories.
    Why it is usedExecutives see investment, progress and risk across many teams; Product Management connects strategy to delivery without status meetings.
    How it worksTeam work syncs from Jira; Align rolls it into feature and epic progress, PI objectives, dependency maps, program increments and capacity planning; WSJF and other scores rank epics and features.
    How to use itMaintain a clean parent chain, plan in PI Planning with capacity per team, record dependencies as first-class links, review progress against PI objectives each iteration, run Inspect and Adapt with the data.
    Common mistakesSyncing everything (noise), treating Align as a replacement for team tools, orphan stories with no feature, stale PI objectives, using it to micromanage teams.
    Real-world usageThree ARTs plan quarterly; Align shows 14 cross-team dependencies and flags two red ones before PI Planning ends.
    Interview pointsEpic vs Capability vs Feature; WSJF components; dependency management; what to sync and what not.
    LevelOwnerTypical object
    PortfolioEpic Owner / LPMBusiness and enabler epics
    Program (ART)Product ManagementFeatures, PI objectives
    TeamProduct OwnerStories, sprint goals
    💡
    WSJF = Cost of Delay ÷ Job Size, where Cost of Delay = user/business value + time criticality + risk reduction/opportunity enablement.

    Linear: Opinionated Speed

    A keyboard-first tracker built around cycles, projects and low ceremony.

    ⚡ Linear model and workflow

    PointDetail
    What it isIssue tracker for product teams: Issues, Projects, Cycles, Initiatives, Triage, Views, with fast search and deep GitHub/GitLab links.
    Why it is usedLow overhead and clear ownership. Teams that find Jira heavy get a consistent, fast workflow.
    How it worksCycles are fixed time-boxes; unfinished issues roll over automatically. Triage holds incoming items until a human accepts them. Merged PRs move issues to Done via branch or magic-word links.
    How to use itInitiatives group projects toward strategy; priority is Urgent, High, Medium, Low; estimates are optional; use Asks or integrations to capture Slack/support requests.
    Common mistakesSkipping Triage so the backlog fills with noise, treating cycles as commitments, no project owner, over-customising labels.
    Real-world usageProduct BA reviews Triage daily, links accepted bugs to the customer-facing initiative.
    Interview pointsCycle vs sprint; Triage purpose; Linear vs Jira trade-offs.
    JiraLinear
    CustomisationVery deepIntentionally limited
    Setup effortHighLow
    Best fitLarge, regulated, multi-teamProduct-led engineering teams
    GovernancePermissions, workflows, auditLightweight; relies on team discipline

    Confluence and Trello

    Documentation as a living requirements system, and Kanban boards for lightweight work.

    📚 Confluence for requirements and decisions

    PointDetail
    What it isWiki with spaces, pages, templates, macros, comments and whiteboards.
    Why it is usedDurable context for requirements, decision records, retrospectives and release notes, linked to Jira work.
    How it worksJira Issues macro embeds live queries; Page Properties plus a Page Properties Report build a table across pages (e.g. all PRDs with status and owner).
    How to use itTemplate for PRD and decision log; one canonical page per topic; link stories to the page; archive instead of delete.
    Common mistakesDocuments copied into Jira description and Confluence at once, orphan pages, no owner, status buried in prose.
    Real-world usageA PRD page shows live linked epics and acceptance criteria via macros.
    Interview pointsPage properties report; living vs static docs.

    📌 Trello for simple Kanban

    ConceptUse
    Boards, lists, cardsFlow stages and work items
    Butler automationRules, card buttons, due-date commands to cut repetitive moves
    Power-UpsAdd calendar, custom fields, integrations; WIP limits often through convention or Power-Up
    Best fitSmall teams, marketing, simple intake
    LimitsWeak traceability, reporting and dependency support at scale
    📌
    Common mistake: using Trello for a regulated multi-team product. Move to Jira or Linear when you need traceability and flow reporting.

    Productboard: Discovery to Roadmap

    Turning customer insight into prioritised, evidence-backed features and roadmaps.

    🧭 Insights to features to roadmap

    PointDetail
    What it isProduct management platform: Insights, Features, Objectives, Releases, Roadmaps, Portal.
    Why it is usedShows evidence behind priorities and aligns stakeholders without a spreadsheet of opinions.
    How it worksCapture feedback from support, sales and research as notes, link each to a feature, score features by drivers (impact, effort, strategic fit), group under Objectives, push committed work to Jira and sync status.
    How to use itDefine Objectives first; tag segments; review top insights monthly; keep Productboard for what and why, Jira for how and when.
    Common mistakesDuplicating delivery detail in both tools, unlinked insights, scoring without calibration, a roadmap promising dates.
    Real-world usage200 notes cluster behind "bulk export"; scoring lifts it above a requested dashboard, evidence goes to the exec review.
    Interview pointsDiscovery vs delivery tool split; RICE; how to avoid HiPPO prioritisation.
    ModelFormula or ideaBest for
    RICE(Reach × Impact × Confidence) ÷ EffortComparable feature scoring
    WSJFCost of Delay ÷ Job SizeSequencing at scale / SAFe
    MoSCoWMust, Should, Could, Won'tScope for a fixed release
    KanoBasic, performance, delighterCustomer value insight

    Tool Selection and Expert Practice

    Choosing and integrating the stack, measuring flow, and defending your choices in interview.

    🧩 Stack, metrics and anti-patterns

    NeedTypical tool
    Discovery and roadmapsProductboard
    Team delivery trackingJira or Linear
    Portfolio and PI planningJira Align
    DocumentationConfluence
    Simple KanbanTrello
    Flow metricMeaning
    Lead timeRequest to delivery
    Cycle timeWork started to done
    WIPItems in progress
    ThroughputItems finished per period
    💡
    Little's Law: average WIP = throughput × average cycle time. To cut cycle time, reduce WIP.
    PointDetail
    Expert insightsPick tools by decision and maturity; define one system of record per object; integrate through APIs not copy-paste; measure flow; review the tool set yearly.
    Interview pointsJustify Jira vs Linear; explain Align's value; describe an end-to-end flow from Productboard insight to Jira release.

    Backlog Fundamentals

    What a backlog is, what lives in it, how it is structured, and who owns what — the vocabulary every Agile BA must have cold.

    📚 What is a Product Backlog?

    The Product Backlog is an ordered, emergent list of everything known to be needed to improve the product. It is the single source of work for the team. “Ordered” means the top is most valuable/urgent; “emergent” means it is never finished — it changes as you learn.

    TermWhat it isOwner
    Product BacklogOrdered list of all desired work for the productProduct Owner (accountable)
    Sprint BacklogItems selected for the sprint + the plan to deliver them + the Sprint GoalDevelopers
    Product GoalLong-term objective the backlog serves (one at a time)Scrum Team, set by PO
    IncrementA usable, Done step toward the Product GoalDevelopers
    RoadmapHigh-level, time-oriented view of intent (not a commitment list)PO / Product Manager
    📌
    The BA usually does not “own” the backlog — the PO is accountable for ordering. The BA shapes, clarifies and quality-checks it: that is where most BA value is created.

    🧩 Types of Backlog Items (PBIs)

    🏔️
    Epic
    Large body of work (weeks–months) that must be split before it can be built.
    🧱
    Feature
    A service/capability that fulfils a need; usually spans several stories.
    👤
    User Story
    Small slice of value for a user, completable within one sprint.
    🐞
    Bug / Defect
    Behaviour that deviates from what was agreed or expected.
    🔬
    Spike
    Time-boxed research to reduce uncertainty. Output is knowledge, not shippable code.
    🔧
    Tech Debt / Enabler
    Work that keeps the product healthy or enables future features (refactor, upgrade, infra).
    LevelTypical sizeExample (e-commerce)
    Theme / InitiativeQuarter(s)Improve repeat purchase rate
    Epic1–3 monthsSubscription & reorder
    Feature2–6 weeksOne-click reorder
    Story1–5 daysAs a returning customer I can reorder a past order from order history
    TaskHoursAdd API endpoint /orders/{id}/reorder

    🌊 The DEEP Backlog (Roman Pichler)

    A healthy backlog is DEEP:

    LetterMeaningWhat it looks like
    DDetailed appropriatelyTop items are small and clear; far-future items stay coarse (epics). Never detail what you may not build.
    EEstimatedEvery item has an estimate whose precision matches its closeness to delivery.
    EEmergentItems are added, split, merged and deleted continuously as you learn.
    PPrioritisedStrict order — “everything is #1” means nothing is. Top = next to build.
    💡
    Quick health check: can the team pull the next two sprints of work without a meeting? If not, the top of the backlog is not ready.

    🚦 Definition of Ready vs Definition of Done

    Definition of Ready (DoR)

    • Entry gate for a story into a sprint
    • Understood, small enough, testable, dependencies known
    • Owned by the team — a guideline, not a rigid rule
    • Not part of the Scrum Guide, but widely used

    Definition of Done (DoD)

    • Exit gate — quality bar for any Increment
    • Coded, reviewed, tested, documented, deployable
    • Part of Scrum (commitment for the Increment)
    • Applies to all items equally
    ⚠️
    Using DoR as a hard gate turns it into a mini-waterfall (“no story enters until BA signs it off”). Treat it as a conversation checklist.

    User Stories & the INVEST Criteria

    Write stories that are conversation starters, not mini-specifications — and test each one against INVEST.

    📝 Anatomy of a User Story

    A story is a promise of a conversation. Ron Jeffries’ 3 Cs: Card (short written token), Conversation (details emerge in discussion), Confirmation (acceptance criteria prove it is done).

    storyAs a <persona>
I want <capability>
So that <business outcome / benefit>

As a returning customer
I want to reorder a past order in one click
So that I save time on repeat purchases
    FormatTemplateUse when
    Classic storyAs a … I want … so that …Default; user-centred
    Job story (JTBD)When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]Context matters more than persona
    Feature-drivenAction + result + object (“Calculate the total for an order”)Technical/domain-heavy teams
    Connextra / “in order to”In order to … as a … I want …Put value first so it is never skipped

    ✅ INVEST — the six quality tests

    LetterTest questionViolation exampleFix
    IndependentCan it be built/ordered without another story?“Pay by card” requires “Create cart” first and shares a half-built formRe-slice along value; or accept and order explicitly
    NegotiableIs it a goal, not a contract on how?“Use a dropdown with 14 options sorted by …”State the need; let design/dev propose the control
    ValuableDoes a user or the business notice it?“Create the orders database table”Merge into the first story that uses the table
    EstimableCan the team size it?“Integrate with partner API” (unknown API)Spike first, then estimate
    SmallFits a sprint, ideally ≤ ~⅓ of it?“Manage customer accounts”Split (see Story Slicing tab)
    TestableCan you write a pass/fail check?“The page should feel fast”Make measurable: “p95 load ≤ 2 s on 4G”
    💡
    INVEST is a diagnostic, not a gate. Use the failing letter to decide the fix: not Estimable → spike; not Small → split; not Valuable → merge or reframe.

    🏷️ Types of stories and “what is what”

    TypeDefinitionExample
    User storyDelivers user-visible valueReset password via email link
    Enabler storyEnables future value (architecture, infrastructure, compliance, exploration)Migrate auth to OAuth2 provider
    SpikeTime-boxed investigation (technical or functional)2-day spike: can vendor X return real-time stock?
    BugDefect against an existing, agreed behaviourCoupon applies twice on refresh
    Constraint / NFR storyQuality attribute attached as a criterion or standalone itemCheckout must handle 500 TPS
    PersonaArchetype representing a user group“Priya, busy parent, mobile-first”
    ⚠️
    Anti-patterns: technical-task stories (“As a developer I want an index…”), story-as-spec (30 lines of rules in the description), and “As a user I want… so that I can use the system” (empty benefit).

    🛠️ Worked rewrite: bad → good

    VersionTextProblem / improvement
    BadAs a user I want a dropdown of statuses so I can filter.Solution-baked; vague user; no benefit
    BetterAs a support agent I want to filter tickets by status so that I can find urgent unresolved cases quickly.Persona + outcome; control is open
    BestAs a support agent, I want to filter the queue by status so that I can reach tickets breaching SLA first. AC: Given 200 open tickets, when I filter “Escalated”, then only escalated tickets show within 1 s.Value, testable, measurable

    Story Slicing

    Turn epics into thin, vertical, shippable slices — SPIDR, Hamburger, Lawrence’s patterns and story mapping.

    🍰 Why slice? Horizontal vs vertical

    Slicing reduces size and risk and creates earlier feedback. The golden rule: slice vertically — every slice crosses UI, logic and data so it can be demonstrated and, ideally, released.

    Vertical slices deliver value each sprint; horizontal slices defer all value to the end.
    📌
    A walking skeleton is the thinnest end-to-end implementation (e.g. one hard-coded product → add to cart → pay with a test card). It proves the architecture early; later slices add flesh.

    🔪 Slicing techniques

    SPIDR (Mike Cohn)

    LetterSlice byExample: “Customer pays for order”
    SSpikeInvestigate payment gateway sandbox limits
    PPathsPay by card → later PayPal → later UPI
    IInterfacesWeb checkout first → mobile app later
    DDataDomestic currency first → multi-currency later
    RRulesNo fraud checks first → add 3-D Secure rule later

    Other patterns (Richard Lawrence – “Patterns for splitting”)

    PatternIdea
    Workflow stepsSplit a long process into steps; deliver first and last step first (thin end-to-end)
    Business-rule variationsOne rule at a time (“search by price” → by brand → by rating)
    Major effortDeliver the first “hard” case, then the cheap variations
    Simple / complexStart with the simplest version; add complexity later
    Variations in dataSupport one data type/region first
    Data-entry methodsPlain form first; drag-and-drop/autocomplete later
    Defer performanceMake it work → make it fast (only if NFR allows)
    Operations (CRUD)Create → Read → Update → Delete as separate stories
    Break out a spikeIsolate the unknown so the rest is estimable

    Hamburger method (Gojko Adzic)

    List the steps of the task as layers of a burger; write 2–4 implementation options per layer (quality low → high); pick one option per layer for the first slice, then improve the weakest layer next. The first “burger” is thin but edible.

    🧮 Worked example: slice an epic

    slicingEPIC: Customers can manage subscriptions (too big - 80 pts)

Story 1 (walking skeleton): Subscribe to one product, monthly, card only      -> 5 pts
Story 2: Pause a subscription for 1-3 months                                  -> 3 pts
Story 3: Skip next delivery                                                   -> 2 pts
Story 4: Change delivery frequency (Path variation)                           -> 3 pts
Story 5: Cancel with reason survey (Rule: retention offer shown)              -> 5 pts
Story 6: Subscription renewal reminder email 3 days before charge             -> 3 pts
Story 7: Pay with UPI/PayPal (Path)                                           -> 5 pts
Spike A: Can payment provider support off-session recurring SCA?              -> 2 days
    💡
    Each slice passes INVEST: independent enough to reorder, valuable, estimable, small. The spike is placed before any story it unblocks.
    ⚠️
    Slicing smells: slice names that contain layer words (“backend of…”), a “Part 2” suffix, stories that all finish on the last day of the sprint, or a slice that cannot be shown to a user.

    🗺️ User Story Mapping (Jeff Patton)

    A story map arranges the user’s journey left-to-right (the backbone: activities → steps) and stories top-to-bottom by priority. Horizontal lines drawn across the map become release slices; the top row is the walking skeleton.

    Map elementMeaning
    Backbone / activitiesBig things users do (Browse, Buy, Receive)
    Steps / tasksSub-steps within an activity (Add to cart, Choose delivery)
    Details / storiesVariations listed vertically; most important at the top
    Release sliceA horizontal cut that forms a coherent, releasable outcome
    ✅
    Use a story map for shared understanding and release planning; a flat backlog hides gaps (“we forgot returns”) that the 2-D map reveals instantly.

    Backlog Grooming (Refinement)

    Keep the top of the backlog ready: cadence, participants, estimation techniques, ordering and health metrics.

    🧹 What is refinement?

    Backlog refinement (formerly “grooming”) is the ongoing act of adding detail, estimates and order to items. The Scrum Guide says it is a continuous activity and typically consumes no more than ~10% of the Developers’ capacity. It is not an official Scrum event, so teams choose the format.

    ActivityPurpose
    ClarifyRemove ambiguity; capture AC and open questions
    SplitSlice epics/large stories until they fit a sprint
    EstimateCreate a shared forecast; expose misunderstanding
    OrderPO sequences by value, risk, dependency and cost of delay
    PruneDelete items that will never be built (stale > 6 months)
    De-riskRaise dependencies, spikes, NFRs and compliance needs early
    📌
    “Grooming” is being replaced by “refinement” in many orgs because of the term’s other connotations. Know both words for exams and interviews.

    🗓️ Who, when, how long

    AspectTypical practice
    ParticipantsPO (decides), Developers (size/feasibility), BA (clarity/AC), UX, QA; SM facilitates
    Cadence1–2 sessions per sprint, 45–60 min; or continuous “just-in-time” pairing
    LookaheadKeep 1.5–2 sprints of Ready stories at top (more = waste, less = starvation)
    Pre-work (BA)Draft story + AC, mock-ups, data rules, links to policies, list of open questions
    OutputUpdated items, estimates, split items, new spikes, agreed order
    agendaRefinement agenda (60 min)
 5  min  Review sprint goal progress; confirm what we need ready for sprint N+1
35  min  Top 5-6 items: BA walkthrough (2 min) -> questions -> split -> estimate
10  min  New items / epics: rough sizing (T-shirt) only
 5  min  Re-order with PO; mark Ready
 5  min  Parking lot: spikes, dependencies, risks, actions

    🎯 Estimation techniques

    TechniqueHow it worksBest for
    Planning PokerEach estimator shows a Fibonacci card simultaneously; discuss outliers, re-voteStory-level estimates with cross-functional team
    T-shirt sizingXS–XL, mapped to point ranges laterRoadmap-level / epics
    Affinity (wall) estimationSilently sort items by relative size on a wallLarge backlog quickly (50+ items)
    Bucket systemItems placed into buckets 1,2,3,5,8,13,20,40,100Very large lists
    Three-point (PERT)(O + 4M + P) / 6Spikes, vendor work, unknowns
    #NoEstimates / count of itemsSlice to similar size; forecast with throughputMature teams with stable story size
    💡
    Story points are relative measures of effort + complexity + uncertainty. They are not hours and must never be used to compare teams.
    ⚠️
    A big spread in poker votes (2 vs 13) is the real output: someone knows something others don’t. Ask the high and low voter to explain before re-voting.

    📈 Ordering & backlog health metrics

    SignalHealthyWarning
    Ready depth1.5–2 sprints of Ready items< 1 sprint or > 4 sprints
    Age of top-50 itemsMostly < 3 monthsItems older than a year with no decision
    Churn at top 10 per sprintLow; changes explained by learningRe-ordered every week (no strategy)
    Carry-over< 10–15% of committed storiesChronic spill-over → slicing/refinement problem
    Backlog sizeHundreds is fine if coarse items are fewThousands of detailed items
    Defect ratioStable or decreasingBugs crowd out value work

    Ordering factors: value, urgency (cost of delay), risk/uncertainty, dependencies, effort. See the Product Strategy module for RICE/WSJF/MoSCoW.

    Acceptance Criteria & Gherkin (BDD)

    Write unambiguous, testable acceptance criteria — rule-based and scenario-based — and master Given-When-Then, Scenario Outlines and Example Mapping.

    🎯 Acceptance criteria (AC) basics

    AC are the conditions a story must satisfy to be accepted by the PO. They are the “Confirmation” of the 3 Cs. Good AC are specific, observable, independent of implementation and have a clear pass/fail.

    StyleFormatExample
    Rule-oriented (checklist)Bullet list of rules• Password ≥ 12 chars • Must contain a digit • Lock after 5 failed attempts
    Scenario-orientedGiven–When–ThenGiven 5 failed attempts, when I try again, then the account is locked for 15 min
    Table / decision tableInputs → outputsOrder value × customer tier → discount %
    Visual / prototypeWireframe + annotationsUsed with UX for layout-heavy stories
    Acceptance CriteriaDefinition of Done
    ScopeSpecific to one storySame for every story / Increment
    Question answered“Did we build the right thing?”“Did we build it right / to our quality bar?”
    ExamplesCoupon cannot exceed 50% of cartPeer-reviewed, unit-tested, security-scanned, deployed to staging
    OwnerPO + BA + teamScrum Team (organisation may set a baseline)

    🥒 Gherkin syntax — the keywords

    KeywordPurpose
    FeatureNames the capability; free-text description below it
    BackgroundSteps run before every scenario in the file (shared context)
    Scenario / ExampleOne concrete example of behaviour
    Scenario Outline + ExamplesTemplate run once per data row (use <placeholders>)
    GivenContext / precondition (state, not action)
    WhenThe single event or action that triggers behaviour
    ThenObservable outcome (assertion)
    And / ButContinue the previous step type
    RuleGroups scenarios that illustrate one business rule (Gherkin 6+)
    @tagsLabel scenarios (@smoke, @regression, @wip) for filtering
    Data table / Doc stringPass structured data or multi-line text into a step
    gherkinFeature: Reorder a previous purchase
  As a returning customer I want to reorder in one click
  so that I save time on repeat purchases.

  Background:
    Given I am logged in as a customer with order history

  @smoke
  Scenario: Reorder with all items in stock
    Given my order "A-1001" contains 3 items that are in stock
    When I click "Reorder" on order "A-1001"
    Then all 3 items are added to my cart
    And the cart total equals the current prices of the items

  Scenario: Reorder with an out-of-stock item
    Given my order "A-1002" contains 2 items and 1 is out of stock
    When I click "Reorder" on order "A-1002"
    Then 1 item is added to my cart
    And I see the message "1 item is currently unavailable"

    🧪 Scenario Outline, Rules & data

    gherkinRule: Free delivery applies at or above the threshold

  Scenario Outline: Delivery fee by cart value
    Given my cart value is <value>
    And my membership tier is "<tier>"
    When I proceed to checkout
    Then the delivery fee is <fee>

    Examples:
      | value | tier   | fee  |
      | 499   | Basic  | 49   |
      | 500   | Basic  | 0    |
      | 100   | Prime  | 0    |
      | 0     | Prime  | 0    |
    💡
    Boundary-value rows (499 / 500) are where defects hide. A BA’s edge is choosing revealing examples, not just happy-path ones.

    Declarative vs imperative

    ❌ Imperative (UI-scripted)

    • Given I open "login.html"
    • And I type "bob" in field #user
    • And I click the button with id "go"
    • Brittle; breaks on UI change; hides intent

    ✅ Declarative (behaviour)

    • Given a registered customer "Bob"
    • When Bob signs in with valid credentials
    • Then Bob sees his dashboard
    • Readable by business; stable over UI change

    🤝 BDD, Three Amigos & Example Mapping

    BDD (Behaviour-Driven Development) is a collaboration practice: Discovery (explore with examples) → Formulation (write Gherkin) → Automation (Cucumber, SpecFlow/Reqnroll, Behave, Karate…). Gherkin files become living documentation.

    Three Amigos = Business (PO/BA), Development, Testing talk before build. Example Mapping (Matt Wynne) runs in ~25 min per story with coloured cards:

    Card colourContent
    🟡 YellowThe story
    🔵 BlueBusiness rules / acceptance criteria
    🟢 GreenConcrete examples illustrating each rule
    🔴 RedOpen questions / assumptions (must be answered or spiked)
    ✅
    Rule of thumb: many red cards → not ready; many blue cards → split the story; no green cards → rules are abstract.
    ⚠️
    Gherkin anti-patterns: multiple When steps in one scenario, scenarios that test UI mechanics, scenario sprawl (50 scenarios for one story), conjunctive Then (“…and also…” ×5), and “testers write Gherkin alone after the code is done” (loses the collaboration benefit).

    Backlog at Scale & Quality Management

    Dependencies, NFRs, technical debt, multi-team backlogs, traceability and flow metrics — the advanced BA toolbox.

    🏗️ Structuring a large backlog

    ApproachHowTrade-off
    Single backlog, multiple teamsOne PO, one ordered list; teams pull by area/skillBest alignment (LeSS/Nexus); needs shared ownership
    Backlog per team + shared epicsTeam-level backlogs fed from feature backlog (SAFe)Autonomy; risk of dependency drift
    Component teamsBacklog per technical componentHandoffs; value delivered only after integration
    Feature teamsBacklog per end-to-end customer capabilityPreferred; needs broad skills
    Now-Next-LaterOutcome buckets instead of datesHonest about uncertainty; less predictable for stakeholders
    📌
    Conway’s Law: the backlog structure tends to mirror the org structure. If you want end-to-end flow, fix team topology first, then the backlog.

    🔗 Dependencies, NFRs & technical debt

    TopicAdvanced practice
    DependenciesClassify: data, API, release, knowledge, regulatory. Make visible (dependency board, links in Jira). Prefer re-slicing or sequencing over “coordination meetings”.
    NFRs (performance, security, accessibility)Attach as constraints to relevant stories and the DoD; split out only when a distinct deliverable exists (e.g. “Pen-test remediation”).
    Technical debtMake it visible in the backlog; reserve capacity (e.g. 15–20%) or tie debt to features (“boy-scout rule”). Quantify cost: slower cycle time, defect rate.
    Compliance / auditLink story → AC → test → release (traceability). Use tags such as SOX, GDPR for filtering.
    Cross-cutting storiesLogging, i18n, accessibility → add to DoD instead of repeating in every story
    ruleCapacity allocation example (per sprint, 40 pts)
  60%  new product value
  20%  technical debt / enablers
  10%  bugs & support
  10%  unplanned / buffer

    🧾 Traceability & quality gates

    Agile does not abandon traceability — it shifts it to links rather than documents. A lean traceability chain: Objective → Epic → Story → AC/Gherkin scenario → automated test → build → release. Tools generate the Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM) on demand.

    Quality gateWhenChecks
    Idea / epic gateBefore refinementLinked to objective; value hypothesis; rough size
    Ready gate (DoR)Before sprint planningINVEST, AC, UX, dependencies, no red cards
    Sprint reviewEnd of sprintAC met; stakeholder feedback captured as new items
    Done gate (DoD)During sprintCode review, tests, security, docs, deployable

    📊 Flow & backlog metrics (advanced)

    MetricDefinitionInsight
    Lead timeRequest → deliveredCustomer-visible speed
    Cycle timeWork started → doneProcess speed; use percentiles (50/85/95)
    ThroughputItems completed per periodForecast with Monte Carlo
    Flow efficiencyActive time ÷ total lead timeTypical 5–15% → massive queueing waste
    Story/requirement churn% items changed after ReadyQuality of discovery
    Escaped defectsBugs found after releaseQuality of AC and tests
    💡
    Little’s Law: Average WIP = Throughput × Average Cycle Time. Cutting WIP is the fastest lever to reduce cycle time.

    Expert Playbook & End-to-End Case

    One epic taken from vague idea to ready, sliced, Gherkin-backed backlog — plus an expert checklist and interview talking points.

    🏦 End-to-end case: “Instant Refunds”

    Context: A fintech wallet app. Support receives 4,000 tickets/month about refunds taking 5–7 days. Goal: reduce refund tickets by 40% in two quarters.

    StepBA actionOutput
    1. FrameWrite outcome + metricObjective: refund tickets −40%; KR: median refund time ≤ 2 h
    2. DiscoverInterview 6 agents, analyse 200 tickets, map current flowTop reasons: unclear status (46%), bank delay (30%), manual approval (24%)
    3. EpicCreate epic with hypothesisInstant refunds to wallet; fall back to bank with status tracking
    4. SliceSPIDR + story map8 stories + 1 spike (below)
    5. RefineThree Amigos + Example MappingRules clarified; 3 red cards → 1 spike, 2 PO decisions
    6. SpecifyGherkin for riskiest rulesScenarios for limits, partial refund, fraud hold
    7. OrderWith PO: value + risk + dependencySkeleton first; spike earliest
    slicingSpike: Can the payment provider reverse a settled UPI payment in < 1 minute?   (2 days)
S1 (skeleton)  Full refund to wallet for orders < 500, auto-approved             5
S2             Refund status tracker (Requested -> Processing -> Completed)    3
S3             Partial refund                                                  3
S4             Refund to original bank account (async)                         8
S5             Fraud hold: manual review when > 3 refunds in 24 h              5
S6             Agent console: manual override with audit trail                 5
S7             Push + email notifications on status change                     3
S8             Refund analytics dashboard (KR tracking)                        3
    gherkinRule: Auto-approve small refunds; hold risky ones

  Scenario: Small refund is instant
    Given a delivered order "O-77" of value 450
    And the customer has made 0 refunds in the last 24 hours
    When the customer requests a full refund
    Then 450 is credited to the wallet within 60 seconds
    And the status becomes "Completed"

  Scenario: Velocity check triggers fraud hold
    Given the customer has made 3 refunds in the last 24 hours
    When the customer requests another refund
    Then the refund status is "Under review"
    And no wallet credit is made
    And the case appears in the agent review queue

    🏁 Expert checklist

    • Every top-10 item links to an objective/outcome, not just a stakeholder request.
    • Epics carry a hypothesis + metric; stories carry AC; unknowns have a spike with a time box.
    • Backlog shows NFR constraints, dependencies, compliance tags.
    • Slices are vertical; first slice is a walking skeleton.
    • Estimates reflect team consensus; large spreads trigger conversation.
    • Ready depth is ~2 sprints; stale items are deleted, not hoarded.
    • Examples include boundaries and negative cases; Gherkin is declarative.
    • Metrics (cycle time, carry-over, escaped defects) are reviewed in retros.

    🎤 Interview-ready talking points

    Likely questionStrong answer shape
    How do you split a large story?Name the technique (SPIDR/workflow/rules), show a vertical example, mention INVEST and walking skeleton
    Difference between AC and DoD?AC = story-specific; DoD = universal quality bar; give one example each
    How do you keep the backlog healthy?DEEP, ready depth of ~2 sprints, pruning, metrics (age, churn, carry-over)
    A story keeps spilling over — why?Too large / unclear AC / hidden dependency / WIP too high — diagnose with data, then re-slice and limit WIP
    How do you write Gherkin that doesn’t rot?Declarative, one behaviour per scenario, Rules grouping, run in CI, delete obsolete scenarios
    Product Owner vs BA at backlog?PO accountable for value and order; BA elicits, clarifies, models, validates AC and supports slicing

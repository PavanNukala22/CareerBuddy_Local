    Governance Foundations

    What data governance is, who is accountable for what, and the policies that keep data usable, safe and trusted.

    🏛️ What governance is and why it exists

    Data governance is the set of roles, policies, standards and processes that make sure data is available, accurate, consistent and secure, and used as intended. It answers: who owns this data, what does it mean, who may use it and how good must it be?

    PillarQuestion answeredExample artifact
    Ownership & rolesWho is accountable?RACI, owner list
    Definitions & metadataWhat does it mean?Glossary, data dictionary, catalog
    QualityIs it fit for use?DQ rules, scorecards
    Security & privacyWho may see it?Access policy, classification
    LifecycleHow long do we keep it?Retention schedule
    Architecture & integrationWhere does it flow?Lineage maps, data contracts
    📌
    Governance vs management: governance sets the rules and decides; data management (engineering, DBA, BI) executes them. Frameworks: DAMA-DMBOK (11 knowledge areas), DCAM, COBIT.

    👥 Roles and data classification

    RoleResponsibility
    Data ownerSenior accountable person for a domain; approves access and quality targets
    Data stewardDay-to-day guardian; maintains definitions, resolves issues
    Data custodianTechnical operation: storage, backups, access controls
    Data consumer / analystUses data per policy; reports issues
    Governance councilCross-domain decisions, policies, priorities
    ClassificationExamplesTypical control
    PublicPress releasesNone
    InternalOrg charts, internal KPIsEmployee access
    ConfidentialCustomer records, financialsRole-based access, encryption
    Restricted / sensitivePII, health, payment dataStrict need-to-know, masking, audit logs
    💡
    BA role: translate business terms into governed definitions, document rules in requirements, and make sure each critical data element has a named owner.

    📝 Building a data dictionary

    Attributecustomer_email
    Business nameCustomer email address
    DefinitionPrimary email used for order notifications
    Table / columndw.dim_customer.customer_email
    Data type / lengthVARCHAR(254)
    Nullable / keyNo / unique per active customer
    Format ruleValid email pattern, lowercase, trimmed
    Allowed valuesn/a (free text, validated)
    Source systemCRM.contacts.email
    TransformationLowercased and trimmed in ETL
    SensitivityPII, Confidential; masked for non-privileged roles
    Owner / stewardHead of CRM / Customer data steward
    RefreshDaily 05:00 UTC
    Quality ruleCompleteness >= 99%, validity >= 99.5%
    1. Inventory critical data elements (CDEs) first, not every column.
    2. Interview owners and read source documentation; agree one definition per term.
    3. Fill the template, including rules and sensitivity.
    4. Review with stewards; publish in the catalog; link to metrics.
    5. Version and update through change control whenever schemas change.
    ⚠️
    Common mistakes: copying column names as definitions ("Customer ID is the ID of the customer"), no owner, never updated, and documenting in a file nobody can find.

    Data Quality Dimensions

    Measure data quality with a shared vocabulary: six core dimensions, profiling, scorecards and SQL checks.

    🧭 The core dimensions

    DimensionQuestionExample check
    AccuracyDoes it reflect real-world truth?Postal code exists for the city
    CompletenessIs required data present?Email not NULL for 99%+ of customers
    ConsistencySame fact, same value across systems?Customer status equal in CRM and billing
    TimelinessIs it available when needed and current?Orders loaded within 1 hour
    ValidityDoes it conform to format and rules?Date is a date; status in allowed list
    UniquenessIs each entity recorded once?No duplicate customer IDs
    Integrity (extra)Do references resolve?Every order has a valid customer_id
    📌
    Fit for purpose: the required quality level depends on the use. Monthly trend reporting can tolerate 1% missing values; regulatory reporting cannot.

    🔬 Profiling, measuring and scoring

    sqlSELECT COUNT(*) AS total,
  100.0 * COUNT(email) / COUNT(*) AS email_completeness,
  COUNT(*) - COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id) AS duplicate_ids,
  SUM(CASE WHEN email NOT LIKE '%_@_%._%' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS invalid_email,
  SUM(CASE WHEN created_at > CURRENT_TIMESTAMP THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS future_dates,
  MAX(loaded_at) AS last_load
FROM dim_customer;
    PointDetail
    Data profilingAutomated examination of a dataset: null counts, distinct counts, min/max, patterns, frequency of values, relationships between columns. Do this before writing requirements or rules.
    DQ scorePassed records / total records per rule, weighted by criticality, rolled up per dataset and domain into a scorecard with red/amber/green thresholds.
    Root causeTrace issues to source: manual entry, missing validation, integration mapping, timing, or definition mismatch. Fix upstream, not only in reports.
    Cost of poor qualityRework, wrong decisions, failed campaigns, compliance fines. Quantify it to win funding.
    Common mistakesMeasuring everything and acting on nothing, no thresholds, no owner for issues, and cleaning data in the report layer only.

    📈 Advanced: Statistical Quality, Observability & Scoring Advanced

    TopicDetail
    Data observability pillarsFreshness, volume, schema, distribution, lineage
    Anomaly detectionCompare row counts and null rates to seasonal baselines using z-scores, IQR or forecast bands, not fixed thresholds alone
    Critical data elements (CDEs)Prioritise quality investment by regulatory and business impact
    Weighted DQ scoreScore = Σ(weight × dimension pass rate); weight by criticality so a cosmetic field cannot mask a key failure
    Error budgetsDefine acceptable defect rate per dataset (e.g. 0.5%) and escalate when exceeded
    Cost of poor qualityRework, failed decisions, fines, lost revenue; quantify to win funding
    Acceptance samplingInspect a statistically valid sample when full checks are too costly
    ReconciliationControl totals, row counts and hash totals between source and target at each hop
    sql-- Distribution drift: today's null rate vs 28-day baseline
WITH d AS (SELECT load_date, AVG((email IS NULL)::int) AS null_rate FROM stg_customer GROUP BY 1),
     s AS (SELECT AVG(null_rate) mu, STDDEV(null_rate) sd FROM d WHERE load_date < CURRENT_DATE)
SELECT d.load_date, d.null_rate, (d.null_rate - s.mu)/NULLIF(s.sd,0) AS z
FROM d, s WHERE d.load_date = CURRENT_DATE;   -- alert when |z| > 3

    Data Validation Rules & Controls

    Design rules that catch bad data early: rule types, where to place them, automated tests, reconciliation and incident handling.

    ✅ Rule types and where to apply them

    Rule typeExample
    Not null / mandatorycustomer_id required
    Data type and formatISO date; phone matches pattern
    Range / domain0 <= discount <= 100; status IN (...)
    UniquenessOne row per order_id
    Referential integrityOrder must reference an existing customer
    Cross-fieldship_date >= order_date
    Cross-system / reconciliationWarehouse revenue equals finance ledger within 0.1%
    Statistical / anomalyDaily row count within 3 SD of 28-day average
    FreshnessLatest record no older than 2 hours
    Control pointPurpose
    At entry (forms, APIs)Prevent bad data at the source: cheapest fix
    At ingestionSchema and type checks; quarantine rejected rows
    In transformationTests on staging and marts (dbt tests, Great Expectations)
    Before publicationReconciliation and thresholds block a bad release
    In monitoringFreshness, volume and anomaly alerts

    🛠️ Implementing and operating rules

    yaml# dbt schema tests
models:
  - name: dim_customer
    columns:
      - name: customer_id
        tests: [unique, not_null]
      - name: status
        tests:
          - accepted_values: {values: ['active', 'inactive', 'prospect']}
      - name: country_id
        tests:
          - relationships: {to: ref('dim_country'), field: country_id}
    sql-- Reconciliation: warehouse vs finance ledger by month
SELECT w.m, w.rev AS dw_rev, f.rev AS ledger_rev, w.rev - f.rev AS diff
FROM dw_monthly w JOIN ledger_monthly f USING (m)
WHERE ABS(w.rev - f.rev) > 0.001 * f.rev;
    PointDetail
    SeverityCritical (block the pipeline), warning (alert and continue), info (log). Match severity to business impact.
    ThresholdsDefine pass rates per rule (for example 99.5%) and agree them with the owner.
    Issue managementLog, triage, assign to a steward, fix at root cause, retest, close; track time to resolve.
    Data SLAsAgreed freshness, completeness and availability between producers and consumers.
    BA deliverableA rule catalog: rule ID, description, dataset, logic, severity, threshold, owner, action on failure.
    ✅
    Good rule: specific, testable, automated, owned, and tied to a business consequence, like any good requirement.

    ⚙️ Advanced: Rule Engines, Quarantine & Schema Drift Advanced

    PatternDescription
    Severity tiersWarn (log), error (fail pipeline), quarantine (route bad rows aside); match to business impact
    Circuit breakerStop downstream loads when critical checks fail so bad data never reaches dashboards
    Write-Audit-PublishWrite to staging, audit against rules, publish (swap) only if passing
    Dead-letter / quarantine tablesStore rejected rows with reason codes for stewards to fix and replay
    Cross-field and cross-table rulesship_date ≥ order_date; every fact key exists in the dimension; totals reconcile across systems
    Schema driftDetect added/removed/retyped columns; apply contracts and compatibility rules (additive allowed, breaking needs versioning)
    ToolsGreat Expectations, dbt tests, Soda, Deequ, Delta expectations; keep rules in version control
    Idempotent remediationFixes must be safely re-runnable and logged with before/after values
    sql-- Quarantine pattern: split valid and rejected rows with reasons
INSERT INTO quarantine_orders
SELECT o.*, CASE WHEN amount < 0 THEN 'NEG_AMOUNT'
                 WHEN customer_id IS NULL THEN 'NO_CUSTOMER'
                 WHEN ship_date < order_date THEN 'BAD_DATES' END AS reason
FROM stg_orders o
WHERE amount < 0 OR customer_id IS NULL OR ship_date < order_date;

    Lineage, MDM & Compliance

    Trace data from source to dashboard, keep a single trusted view of core entities, and meet privacy and regulatory obligations.

    🧬 Data lineage tracing

    PointDetail
    What it isA map of where data comes from, how it is transformed and where it is used: source system, pipeline, tables, columns, reports.
    TypesBusiness lineage (concepts and systems), technical lineage (tables, jobs), column-level lineage (field to field with transformation logic). Forward lineage shows downstream impact; backward lineage shows origin.
    Why it mattersImpact analysis before a change, root cause of a wrong number, audit and regulatory evidence (BCBS 239, SOX), and trust in KPIs.
    How it is capturedParsed from SQL and ETL metadata, dbt ref() graph, BI tool metadata, or documented manually for legacy flows. Tools: DataHub, Collibra, Purview, OpenLineage, dbt docs.
    How to trace a numberStart at the KPI, open its definition, follow the dataset to staging models, then to the source column and system owner; compare each hop to the previous.
    textSource CRM.orders.amount_usd
  -> stg_orders.amount        (cast to decimal, trim)
  -> fact_orders.net_amount   (amount - discount - tax)
  -> mart_revenue.revenue     (SUM by month, excludes status = 'cancelled')
  -> Dashboard 'Executive Revenue' tile 'Monthly Revenue'
    💡
    Impact analysis example: renaming discount in the source breaks fact_orders, mart_revenue and 3 dashboards. Lineage lets you list and notify owners before the release.

    🔒 Master data, privacy and compliance

    TopicKey ideas
    Master Data Management (MDM)One golden record for core entities (customer, product, supplier) built by matching, merging and survivorship rules; styles: registry, consolidation, coexistence, centralized
    Reference dataControlled code lists (country, currency, status) with one owner
    Data contractsProducer-consumer agreement on schema, semantics, SLAs and change process, enforced in CI
    PII & GDPR / CCPA / DPDPLawful basis, consent, purpose limitation, data subject rights (access, erasure), breach notification, privacy by design
    Protection techniquesMasking, tokenization, pseudonymization, encryption at rest and in transit, anonymization, least-privilege access
    RetentionKeep data only as long as required; schedule archiving and deletion
    AuditAccess logs, change logs, evidence of control operation
    Maturity levelDescription
    1 InitialAd hoc, heroics
    2 ManagedBasic policies, some owners
    3 DefinedStandard processes, catalog, stewards
    4 MeasuredKPIs for quality and governance
    5 OptimizedAutomated, continuously improved
    ⚠️
    Governance fails when it is a policy document with no owner, no tooling and no link to business value. Start with the few critical data elements that drive decisions and regulation.

    🧩 Advanced: MDM Matching, Lineage Depth & Privacy Engineering Advanced

    TopicDetail
    MDM stylesRegistry (index only), consolidation (hub for analytics), coexistence (sync both ways), centralised (hub is the system of entry)
    MatchingDeterministic (exact keys) vs probabilistic (Fellegi-Sunter weights, fuzzy name/address similarity) with review band between auto-merge and no-match thresholds
    SurvivorshipRules choose the golden value per attribute: most recent, most trusted source, most complete
    Lineage granularityTable, column, and (rarely) row-level; capture via SQL parsing and OpenLineage events; use for impact analysis and audits
    RegulationsGDPR (lawfulness, minimisation, erasure, DPIA), India DPDP Act 2023 (consent, data fiduciary duties), CCPA/CPRA, HIPAA, SOX/BCBS 239 for reporting integrity
    De-identificationMasking, tokenisation, pseudonymisation (reversible, still personal data), anonymisation (irreversible)
    k-anonymity / differential privacyEach record indistinguishable among k; DP adds calibrated noise so individuals cannot be inferred
    Retention and erasureRetention schedules, legal holds, and propagation of deletion requests across copies, backups and downstream marts
    Privacy by designMinimise collection, purpose-limit, classify at ingestion, enforce with dynamic masking and access reviews

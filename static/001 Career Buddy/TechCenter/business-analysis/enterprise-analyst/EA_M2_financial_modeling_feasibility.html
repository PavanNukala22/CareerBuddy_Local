    Financial Foundations & Feasibility

    Time value of money, cash-flow thinking, cost and benefit types, and the five feasibility lenses — the basics every financial case rests on.

    💰 Why Financial Modeling Matters to a BA

    Fresher

    A financial model is a structured, numerical representation of a decision: what it costs, what it returns and when. An Enterprise Analyst uses it to answer the executive question: “Is this worth doing, compared with the alternatives?”

    PointDetail
    What it isA spreadsheet-based or tool-based forecast of costs, benefits and cash flows over a defined period.
    Why it is usedFunding is scarce. Models make assumptions visible, allow comparison of options and expose which inputs the decision depends on.
    How it worksInputs (assumptions) → calculations (cash flows, discounting) → outputs (NPV, IRR, payback, ROI) → sensitivity and scenarios.
    Who consumes itCFO, investment committee, PMO, sponsors, procurement and audit.
    💡
    A model is only as credible as its assumptions. Always keep an Assumptions sheet with source, owner and confidence for each input.

    ⏳ Time Value of Money (TVM)

    FresherIntermediate

    A rupee/dollar today is worth more than one received later, because it can earn a return and because of inflation and risk. Finance therefore discounts future cash flows.

    formulasFuture Value   FV = PV x (1 + r)^n
Present Value  PV = FV / (1 + r)^n
Example: 110 received in 1 year at r = 10%  ->  PV = 110 / 1.10 = 100

Discount factor (year t) = 1 / (1 + r)^t
    TermMeaning
    Discount rate (r)Required rate of return; often WACC or a hurdle rate (e.g., 10–12%)
    Nominal vs realNominal includes inflation; real excludes it. Never mix them in one model
    HorizonYears modelled, typically 3–7 for IT, 10+ for infrastructure
    Terminal valueValue assumed beyond the horizon; use cautiously
    ⚠️
    Benefits that arrive late (year 4–5) are heavily discounted. Delaying delivery by a year can turn a positive NPV into a negative one.

    🔍 Costs, Benefits and the Five Feasibility Lenses

    FresherIntermediate

    Cost types

    • CapEx — one-off asset purchase (hardware, build)
    • OpEx — recurring run cost (licences, support)
    • Direct — traceable to the project
    • Indirect — shared overheads
    • Sunk — already spent; ignore in decisions
    • Opportunity cost — value of the best alternative forgone

    Benefit types

    • Tangible/hard — cost savings, revenue uplift
    • Intangible/soft — brand, morale, agility
    • Cost avoidance — fines or incidents prevented
    • Revenue growth — new sales, retention
    • Productivity — hours saved (only real if redeployed or removed)
    FeasibilityQuestionEvidence
    TechnicalCan we build/buy and run it?Architecture review, proof of concept
    EconomicAre returns worth the cost?NPV, IRR, payback
    OperationalWill people use and support it?Change readiness, process fit
    Legal/RegulatoryIs it allowed and compliant?Legal opinion, regulatory mapping
    ScheduleCan it land in time?Plan, dependencies, resource availability
    📌
    Soft savings caution: “Saves 2,000 staff hours” is not a financial benefit unless headcount, overtime or capacity is actually released.

    Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA)

    A complete, step-by-step CBA: identifying and valuing costs and benefits, discounting, comparing options and testing sensitivity.

    ⚖️ CBA: What It Is and Types

    Fresher

    Cost-Benefit Analysis compares the total expected costs of a decision with its total expected benefits, expressed in monetary terms and discounted to present value.

    TypeUseOutput
    Simple CBA (undiscounted)Small, short decisionsTotal benefits − total costs
    Discounted CBAMulti-year investmentsNPV, Benefit-Cost Ratio
    Cost-Effectiveness AnalysisBenefits cannot be monetized (e.g., public health)Cost per unit of outcome
    Social CBAPublic policy with externalitiesNet social value
    Incremental CBAChoosing between optionsCost/benefit of upgrading option A → B
    CBA workflow

    🧮 Worked Example: Digital Claims Platform

    Intermediate

    A company can invest 500 (thousands) now. It expects 200 per year of benefits for 5 years and 30 per year of running costs. Discount rate = 10%.

    cbaYear      0      1      2      3      4      5
Benefits  0    200    200    200    200    200
Costs   500     30     30     30     30     30
Net    -500    170    170    170    170    170

PV(benefits) = 758.2
PV(costs)    = 613.7
NPV          = 144.4      (PV benefits - PV costs)
BCR          = 1.24     (PV benefits / PV costs)
IRR          = 20.8%
Payback      = 2.9 years (simple), 3.7 years (discounted)
ROI (simple) = 70% = (850-500)/500
    ✅
    NPV is positive and BCR > 1 → the project creates value at a 10% required return. IRR (20.8%) exceeds the hurdle rate.

    🛠️ Valuing Hard-to-Measure Benefits

    IntermediateAdvanced
    BenefitHow to value itCaution
    Time savedHours × loaded cost × % actually redeployedOnly count redeployed/released capacity
    Error reductionError rate drop × cost per error (rework, penalties)Use audited baseline
    Customer retentionChurn drop × customer lifetime valueAttribute carefully; avoid double counting
    Risk reductionProbability × impact (expected loss avoided)Document probability source
    ComplianceFine/penalty avoided + audit cost savedTreat mandatory items as ‘must-do’, not ROI-ranked
    Brand / satisfactionProxy via NPS-to-revenue link, or keep unmonetizedNever invent figures; list as qualitative
    ⚠️
    Double counting: “reduced churn” and “higher revenue” may be the same benefit. Maintain a benefit register with unique IDs and owners.

    🧪 Sensitivity, Scenarios and Break-even

    AdvancedExpert

    Sensitivity analysis

    • Change one input at a time (±10/20%)
    • Tornado chart ranks drivers
    • Reveals which assumption matters most

    Scenario analysis

    • Change sets of inputs together: Best / Base / Worst
    • Reflects correlated risk
    • Used in investment committee papers
    sensitivityBase NPV = 144.4
Benefits -20%  (160/yr) -> NPV = -7.2
Discount 14%             -> NPV = 83.6
Capex +30% (650)         -> NPV = -5.6
Break-even annual net benefit = 500 / 3.791 = 131.9
    💡
    Break-even tells executives how wrong we can be before the case fails — a more honest message than a single NPV.

    Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)

    All the costs of acquiring, running, supporting and retiring an asset over its life — including the hidden ones.

    🧊 TCO: What It Is and the Iceberg

    Fresher

    Total Cost of Ownership is the full lifecycle cost of an asset or service: purchase price plus everything needed to implement, operate, support, upgrade, secure and eventually dispose of it.

    Cost layerExamplesVisible?
    AcquisitionLicences, hardware, implementation feesYes
    ImplementationIntegration, data migration, configuration, testingPartly
    OperationsSupport, hosting, monitoring, admin staff, powerOften missed
    PeopleTraining, change management, productivity dipOften missed
    Risk & complianceSecurity, audits, downtime costHidden
    End of lifeDecommissioning, data archival, exit penaltiesHidden
    💡
    Sticker price is the tip of the iceberg: operations and people costs usually exceed acquisition costs over 5 years.

    🧮 Worked TCO: On-Premise vs Cloud (5 Years)

    Intermediate
    tco                       Year0   Y1   Y2   Y3   Y4   Y5   Total
On-premise
  Hardware+licences     550
  Support+staff+power         175  175  175  175  175
  Total                 550   175  175  175  175  175  1425

Cloud
  Migration             120
  Subscription+staff+ops      275  275  275  275  275
  Total                 120   275  275  275  275  275  1495

NPV of costs @10%:  On-prem = 1213   Cloud = 1162

    On raw 5-year spend, on-premise is cheaper (1425 vs 1495). The decision shifts when you add what the table omits: hardware refresh in year 6, capacity for peaks, resilience, speed of scaling and security tooling.

    📌
    TCO answers “what will it cost?”, not “is it worth it?”. Combine with benefits (CBA) to decide.

    🪤 TCO Pitfalls and Expert Practice

    AdvancedExpert

    Pitfalls

    • Comparing 3-year cost of one option with 5-year of another
    • Ignoring internal staff time
    • Leaving out exit and lock-in costs
    • Using vendor quotes without usage growth
    • Ignoring productivity dip during rollout

    Expert practice

    • Same horizon and discount rate for all options
    • Cost drivers: users, volume, data growth
    • Include contingency (10–20% by maturity)
    • Model three usage scenarios
    • Review actual vs. planned TCO post go-live
    Model typeCharacteristicExample
    FixedDoesn't vary with volumeAnnual licence
    VariableScales with usagePer-transaction fee, cloud compute
    Step-fixedJumps at thresholdsAnother admin per 500 users

    ROI and Investment Metrics

    ROI, NPV, IRR, payback, profitability index and EVA — what each measures, when each misleads and how to present them together.

    📈 The Core Metrics: What Is What

    FresherIntermediate
    MetricFormula / ideaReads asWeakness
    ROI(Total gain − cost) / cost% return on costIgnores time and timing
    PaybackTime to recover investmentYears/monthsIgnores value after payback
    Discounted paybackPayback using discounted cash flowsYearsStill ignores post-payback value
    NPVΣ discounted cash flows − investmentValue created in money todayNeeds a defensible discount rate
    IRRRate where NPV = 0% annualized returnMultiple IRRs with sign changes; hides scale
    Profitability Index (PI)PV benefits / PV costs (or NPV/investment)Value per unit investedNeeds capital rationing context
    EVAOperating profit − capital chargeValue above cost of capitalAccounting-based
    metricsUsing the example (invest 500, net 170/yr for 5 years, r=10%):
ROI        = 70%      (simple)
NPV        = 144.4
IRR        = 20.8%
Payback    = 2.9 yrs
PI         = 1.24

    🧭 Which Metric When?

    Advanced
    SituationPreferReason
    Single project, absolute value mattersNPVValue in money; additive across projects
    Comparing projects of different sizeIRR + PINormalizes for scale
    Cash-constrained firmPayback / discounted paybackLiquidity risk
    Mutually exclusive projectsNPVIRR can mislead when scales or timing differ
    Quick screeningROI, paybackEasy but incomplete
    ⚠️
    NPV vs IRR conflict: when they disagree on mutually exclusive projects, trust NPV; use incremental IRR if needed.

    🧠 Risk-Adjusted and Advanced Techniques

    Expert
    • Risk-adjusted discount rate: add a premium for risky projects (e.g., +2–5%).
    • Probability-weighted benefits: multiply by confidence (80% hard, 50% medium, 20% soft).
    • Monte Carlo simulation: sample inputs from ranges to produce NPV distribution; report P10/P50/P90.
    • Real options: value of flexibility (phase, defer, expand, abandon) — staged funding reduces regret.
    • Cost of delay: value lost per month of delay; central in prioritization (e.g., WSJF).
    • Benefits tapering: adoption curves — benefits ramp 25% / 60% / 100%, not instantly.
    rampBenefit steady-state 200/yr but adoption ramp: Y1 40%, Y2 75%, Y3-5 100%
Benefits = 80, 150, 200, 200, 200  ->  NPV falls versus the 'flat' assumption

    Business Case Justification

    Building and defending the business case: structure, options, risk, benefits realization and executive communication.

    📑 Anatomy of a Business Case

    FresherIntermediate
    PointDetail
    Executive summaryDecision requested, recommended option, headline NPV/payback, ask (funding, approvals).
    Problem / opportunityEvidence-based statement, linked to a strategic objective.
    Options consideredDo nothing, minimum, recommended, premium; each with cost, benefit, risk.
    Financial analysisAssumptions, cash flows, NPV/IRR/payback, sensitivity.
    Benefits & ownershipBenefit register with KPI, baseline, target, owner and measurement date.
    Risks & dependenciesTop risks with probability, impact, mitigation, contingency.
    Delivery & governanceTimeline, milestones, funding stages, decision gates.
    RecommendationClear ‘approve / defer / reject’ with conditions.
    💡
    The business case is a decision document, not a project plan. The first page must stand alone.

    🧩 The Five-Case Model and Options Analysis

    Advanced
    CaseQuestionEvidence
    StrategicWhy do it? Does it fit strategy?Alignment, need, objectives
    EconomicWhich option gives best value?CBA, NPV, options
    CommercialCan we buy it well?Procurement, vendor terms
    FinancialCan we afford it?Budget, cash flow, funding source
    ManagementCan we deliver it?Plan, resources, risks, governance

    Always include ‘Do Nothing’ (the baseline). Incremental value is measured against it — including the cost of doing nothing (rising maintenance, regulatory exposure, lost customers).

    optionsOption 0  Do nothing         NPV -120  (costs grow, penalties)
Option 1  Minimum fix        NPV  +40
Option 2  Replace platform   NPV +145  <- recommended
Option 3  Replace + AI suite NPV  +90  (higher risk, late benefits)

    🎤 Defending the Case in Front of Finance

    AdvancedExpert
    • Anticipate the CFO's questions: What if benefits are half? What is the payback? Who owns each benefit? What happens if we wait a year?
    • Baseline credibility: use audited volumes and cost data; label estimates.
    • Show confidence levels per benefit and exclude low-confidence items from the headline.
    • Stage-gated funding: release money in tranches as milestones prove assumptions.
    • Post-implementation review (PIR): commit to measuring actual vs. planned benefits at 6 and 12 months.
    ✅
    Winning cases are conservative on benefits, realistic on costs, explicit on risk. Over-optimism destroys credibility for the next case.

    📗 Model Structure and Hygiene

    IntermediateAdvanced
    SheetContent
    README / CoverPurpose, version, owner, colour legend
    AssumptionsAll inputs in one place; units, source, owner
    CalculationsYearly cash flows, formulas only, no hard-coding
    OutputsNPV, IRR, payback, charts
    SensitivityData tables / scenarios
    ChecksBalance checks, sum tests, error flags
    excel=NPV(rate, Y1:Y5) + Year0            // NPV (Year0 not discounted)
=IRR(Year0:Year5)                  // IRR
=XNPV(rate, values, dates)         // irregular dates
=PMT(rate, nper, -loan)            // repayment
=SUMPRODUCT(units, price)          // revenue
=INDEX/MATCH or XLOOKUP            // scenario switch
    ⚠️
    Excel's NPV() assumes the first value occurs at end of period 1. Add the time-zero investment outside the function.

    🧪 Mini Case: ERP Replacement

    Expert
    PointDetail
    ContextManufacturer with 18-year-old ERP; downtime 3 days/yr; manual reconciliations; audit findings.
    OptionsDo nothing, patch, replace with cloud ERP, replace + advanced planning.
    CostsImplementation 2.4M, annual subscription 0.6M, internal team 0.4M for 2 years, training 0.15M.
    BenefitsInventory reduction (working capital), 14 FTE reconciliation work released (redeployed), audit fine avoidance, faster close.
    Key assumptions3-year adoption ramp; 70% confidence on inventory benefit; discount rate 11%.
    DecisionCloud ERP replacement positive NPV; staged funding; PIR at month 12.
    💡
    Practice: rebuild this case in a spreadsheet, then run Best / Base / Worst scenarios and compute break-even adoption rate.

    🚫 Common Failures and Checklist

    Expert

    Failures

    • Hard-coded numbers in formulas
    • Mixing nominal and real values
    • Double-counting benefits
    • Treating soft savings as cash
    • Ignoring ramp-up and change costs
    • Single-point estimate with no sensitivity

    Checklist

    • Assumptions sourced and owned?
    • Same horizon/discount for all options?
    • Do nothing included?
    • Sensitivity and break-even shown?
    • Benefit owners and KPIs defined?
    • PIR planned?

    Tools, Quality & Best Practices

    Workflow, review checklist and habits.

    🔧 Tools & Workflows

    Define options → estimate costs (TCO) → quantify benefits → discount cash flows → test sensitivity → write the business case → recommend.

    ✅ Testing & Quality

    • Assumptions are listed and sourced
    • Discount rate and time horizon are stated
    • Sensitivity shows the decision is robust

    ⭐ Best Practices

    • State every assumption and its source
    • Include hidden and exit costs
    • Show sensitivity, not a single number

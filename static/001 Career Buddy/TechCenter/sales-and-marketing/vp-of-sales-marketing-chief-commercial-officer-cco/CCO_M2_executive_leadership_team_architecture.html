    Foundations of Commercial Leadership

    What a VP of Sales & Marketing / CCO leads, which roles report to them and the common organisation models.

    What does the CCO lead?

    The CCO leads the commercial organisation: marketing, sales, sales development, solutions engineering, partnerships, RevOps and often customer success. The job is not doing the work of each function; it is setting direction, building the right team, creating the operating system and developing leaders (directors and department heads).

    RoleCore responsibilityTypical reports to CCO?
    VP / Head of MarketingBrand, demand gen, product marketingYes
    VP / Head of SalesNew business and quota deliveryYes
    Head of Customer SuccessOnboarding, retention, expansionOften
    Head of RevOpsProcess, data, systems, forecastingYes
    Head of PartnershipsChannels, alliances, marketplacesOften
    Sales Enablement leadTraining, content, playbooksThrough sales or RevOps

    Organisation models

    FunctionalTeams by function (marketing, SDR, AE, CS). Clear expertise; risk of silos.
    Segment-basedTeams by customer size (SMB, mid-market, enterprise). Tailored motion per segment.
    Vertical / industryTeams by industry. Deep domain expertise; needs scale.
    GeographicTeams by region. Local relevance; duplicated overhead.
    Pod / squadCross-functional unit (AE + SDR + SE + CSM) owning a patch. Fast, accountable.
    💡
    No model is perfect. Choose the one that best fits your buyer, deal complexity and stage, and revisit as you scale.

    Leadership vs management

    • Leadership: vision, direction, culture, inspiring change.
    • Management: planning, process, performance, resources.
    • At executive level you need both, plus judgement under uncertainty and influence across peers (CFO, CPO, CEO).

    Designing the Commercial Team

    Spans, layers, ratios, specialisation and the logic of how to structure for growth.

    Principles of org design

    1. Start from strategy: segments, motions and targets determine roles.
    2. Clear ownership: every account, lead and metric has one owner.
    3. Minimise handoffs where customers feel friction.
    4. Right span of control: managers have enough capacity to coach.
    5. Design for the next 12–18 months, not only today.

    Typical ratios and spans

    RatioTypical rangeWhy it matters
    Front-line sales manager : AEs1 : 6–8Enough time for coaching and deal inspection
    SDR : AE1 : 2–3 (varies)Pipeline supply for each AE
    AE : Solutions Engineer3–5 : 1Technical coverage of demos and POCs
    CSM : accountsDepends on ACV (e.g. 1 : 40 mid-market)Retention and expansion capacity
    Marketing : sales spendCompany specificBalance of demand creation and conversion

    Example: 24 AEs at a 1:8 span need 3 front-line managers; at an SDR:AE ratio of 1:3 they need 8 SDRs.

    Centralise or embed?

    Marketing can be a centralised hub (shared services, consistent brand) or embedded in segments or product lines (speed and context). Many firms use hub-and-spoke: a central core for brand, analytics and operations, with embedded demand and product-marketing specialists.

    🏢
    Illustrative case. A growing SaaS firm moved from one generalist sales team to SMB, mid-market and enterprise pods. SMB cycle time halved because reps stopped being pulled into long enterprise deals, and enterprise win rates rose with dedicated solution engineers.

    Practice: design the team

    ✍️
    Exercise. You must deliver $30M new ARR next year with a $60k average deal. Propose the structure (segments, pods), headcount for SDR, AE, SE, CSM and managers, and reporting lines. State three assumptions and one risk.

    Hiring Directors and Department Heads

    Build a bench of leaders who can scale their functions and collaborate across the revenue engine.

    Defining the role with a scorecard

    A hiring scorecard defines the mission, 3–5 outcomes for the first year, required competencies and cultural attributes before any interview.

    Role: Director of Demand Generation
Mission: Build predictable pipeline from $18M to $30M
Outcomes (12 mo): 1) Pipeline created $45M  2) CPL -15%
  3) SQL conversion +20%  4) Team of 8 hired and ramped
Competencies: analytics, channel mix, coaching, cross-functional influence

    Structured interviewing

    • Use the same questions and rating rubric for every candidate to reduce bias.
    • Behavioural questions: "Tell me about a time you rebuilt a team that missed target."
    • Work sample or case: plan a 90-day pipeline recovery with real data.
    • Reference checks: ask peers, direct reports and the former boss.
    • Evaluate how they scale: have they led leaders before, or only individual contributors?

    Hire, promote or build?

    OptionProsCons
    External hireNew skills and perspectivesRamp time, culture risk, cost
    Internal promotionContext, retention, motivationMay lack skills for next level
    Interim / fractionalSpeed, flexibilityLess commitment
    ⚠️
    A top performer is not automatically a good manager. Promoting the best AE into management without support often loses a great seller and gains an overwhelmed manager.

    Onboarding and ramp

    Plan 30-60-90 days: learn product and customers, shadow key activities, take on a first win, then own a target. Typical AE ramp is 3–6 months for mid-market and 6–9 for enterprise; budget capacity accordingly.

    Leading, Coaching and Holding Leaders Accountable

    How a CCO gets results through directors and heads: clarity, cadence, coaching and courage.

    Clarity: goals and decision rights

    • Give each leader a mission, 3–5 measurable goals and decision rights.
    • Use RACI to remove ambiguity between marketing, sales and CS.
    • Decide what you will decide, what you will delegate and what needs consultation.
    DecisionCCOVP SalesVP MarketingCFO
    Annual quota allocationARCC
    Brand campaign spendACRC
    Pricing changeACCR/C

    Cadence: 1:1s and team forums

    • Weekly 1:1s: priorities, blockers, development (not just status).
    • Monthly leadership meeting: cross-functional issues, budget, hiring.
    • Quarterly offsite: strategy, learning, relationship building.
    • Use written pre-reads; protect meeting time for decisions.

    Coaching and performance

    Use situational leadership: direct for new leaders, coach for developing, support for capable, delegate for experts. Give specific, timely feedback (situation–behaviour–impact). For underperformance: clarify expectations in writing, agree a time-bound plan, support, then decide.

    💡
    Praise in public, correct in private, and keep promises. Trust is the currency of executive leadership.

    Managing cross-functional conflict

    Typical conflicts: lead quality, territory, discounting, credit for revenue. Approach: restate the shared goal, use data, clarify decision rights, agree the experiment or rule, and review results. Escalate only after peers have tried to resolve it.

    Compensation, Enablement and Culture

    The systems that shape behaviour: pay plans, training, careers and the climate you create.

    Sales compensation basics

    TermMeaningExample
    OTEOn-target earnings: base + variable at 100% quota$200k OTE
    Pay mixBase / variable split50/50
    QuotaTarget the rep must achieve$1.0M new ARR
    Commission rateVariable ÷ quota$100k ÷ $1.0M = 10%
    AcceleratorHigher rate above quota15% above 100%
    SPIFFShort-term incentive$1k per pilot booked
    ⚠️
    You get what you pay for. Comp for bookings without retention rules invites discounting and poor-fit deals; comp for activity invites low-quality behaviour.

    Enablement and development

    • Onboarding bootcamp and certification before quota carrying.
    • Playbooks for discovery, demo, objection handling and negotiation.
    • Call coaching using recordings and rubrics.
    • Career paths: IC (senior AE, principal) and management ladders.
    • Measure enablement by ramp time, win rate and quota attainment, not attendance.

    Culture and retention

    • Define values in behaviours: "we share pipeline", "we tell the truth in the forecast".
    • Recognise teamwork, not only individual quota.
    • Monitor attrition by manager, tenure and stage; exit interviews reveal systemic issues.
    • Create inclusive hiring and promotion practices.
    🏢
    Illustrative case. A firm saw 35% annual rep attrition. Analysis showed most exits were within 9 months and clustered under two managers. After manager coaching, better territory balance and a revised ramp quota, attrition fell to 18% and attainment rose.

    Scaling the Org, Reorganising and Leading Change

    When and how to evolve the structure, and how to lead people through change.

    Org evolution by stage

    StageTypical structureCCO focus
    Early (seed–A)Founder-led sales, generalistsRepeatable motion, first leaders
    Growth (B–C)Segments, SDR/AE/CS split, RevOpsProcess, hiring, capacity
    Scale (D+)Regions, verticals, specialist teams, enablementLeaders of leaders, efficiency
    MaturePlatform and portfolio, M&A integrationProfit, retention, innovation

    Leading a reorganisation

    1. Diagnose: what problem will the new structure solve? Use data.
    2. Design options and test with front-line leaders.
    3. Decide and communicate the why, the what and the what happens to me.
    4. Implement quickly with clear transition plans (accounts, comp, targets).
    5. Review at 30/90 days and adjust.
    ⚠️
    Frequent reorganisations destroy trust and account relationships. Change when the evidence is clear, not for novelty.

    Change management and succession

    • Use a framework such as Kotter (urgency, coalition, vision) or ADKAR (awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement).
    • Identify change champions and address resistance with listening and clarity.
    • Maintain a succession plan: ready-now and ready-in-2-years successors for each key role.
    • When restructuring or reducing headcount, act with fairness, legal compliance and dignity.

    Integrating acquired teams

    In M&A, decide early on the operating model (integrate, keep separate, hybrid), harmonise comp and CRM, protect key customers and retain key talent with clarity on roles and incentives.

    Cases, Risk Register & 90-Day Roadmap

    Apply the module to realistic leadership dilemmas.

    Case studies

    SituationDiagnosisAction
    Star AE promoted, team misses planSkill gap and no manager supportCoaching, manager training, shadowing, clear scorecard
    Marketing and sales blame each otherUnclear shared goalsJoint pipeline target, SLA, weekly forum
    Attrition spikes after quota riseUnattainable quotasReview quota-setting, territory design, comp
    New director fails at month 6Hired for IC strength, not leadershipScorecard, structured interviews, leader-of-leaders criteria

    Risk register

    • Key-leader departure without successor.
    • Over-hiring before the model is validated.
    • Misaligned comp that drives wrong behaviour.
    • Reorganisation fatigue.
    • Culture dilution during rapid growth.
    • Loss of customer relationships when accounts move.

    90-day plan for a new CCO

    WindowFocus
    Days 1–30Meet every leader and top customers; review org, comp, attrition, pipeline
    Days 31–60Set mission and goals for each leader; fix top 2 structural issues; define cadence
    Days 61–90Make hiring/structure decisions; launch enablement and comp adjustments; communicate the plan

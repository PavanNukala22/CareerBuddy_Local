    Foundations of Revenue & GTM

    What the CCO owns, what a go-to-market strategy is, and the vocabulary every revenue leader must speak fluently.

    What is revenue strategy and GTM ownership?

    A Chief Commercial Officer (CCO) or VP of Sales & Marketing is the single accountable executive for how a company acquires, converts, retains and grows customers. Go-to-market (GTM) strategy is the plan for reaching target customers, winning them and scaling revenue: it covers market, positioning, pricing, channels, sales motion and customer lifecycle. Revenue strategy connects that plan to targets, capacity, budget and measurement.

    TermMeaningExample
    GTM strategyHow you reach, win and grow target customersMid-market SaaS sold via inside sales plus partners
    Revenue engineSales, marketing, RevOps and customer success working to one targetOne shared pipeline and retention goal
    RevOpsProcess, data, systems and reporting across the revenue teamsSingle CRM, common funnel stages, one forecast
    ICPIdeal Customer Profile: the accounts most likely to buy, succeed and stay200-1,000 employee fintechs in India and the UK
    ARR / MRRAnnual / monthly recurring revenue$12M ARR = about $1M MRR
    NRRNet revenue retention: revenue from existing customers incl. expansion minus churn112% NRR means the base grows 12% with no new logos
    💡
    Think of the CCO as the owner of the whole revenue outcome, not of a department. Marketing, sales and customer success are levers; the target is shared.

    Types of GTM motions

    Sales-ledReps drive discovery, demos and negotiation. Best for complex, high-ACV, multi-stakeholder deals.
    Product-led (PLG)Free trial or freemium; the product converts and expands users. Best for low-friction, self-serve products.
    Marketing-ledDemand generation creates and qualifies most pipeline for sales to close.
    Partner / channel-ledResellers, integrators and marketplaces sell and implement. Good for reach and local markets.
    Community-ledUsers and peers drive adoption and advocacy; strong for developer tools.

    Most scaled companies run a hybrid: for example PLG for entry, sales-assist for expansion and enterprise sales for large accounts.

    Why alignment fails

    • Different definitions: marketing counts MQLs, sales counts opportunities, finance counts bookings.
    • Different incentives: leads rewarded for volume, reps rewarded for revenue.
    • Different data: separate tools, spreadsheets and "versions of truth".
    • No shared owner of the end-to-end funnel and post-sale revenue.
    ⚠️
    Misalignment is expensive: leads go stale, reps ignore marketing, customers get inconsistent messages and forecasts become guesses.

    Aligning Sales, Marketing, RevOps & Customer Success

    Build one revenue engine with shared definitions, shared targets, shared data and clear handoffs.

    From funnel to bowtie

    The classic funnel ends at "closed-won". The bowtie adds the right half: onboarding, adoption, renewal and expansion, because in subscription businesses most lifetime value comes after the first sale.

    StageOwner (primary)Key metric
    Awareness / demandMarketingQualified pipeline created
    Qualification and sellingSales (SDR/AE)Stage conversion, win rate, cycle time
    OnboardingCustomer successTime to first value
    Adoption and renewalCustomer successGross retention, churn
    ExpansionCS + salesNet revenue retention, expansion ARR

    Shared definitions and SLAs

    Alignment starts with stage definitions with exit criteria that everyone follows.

    TermDefinition (example)
    MQLLead meeting ICP fit + engagement score threshold
    SALMQL accepted by sales within SLA
    SQLLead qualified by sales with budget, need and authority signals
    OpportunityQualified deal with a named buyer, problem, timeline and amount in CRM
    Closed-wonSigned contract; booked in finance system

    Marketing-to-sales SLA: marketing delivers an agreed volume and quality of MQLs; sales follows up within (for example) 1 business hour for hot leads and gives disposition feedback within 5 days. Closed-loop reporting feeds outcomes back to marketing.

    RevOps as the connective tissue

    • Process: lead routing, handoffs, stage governance, deal desk.
    • Data: clean CRM, enrichment, one definition of account and contact.
    • Systems: CRM, marketing automation, CS platform, forecasting, billing integrations.
    • Analytics: funnel, forecast, attribution, capacity and territory planning.
    🏢
    Illustrative case. A SaaS firm had 3 funnel definitions and 3 dashboards. The CCO created a single funnel, SLAs and a weekly joint pipeline review. Within two quarters lead follow-up time fell from 2 days to 3 hours and SQL-to-opportunity conversion rose 18% because disputes became data conversations.

    Market Sizing, ICP, Segmentation & Positioning

    Decide where to play and how to win before deciding how much to spend.

    TAM, SAM and SOM

    MeasureMeaningExample
    TAMTotal revenue if every possible customer bought500,000 firms × $20k = $10B
    SAMPortion you can serve with your product, geography and channels120,000 firms × $20k = $2.4B
    SOMShare you can realistically win in the plan period1,200 firms × $20k = $24M

    Top-down sizing uses analyst data; bottom-up sizing multiplies real accounts by realistic price. Boards trust bottom-up more.

    ICP and segmentation

    Build the ICP from your best customers: highest retention, expansion, margin and shortest sales cycle. Typical attributes: industry, size, region, tech stack, trigger events and buying committee.

    SegmentACVMotionCycle
    SMB$3–10kPLG + inside sales1–4 weeks
    Mid-market$20–80kInside sales + partners1–3 months
    Enterprise$150k+Field sales + ABM + SE6–12 months
    ⚠️
    ICP drift: chasing any deal to hit the quarter fills the base with poor-fit customers who churn and raise support cost.

    Positioning and messaging

    • Positioning: the category you claim, for whom, with what unique value versus alternatives.
    • Messaging hierarchy: value proposition → three pillars → proof points → persona-specific variants.
    • Competitive framing: why you win, where you lose, how to handle each objection.
    • Proof: case studies, benchmarks, analyst validation, references.

    Pricing and packaging as GTM levers

    Pricing signals value and decides which motion works. Per-seat, usage-based and platform-plus-modules pricing each suit different buyers. Packaging (good/better/best) enables upsell paths. Discount policy and approval limits protect margin.

    Revenue Planning, Funnel Math & Capacity Modelling

    Turn an ambitious target into a funded, resourced and testable plan.

    Top-down meets bottom-up

    Top-down targets come from the board or investors (for example grow 50%). Bottom-up plans come from funnel conversion and sales capacity. The CCO reconciles the two and presents the gap with options to close it.

    Worked example: reverse funnel

    Target: $20M new ARR. Average deal size $50k, win rate 25%, SQL-to-opportunity 50%.

    StepCalculationResult
    Deals needed$20M ÷ $50k400 wins
    Opportunities400 ÷ 25%1,600
    SQLs1,600 ÷ 50%3,200
    Pipeline (3× coverage)$20M × 3$60M pipeline

    Capacity: quota $1.2M per rep with 70% expected attainment → effective $840k. Reps needed = $20M ÷ $0.84M ≈ 24 ramped reps. Add ramp time (3–6 months), attrition and territory design.

    rep_capacity = quota * expected_attainment      # 1.2M * 0.70 = 840k
reps_needed  = ceil(target / rep_capacity)         # ceil(20M / 840k) = 24
coverage     = open_pipeline / remaining_target     # healthy: 3x-4x

    Pipeline coverage and timing

    • Coverage targets depend on win rate: at 25% win rate you need about 4× theoretical coverage; 3× assumes ~33%.
    • Pipeline created now converts after the sales cycle; late pipeline cannot save this quarter.
    • Track pipeline velocity = (opportunities × win rate × average deal) ÷ cycle length.
    💡
    Always tie hiring and marketing spend to the funnel model. If the model needs 3,200 SQLs, show where they come from by channel.

    Practice: build a plan

    ✍️
    Exercise. Your target is $12M new ARR, ACV $40k, win rate 20%, quota $900k, attainment 75%. Calculate wins, opportunities, pipeline at 4× coverage and reps required. State two assumptions you would challenge.

    Activity / demandMeetings held, qualified leads, content-influenced pipelineLeading
    EfficiencyCAC, payback, magic number, sales productivityLagging

    Review cadence

    CadenceForumDecisions
    WeeklyRevenue review (CCO, sales, marketing, RevOps, CS)Pipeline gaps, deal risk, campaign fixes
    MonthlyBusiness reviewBudget shifts, hiring, channel mix
    QuarterlyQBR / planningTargets, OKRs, territory and comp changes
    AnnualStrategy and budgetSegments, pricing, org design

    OKRs for the revenue team

    Objective: Win the mid-market fintech segment. KR1: $9M new ARR. KR2: 35% win rate in ICP accounts. KR3: NRR 110%. KR4: Pipeline coverage at 3.5× by week 4 of every quarter.

    ⚠️
    Avoid vanity KRs (leads, impressions) without a revenue link; they reward activity rather than outcome.

    Forecasting discipline

    • Use stage-weighted and historical conversion methods together.
    • Stage exit criteria and next-step dates reduce rep optimism.
    • Categories: commit, best case, pipeline; track forecast accuracy by week.
    • Inspect aging deals and single-threaded opportunities.

    Scaling GTM: Hybrid Motions, Partners, New Markets

    Expert judgement on trade-offs when growth, efficiency and risk collide.

    Hybrid PLG and sales-led

    PLG creates users; sales captures enterprise value. Use product-qualified leads (PQLs) (usage signals such as team invites or limits hit) to route accounts to sales-assist. Define rules of engagement to avoid channel and comp conflicts between self-serve and reps.

    Account-based and enterprise GTM

    • Tier accounts (1:1, 1:few, 1:many) by potential and fit.
    • Coordinate marketing, SDRs, AEs, solutions engineers and executives around each key account.
    • Measure account engagement, buying-committee coverage and pipeline per target account.

    Partners, channels and new markets

    Use partners to scale reach and local credibility. Provide enablement, deal registration, margin and conflict rules. For new regions, validate with a pilot (partner or small team) and unit economics before building a large direct force.

    🌍
    Illustrative case. A software firm entering the Middle East signed two regional integrators, ran 12 pilot accounts and confirmed payback under 14 months before hiring local sales managers, saving an estimated year of fixed cost.

    Efficiency and measurement at scale

    • Magic number (annualised net new recurring revenue ÷ prior-period S&M spend) indicates sales efficiency.
    • Incrementality tests and marketing-mix modelling show causal channel impact beyond last-click.
    • Watch CAC trend, ICP drift and discounting as early warnings.
    • AI can assist with scoring, forecasting and content, but data quality and governance come first.

    Cases, Risk Register & 90-Day Roadmap

    Apply the module: diagnose, decide and sequence.

    Case studies

    SituationDiagnosisAction
    High traffic, low pipelineWeak MOFU/BOFU, poor ICP targetingRebuild ICP, add intent content, SDR follow-up SLA
    Pipeline 5×, win rate 8%Poor qualification and weak differentiationTighten SQL criteria, battlecards, win-loss review
    Win rate 45%, few dealsInsufficient pipeline generationInvest in demand gen, outbound, partners
    NRR 85% with fast logo growthLeaky bucketCS-led onboarding, health scores, expansion plays

    Risk register

    • Forecast misses from optimistic stage hygiene.
    • Sales and marketing conflict over sourcing credit.
    • Over-hiring ahead of validated demand.
    • Dependence on one channel, partner or large customer.
    • Competitor discounting eroding price.
    • Poor data quality undermining decisions.

    90-day CCO roadmap

    WindowFocus
    Days 1–30Listen: customers, reps, funnel data, win-loss; audit definitions, tools and plan assumptions
    Days 31–60Align: shared funnel, SLAs, ICP, forecast process, capacity model
    Days 61–90Execute: reallocate budget, fix top conversion leak, launch weekly cadence, report first board view
    ⚠️
    Common pitfalls: changing everything at once, hiring before the model supports it, discounting to hit quarter, ignoring retention.

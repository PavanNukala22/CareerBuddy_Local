    Module 1

    Foundations

    What expansion is, its main types, and why it matters.

    What is expansion?

    Expansion is growing revenue from existing customers. It is usually cheaper and faster than new-logo acquisition because trust, data and a relationship already exist. Strong expansion is what pushes Net Revenue Retention above 100%.

    Types of expansion

    TypeDefinitionExample
    UpsellMove to a higher tier or more capacity of the same productStandard → Premium plan; 50 → 100 seats
    Cross-sellSell a different product or moduleAdd analytics module to a CRM
    Add-on / usage-basedPay more as consumption growsMore API calls, storage or messages
    Renewal upliftPrice or term improvement at renewalMulti-year deal with inflation uplift
    Referral / new-divisionAnother team or subsidiary adoptsSecond business unit signs

    Key terms

    • ARR / MRR: annual / monthly recurring revenue.
    • Expansion ARR: new recurring revenue from existing customers.
    • Whitespace: unsold potential in an account (users, teams, products).
    • Qualified expansion lead: a documented need, sponsor and budget path.
    • Renewal: contract continuation; first protect it, then grow it.

    Who sells expansion?

    Models differ: the CSM may own small expansions, an Account Manager owns quota-carrying growth, or CSMs source and sales closes. Whichever model, trust and value delivered are the prerequisites.

    Identifying Opportunities

    Finding and qualifying cross-sell, upsell and renewal opportunities.

    Where signals come from

    SourceSignalLikely opportunity

    UsageHeavy use of a workaroundCross-sell module that solves it
    Conversations"Can your tool also…?"Cross-sell
    Org changeNew division, acquisition, hiring surgeSeats, new BU
    Success outcomesGoal exceededExpand scope, executive sponsorship
    ContractRenewal in 6-9 monthsMulti-year renewal + uplift

    Whitespace analysis

    Map the account: people (departments not yet using), products (modules not bought) and geographies. Compare against your best accounts: "customers like this typically use 3 modules; this one uses 1".

    Qualifying an expansion

    • Need: is there a business problem the add-on solves?
    • Value: can you quantify the benefit?
    • Sponsor: who owns it?
    • Budget and timing: is there a funding path and a trigger event?
    • Readiness: is the current product healthy? Never sell on top of a failing deployment.
    ⚠️
    Selling while the customer has open critical issues damages trust. Fix, then grow.

    Module 3

    Selling & Renewal Execution

    Having the conversation, building the business case and running renewals.

    Consultative expansion conversation

    1. Recap results achieved.
    2. Ask about new goals and obstacles.
    3. Link the proposed addition to that goal.
    4. Quantify impact.
    5. Agree next steps and decision timeline.

    Sell the outcome, not the SKU.

    Business case

    A simple business case: ROI = (Annual benefit − Annual cost) ÷ Annual cost. Use the customer's own numbers, label assumptions and show payback period.

    🌍
    Real-world example — HR platform

    Customer paid $60k/year; adding a recruiting module at $20k would cut time-to-hire by 6 days, worth about $55k annually. ROI = (55−20)/20 = 175%, payback about 4.4 months.

    Running the renewal

    Start 120-180 days out: health review, value summary, stakeholder alignment, then commercial proposal. Offer options (1-year, 3-year with benefit), identify blockers, and involve procurement early. Keep the value story ahead of the price discussion.

    Practice

    🛠️
    Hands-on exercise — ROI calculation

    Add-on costs $30k/year and is expected to save 1,200 hours at $45/hour. Compute ROI and payback.

    Show model answer

    Benefit = 1,200 × 45 = $54,000. ROI = (54−30)/30 = 80%. Payback = 30/54 × 12 ≈ 6.7 months.

    Module 4

    Expert Expansion

    Account planning, pricing strategy, handling procurement and scaling expansion.

    Strategic account planning

    Plan growth by account: business objectives, stakeholder map, whitespace, competitive landscape and a sequenced roadmap of land → expand → extend. Align internal resources (sales, product, services) and agree success metrics with the customer.

    Pricing and packaging

    Understand your pricing metrics (seat, usage, outcome-based). Align price to value so growth in customer value produces growth in revenue. Use packaging to guide natural upgrades. Avoid discounts without trade-offs (term, volume, reference).

    Procurement and negotiation

    Expect procurement to focus on price and risk. Prepare a value summary for your sponsor to use internally, keep optional concessions in reserve, trade rather than give (term for price), and escalate through the executive sponsor, not around them.

    Scaling expansion

    • Define expansion plays tied to signals.
    • Use propensity models to prioritise accounts.
    • Measure expansion rate, win rate, cycle time and NRR by segment.
    • Align incentives so CSMs are not punished for protecting customer health.

    Practice

    🛠️
    Hands-on exercise — Negotiate a renewal

    Procurement demands a 15% discount on a $200k renewal. Your sponsor values the product. How do you respond?

    Show model answer

    Equip the sponsor with a value summary, offer a trade: a 3-year commitment for a smaller discount (for example 6-8%) or bundled services, and preserve the price/value link. Avoid unconditional discounts.

    Pricing & Packaging for Growth

    How price metrics and packaging create natural expansion paths.

    Pricing metrics

    ModelHow it chargesExpansion mechanismExample
    Per seatUsersAdd seats as team growsCollaboration tools
    Usage-basedConsumptionUsage grows with valueCloud, messaging APIs
    Tiered / featurePlan with features and limitsUpgrade at limit or for advanced featuresGood / Better / Best
    Platform + modulesCore plus add-onsCross-sell modulesCRM + analytics
    Outcome-basedShare of resultsValue-linked growthPayments, recovery

    Good-better-best packaging

    What good packaging does

    • Clear differentiators between tiers
    • Natural trigger to upgrade
    • Simple to explain
    • Aligns with customer value

    Warning signs

    • Too many SKUs
    • Key value hidden in a low tier
    • Frequent discount exceptions
    • Customers unclear what they paid for

    Value-based pricing and discounts

    • Anchor to the value created, not cost.
    • Give discounts only for trade-offs: term, volume, prepayment, case study.
    • Use pricing guardrails and approvals.
    • Review discount leakage: average discount by segment and by rep.
    💡
    Calc: 10% discount on $200k = $20k lost ARR; a 3-year term trade can protect more value than it costs.

    Real-world example

    🌍
    Real-world example — Data platform

    Customers on a flat plan hit storage limits and complained. Moving to base + usage tiers let customers grow naturally. Expansion ARR rose 35% in a year with no increase in discounts, because price tracked value.

    Module 6

    Renewal Management

    Running renewals as a repeatable motion from 180 days out to signature.

    Renewal timeline

    Start early; late starts invite discounts.

    Renewal readiness checklist

    ItemQuestion
    ValueDo we have documented outcomes?
    AdoptionAre key personas using key features?
    SponsorIs the executive sponsor engaged and aligned?
    RisksOpen blockers or competitor activity?
    CommercialsPricing, term, uplift and approval path?
    ProcurementContacts, process and lead time?

    Handling price increases

    1. Share value delivered before price.
    2. Notify early with a clear rationale (new features, support, inflation).
    3. Offer options: multi-year lock, tier change.
    4. Give executives a short summary they can use internally.
    5. Escalate to the sponsor, not around them.
    ⚠️
    Do not send the renewal quote as the first renewal conversation.

    Auto-renew, multi-year and co-terming

    Auto-renew

    • Reduces effort and risk
    • Requires notice periods and consumer-friendly practice
    • Still needs a value conversation

    Multi-year and co-term

    • Protect against churn and price rises
    • Trade discount for term
    • Co-terming aligns multiple contracts to one date

    Real-world example

    🌍
    Real-world example — Security software

    A rep sent a 12% uplift quote 20 days before renewal. The customer pushed back and procurement asked for an RFP. The next year the team began at T-150, brought a value summary, and offered a 3-year lock at 5% uplift. Result: signed 45 days early.

    Practice

    🛠️
    Hands-on exercise — Plan a renewal

    A $300k account renews in 150 days. Adoption is 55%; the sponsor is supportive; a competitor has presented to the IT director. Build the 90-day plan.

    Show model answer

    Now: health review and adoption push on the missing 45%. Day 30: executive value summary and IT director meeting to address competitor. Day 60: proposal with 1-year and 3-year options. Day 90: negotiation with the sponsor as advocate; paperwork opened early.

    Play library

    TriggerPlayOutcome
    Seat utilisation > 90%True-up conversationSeat upsell
    Goal exceededSuccess story + roadmap sessionScope expansion
    New business unit announcedExecutive intro + whitespace mapNew BU deal
    Workaround usageDemo the module that removes itCross-sell

    Expansion forecasting

    • Stage the pipeline: identified, qualified, proposal, negotiation, closed.
    • Track expansion rate, win rate, cycle time, average deal size.
    • Review forecast accuracy by segment.
    • Calc: weighted pipeline = Σ(amount × probability).
    💡
    Example: $100k at 60% + $50k at 30% = $75k weighted.

    Case study A: From usage to cross-sell

    🌍
    Real-world example — HR platform

    Analytics showed customers exporting data to spreadsheets weekly. The CSM team ran a play: show the native reporting module, quantify hours saved, align with the VP People. 28% of targeted accounts bought; $1.2M expansion ARR.

    Case study B: Expansion that backfired

    🌍
    Real-world example — Collaboration tool

    A rep pushed a premium upgrade while two P1 bugs were open. The customer paused the renewal, and escalated to the CEO. The company introduced a "health gate": no expansion pitch with open critical issues or Red health.

    Common mistakes to avoid

    • Selling features instead of outcomes
    • Pitching expansion while the account is unhealthy
    • Going around the sponsor
    • Discounting without trade-offs
    • Ignoring procurement lead times
    • Mixing support and sales roles without clear rules

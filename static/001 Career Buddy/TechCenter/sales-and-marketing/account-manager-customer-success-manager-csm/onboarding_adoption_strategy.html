    Module 1

    Foundations

    What onboarding and adoption are, and why the first 90 days decide the account.

    What is onboarding?

    Onboarding is the structured process that takes a newly signed customer from contract to first meaningful use of your product. Adoption is what happens after: users repeatedly apply the product to their daily work. Implementation is the technical part of onboarding (configuration, integrations, data migration).

    💡
    Customers do not renew because they bought software. They renew because the software changed an outcome they care about.

    Key terms

    • Time-to-Value (TTV): days from contract signature to the first measurable outcome the customer wanted.
    • Time-to-First-Value (TTFV): the first small win, such as a first report or first automation.
    • Kickoff call: the meeting that aligns goals, owners, scope and timeline.
    • Success plan: a shared document of goals, milestones and owners.
    • Go-live: the date the product is used in production.
    • Champion: the internal advocate who drives adoption.

    Who does what?

    Sales owns the promise, Implementation/Professional Services owns configuration, the CSM owns outcomes and adoption, and Support owns issues. A clean sales-to-CS handoff (goals, stakeholders, success criteria, risks promised during the deal) is the first deliverable of any onboarding.

    Types of onboarding

    The right model depends on contract value and product complexity.

    Module 2

    Enablement & Implementation

    Designing time-to-value, enabling users, and running implementations.

    Measuring and shrinking TTV

    Define the first value moment per segment (for example "first dashboard shared with a manager"). Then work backwards: every step between signature and that moment is a candidate for removal, automation or parallelisation.

    • Pre-collect data and access during the sales cycle.
    • Use templates and pre-built integrations.
    • Run configuration and training in parallel, not in sequence.
    • Track TTV by segment, not as one average.

    Customer enablement

    Enablement turns purchasers into proficient users. Use role-based paths (admin, power user, executive viewer) and mix formats:

    Live training
    Best for admins and complex workflows.
    Self-paced academy
    Scales to many users, supports refreshers.
    In-app guidance
    Walkthroughs and tooltips at the moment of need.
    Office hours & community
    Peer learning and cheap ongoing support.

    Measure enablement by behaviour change (feature use after training), not attendance.

    Implementation phases

    1. Discovery: goals, requirements, stakeholders, data sources.
    2. Design: configuration plan, integration map, success criteria.
    3. Build/Configure: setup, integrations, migration.
    4. Test/UAT: customer validates against agreed criteria.
    5. Launch/Go-live: training, comms, hypercare.
    6. Handover to adoption: shift from project mode to success mode.
    ⚠️
    Scope creep is the number one cause of delayed onboarding. Use a change-request process and tie extras to dates or cost.

    Real-world example

    🌍
    Real-world example — Analytics SaaS

    A BI vendor found customers who built their first dashboard within 14 days renewed at 94% versus 71% for the rest. The CSM team moved dashboard-building into the kickoff week, shipped five industry templates, and cut median TTV from 38 to 17 days. Gross retention at 12 months rose 6 points.

    Module 3

    Adoption Strategy

    Moving from login to habit: adoption depth, breadth and stickiness.

    Dimensions of adoption

    • Breadth: share of licensed users who are active.
    • Depth: share of key features used.
    • Frequency: how often users return (DAU/MAU ratio is a stickiness proxy).
    • Quality: whether usage maps to the outcomes that were sold.
    💡
    Seat activation without outcome-linked usage is "shelfware in disguise".

    Adoption playbooks

    A playbook is a repeatable response to a trigger. Examples: no login in 7 days after go-live → outreach to champion; admin never created a workflow → offer a 30-minute build session; usage dropped 30% month-on-month → health check call.

    Change management

    Adoption is a people problem. Apply the basics: executive sponsorship, a visible champion network, clear "what changes for me" messaging, early wins shared internally, and removing the old tool so there is no fallback.

    Practice

    🛠️
    Hands-on exercise — Diagnose low adoption

    A customer bought 300 seats; 90 are active, mostly admins. Name three hypotheses and the data that would test each.

    Show model answer

    (1) Enablement gap — check training attendance by role. (2) Workflow not embedded — compare usage to the old tool's retirement date. (3) Wrong persona purchased — look at which job titles hold licences versus who the product helps. Then pick one intervention per hypothesis.

    Module 4

    Expert Practice

    Segmented onboarding at scale, risk-based escalation and ROI proof.

    Segmented, data-driven onboarding

    Treat onboarding as a product. Segment by ARR, complexity and use case, assign an onboarding model per segment, and instrument each stage with stage-conversion and stage-duration metrics. Find the stage where cohorts stall and fix that stage first.

    Risk-based escalation

    Add a human to a tech-touch journey when signals appear: missed milestone, stakeholder turnover, a first support ticket tagged "blocker", or an integration failing twice. Model this as a hybrid motion, so capacity is spent where risk is highest.

    Proving ROI early

    Capture a baseline before go-live, agree the metric and formula in the success plan, and report the delta at day 60-90. A simple model: Value = (hours saved × loaded hourly cost) + (error cost avoided) − subscription cost.

    🌍
    Real-world example — Customer support platform

    Baseline average handling time was 11 minutes. After 60 days it was 8. At 40 agents, 160 hours/month, the CSM built a one-page ROI view that the champion used to win a seat expansion.

    Practice

    🛠️
    Hands-on exercise — Redesign onboarding for scale

    You have 800 SMB accounts and 4 CSMs. Propose a hybrid onboarding model.

    Show model answer

    Tech-touch default (welcome sequence, in-app checklist, webinar cohort). Define triggers (checklist <50% by day 14, no data connected by day 7) that route accounts to a pooled CSM queue. Reserve live calls for top 15% by ARR. Review stage metrics monthly.

    Module 5

    Enablement & Training Design

    Designing role-based learning paths, certification and measuring training impact.

    The enablement lifecycle

    Enablement is a loop, not a one-off webinar.

    Start by listing the personas who must change behaviour (admin, power user, manager, executive viewer) and the three to five behaviours each must perform to reach the first value moment. Training that is not tied to a behaviour is content, not enablement.

    Role-based learning paths

    PersonaGoalFormatDurationSuccess signal
    Admin / ownerConfigure and govern the productLive workshop + sandbox lab2 × 90 minWorkflows created, permissions set
    Power userBuild reports, automationsSelf-paced academy + office hours4 hoursWeekly use of advanced features
    End userDo daily tasks fasterIn-app guides + 10-min videos30 minTask completion, low ticket rate
    ManagerUse outputs to manage the teamDashboard walkthrough45 minWeekly dashboard views
    ExecutiveSee outcomes and ROIOne-page summary in QBR15 minSponsor engagement
    💡
    Match format to persona. Admins need hands-on practice; executives need a result they can quote.

    Choosing delivery formats

    Live (instructor-led)

    • Best for complex configuration
    • Immediate Q&A, builds relationships
    • Expensive to scale, hard to schedule

    Self-paced / in-app

    • Scales to thousands of users
    • Available at moment of need
    • Needs good design; completion can be low

    Blend: live for admins in the first two weeks, self-paced for everyone else, in-app tips for reinforcement, and office hours for questions.

    Measuring training impact (Kirkpatrick levels)

    LevelQuestionExample metric
    1 ReactionDid they like it?Session CSAT
    2 LearningDid they learn?Quiz or certification pass rate
    3 BehaviourDo they act differently?Feature use 30 days after training
    4 ResultsDid the business improve?Time-to-value, ticket deflection, renewal

    Most teams stop at levels 1-2. A CSM earns trust by showing level 3 and 4.

    Real-world example

    🌍
    Real-world example — Marketing automation vendor

    Completion of a 6-hour admin course was 22%. The team split it into six 12-minute lessons, added a sandbox lab and sent a day-3 nudge when the first campaign was not built. Completion rose to 61% and the share of accounts sending a campaign in 14 days went from 38% to 64%.

    Practice

    🛠️
    Hands-on exercise — Design a learning path

    A customer is rolling out a ticketing tool to 120 agents, 8 team leads and 2 admins. Design the enablement plan for week 1-4.

    Show model answer

    Admins: two live workshops in week 1 plus sandbox. Leads: 45-minute dashboard session in week 2. Agents: three 10-minute videos + in-app checklist in weeks 2-3, with a champion in each shift for questions. Week 4: measure first-contact resolution and ticket tagging accuracy (levels 3-4), then run a refresher on the weakest behaviour.

    Module 6

    Implementation Management

    Running implementations with plans, RACI, risk control and a go-live checklist.

    The implementation lifecycle

    Gate each stage with an agreed exit criterion.

    RACI: who is accountable?

    TaskCustomer sponsorCustomer adminImplementation leadCSMSupport
    Define success criteriaACCRI
    Provide data & accessARCII
    Configure productICA/RII
    UAT sign-offARCCI
    Training deliveryICRAI
    Post go-live issuesICCAR

    R = Responsible, A = Accountable, C = Consulted, I = Informed. Exactly one A per task.

    Common risks and mitigations

    RiskEarly signalMitigation
    Scope creepWeekly new "small" requestsChange-request process with impact on date and cost
    Late data / accessBlocked tasks waiting on customerCollect during sales cycle; escalate to sponsor at day 5
    Integration surprisesUndocumented legacy systemsTechnical discovery call; sandbox test early
    Champion turnoverCancelled project meetingsSecondary champion + executive sponsor check-in
    Low UAT engagementUAT testers not assignedName testers in the plan; block calendars
    Unclear success criteriaDifferent definitions of "done"Written, measurable criteria signed in kickoff

    Go-live checklist

    • Success criteria met and signed in UAT
    • Users trained and access provisioned
    • Integrations monitored with alerts
    • Rollback or fallback plan agreed
    • Support routing and escalation contacts shared
    • Hypercare schedule (daily for week 1, then weekly)
    • Communication sent to all end users
    • Baseline metrics captured for ROI
    ⚠️
    Never go live on a Friday afternoon without hypercare coverage.

    Real-world example

    🌍
    Real-world example — Payments platform, 9-month enterprise rollout

    Original plan: 6 months. The implementation lead found at week 3 that the customer's ERP export was undocumented. A two-day technical workshop and a sandbox integration test saved an estimated six weeks. Go-live slipped one month but UAT passed first time, and the customer renewed for three years.

    Practice

    🛠️
    Hands-on exercise — Build a risk register

    Your customer wants go-live in 8 weeks across 3 regions with an SSO integration they have not specified. List the top 3 risks, signals and mitigations.

    Show model answer

    (1) SSO unspecified: signal — no IdP admin named; mitigation — technical call in week 1, test in sandbox by week 3. (2) Regional rollout: signal — no local champions; mitigation — phased launch, pilot region first. (3) Compressed timeline: signal — UAT not scheduled; mitigation — fixed UAT window in plan and sponsor sign-off on scope trade-offs.

    Case study A: Cutting TTV in SMB

    🌍
    Real-world example — Invoicing SaaS

    Problem: 52 days median TTV, 30% early churn. Analysis: 70% of the delay was waiting for customers to import customer lists. Fix: CSV import wizard, 5-minute starter template, day-2 nudge email. Result: TTV 19 days, early churn 17%, with no extra headcount.

    Case study B: Enterprise stall

    🌍
    Real-world example — HR platform

    Problem: a 4,000-employee customer stalled after sign-off; adoption at 11% at day 90. Analysis: only HR admins had been trained; managers (key users) were never enabled. Fix: manager workshops, weekly sponsor update and a champion network of 25 managers. Result: 63% adoption at day 150; expansion to a second region.

    Common mistakes to avoid

    • Treating onboarding as a project that ends at go-live instead of a path to outcomes
    • Training admins only
    • No agreed definition of first value
    • Over-customising instead of using templates
    • Letting sales promises bypass the success plan
    • Reporting activity (meetings held) instead of results

    🛠️
    Hands-on exercise — Case review

    You inherit an account with 90 days elapsed, 14% activation and an unhappy sponsor. What are your first three moves?

    Show model answer

    (1) Meet the sponsor: listen, restate goals, agree on a 30-day recovery plan. (2) Diagnose the blocker with data: which onboarding stage stalled and which personas are missing. (3) Run a targeted enablement sprint and an early win for the sponsor, then report weekly progress.

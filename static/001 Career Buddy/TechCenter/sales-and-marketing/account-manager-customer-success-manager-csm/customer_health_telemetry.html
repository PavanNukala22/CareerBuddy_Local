    Module 1

    Foundations

    What telemetry and health scores are, and the tools used to work with them.

    What is customer health telemetry?

    Telemetry is the automatic collection of data about how customers use your product and interact with your company. A customer health score combines those signals into a simple indicator (for example Green / Yellow / Red) of how likely the account is to renew and grow.

    Data sources

    Product usage
    Logins, feature events, active users, depth of use.
    Support
    Ticket volume, severity, resolution time, CSAT.
    Relationship
    Sponsor access, meeting cadence, NPS, champion status.
    Commercial
    Contract term, payment status, renewal date, ARR.
    Outcomes
    Customer-defined goals and progress.

    Types of tools

    CategoryExamplesWhat it does
    Customer Success platformsGainsight, Totango, ChurnZero, CatalystHealth scores, playbooks (CTAs), journey orchestration, renewals
    Product analyticsMixpanel, Amplitude, Heap, PendoEvent tracking, funnels, cohorts, retention curves
    CRMSalesforce, HubSpotAccounts, contacts, opportunities, contracts
    Data warehouse / BISnowflake, BigQuery, Looker, TableauCentral storage and analysis
    Support toolsZendesk, IntercomTickets and conversations

    Key vocabulary

    • Event: a tracked user action, e.g. "report_created".
    • Property: detail attached to an event or user.
    • Cohort: a group of users or accounts sharing a trait or start date.
    • Funnel: ordered steps toward an outcome.
    • DAU / WAU / MAU: daily / weekly / monthly active users.
    • CTA (Gainsight): Call to Action, a task triggered by a signal.

    Module 2

    Health Scores & CS Platforms

    Designing health scores and working in Gainsight, Totango and similar platforms.

    Building a health score

    1. Choose dimensions (usage, support, relationship, commercial, outcomes).
    2. Pick 1-3 measures per dimension.
    3. Define thresholds (Green/Yellow/Red).
    4. Assign weights (higher for predictors of renewal).
    5. Validate against historical renewals and churn.
    6. Review quarterly.
    DimensionMeasureWeightGreenRed
    UsageWeekly active users ÷ licensed30%> 60%< 30%
    OutcomesGoal progress25%On trackOff track
    SupportOpen P1 tickets15%0≥ 2
    RelationshipExecutive sponsor engaged20%YesNo
    CommercialPayment status10%Current> 60 days late

    How the tools differ

    • Gainsight: Scorecards, Rules Engine, CTAs and Playbooks, Journey Orchestrator, Success Plans; strong for enterprise CS operations.
    • Totango: SuccessBLOCs, segmentation, health scoring and modular templates; often quicker to deploy.
    • Mixpanel / Amplitude: behavioural analytics: events, funnels, retention, cohorts; feed insights or data into the CS platform.

    Data flow

    Typical architecture: product events → analytics tool or warehouse → CS platform (via connector or reverse ETL) → CSM tasks and dashboards. Keep an account ID that matches across systems; mismatched IDs are the commonest failure point.

    ⚠️
    Garbage in, garbage out. Audit data quality (completeness, freshness, mapping) before trusting a score.

    Module 3

    Product Analytics

    Using Mixpanel and Amplitude for funnels, cohorts and retention.

    Event design

    Name events as object_action (for example report_shared), keep a tracking plan, attach properties (account_id, plan, role), and track only what answers a question. Poor naming and duplicate events make analysis unreliable.

    Core analyses

    AnalysisQuestion answeredExample
    FunnelWhere do users drop off?Signup → connect data → first report
    RetentionDo users come back?Week-4 retention of new users
    CohortDo newer customers behave differently?Q1 vs Q3 onboarding cohorts
    SegmentationWhich groups use what?Admins vs viewers
    Feature adoptionWhich features drive stickiness?Users who use alerts retain 2x

    Stickiness and activation

    Stickiness = DAU ÷ MAU. An activation event is the action that best predicts long-term retention (the "aha moment"). Find it by comparing retained and churned cohorts and test it with experiments.

    🌍
    Real-world example — Collaboration tool

    Analysis showed teams that invited 3+ colleagues in the first week retained 80% at 90 days versus 35%. The CS team made "invite three teammates" the first onboarding milestone and 90-day retention for new accounts rose 12 points.

    🛠️
    Hands-on exercise — Read a funnel

    Funnel: Signup 1,000 → Data connected 600 → First report 240 → Shared report 150. Where is the biggest drop and what do you do?

    Show model answer

    Module 4

    Expert Telemetry

    Predictive health, governance of data and closing the loop.

    Data-driven weights and models

    Rather than guessing weights, fit them with regression or gradient-boosted models on historical outcomes. Evaluate with precision/recall and lift. Keep the model explainable: CSMs must understand why an account is Red to act on it. Re-train as the product and customer mix change.

    Governance and quality

    • Assign data owners and a tracking-plan review process.
    • Monitor data freshness and anomaly alerts.
    • Document score definitions and changes (versioning).
    • Respect privacy: minimise personal data, honour consent and regional rules (such as GDPR).

    Closing the loop

    Telemetry only matters if it changes behaviour. Connect scores to CTAs and playbooks, measure play outcomes, feed product insights back to the roadmap, and share customer-facing health views in QBRs. Review score accuracy against renewals each quarter.

    Common pitfalls

    • Vanity metrics (logins) instead of outcome metrics.
    • Too many measures, so no one knows what drives the score.
    • One score for all segments; define segment-specific scores.
    • No override rules for critical events.
    • Alert fatigue from poorly tuned CTAs.

    Practice

    🛠️
    Hands-on exercise — Audit a health program

    Scores are Green for 80% of accounts, yet churn is 18%. List three likely causes.

    Show model answer

    (1) Thresholds too lenient or weights not validated against outcomes. (2) Missing leading signals such as sentiment or sponsor changes. (3) Data quality or mapping gaps hide risks. Fix by back-testing against churned accounts, adding override rules and auditing data.

    From signal to action

    Typical flow in a CS platform.

    Setting up a CTA playbook

    1. Define the trigger (for example Usage score Red for 2 weeks).
    2. Choose CTA type: Risk, Opportunity, Lifecycle, Objective.
    3. Set priority, owner and due date logic.
    4. Add tasks: review data, call champion, update success plan.
    5. Add email templates and links to resources.
    6. Close with a reason code and outcome to learn.

    Real-world example

    🌍
    Real-world example — Project management SaaS

    A Gainsight rule created a Risk CTA when weekly active users dropped 30% for two weeks. Playbook: call the champion, review feature use, offer a workshop. CTA closure with outcome was required. In 6 months, 41% of Risk CTAs were closed as "saved", and total at-risk ARR fell 22%.

    From analytics to the CS platform

    Rolled-up account metrics feed the health score.
    -- Example: weekly active users per account (warehouse SQL)
SELECT account_id,
       DATE_TRUNC('week', event_time) AS week,
       COUNT(DISTINCT user_id)        AS weekly_active_users
FROM   product_events
WHERE  event_time >= CURRENT_DATE - 90
GROUP  BY 1, 2;

    Data quality and privacy

    • Document the tracking plan and review changes.
    • Validate with test accounts; monitor event volume anomalies.
    • Avoid collecting personal data you do not need; follow GDPR/CCPA consent and retention rules.
    • Version metric definitions (what counts as "active").

    Module 7

    Roadmap, Cases & Governance

    Implementing a health program and learning from real programs.

    Health program roadmap

    Treat the health score like a product.

    Back-testing a score

    Score bandAccountsRenewedChurnedRenewal rate
    Green6005524892%
    Yellow2501906076%
    Red150609040%

    Good scores separate outcomes clearly (92% vs 40%). If Green and Red renew at similar rates, the score is not predictive.

    Governance of the program

    • Owner: CS Operations, with a steering group (CS, product, data).
    • Change log and versioning of score definitions.
    • Quarterly accuracy review and re-weighting.
    • Training for CSMs on how to read and act on scores.
    • Clear data privacy and access controls.

    Case study A: Over-engineered score

    🌍
    Real-world example — B2B software

    A score with 38 measures was untrusted by CSMs. The team cut it to 8, added override rules and explained the top drivers on each account page. CSM usage of the score went from 35% to 90%, and predictive power rose.

    Case study B: Segment-specific scoring

    🌍
    Real-world example — Fintech platform

    One score for SMB and enterprise misled both: SMB accounts were Red for low logins even though they used the API heavily. Segment-specific scores (UI usage for SMB, API volume for enterprise) cut false positives by 45%.

    Common mistakes to avoid

    • Weights chosen by opinion and never validated
    • Too many measures
    • No override rules for critical events
    • Ignoring data freshness and ID mapping
    • Scores without playbooks
    • Never retiring rules or alerts

    Practice

    🛠️
    Hands-on exercise — Evaluate a score

    Back-test shows Green renewal 84%, Yellow 80%, Red 78%. What do you conclude and do?

    Show model answer

    The score barely separates outcomes, so it is not predictive. Investigate measures and weights, add leading signals (sentiment, sponsor change), use segment-specific scoring, and re-test on a hold-out period.

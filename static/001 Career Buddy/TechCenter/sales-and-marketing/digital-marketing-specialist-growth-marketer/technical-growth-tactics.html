    Growth Fundamentals

    Growth loops, funnels, and key metrics.

    🧭 What is what

    Fresher
    • Growth marketing: rapid experiments across acquisition, activation, retention, referral and revenue (AARRR). Viral loop: users bring in new users. K-factor = invites per user × invite conversion rate. PLG (product-led growth): the product drives acquisition and expansion.
    Loop typeHow it worksExample
    ReferralReward for invitingDropbox storage
    CollaborationUsing the product requires othersSlack, Figma
    Content/UGCUsers create public contentPinterest, YouTube

    📊 Key metrics

    Fresher
    • Activation rate, time-to-value (TTV), viral coefficient, viral cycle time, referral rate, retention curves, North Star metric.
    💡
    Example: 5 invites × 20% conversion = K 1.0. Above 1 grows on its own; most products land at 0.1–0.5 and use virality as a booster.

    Referral Programs

    Designing incentives, triggers, and fraud controls.

    📊 Program design

    FresherIntermediate
    • Pick the trigger moment (after a win or "aha"), the reward (one-sided or two-sided; cash, credit, upgrade), and a frictionless share flow (link, code, prefilled message).
    • Rewards should pay on activation or purchase, not just signup.

    🛠️ Fraud and economics

    FresherIntermediate
    • Limit self-referrals, require verified email/payment, cap rewards, monitor spikes by IP/device.
    • Check unit economics: reward cost must sit below CAC from other channels.

    🎯 Case study and exercise

    FresherIntermediate
    • Case: A fintech app gave $20 to both sides after the friend’s first transfer; referrals reached 28% of new users at 40% lower CAC.
    • Exercise: design a referral scheme for a budgeting app: trigger, reward, guardrails, KPIs.

    Viral Loops & Product-Led Growth

    Building shareable mechanics into the product.

    🛠️ Viral mechanics

    Intermediate
    • Invite flows, shareable outputs (reports, boards, links), public profiles, waitlists with position sharing, templates and marketplaces.
    • Reduce viral cycle time: shorter loops compound faster than higher K alone.

    Onboarding & Activation Funnels

    From signup to first value.

    🎯 Onboarding principles

    Intermediate
    • Find the aha moment by comparing retained and churned cohorts (e.g., "3 teammates invited in 7 days").
    • Shorten time-to-value: fewer steps, templates, sample data, progress checklists, empty-state guidance.

    Rapid Experimentation Engine

    Running growth sprints and testing in the product.

    📈 Growth process

    IntermediateAdvanced
    • Backlog → ICE score → hypothesis → build smallest test → run → learn → scale or kill. Weekly cadence with a shared experiment log.
    • Tools: feature flags, event analytics (Amplitude, Mixpanel), A/B platforms, warehouse + SQL.

    🧪 Experiment design in product

    IntermediateAdvanced
    • Randomize by user or account; define activation as the primary metric; watch retention and revenue as guardrails.
    • Use holdouts to measure the long-term effect of onboarding or referral changes.
    ⚠️
    A growth hack that lifts signups but lowers retention is not growth.

    Advanced Growth Strategy

    Loops at scale, channels, and sustainability.

    🧪 Systems thinking

    Advanced
    • Model loops as inputs → actions → outputs; find the bottleneck stage; combine paid, content, referral and product loops.
    • Network effects: direct, indirect, data; cold-start strategies via single-player value and seeding a niche.

    🧭 Risks and ethics

    Advanced
    • Avoid dark patterns, spam invites and privacy violations; comply with platform policies and consent laws.
    • Track quality metrics (retention, NPS) beside growth rate.

    📊 Capstone

    Advanced
    • Design a 90-day growth plan for a B2B collaboration app: loop, onboarding fixes, 6 experiments with ICE scores, KPI targets and guardrails.

    Outbound Prospecting & Lead Generation

    Identifying target accounts and reaching them through multichannel outreach — cold calling, email sequences, and social selling via LinkedIn Sales Navigator.

    📞 Overview & Channels

    An SDR's core mandate is to build a healthy pipeline from scratch. That starts with identifying target accounts that fit the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP), then reaching them through multichannel outreach — cold calling, structured email sequences, and social selling via LinkedIn Sales Navigator. No single channel works alone anymore; the skill is weaving them into one coherent cadence.

    🎯
    Account Targeting
    Using ICP filters (industry, size, tech stack, hiring signals) to build target lists instead of spraying contacts randomly.
    ☎️
    Cold Calling
    Opening with a pattern-interrupt, stating purpose fast, and earning 30 seconds before pitching anything.
    ✉️
    Email Sequences
    3–7 touch cadences that mix value, social proof, and a clear, low-friction call to action.
    💼
    Social Selling
    Using LinkedIn Sales Navigator to find decision-makers, track job changes, and warm up cold outreach.
    Figure · Call → email → LinkedIn, sequenced around one target account
    💡
    Sequence, don't scatter. A prospect who gets one touch is easy to ignore; one who gets a coordinated call → email → LinkedIn message within the same week is far more likely to respond. Cadence beats volume.

    🧑‍💼 SDR, BDR & AE — Who Does What

    Sales teams split the work so each person can specialise. Job titles vary by company, but the pattern is consistent: SDRs and BDRs create qualified conversations; Account Executives (AEs) turn them into revenue.

    RoleTypical FocusHands Off / Owns
    SDR (Sales Development Rep)Qualifies leads — often inbound leads such as demo requests — and runs some outboundHands a qualified meeting to an AE
    BDR (Business Development Rep)Outbound prospecting into target accounts that haven't raised their handHands a qualified meeting to an AE
    AE (Account Executive)Runs demos, builds the business case, negotiates and closesOwns the deal and the revenue number
    📌
    Titles overlap. Many companies use SDR and BDR interchangeably. What matters is the mandate: book qualified meetings, not close deals.

    🔀 Inbound vs Outbound

    Leads arrive in two basic ways. Knowing the difference explains why outbound needs more research and more persistence.

    🟢 Inbound

    The prospect raised their hand first.

    • Demo request, form fill, content download
    • Warmer — they already know they have a problem
    • Volume depends on marketing

    🔵 Outbound

    The rep starts the conversation.

    • Cold call, cold email, LinkedIn message
    • You choose exactly who to target
    • Colder — you must earn attention first

    🧰 The SDR Tech Stack

    Four kinds of tools show up in almost every outbound team. Each has one clear job.

    🗄️
    CRM
    Salesforce or HubSpot. The system of record for accounts, contacts and every logged activity.
    🔁
    Sales Engagement
    Outreach or Salesloft. Runs multi-step cadences across calls, emails and tasks.
    💼
    Sales Navigator
    LinkedIn's prospecting tool: lead and account filters, saved searches and job-change alerts.
    📇
    Data Providers
    Tools like ZoomInfo or Apollo that supply company data, emails and phone numbers.
    💡
    Log everything in the CRM. If a call or email isn't recorded, your team can't see it, can't report on it, and may contact the same prospect twice.

    📈 Funnel & Core Metrics

    SDR performance is tracked with two kinds of numbers. Activity metrics measure effort; outcome metrics measure results.

    MetricTypeWhat It Tells You
    Calls dialed, emails sent, LinkedIn touchesActivityHow much outreach you did
    Connect rate, reply rateActivity → outcomeWhether the outreach is landing
    Meetings bookedOutcomeConversations that reached a calendar
    Qualified opportunities createdOutcomeMeetings an AE accepts as real deals
    Pipeline generatedOutcomeTotal potential deal value you created
    📌
    SDRs are usually measured on meetings booked and qualified opportunities — not closed revenue. Activity is a means to that end, never the goal on its own.

    ICP & Pain Point Breakdown

    Mapping who you're targeting and why they'd care — before writing a single message.

    🧭 ICP & Pain Point Breakdown

    Before writing a single message, an SDR maps the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) and the pain points that profile actually feels — generic outreach fails precisely because it skips this step.

    Pain PointWhy It HurtsHow Outreach Should Address It
    Reply rates are collapsingHigh-volume, generic sequences get ignored or marked spam.Lead with one specific, researched trigger — not a generic opener.
    Tools don't talk to each otherReps lose track of who was touched, when, and on which channel.Plan the cadence as one connected sequence, not three separate efforts.
    New reps take months to rampInconsistent messaging slows pipeline generation.Reuse a proven cadence/template rather than freelancing every message.
    📌
    ICP first, message second. Nail down who you're targeting and why they'd care before drafting any email or call script.

    🏗️ Building an ICP

    An ICP describes the account that gets the most value from your product — and gives you the most value back. Build it from evidence, not guesses: start with your best existing customers (high retention, fast sales cycles, happy referrals) and look for what they share.

    DimensionExamplesQuestion It Answers
    FirmographicIndustry, employee count, revenue, regionIs this the right kind of company?
    TechnographicTools and platforms they already useDoes our product fit their stack?
    Behavioural / intentHiring, funding, product launches, content they readAre they likely to buy now?
    Pain & needSlow ramp time, low reply rates, tool sprawlDo they feel the problem we solve?
    💡
    ICP vs persona: the ICP describes the account (company); a persona describes the individual inside it (title, goals, worries). You need both.

    👥 Buyer Personas & the Buying Committee

    B2B purchases rarely depend on one person. Outbound works best when you know which role you are talking to and what that role cares about.

    💰
    Economic Buyer
    Holds or approves the budget. Cares about ROI and risk.
    📣
    Champion
    An internal advocate who wants you to win and helps you navigate the company.
    🧑‍💻
    End User
    Uses the product daily. Cares about ease of use and time saved.
    🚧
    Blocker / Gatekeeper
    Can slow or stop a deal — often IT, security, legal or procurement.

    ✅ Qualification Frameworks: BANT & MEDDIC

    A qualification framework is a checklist of questions that tells you whether a lead deserves more of your (and the AE's) time.

    FrameworkStands ForBest Suited To
    BANTBudget · Authority · Need · TimelineSimple, faster deals and early screening
    MEDDICMetrics · Economic buyer · Decision criteria · Decision process · Identify pain · ChampionComplex, multi-stakeholder enterprise deals
    📌
    SPIN is a questioning method rather than a checklist: ask about the Situation, then the Problem, then the Implication of that problem, then the Need-payoff of fixing it. It pairs well with either framework.

    🪜 Account Tiering & ABM

    You can't research every account equally deeply. Account-Based Marketing (ABM) style tiering decides where to spend your time.

    TierAccount ProfileOutreach Style
    Tier 1Best fit, highest potential valueDeep, hand-researched 1:1 outreach
    Tier 2Strong fit, mid valueSemi-personalised by segment or role (1:few)
    Tier 3Good fit, lower valueTemplated sequences with light personalisation (1:many)
    💡
    Match effort to value. Spending 30 minutes researching a low-value account is as wasteful as sending a generic template to your dream customer.

    Cold Calling Playbook

    Opening, qualifying and booking the next step in under a minute — and handling the objections that follow.

    ☎️ Cold Calling Basics

    The goal of a cold call is not to close. It is to earn enough interest to book the next step — usually a meeting with an AE. A reliable call has five beats:

    1. Open — your name and a quick pattern-interrupt
    2. Reason — why you, why them, why now
    3. Question — one relevant, open question
    4. Respond — listen and handle the reaction
    5. Close — propose a specific next step
    cold-call-script.txtYou:  Hi Priya, it's Sam from Acme — I know I'm calling out of the
      blue. Can I take 30 seconds and you can tell me if it's relevant?
Them: Sure, go ahead.
You:  I saw you're hiring three SDRs. Teams in that position often
      tell us reps lose time switching tools. Is that something
      you're seeing, or is it handled?
Them: It's a bit of a problem, actually.
You:  Makes sense. Worth 15 minutes Thursday to see how similar
      teams fixed it — 2 p.m. or 4 p.m.?
    💡
    Ask for a small, specific next step. "Thursday at 2 or 4?" is easier to say yes to than "Would you like to learn more?"

    ⚡ Opening & Pattern Interrupts

    Prospects decide in seconds whether you are a stranger worth hearing. A pattern interrupt breaks the script they expect from a salesperson.

    ❌ Predictable

    Triggers the "salesperson" reflex.

    Hi, how are you doing today?
I'm calling to tell you about our
industry-leading platform...

    ✅ Pattern interrupt

    Honest, short, specific.

    Hi Priya, it's Sam from Acme —
I know I'm calling out of the blue.
Can I take 30 seconds and you can
tell me if this is relevant?
    📌
    Earn the first 30 seconds. Don't pitch. State who you are and why you called, then hand the conversation back with a question.

    🛡️ Gatekeepers & Voicemail

    Many calls end with a receptionist, an assistant or a voicemail box. Treat both as part of the cadence, not as failures.

    🙋
    Gatekeepers
    Be polite, direct and honest. Give a brief real reason for calling and ask for help — never pretend to be a friend of the prospect.
    📨
    Voicemail
    Keep it to roughly 30 seconds: name, company, one-line reason, a clear next step, and your number said slowly (twice if you can).
    🔗
    Pair It
    Mention that you'll send a short email, then actually send it. The voicemail makes the email familiar.

    🧱 Objection Handling

    Objections are normal — often reflexes, not final answers. A simple pattern: Acknowledge → Ask → Advance. Agree the concern is fair, ask one curious question, then propose a small next step.

    Prospect SaysWhat It Often MeansBetter Response
    "Not interested."A reflex — they don't yet see relevanceAcknowledge it, then ask one short question tied to a specific trigger
    "Just send me some info."A polite brush-off — or genuine curiosityAgree to send it, ask what would be most useful, and lock in a follow-up time
    "We already use [competitor]."They have a solution and no urgencyDon't criticise it. Ask what's working and what isn't; position as a complement
    "I'm busy — call me later."Bad timing, not a noOffer a specific time instead of an open-ended "later"
    "We have no budget."No priority yet, or no clear valueAsk about the cost of the problem; agree when to revisit
    ⚠️
    Never argue. If you win the debate, you usually lose the meeting. Aim to keep the conversation going, not to prove the prospect wrong.

    🕒 Call Blocks & Persistence

    Most prospects won't answer on the first try. Consistency beats occasional bursts.

    • Call blocks: dedicate focused, uninterrupted stretches (for example 60–90 minutes) to dial in batches — it builds momentum and avoids context switching.
    • Vary the timing: try different times of day across attempts, then check your own connect-rate data.
    • Leave voicemails selectively, not on every attempt, so you don't sound repetitive.
    • Log every attempt in the CRM with a short note so the next call starts where this one stopped.

    Cold Email Sequence

    From writing a single clear email to running a 3-email cadence — plus the deliverability, compliance and measurement that keep it working.

    🧬 Anatomy of a Cold Email

    A good cold email is short enough to read on a phone in ten seconds. Every part has one job:

    PartJobRule of Thumb
    Subject lineGet it opened without tricking anyoneShort, specific, lowercase-casual, no clickbait
    OpenerProve it's not a mass blastOne researched trigger about them
    Value lineConnect their situation to a resultOne problem, one outcome — not a feature list
    CTA (call to action)Make replying easyOne low-friction ask, such as a 15-minute chat
    💡
    One email, one ask. Offering three different next steps makes the prospect do the work of choosing — and they usually choose none.

    ✉️ Cold Email Sequence

    A typical outbound sequence runs 3 emails over 1–2 weeks: an initial value-driven touch, a follow-up with proof, and a graceful breakup.

    email-1-initial.txtSubject: quick question about {{company}}'s outbound

Hi {{first_name}},

Noticed {{company}} posted 3 new SDR roles this quarter — usually a
sign pipeline targets just went up.

Most sales leaders in that spot tell us the bottleneck isn't
headcount, it's that reps spend more time switching tools than
talking to prospects.

Worth a 15-minute look at how teams like {{similar_company}} added
35% more booked meetings without hiring more reps?

{{your_name}}
    email-2-followup.txtSubject: the 35% number

Hi {{first_name}},

Following up on my note — thought this might be useful either way.

{{similar_company}}'s SDR team was sending ~80 emails/day per rep
with a 2% reply rate. After consolidating calls, email, and
LinkedIn into one connected cadence, replies went to 7% and
meetings booked rose 35% in 60 days — same headcount.

Happy to send the short case study, or just grab 15 minutes if
that's more useful.

{{your_name}}
    email-3-breakup.txtSubject: should I close the loop?

Hi {{first_name}},

I'll take the hint that now isn't the right time — I'll stop here.

If outbound efficiency becomes a priority again down the line,
happy to pick this back up. Wishing you a strong quarter either way.

{{your_name}}
    💡
    Each email has one job. Email 1 earns attention with a trigger, Email 2 earns trust with proof, Email 3 earns a reply through graceful exit — don't repeat the same pitch three times.

    📬 Deliverability Basics

    The best email in the world fails if it lands in spam. Deliverability is about proving you are a legitimate sender.

    🔐
    SPF, DKIM & DMARC
    DNS records that authenticate your sending domain and tell mail providers your emails are genuine.
    🌐
    Separate Domain
    Send cold outreach from a secondary domain so a problem never damages your company's main email reputation.
    🔥
    Warm-Up & Volume
    Start new inboxes slowly and keep daily volume modest. Sudden spikes look like spam.
    🧹
    Clean Lists
    Verify addresses before sending. High bounce rates and spam complaints hurt your sender reputation.
    ⚠️
    Volume is not strategy. Blasting a large list of generic emails lowers reply rates and raises the chance of being marked as spam — which makes every later email less likely to arrive.

    ⚖️ Compliance & Opt-Outs

    Cold outreach is regulated, and the rules differ by country. Two principles hold almost everywhere: be honest about who you are and respect opt-outs.

    • CAN-SPAM (US): accurate sender information and subject line, a physical postal address, and a clear way to opt out that you honour within 10 business days.
    • GDPR / ePrivacy (EU & UK): you need a lawful basis for processing personal data, must be transparent, and must stop when someone objects. Rules vary by country.
    • Always: keep a suppression list so people who opted out are never contacted again.
    📌
    This is a starting point, not legal advice. Check your company's policy and ask legal when entering a new region.

    📉 Measuring Email Performance

    Pick metrics that reflect real interest, not vanity.

    MetricReliabilityNotes
    Open rateLowPrivacy features (such as Apple Mail Privacy Protection) can inflate or hide opens
    Reply rateMediumIncludes unsubscribes and "not interested" replies
    Positive reply rateHighReplies showing real interest — the number to improve
    Meetings booked per 100 emailsHighConnects the email directly to pipeline

    LinkedIn Outreach Sequence

    Set up your profile, warm up prospects, and run a light two-step sequence that earns familiarity instead of pitching.

    🪪 Profile & Sales Navigator Basics

    Prospects check your profile before they answer. Make it look like a helpful person, not a pitch deck.

    • Headline & About: say who you help and how, in plain language.
    • Photo & activity: a clear professional photo, and occasional useful posts or comments.
    • Sales Navigator: lead and account filters, saved searches, and lead alerts that flag job changes and activity.
    💡
    Job-change alerts are timing gold. A new role often brings new priorities and budget — a natural reason to reach out.

    💼 LinkedIn Outreach Sequence

    LinkedIn Sales Navigator supports a lighter, two-step sequence: a short connection note, followed by a no-pitch conversation starter once accepted.

    🔵 Connection Request < 300 chars

    Short, specific, no pitch.

    Hi {{first_name}} — saw {{company}} is scaling the
SDR team. I work with sales leaders on connecting
outbound tools so reps spend more time talking to
prospects than switching tabs. Would love to connect.

    🟢 Follow-up (after accept)

    Open question, still no pitch.

    Thanks for connecting, {{first_name}}! Curious how
{{company}}'s SDR team currently splits time between
calling, email, and LinkedIn — mostly manual, or
already stitched into one cadence?
    📌
    LinkedIn earns the warm-up, not the close. Its job is to make the next email or call feel familiar, not to pitch the product in the first message.

    🔥 Warming Up Before You Connect

    Cold-to-connected goes better when the prospect has already seen your name.

    1. View their profile so your name appears in their notifications.
    2. Engage with a post — add a thoughtful comment that adds something, not just "great post!".
    3. Send the connection request with a short, specific note.
    4. After they accept, start a no-pitch conversation.

    ✉️ Connection Request vs InMail

    Two ways to reach someone on LinkedIn — each with trade-offs.

    🔗 Connection Request

    Free; needs acceptance first.

    • Short note, strict character limit
    • Builds a lasting connection
    • Weekly invite limits apply

    📩 InMail

    Paid credits; messages a non-connection.

    • Reaches people you aren't connected to
    • Uses limited credits
    • Works best when highly relevant
    ⚠️
    Don't spam invites. Mass, copy-paste requests get reported and can lead LinkedIn to restrict your account.

    Personalization Framework

    A fast, repeatable research routine that makes outreach feel 1:1 without writing from scratch every time.

    🔎 Personalization Framework

    Scaled personalization comes from a fast, repeatable research routine — not writing every message from scratch.

    👤
    LinkedIn — 30 sec
    Recent job change, promotion, post, or comment from the prospect.
    📰
    Company News — 30 sec
    Funding, product launch, or leadership hire in the last 90 days.
    🧑‍💻
    Job Postings — 30 sec
    Open roles are a strong buying-intent signal (e.g. hiring 3 SDRs).
    🛠️
    Tech Signals — 30 sec
    Job descriptions or firmographic tools reveal which tools they already use.
    💡
    One relevant detail beats five generic ones. A single specific, researched fact about the account in the opening line is more convincing than a long but generic list of product benefits.

    🎚️ Three Levels of Personalization

    Not every message needs the same depth. Match the level to the account.

    LevelWhat ChangesBest For
    LightMerge fields only (name, company)Tier 3, high volume — and easily spotted as a template
    MediumRole- or industry-specific pain point and proofTier 2 segments
    DeepA researched, account-specific trigger written 1:1Tier 1 accounts

    🎯 Triggers & Buying Signals

    A trigger event is a recent change that gives you a legitimate reason to reach out now.

    💵 Funding round🧑‍💼 New leadership hire📣 Product launch🧑‍💻 Hiring surge🌍 Expansion🔧 New tool adopted
    💡
    Recent beats impressive. A signal from the last 90 days is far more convincing than an old achievement everyone already knows.

    🧪 The Trigger → Relevance → Ask Formula

    Turn research into an opening line in three beats.

    BeatQuestionExample
    TriggerWhat did I notice?"Saw you opened three SDR roles this quarter."
    RelevanceWhy does it matter to them?"Teams scaling that fast often lose time switching tools."
    AskWhat small step do I want?"Worth a 15-minute look at how others fixed it?"

    🚫 Personalization Mistakes to Avoid

    • Fake personalization: "Loved your recent post!" with no detail proves you didn't read it.
    • Creepy details: avoid personal social media, family or anything unrelated to their work.
    • Broken merge fields: "Hi {{first_name}}" in a sent email is worse than no personalization.
    • Too much research, too little message: use one strong detail; don't list everything you found.

    Advanced Outbound Playbook

    Cadence design, multi-threading, pipeline math, testing and clean handoffs — how top SDRs run outbound as a system.

    🗓️ Designing a Multichannel Cadence

    A cadence is a planned sequence of touches. The example below is illustrative — tune the days and channels using your own data.

    DayChannelTouch
    1Call + LinkedIn profile viewQuick call; leave a short voicemail if no answer
    2Email 1Trigger-led opener
    4LinkedInConnection request with a short note
    7Email 2Proof — stat or case study
    9LinkedIn messageNo-pitch conversation starter (if connected)
    12CallSecond attempt at a different time of day
    14Email 3Breakup email
    💡
    Vary channels and space the touches. Three touches in one day feel like harassment; three over ten days feel like persistence.

    🧵 Multi-Threading an Account

    Multi-threading means engaging several relevant stakeholders at the same account, so a deal doesn't depend on one person who might be busy, leave, or say no.

    • Pick roles that care about different parts of your value (for example a user, a manager and a budget holder).
    • Tailor each message to that person's priorities — don't forward the same text to everyone.
    • Space the outreach and coordinate with the AE so the account doesn't receive a flood from your team.

    🧮 Pipeline Math: Work Backwards

    Start from the target and work back to the activity required. The numbers below are an illustration — use your own conversion rates.

    StepIllustrative RateResult
    Goal: qualified opportunities—10
    Held meetings → qualified opportunity50%10 ÷ 0.50 = 20 held meetings
    Booked meetings → held (show rate)80%20 ÷ 0.80 = 25 booked meetings
    Prospects contacted → meeting booked5%25 ÷ 0.05 = 500 prospects
    📌
    Quotas become a plan. Once you know your rates, "book 25 meetings" turns into a concrete number of prospects and touches per week.

    🧫 A/B Testing & Iterating

    Improve outreach by testing, not by guessing.

    1. Change one variable at a time (subject line, opener or CTA).
    2. Send enough volume before judging — tiny samples produce misleading winners.
    3. Judge by positive replies and meetings, not opens.
    4. Review weekly, keep the winner, and test the next variable.

    🤝 Handing Off to the AE

    A great meeting can still die if the AE walks in blind. Hand over the context.

    • Pain: what problem did the prospect confirm, in their words?
    • People: who attended, and who else is involved in the decision?
    • Timeline & next step: what was agreed and by when?
    • No-shows: follow up promptly and offer to reschedule rather than giving up.

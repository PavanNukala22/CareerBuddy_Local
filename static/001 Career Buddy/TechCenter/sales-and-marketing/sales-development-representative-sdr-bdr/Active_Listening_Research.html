    Active Listening & Research

    Gathering account intelligence to personalize messaging at scale.

    🔎 Why Research and Listening Matter

    Prospects ignore messages that could have been sent to anyone. Research tells you what to say before you reach out; active listening tells you what to say next once they respond. Together they make outreach feel relevant instead of random.

    🏢
    Account Intelligence
    What the company does, what’s changing, and what it is likely to care about.
    👤
    Contact Insight
    The person’s role, background and where they sit in the decision.
    👂
    Active Listening
    Hearing the pain behind the words, in calls and in replies.
    ⚙️
    Scale
    A repeatable routine so relevance doesn’t slow you to a crawl.
    💡
    Relevance earns attention. One accurate, specific line about their situation does more than three paragraphs about your product.

    🔁 How the Loop Works

    Research and listening are not separate steps; they form a loop.

    Figure · Research → Personalize → Listen → Book, then repeat
    📌
    Every call is research. What the prospect tells you should be written down and used in the next touch and the AE handoff.

    📐 What “Personalization at Scale” Means

    It does not mean writing every email from scratch. It means building a repeatable system: tier your accounts, run a short research routine, then drop a relevant, accurate line into a proven structure.

    ❌ Fake personal

    Only the name changes.

    Hi {{first_name}},
I help companies like
{{company}} grow faster…

    ✅ Relevant

    Specific, short, true.

    Hi Priya, saw you’re hiring
three SDRs. Teams ramping
reps often struggle with…

    🧭 Ethics & Boundaries

    • Stay professional: use role, company news, public posts about work and industry.
    • Skip the personal: never reference family, home, health or private details.
    • Be accurate: a wrong fact is worse than no personalisation.
    • Respect privacy rules: use legitimately sourced data and follow your company’s compliance guidance.
    ⚠️
    If it feels creepy, it is. Mentioning something the prospect didn’t expect you to know damages trust instantly.

    ⚖️ Matching Effort to Account Value

    TierAccount TypeResearch Depth
    Tier 1Highest value, best fitDeep: 15–20 minutes, custom message
    Tier 2Good fit, mid valueModerate: 5–8 minutes, trigger + relevance line
    Tier 3Broad list, lower valueLight: segment-level template, one proven variable
    💡
    Spend research time where it pays. Over-researching low-tier accounts kills your volume; under-researching top accounts wastes your best chances. Times shown are illustrative.

    Account Research

    Finding out what a company does, what it prioritises, and what is changing — using quick, reliable public sources.

    🗺️ Where to Look

    SourceWhat You Learn
    Company website & blogWhat they sell, who they serve, how they describe themselves
    Press releases & newsLaunches, partnerships, funding, leadership changes
    LinkedIn company pageHeadcount, new hires, announcements, posts
    Job postingsGrowth areas, priorities, tools they use
    Annual reports & earnings callsStrategy and stated priorities (public companies)
    Review sitesWhat customers say about their products and tools
    Podcasts & interviewsLeaders’ own words about challenges and goals
    📌
    Use more than one source. A single page can be outdated or incomplete; cross-checking keeps you accurate.

    ⏱️ The Five-Minute Research Routine

    1. What they do: one sentence from their homepage or LinkedIn.
    2. Who they serve: customers, market, size.
    3. What’s new: latest news, launch, funding or hiring.
    4. Fit check: does this match your ICP and pain points?
    5. One angle: pick the single most relevant line to use.
    💡
    Stop when you have one good angle. You’re looking for a reason to reach out, not a complete biography.

    📋 Reading Job Postings

    Job postings are one of the best free sources of intelligence.

    What You SeeWhat It Can Suggest
    Many open SDR or sales rolesGrowth push; ramping and training challenges
    New role for a function you serveA new priority or budget
    Tools listed in requirementsTheir current tech stack
    “Build the team from scratch”Process and tooling decisions ahead

    📑 Public Company Documents

    For public companies, annual reports and earnings calls show what leadership says matters.

    • Scan for repeated themes: growth, cost, efficiency, expansion.
    • Note exact priorities in their wording, then reflect them in your message.
    • Look for risks or challenges they admit to.
    • Remember that smaller or private companies rely more on news, LinkedIn and job posts.

    ✅ Verifying & Recording Facts

    ❌ Unverified

    Risky to use.

    “I saw you have 1,200 employees…”
(source: one outdated page)

    ✅ Verified

    Cross-checked or softened.

    “It looks like your team is
growing quickly…”
(supported by hiring data)
    ⚠️
    When sources disagree, don’t quote the number. Use a safer, softer statement or confirm on the call.

    Buying Signals

    Spotting the events that create a reason to act now, and ranking them by freshness and relevance.

    ⚡ What Is a Trigger?

    A trigger is a change at a company that gives you a legitimate reason to reach out now, not “whenever.”

    💵
    Funding round
    New budget and pressure to grow.
    🧑‍💼
    New leadership
    New leaders often review tools and processes.
    📣
    Product launch
    New goals, new needs, new messaging.
    🧑‍💻
    Hiring surge
    Rapid growth that strains systems and training.
    🌍
    Expansion
    New markets, offices or teams.
    🔧
    Tool change
    A new platform adopted, or one removed.

    🎯 Fit Signals vs Intent Signals

    TypeMeaningExample
    Fit signalThey match your ICPRight industry, size, tech stack
    Timing signalSomething changed recentlyFunding, hire, launch
    Intent signalThey’re showing interestVisited your pricing page, replied to an email, engaged with a post
    💡
    Fit tells you who; timing and intent tell you when. The best outreach combines all three.

    🏆 Ranking Signals

    1. Freshness: something from last week beats something from last year.
    2. Relevance: does it connect to a problem you actually solve?
    3. Seniority: a signal tied to your buyer’s own area is stronger.
    4. Specificity: a concrete event beats a general trend.
    ⚠️
    Stale triggers look careless. Referencing news from a year ago signals a mass blast, not real attention.

    📡 Where Signals Come From

    SourceSignals You Can Catch
    LinkedIn and Sales Navigator alertsJob changes, company news, posts
    News and press alertsFunding, launches, partnerships
    Job boardsHiring surges and new roles
    Your CRM and engagement toolReplies, clicks, past conversations
    Website visits (if tracked by your company)Interest in specific pages
    📌
    Set up alerts once. Saved searches and news alerts bring signals to you instead of making you hunt every morning.

    🔧 Turning a Signal into an Opening

    signal-to-opening.txtSignal:     VP of Sales hired last week; 3 open SDR roles
Relevance:  New leaders often rebuild the outbound motion
Opening:    “Congrats on joining — saw you’re hiring SDRs.
            Teams in that spot often struggle with ramp-up.”
    💡
    Signal → relevance → ask. Never stop at “I saw your news” — connect it to why you’re calling.

    Contact Research

    Understanding the person you’re contacting: role, background, priorities and place in the buying group.

    👤 What to Check on a Person

    WhereWhat to Note
    LinkedIn profileRole, time in role, past companies, headline
    Recent activityPosts and comments on work topics
    Career movesNew role, promotion, change of company
    Shared groundMutual connections, groups, events, past employers
    📌
    Two minutes is enough. You’re looking for one relevant professional detail, not a dossier.

    🎭 Persona and Role Context

    The same company has several people who care about different outcomes.

    RoleLikely Cares About
    Executive / VPResults, growth, risk, budget
    ManagerTeam performance, process, tools
    End userDaily workflow, time saved, ease of use
    Operations / ITIntegration, security, rollout
    💡
    Match the message to the role. A VP wants outcomes; an end user wants fewer clicks.

    🧩 Mapping the Buying Committee

    • Identify a likely champion who feels the pain.
    • Find the decision maker who owns the budget.
    • Spot influencers and blockers such as IT or finance.
    • Add them to the account record in your CRM.
    📌
    Different stakeholders, different messages. Don’t forward the same email to everyone.

    📰 Using Posts and Job Changes

    ✅ Relevant

    Work-focused and specific.

    “Your post on onboarding new
reps resonated — we hear the
same from similar teams.”

    ❌ Shallow

    Flattery with no point.

    “Great post!! Love your
content! Let’s connect.”
    • New in role: congratulate briefly, then tie to a relevant priority.
    • Changed company: note what they did before; it may shape their preferences.

    🚫 What Not to Use

    • Family, relationships, children or home details.
    • Health, religion, politics or other sensitive topics.
    • Photos or posts from private life.
    • Anything you wouldn’t say to them face to face at a conference.
    ⚠️
    Stay strictly professional. Even if it’s publicly visible, personal details cross a line most prospects don’t forgive.

    Active Listening

    Hearing what the prospect actually means, asking better follow-ups, and capturing it for the next step.

    👂 What Active Listening Is

    Active listening means fully concentrating on what the person says, understanding it, and responding thoughtfully — instead of just waiting for your turn to talk.

    🤫
    Pause
    Leave a beat after they finish. People often add the most useful detail in that silence.
    🔁
    Paraphrase
    Restate it in your own words to confirm you understood.
    🏷️
    Label
    Name the situation or feeling: “Sounds like that’s been frustrating.”
    ❓
    Probe
    Ask one follow-up that goes a layer deeper.

    ❓ Open Questions That Dig

    ✅ Open

    Invites a full answer.

    “How are you handling ramp-up
today?”
“What’s made that hard?”
“Tell me more about that.”

    ❌ Closed

    Gets a yes or no.

    “Do you have a ramp-up problem?”
“Is that a priority?”
“Are you using a tool?”
    💡
    Start with how, what, tell me. Closed questions are useful later to confirm details, not to open a topic.

    🎙️ Paraphrase & Label in Action

    listening-call.txtThem: We use spreadsheets, but reporting takes forever.
You:  So the spreadsheets work, but reporting is where the
      time goes — is that right?
Them: Exactly. Our managers spend Fridays on it.
You:  Sounds frustrating. What does that cost you each week?
    📌
    Notice the structure: paraphrase, confirm, label, then a deeper question.

    🖊️ Taking Notes That Matter

    • Capture the prospect’s own words — they’re your best messaging.
    • Note pains, goals, timelines, people and next steps.
    • Mark anything you promised to do.
    • Log it in the CRM right after the call.
    call-notes.txtPain:   “Managers lose Fridays to reporting”
Goal:   Cut ramp-up from 5 months to 3
People: Dev (Sales Ops) will review tools
Next:   Demo Thu 2 pm; send two examples

    🚧 Common Listening Mistakes

    MistakeWhy It HurtsFix
    InterruptingSignals you’re not interestedWait until they finish, then pause
    Planning your next lineYou miss the real answerFocus on their words
    Jumping to your pitchTurns a conversation into a scriptAsk one more question first
    Forgetting detailsLooks carelessTake notes as you talk
    💡
    Aim to listen more than you talk. A common rule of thumb is that the prospect should speak for most of a discovery conversation.

    Personalization at Scale

    Turning research into short, relevant messages you can produce quickly, accurately and repeatedly.

    🎚️ Three Levels of Personalization

    LevelWhat It UsesBest For
    SegmentIndustry, role or company sizeLarge lists, Tier 3
    AccountA fact or trigger about the companyTier 2
    1:1Company trigger plus personal professional detailTier 1
    📌
    Match depth to value. Not every message needs 1:1 detail.

    🧪 The Trigger → Relevance → Ask Formula

    A reliable structure for a short, personalised opener.

    1. Trigger: the specific, fresh observation.
    2. Relevance: why it matters to a problem you solve.
    3. Ask: a small, specific next step.
    formula-email.txtHi Priya,

Saw you’re hiring three SDRs — congrats on the growth.
Teams ramping new reps often tell us onboarding takes
longer than planned.

Worth 15 minutes Thursday to compare notes?

Sam

    🧱 Building Reusable Blocks

    • Opening lines by trigger type (funding, hiring, launch, new role).
    • Relevance lines by persona and pain point.
    • Proof lines with one short result.
    • Asks with two time options.
    • Swap one block and keep the rest.
    💡
    Personalise one line, not the whole email. A strong, accurate first line plus a proven body is the scalable version of 1:1.

    🧩 Merge Fields & Variables

    ✅ Good use

    Simple, accurate fields.

    {{first_name}}, {{company}},
{{trigger_line}} (written by you)

    ❌ Risky use

    Auto-generated flattery.

    {{ai_compliment}} about your
company’s “amazing growth”
    ⚠️
    Check every variable. A wrong name, company or fact in a “personal” line is worse than none. Preview before sending.

    🚫 Personalization Mistakes

    MistakeBetter
    Mentioning something unrelated to your offerPick a trigger tied to a problem you solve
    Long first paragraph about themOne short line, then relevance
    Over-flatteringBe specific and factual
    Outdated informationCheck the date and source
    Pretending to be 1:1 when it isn’tBe honest; relevance beats fake familiarity

    Advanced Playbook

    Running research as a system: account plans, notes, tools, testing and clean handoffs.

    🗂️ Building a Simple Account Plan

    SectionWhat to Include
    SnapshotWhat they do, size, fit
    TriggersRecent changes and dates
    PeopleChampion, decision maker, influencers
    PainsHypotheses from research, confirmed by calls
    Touch planWhich channel, when, with which message
    💡
    Keep it to one page. A plan you’ll actually update beats a long one nobody reads.

    🗄️ Research Notes in the CRM

    • Log triggers with dates so others can see how fresh they are.
    • Record what the prospect said, in their own words.
    • Tag the source of key facts.
    • Update notes after every conversation.
    • Share useful finds with teammates covering the same account.
    📌
    Shared notes prevent repeated research. If someone already learned it, you shouldn’t need to find it again.

    🤖 Using AI and Tools Wisely

    Tools can speed up research and drafting, but you remain responsible for accuracy.

    • Use tools to summarise pages, find signals and suggest angles.
    • Verify every fact before it goes into a message.
    • Never send AI-written compliments you haven’t read.
    • Keep the message sounding like you.
    ⚠️
    Unchecked output can be wrong. A confidently stated false fact damages credibility immediately.

    🧫 Testing Personalization

    Test whether your research effort actually improves results.

    1. Compare a personalised version against a generic one, changing one variable.
    2. Send enough volume before judging.
    3. Judge by positive replies and meetings, not opens.
    4. Review weekly and keep what wins.
    💡
    If replies fall, check length and relevance. A long personalised opener can underperform a short relevant line.

    🤝 Call Review & Handoff

    • Listen back for missed follow-up questions and rushed moments.
    • Note which research angles earned a response.
    • Hand the AE the pain, the people and the timeline in the prospect’s words.
    • Correct any facts you got wrong — thank the prospect when they fix you.
    📌
    Own your mistakes. A quick “thanks for the correction” keeps trust intact and fixes your notes.

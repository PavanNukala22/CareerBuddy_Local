    Sales Tech Stack

    CRM fluency (Salesforce, HubSpot) alongside sales engagement platforms (Salesloft, Apollo, Outreach) — the tools an SDR lives in every day.

    🧰 Why the Stack Matters

    An SDR’s results depend on how well they use their tools. A strong rep keeps the CRM clean, runs engagement sequences that sync back to it, and uses data tools to build accurate lists. Each tool has one clear job.

    🗄️
    CRM
    Salesforce or HubSpot. The record of every account, contact, deal and activity.
    🔁
    Sales Engagement
    Salesloft, Outreach or Apollo. Runs multi-step cadences of calls, emails and tasks.
    📇
    Data & Enrichment
    Apollo or ZoomInfo. Finds contacts, emails and phone numbers.
    🎧
    Dialer & Insights
    Calling tools and conversation intelligence that record and analyse calls.
    💡
    Tools don’t book meetings — process does. A tool with a weak process only helps you send weak outreach faster.

    🔗 How the Tools Connect

    The tools are designed to pass data to each other. Understanding the flow explains why one wrong field or missed log can ripple through the whole system.

    Figure · Data → CRM ⇄ Engagement platform → booked meeting
    📌
    Most engagement platforms sync with the CRM. Emails, calls and tasks you complete in the engagement tool are normally written back to the CRM — if the integration is set up correctly.

    🧭 System of Record vs System of Action

    Think of the stack in two layers.

    🗄️ System of Record

    Where the truth is stored.

    • CRM: Salesforce, HubSpot
    • Owns accounts, contacts, deals, history
    • Used by sales, marketing, management

    ⚡ System of Action

    Where the daily work happens.

    • Salesloft, Outreach, Apollo
    • Runs sequences, task queues, dialing
    • Writes activity back to the CRM

    📌

    🗓️ An SDR Day Inside the Stack

    1. Morning: open your task queue in the engagement tool and review today’s calls and emails.
    2. Call block: dial from the platform so each call is logged automatically.
    3. Mid-day: update CRM statuses, add notes, and create tasks for follow-ups.
    4. Afternoon: research new accounts, enrich contacts, and add them to a sequence.
    5. End of day: check replies, book meetings, and confirm everything synced to the CRM.
    💡
    Work from one queue. A single prioritised task list beats hopping between tabs and keeping notes in your head.

    CRM Fundamentals

    What a CRM stores, how records relate, and the data habits that keep it trustworthy.

    🗄️ What a CRM Stores

    A CRM (Customer Relationship Management system) is a shared database of who you’re selling to and everything that has happened with them.

    RecordWhat it RepresentsExample
    Account / CompanyAn organisationAcme Logistics
    ContactA person at an accountPriya Nair, VP Sales
    LeadAn unqualified person or company not yet worked into a dealA demo-request form fill
    Opportunity / DealA potential sale with stage and valueAnnual licence, Stage 2
    ActivityA logged call, email, meeting or taskCall: left voicemail, Thursday

    🔀 How Records Relate

    Records are linked. Several contacts belong to one account, and an account can have several opportunities. The links let everyone see the full picture.

    crm-hierarchy.txtAccount:      Acme Logistics
  ├─ Contact:   Priya Nair (VP Sales)
  ├─ Contact:   Dev Rao (Sales Ops)
  └─ Opportunity: Annual licence — Stage 2
        └─ Activities: 2 calls, 3 emails, 1 meeting
    💡
    Always attach contacts to the right account. Orphan contacts can’t be found by account-level searches, reports or territories.

    🪜 Lifecycle Stages & Statuses

    CRMs track where a record is in the journey. Names differ per company, but the idea is consistent.

    StageMeaningTypical Owner
    New / OpenFresh record, not yet workedSDR
    Attempting contactYou’re calling or emailing, no conversation yetSDR
    Connected / WorkingYou’ve spoken and are qualifyingSDR
    Qualified / Meeting bookedMeets criteria; handed to AESDR → AE
    OpportunityActive deal being workedAE
    Unqualified / Bad timingNot a fit now; keep a reasonSDR

    📝 Fields, Picklists & Required Data

    Fields are the slots that hold data. Picklists give you fixed choices so everyone records things the same way.

    • Use picklists for statuses, sources and reasons so reports can group them.
    • Fill required fields with real information — never a placeholder.
    • Keep names, titles and company names consistently formatted.
    • Add a short reason when you mark someone unqualified.
    ⚠️
    Garbage in, garbage out. Free-text where a picklist belongs makes reporting unreliable, and wrong data sends the wrong people the wrong message.

    🖊️ Logging Activities & Notes

    If it isn’t logged, it didn’t happen — as far as your team can see. A good note is short and specific.

    ❌ Weak note

    Tells the next person nothing.

    Called. Not interested.

    ✅ Useful note

    Outcome, pain, next step.

    Spoke to Priya. Hiring 3 SDRs,
tool switching slows ramp.
Agreed 15-min call Thu 2 pm.
    📌
    Write for the next person. A teammate or AE should understand the situation in ten seconds without calling the prospect.

    Salesforce for SDRs

    The Salesforce objects, views and habits SDRs use every day — and how a lead becomes an opportunity.

    ☁️ The Salesforce Data Model

    Salesforce separates records into standard objects. For an SDR, four matter most.

    🧍
    Lead
    An unqualified person you are prospecting. Sits on its own until converted.
    👤
    Contact
    A person linked to an Account, usually after conversion.
    🏢
    Account
    The company record that Contacts and Opportunities link to.
    💰
    Opportunity
    A potential deal, with amount, stage and close date.
    📌
    Different companies configure Salesforce differently. Extra fields, record types and rules are common — learn your org’s setup.

    🔎 Finding Your Work: List Views & Tasks

    • List views show filtered records, such as “My leads — not contacted this week.”
    • Tasks schedule and log follow-ups like calls and emails.
    • Activity timeline on a record shows every logged touch in order.
    • Lead status shows progress; update it after each meaningful touch.
    💡
    Build a small set of saved list views (new leads, follow-ups due, no activity in 7 days) so your day starts with a ready-made to-do list.

    🔄 How a Lead Is Converted

    When a lead is qualified, it is converted. Conversion typically creates linked records from the lead’s data.

    1. Check whether the Account or Contact already exists, to avoid duplicates.
    2. Confirm the qualification criteria (for example BANT or your team’s checklist) are met.
    3. Convert the lead; Salesforce creates or links an Account and Contact, and can create an Opportunity.
    4. Assign to the AE per your process and hand over the context.
    ⚠️
    Don’t convert early. Creating an Opportunity too soon pollutes pipeline numbers and annoys AEs. Convert when your team’s criteria say so.

    📊 Reports & Dashboards for SDRs

    ReportQuestion It Answers
    Activities by repHow many calls and emails did I log this week?
    Leads by statusWhere are my leads stuck?
    Leads by sourceWhich lead sources create meetings?
    Meetings bookedAm I on track for quota?
    Leads with no activityWho have I forgotten to work?

    ✅ Habits That Keep Salesforce Useful

    • Log every call and email — through the engagement tool or manually.
    • Update lead status immediately after a touch.
    • Fill required fields accurately instead of typing junk to bypass a validation rule.
    • Check ownership before contacting — someone else may already be working that account.
    • Add the reason whenever you disqualify a lead.
    💡
    Treat validation rules as guardrails. If a rule blocks you, supply the real data or ask your admin — don’t work around it.

    HubSpot for SDRs

    HubSpot’s contact-centred model, lifecycle stages, sequences and lists — and how it compares with Salesforce.

    🟠 The HubSpot Data Model

    HubSpot stores data in four core objects, linked by associations.

    👤
    Contacts
    People, with properties like job title, email and lifecycle stage.
    🏢
    Companies
    Organisations that contacts and deals are associated with.
    💼
    Deals
    Potential sales that move through pipeline stages.
    🎫
    Tickets
    Customer support requests, mostly used after the sale.
    📌
    Associate everything. Linking contacts, companies and deals together lets reports and teammates see the whole relationship.

    🔖 Lifecycle Stage vs Lead Status

    These two properties are often confused.

    🧭 Lifecycle stage

    Where the contact is in the overall journey.

    Subscriber → Lead → MQL → SQL
→ Opportunity → Customer

    📞 Lead status

    Where your sales follow-up stands.

    New → Attempting → Connected
→ Bad timing / Unqualified
    💡
    Lifecycle is the big picture; lead status is your working notes. SDRs update lead status most often.

    📨 Sequences, Tasks & Templates

    HubSpot’s sales tools let you automate part of your outreach while keeping it personal.

    • Sequences send a series of emails and create tasks over time; they typically stop when the contact replies.
    • Tasks remind you to call, email or connect on LinkedIn.
    • Templates & snippets are reusable emails and short text blocks.
    • Meetings links let prospects book time directly on your calendar.
    📌
    Some features depend on your plan and seat type. Check which tools your company has enabled.

    🗂️ Lists, Views & Properties

    FeatureWhat It Does
    Active listUpdates automatically as records meet the criteria
    Static listA fixed snapshot of records at one moment
    Saved viewsFiltered tables of contacts or companies for your daily work
    PropertiesThe fields that store data on each record

    Sales Engagement Platforms

    What Salesloft, Outreach and Apollo do, how they differ, and what stays the same across all of them.

    🔁 What an Engagement Platform Does

    A sales engagement platform (SEP) organises your outreach into repeatable sequences and logs everything back to the CRM.

    🗓️
    Sequences
    Multi-step plans of emails, calls and tasks with timing between steps.
    ✅
    Task queue
    Your prioritised list of manual actions due today.
    📞
    Dialer
    Click-to-call with automatic call logging.
    📈
    Analytics
    Reply rates, meetings booked and A/B test results.

    ⚖️ Salesloft, Outreach & Apollo Side by Side

    PlatformMulti-step Plan CalledStrengthNote
    SalesloftCadenceCadences, dialer and call analysisSyncs with major CRMs
    OutreachSequenceSequences, task flow and conversation insightsSyncs with major CRMs
    ApolloSequenceContact database plus outreach in one toolUses credits for data access
    📌
    Names differ, ideas match. Cadence (Salesloft) and sequence (Outreach, Apollo) mean the same thing: a planned series of touches.

    🚀 Apollo: Data and Engagement Together

    Apollo combines prospecting data with outreach tools, so a rep can find, verify and contact someone from one place.

    • Search by title, industry, size and location to build lists.
    • Reveal or export contact details — this usually consumes credits, so watch your plan limits.
    • Use email status/verification to avoid sending to risky addresses.
    • Add contacts to a sequence, then make sure records sync to your CRM.
    ⚠️
    Don’t create a CRM duplicate. Before pushing a contact from Apollo, check the CRM for an existing account or contact.

    🎧 Task Queues, Dialers & Conversation Intelligence

    FeatureUse
    Task queueShows what to do next: calls, manual emails, LinkedIn tasks
    DialerCalls from the platform and logs them automatically
    Call recording & notesLets managers coach and reps review themselves
    Conversation intelligenceFinds themes, objections and talk-time patterns across calls

    ⚡ Learning a New Platform Fast

    You will likely change tools during your career. The concepts map across every platform.

    ConceptWhat to Look For
    Prospect recordHow it links to the CRM contact or lead

    💡

    Sequences & Cadences

    Building a sequence inside the tool: step types, merge fields, exit rules and a pre-launch checklist.

    🧬 Anatomy of a Sequence

    Step TypeHow It RunsExample
    Automatic emailSends on its own at the scheduled timeDay 1 trigger-led opener
    Manual emailCreates a task so you review and sendDay 7 proof email, personalised
    Call taskAppears in your queue to dialDay 3 call + voicemail
    LinkedIn taskPrompts a profile view or connection requestDay 4 connect
    Wait / delaySpacing between steps2 business days
    💡
    Mix automatic and manual steps. Automation saves time; manual steps are where you add real personalisation.

    🧩 Merge Fields & Fallbacks

    Merge fields insert data from the record into the message. A fallback is the default used when the field is empty.

    ❌ No fallback

    Empty field breaks the email.

    Hi ,
I noticed {{company}} is hiring…

    ✅ With fallback

    Safe even if data is missing.

    Hi {{first_name|there}},
I noticed {{company|your team}} is hiring…
    ⚠️
    Merge-field errors look careless. Preview with real contact data before sending. Exact syntax differs by platform.

    🚪 Exit Criteria & Pausing

    A good sequence stops by itself when it should.

    • Prospect replies — stop and respond personally.
    • Meeting is booked — remove from the sequence.
    • Email bounces — stop and fix or remove the address.
    • Prospect opts out — stop immediately and add to suppression.
    • Contact becomes an active opportunity — hand over to the AE.
    ⚠️
    Emailing someone who just booked or opted out is a fast way to lose trust. Check exit rules before launch.

    🗓️ A 14-Day Sequence Example

    DayStepType
    1Call + voicemailCall task
    2Trigger-led intro emailAutomatic
    4LinkedIn connection requestLinkedIn task
    7Proof email with a short case studyManual
    10Second call, different time of dayCall task
    14Breakup emailAutomatic

    🧾 Pre-Launch Checklist

    1. Send a test email to yourself and preview with a real contact.
    2. Check merge fields and fallback values.
    3. Verify exit rules and the unsubscribe or opt-out text.
    4. Confirm the CRM sync and that activities will be logged.
    5. Start with a small batch, review results, then scale.

    Advanced Stack Playbook

    Data sync, deduplication, deliverability controls, routing and reporting — running the stack as a system.

    🔌 Data Sync & Field Mapping

    Most integrations map fields between tools and sync them in one or both directions.

    • Bidirectional sync: a change in either tool updates the other.
    • Field mapping: decides which field in one tool matches which in the other.
    • Conflicts: if the same field is edited in both places, rules decide which tool wins.
    • Lag: syncs can take minutes, so recent activity may not show immediately.
    📌
    Report sync problems early. Missing activities or wrong owners usually mean a mapping or permission issue your admin can fix.

    🧹 Deduplication & Data Hygiene

    • Search by email and company domain before creating a record.
    • Merge duplicates using your company’s process rather than deleting one.
    • Standardise titles, countries and company names.
    • Remove or flag bounced emails and wrong numbers.
    • Review stale records on a schedule.
    ⚠️
    Duplicates create double outreach and broken reports. Two reps calling the same prospect looks unprofessional.

    📬 Deliverability & Compliance Controls

    ControlPurpose
    Daily send limitsProtect sender reputation from sudden volume
    Email verificationReduce bounces before they happen
    Bounce handlingStop sending to invalid addresses
    Suppression / opt-out listsRespect do-not-contact requests across tools
    Unsubscribe link or opt-out textMeet email compliance requirements
    💡
    If bounces spike, pause. Verify the list, check settings with your admin, and only then continue — never push more volume.

    🗺️ Routing, Ownership & Territories

    Routing rules decide who gets which lead.

    • Round robin: assigns leads in rotation for even distribution.
    • Territory: assigns by region, industry or account size.
    • Account ownership: tells you who may contact a company.
    • Handoff rules: define when a lead becomes the AE’s.

    📈 Reporting & Measuring Sequences

    MetricUse It To
    Positive reply rateJudge message quality
    Meetings booked per sequenceFind sequences that work
    Bounce rateSpot data or deliverability problems
    Per-step resultsSee which touch earns replies
    Connect rate by timeChoose better call windows
    📌
    Judge by replies and meetings, not opens. Open tracking is unreliable, and activity alone is never the goal.

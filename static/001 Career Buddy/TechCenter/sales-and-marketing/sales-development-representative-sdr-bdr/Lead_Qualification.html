    Lead Qualification

    Proficiency in frameworks like BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) or MEDDIC to vet prospects.

    ✅ What Qualification Is

    Qualification is the process of deciding whether a prospect is worth an Account Executive’s time. A framework gives you a consistent set of things to learn, so decisions about who moves forward are based on evidence rather than gut feeling.

    🎯
    Fit
    Does the company match your Ideal Customer Profile?
    🩹
    Pain
    Is there a real problem worth solving?
    👥
    People
    Who decides, who influences, who blocks?
    ⏰
    Timing
    Is there a reason to act now, or at a clear future date?
    💡
    Qualifying out is a win. Ending a poor-fit conversation early frees your time for leads that can actually become revenue.

    🪜 Where It Fits in the Funnel

    Qualification happens throughout the conversation, then ends in a decision: hand off, nurture, or disqualify.

    Figure · Lead → conversation → qualify → handoff to the AE
    📌
    Qualified means meeting your team’s agreed criteria. Definitions differ between companies, so learn yours.

    📋 Lead Stages at a Glance

    TermMeaningWho Decides
    MQLMarketing Qualified Lead — shows engagement and fit based on marketing criteriaMarketing
    SQLSales Qualified Lead — vetted by sales as a real opportunitySDR / AE
    SALSales Accepted Lead — an AE has accepted the lead for follow-upAE
    OpportunityAn active deal with stage and valueAE
    📌
    Names and rules vary. Some teams skip stages or define them differently — check your own definitions.

    💬 Qualification Is a Conversation

    ❌ Interrogation

    Feels like a form.

    “What’s your budget?
Who signs? What’s your timeline?
Any other decision makers?”

    ✅ Conversation

    Builds the picture naturally.

    “What’s prompting you to look at
this now?”
“Who else would be involved?”
    ⚠️
    Don’t read a framework out loud like a checklist. Prospects disengage when a call starts to feel like an audit.

    💸 The Cost of Poor Qualification

    • AEs waste time on meetings that were never going to close.
    • Pipeline looks bigger than it really is, which distorts forecasts.
    • Trust between SDRs and AEs breaks down.
    • Good prospects get less attention because time is spread thin.
    💡
    Quality beats volume. A smaller number of well-qualified meetings is worth more than a large number of weak ones.

    BANT

    The classic four-part qualification framework — what each letter means and how to explore it without sounding like a checklist.

    🔠 The Four Letters

    BANT stands for Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline. It was popularised by IBM and remains a common, simple starting point for SDR qualification.

    💵
    Budget
    Can they afford, or can they obtain funding for, a solution?
    🧑‍💼
    Authority
    Can this person decide, or influence the decision?
    🩹
    Need
    Is there a real problem your product addresses?
    ⏳
    Timeline
    When do they plan to decide or act?

    👂 What to Listen For

    LetterStrong SignalWeak Signal
    BudgetFunding allocated, or a clear way to obtain it“No idea”, “We never spend on this”
    AuthorityOwns the decision, or can name who doesNo access to anyone senior
    NeedA specific, costly pain in their own words“Just curious”
    TimelineA date, event or deadline driving action“Maybe someday”

    🗣️ Asking Naturally

    LetterNatural Question
    Budget“How are you thinking about investing in solving this?”
    Authority“Who else would weigh in on a decision like this?”
    Need“What’s making this a priority right now?”
    Timeline“Is there a date or event driving this?”
    💡
    Start with Need. Once the pain is clear, questions about budget, people and timing feel natural.

    🧱 Handling “No Budget”

    no-budget.txtThem: We don’t have budget for this.
You:  Understood. What does the problem cost you today?
Them: Probably a few hours a week, per manager.
You:  Worth knowing. When does planning open for next
      quarter? I could check back then.
    📌
    “No budget” often means “no priority yet.” Exploring the cost of the problem separates real constraints from low urgency.

    ⚖️ Strengths & Limits of BANT

    ✅ Strengths

    Simple and fast.

    • Easy to learn
• Good for early screening
• Shared language with AEs

    ⚠️ Limits

    Can be too rigid.

    • May rule out good leads early
• Budget can be created later
• Misses buying process detail
    ⚠️
    Don’t disqualify on one letter alone. A prospect with a strong need but no budget today may be worth nurturing, not dropping.

    MEDDIC

    A deeper framework for complex, multi-stakeholder deals — what each element means and how SDRs can start gathering it.

    🔠 The Six Elements

    MEDDIC stands for Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain, Champion. It originated in enterprise software sales and is widely used for larger, longer deals.

    LetterElementMeaning
    MMetricsThe measurable outcomes the prospect wants to achieve
    EEconomic BuyerThe person with final authority over the budget
    DDecision CriteriaThe standards used to compare options
    DDecision ProcessThe steps, people and timeline of the purchase
    IIdentify PainThe problem that is costly enough to justify action
    CChampionAn internal advocate with influence who wants you to win

    🔍 What an SDR Can Gather Early

    • Pain: the problem, in their words, and who feels it.
    • Metrics: any numbers they mention — time lost, targets, costs.
    • People: who else is involved, and who might hold the budget.
    • Champion signals: someone who engages, shares information and wants to help.
    📌
    Depth varies by role. SDRs usually collect the first layer; AEs go deeper. Know what your team expects at handoff.

    🧭 Economic Buyer vs Champion

    💼 Economic Buyer

    Holds the money and final say.

    • Owns the budget
• Cares about outcomes and risk
• May be hard to reach early

    🙋 Champion

    Pushes internally for you.

    • Feels the pain
• Has influence and access
• Shares information openly
    💡
    A friendly contact isn’t automatically a champion. A real champion has influence and acts on your behalf, not just enthusiasm.

    🛤️ Decision Criteria & Process

    QuestionWhat It Reveals
    “How will you compare options?”Decision criteria
    “What does a typical purchase look like here?”Decision process
    “Who needs to approve a tool like this?”Approvers and blockers
    “Is there a deadline or review cycle?”Timeline and process

    ➕ MEDDPICC — A Common Extension

    Many teams extend MEDDIC to MEDDPICC, adding two elements.

    📄
    Paper Process
    The legal, procurement and contract steps needed to close.
    🥊
    Competition
    Who else they’re considering, including doing nothing.
    📌
    Variants exist. Use the version your company uses and keep notes in its CRM fields.

    Frameworks Compared

    BANT, MEDDIC and other common frameworks side by side, and how to choose between them.

    ⚖️ Side-by-Side

    FrameworkLettersTypical Fit
    BANTBudget, Authority, Need, TimelineQuick screening, simpler sales
    CHAMPChallenges, Authority, Money, PrioritisationLeads with the problem first
    ANUMAuthority, Need, Urgency, MoneyPuts authority and need ahead of budget
    MEDDICMetrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain, ChampionComplex, multi-stakeholder deals
    MEDDPICCMEDDIC + Paper Process + CompetitionEnterprise deals with procurement

    🧭 Choosing a Framework

    • Deal size and complexity: larger deals with several stakeholders suit MEDDIC.
    • Sales cycle: short cycles favour lighter frameworks.
    • Your role: SDRs often use a lighter version; AEs go deeper.
    • Company standard: use the framework your team and CRM already support.

    🧭 Frameworks Are Guides, Not Scripts

    A framework tells you what to learn, not how to ask.

    ✅ Guide

    Adapts to the conversation.

    Follow the prospect’s thread,
then cover gaps naturally.

    ❌ Script

    Forces a fixed order.

    Q1 budget, Q2 authority,
Q3 need, Q4 timeline.

    🗺️ Mapping Elements Across Frameworks

    IdeaBANTMEDDIC
    The problemNeedIdentify Pain
    Who decidesAuthorityEconomic Buyer
    MoneyBudgetPart of Economic Buyer / Metrics
    TimingTimelineDecision Process
    Internal ally—Champion
    💡
    The ideas repeat. Learn pain, people, money and timing once, then apply any framework’s labels.

    🧩 Combining Frameworks

    • Use BANT-style screening on the first call.
    • Capture MEDDIC elements as they surface in later conversations.
    • Keep fields in your CRM so notes stay consistent.
    • Avoid adding more questions than the conversation can bear.
    📌
    More framework isn’t more qualification. The goal is a clear decision, not a perfectly filled form.

    Discovery Questions

    Questions that uncover pain, people, timing and priority — in a way that keeps the prospect talking.

    🩹 Open Questions for Need

    • “What’s prompting you to look at this now?”
    • “How are you handling this today?”
    • “What’s been hardest about that?”
    • “What have you tried so far?”
    💡
    Open questions first. They give you material; closed questions confirm details afterwards.

    👥 Questions for Authority

    ✅ Natural

    Invites them to name people.

    “Who else would be involved in a
decision like this?”

    ❌ Blunt

    Can feel like a challenge.

    “Are you the decision maker?”

    ⏳ Questions for Urgency & Timeline

    • “Is there a date or event driving this?”
    • “What happens if this stays as it is?”
    • “What would need to happen for this to move up the list?”
    • “When did you last review this?”
    📌
    Ask about consequences. “What happens if nothing changes?” reveals urgency better than “When do you want to buy?”

    🎙️ A Qualification Call Flow

    qualification-call.txtYou:  What’s prompting you to look at this now?
Them: Reps take five months to ramp.
You:  What does that cost you?
Them: We miss quota for two quarters each hire.
You:  Who else cares about that?
Them: Our VP of Sales — she owns the plan.
You:  Is there a date driving this?
Them: Planning closes end of next month.
    💡
    Notice the order: pain, impact, people, timing — and not once did the rep say “budget.”

    🌫️ When the Answers Are Vague

    They SayWhat You Can Try
    “Just browsing.”Ask what made them start looking; check for hidden urgency
    “No timeline.”Ask what would need to happen first; else nurture
    “I’ll know later.”Agree a specific follow-up date
    “We’re fine.”Ask what would make them consider changing
    ⚠️
    Don’t force answers. If after honest discovery there’s no pain, move on or nurture rather than inflating the lead.

    Scoring & Handoff

    Deciding who moves forward, recording why, and handing leads to the AE with the right context.

    📊 Fit vs Engagement Scoring

    ScoreBased OnExample
    FitWho they areIndustry, company size, role, tech stack
    EngagementWhat they doEmail replies, page visits, content downloads
    💡
    Both matter. A perfect-fit account with no interest isn’t ready; an interested lead outside your ICP may not convert.

    🔀 Three Possible Outcomes

    🚀
    Hand off
    Meets criteria; book the AE meeting.
    🌱
    Nurture
    Fit but not ready; set a follow-up date.
    🚫
    Disqualify
    Not a fit; record the reason and stop.

    🤝 Handing Off to the AE

    • Pain: what they confirmed, in their words.
    • People: who attended and who else is involved.
    • Timeline: any dates and the agreed next step.
    • Framework notes: what you learned and what’s still unknown.
    📌
    Be honest about gaps. Telling the AE what you don’t know builds trust better than overselling.

    🗒️ Recording Disqualification

    • Use a picklist reason: no budget, no need, wrong industry, wrong size, competitor locked in.
    • Add a short note so others can learn from it.
    • Remove from active sequences promptly.
    • Revisit only if circumstances clearly change.
    💡
    Reasons improve targeting. Patterns in disqualification data show where your ICP or list-building needs fixing.

    🔧 Aligning with AEs

    SituationAction
    AE rejects many handoffsReview criteria together and update the checklist
    AE wants more detailAdd fields to the handoff note
    Disagreement on “qualified”Agree a written definition and revisit it quarterly
    📌
    Shared definitions prevent conflict. Most SDR–AE friction comes from unclear criteria.

    Advanced Playbook

    Spotting red flags, multi-threading, using the CRM, and measuring whether your qualification is working.

    🚩 Red Flags

    • No access to, or avoidance of, the decision maker.
    • Pain stated vaguely or not at all.
    • Timeline that keeps shifting.
    • Enthusiasm but no action or introductions.
    • Only “just researching” signals.
    ⚠️
    Enthusiasm isn’t qualification. A friendly contact with no pain, power or timeline is a pleasant chat, not a pipeline lead.

    🧵 Multi-Threading During Qualification

    Engage more than one stakeholder so qualification doesn’t hinge on a single person.

    • Ask your first contact to introduce a colleague.
    • Reach out to a second role with a tailored message.
    • Compare what different people say about the same problem.
    • Note differences in the CRM.

    🗄️ Capturing Qualification in the CRM

    • Update fields right after the call, not later.
    • Use the framework’s fields or a structured note.
    • Record the date so freshness is visible.
    • Link contacts to the right account and role.
    💡
    Structured notes help forecasting. Consistent fields let managers see where leads stand without asking.

    🧪 Champion Testing

    ✅ Real champion

    Acts on your behalf.

    • Shares internal information
• Arranges access
• Pushes for next steps

    ❌ Friendly contact

    Nice but passive.

    • Takes calls
• Offers no introductions
• Avoids commitment
    📌
    Test with a small ask. “Can you introduce me to your VP?” shows quickly whether they have influence and intent.

    📈 Measuring Qualification Quality

    MetricWhat It Tells You
    SQL acceptance rateWhether AEs agree with your qualification
    Meeting-to-opportunity conversionWhether meetings turn into real deals
    Opportunity win rate by sourceWhich lead types are worth targeting
    Disqualification reasonsWhere your ICP or lists need work
    💡
    High meetings with low opportunities means loose criteria. Tighten qualification before pushing more volume.

    Foundations

    What brand strategy is, its building blocks and vocabulary.

    What is brand strategy?

    Brand strategy is the long-term plan for building a brand that is chosen, trusted and paid a premium for. It sets who the brand is for, what it stands for, how it is structured and how it is differentiated. A brand is the sum of associations in customers' minds; a trademark is its legal shell; reputation is the brand's standing with all stakeholders.

    💡
    Strategy decides what to be; marketing communications decide how to say it. Confusing them produces logos without direction.

    The strategy stack

    LayerQuestionOutput
    Purpose & visionWhy exist; what future?Purpose statement
    Target & insightWho and what tension?Segments, insight
    PositioningWhat place in the mind?Positioning statement, POD/POP
    ArchitectureHow do brands relate?Portfolio map, naming rules
    Identity & expressionHow does it look and sound?Prism, essence, guidelines
    Equity & measurementIs it working?Tracker, valuation

    Key vocabulary

    • Brand equity: differential effect of brand knowledge on customer response.
    • Brand promise: the consistent benefit customers can rely on.
    • Brand identity vs image: intended vs perceived.
    • Master / sub / endorsed brand: levels in an architecture.
    • Mental availability: likelihood a brand is thought of in buying situations.

    Brand Architecture & Portfolio

    Choosing how brands relate, and managing the portfolio like an investment.

    Architecture models

    ModelMechanicsFits whenRisk
    Branded houseOne master brand, descriptorsShared trust drivers, similar customersContagion of failure
    House of brandsIndependent brandsContradictory positions, regulated or risky linesHigh cost, no shared equity
    EndorsedSub-brand plus parent sealNeed credibility with distinct identityParent exposure
    HybridMixed by segmentAcquisitive groupsComplexity without rules
    📌
    Aaker's brand relationship spectrum runs from branded house to house of brands, with sub-brands and endorsed brands in between: pick the point on the spectrum per business, not once for the company.

    Brand roles in the portfolio

    • Strategic brand: future growth bet, needs investment.
    • Flagship / power brand: carries most revenue and equity.
    • Flanker: defends the flagship's flank (price or segment).
    • Cash cow: harvested for margin.
    • Bridge: moves customers between tiers.

    Rationalization method

    1. Audit: revenue, margin, equity scores, overlap, cost-to-serve.
    2. Cluster by customer need to find overlaps.
    3. Assign roles and investment levels.
    4. Choose per brand: invest, merge, migrate, harvest, divest.
    5. Plan migration with dual naming and customer communication.
    6. Install a gate: new names must clear business-case and architecture rules.

    Practice

    Measure equity and loyalty per brand. Keep the stronger as the survivor, migrate the other with 'now part of' endorsement for a defined window, retain its best distinctive assets, and track churn during migration against a target threshold.

    Identity, Purpose & Values

    The foundation: why the brand exists and how its personality is expressed.

    Purpose, vision, mission, values

    ElementTest
    PurposeCould a competitor sincerely claim it? Does it change decisions?
    VisionIs it specific enough to imagine and measure progress?
    MissionWho, what, how, and for whom, today?
    ValuesObservable behaviours with trade-offs; 3-5 maximum
    ⚠️
    Purpose-washing: stating a purpose that operations contradict. Evidence in product, pricing and policy is mandatory.

    Identity frameworks

    Kapferer's Prism
    Physique, personality, culture, relationship, reflection, self-image.
    Archetypes
    Sage, Hero, Outlaw, Caregiver, Creator and others shape voice and story.
    Brand essence
    2-3 words the whole system must express.
    Golden Circle
    Why → how → what.

    Identity-image gap

    Run a perception study to compare intended identity (internal) with image (external). Close gaps by changing behaviour and product first, communication second.

    Gap = intended association strength - measured association strength
Action if gap > threshold: fix proof points before increasing media

    Segmentation, Targeting & Positioning

    Choosing where to compete and how to be distinct.

    STP in depth

    1. Segment by needs/jobs, occasions, value, behaviour (not demographics alone).
    2. Target using size, growth, margin, accessibility, competitive intensity and fit.
    3. Position versus a defined frame of reference.
    💡
    Broad-reach brands grow by penetration (Sharp), so target the category buyer broadly while still positioning with a clear difference.

    Statement, POD/POP and RTB

    For [target] who [need], [Brand] is the [frame] that [benefit]
because [reason to believe].
    TermDefinition
    POPTable-stakes associations
    PODStrong, favourable, unique, deliverable associations
    RTBProof: technology, data, heritage, endorsement

    Perceptual maps & repositioning

    Choose axes that reflect customer decision criteria, plot brands from survey data, and test white space for demand. Repositioning requires behaviour change plus proof across product, price, channel and communication; expect a multi-year horizon.

    Practice

    Quantify the gap, upgrade product and service proof points, restructure price and channel to match, then change communication. Track premium perception and price tolerance quarterly.

    Brand Equity & Valuation

    Frameworks for building and measuring brand value.

    Equity models

    ModelStructure
    Keller CBBESalience → performance/imagery → judgments/feelings → resonance
    AakerAwareness, perceived quality, associations, loyalty, proprietary assets
    Brand Asset ValuatorDifferentiation, relevance, esteem, knowledge
    Interbrand/BrandZFinancial: brand-attributed earnings × strength

    Measurement system

    • Mindset: awareness, consideration, preference, brand attribution.
    • Behaviour: penetration, loyalty, share of wallet.
    • Financial: price premium, margin, valuation.
    Price premium = (brand price - comparable unbranded price) / unbranded price

    Extensions & dilution

    Evaluate extension with fit, transfer and feedback. Line extensions risk cannibalization; category stretches risk dilution; co-brands share reputation risk and require contractual quality control.

    ⚠️
    Do not let revenue-hungry extensions override the core promise: each stretch should strengthen at least one core association.

    Cases & Architecture Governance

    Real scenarios and the processes that keep strategy intact.

    Real-world cases

    CaseLesson
    Marriott's tiered endorsementEndorsement scales trust while preserving distinct tiers
    Unilever's portfolio pruningConcentrating on fewer, bigger brands lifts growth and margin
    Apple's branded houseOne master brand with descriptors enables shared premium equity
    Gap logo reversalChanging identity without audience buy-in can destroy goodwill fast

    Governance process

    1. Brand council with executive sponsor.
    2. Naming and architecture rulebook.
    3. Gate reviews for launches, extensions, partnerships.
    4. Annual portfolio review with equity data.
    5. Exceptions log with sunset dates.

    Crisis & architecture

    Pre-define firewalls: which brands share the master name, and the playbook for decoupling (dropping an endorsement) under reputational crisis.

    Roadmap, Metrics & Pitfalls

    Turning strategy into a multi-year program with measurable outcomes.

    Brand strategy roadmap

    PhaseActivitiesOutput
    Diagnose (0-3 mo)Audit, tracker baseline, stakeholder interviewsStrategic diagnosis
    Define (3-6 mo)Purpose, segments, positioning, architectureStrategy book
    Activate (6-18 mo)Guidelines, rollout, internal brand trainingConsistent execution
    Govern (ongoing)Tracker, council, portfolio reviewEquity growth

    Metrics to report

    • Awareness, consideration, and brand attribution of advertising.
    • Price premium and share of category buyers.
    • Internal brand alignment score (employees can state the positioning).

    Common pitfalls

    ⚠️
    Generic positioning, over-claiming purpose, architecture sprawl, inconsistent execution, and measuring only awareness.

    Practice

    Separate mental from physical availability: check consideration and distribution/pricing barriers, then relevance of the point of difference. Awareness without relevance or access does not convert.

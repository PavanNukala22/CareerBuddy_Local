    AMCAT

    An employability assessment that turns your aptitude and skills into a profile recruiters can shortlist.

    What is AMCAT?

    The Aspiring Minds Computer Adaptive Test (AMCAT) is an employability assessment used by hundreds of companies across India to evaluate candidates on aptitude, reasoning, language, and domain-specific skills. It is one of the most widely accepted screening tests for entry-level corporate hiring.

    Think of AMCAT less as a pass/fail exam and more as a standardized skill scorecard. Instead of every company running its own aptitude round, they rely on your AMCAT profile to decide who moves forward to interviews.

    💡
    Key Concept — "Employability Assessment"
    AMCAT does not just measure math. It measures multiple dimensions of employability — cognitive ability, language, technical domain knowledge, and behavioral traits — and maps them to what employers look for.

    Who is it for?

    AMCAT is designed primarily for people at the start of their careers who are looking for entry-level corporate jobs, often through off-campus recruitment.

    The Three Core Aptitude Sections

    These three sections are common to almost every AMCAT test and carry high preparation priority. Master them first.

    📚

    English Language

    Grammar, vocabulary, and reading comprehension — synonyms/antonyms, sentence correction, contextual usage.

    🧮

    Quantitative Ability

    Numerical problem-solving — time/speed/distance, percentages, ratios, permutation & combination.

    🧩

    Logical Reasoning

    Pattern recognition and deductive logic — coding-decoding, blood relations, syllogisms.

    A — English Language

    Tests grammar, vocabulary, and reading comprehension.

    • Common mistake: Relying on how a sentence "sounds" rather than strict grammar rules.
    • Quick tip: Read business articles daily to improve comprehension speed.

    B — Quantitative Ability

    Tests numerical problem-solving and basic math.

    • Concept to example: Work done = Rate × Time.
    • Common trap: Memorizing shortcuts without understanding the fundamental algebraic concepts.

    C — Logical Reasoning

    Tests pattern recognition and deductive logic.

    • Key difference: Knowing a rule (Quant) vs. recognizing an abstract pattern (Logic).
    • Quick tip: Use Venn diagrams for syllogisms to avoid mental confusion.

    Diagnosing Verbal Errors

    The English module trips up "fluent" speakers most. Learn to recognize the error pattern behind each mistake.

    Error TypeExampleWhy It HappensCorrective Action
    Subject-Verb "The box of chocolates are missing." Matching the verb to the nearest noun (chocolates) instead of the true subject (box). Identify the core subject before choosing the verb.
    Vocabulary Confusing "Elicit" (draw out) vs "Illicit" (illegal). Memorizing words without understanding usage in a sentence. Learn root words and practice contextual cloze tests.
    Reading Choosing an answer based on outside world knowledge. Failing to restrict conclusions strictly to the passage. If it isn't in the passage, it isn't the right answer.

    Computer Adaptive Testing (CAT)

    AMCAT uses Computer Adaptive Testing. In an adaptive test, the difficulty of your next question depends on whether you answered the previous question correctly. Guessing blindly can lower your ability estimate rapidly.

    Figure — How the Adaptive Engine Adjusts Difficulty
    ✅
    Accuracy over speed: Because difficulty adapts to you, a careful correct answer is worth more than a fast guess. Random guessing can pull your estimate down quickly.

    Domain & Specialist Modules

    Where you prove you can actually do the job — technical domains, coding, spoken English, and personality.

    Domain Modules

    Domain modules are tests specific to your graduation degree — for example Computer Programming, Mechanical Engineering, or Marketing. They matter because companies filter candidates based on relevant technical knowledge.

    🎯
    Why they matter: Two candidates can have identical aptitude scores. The domain module is often what separates a shortlist from a rejection for role-specific hiring.

    AMPI — Personality Inventory

    The Aspiring Minds Personality Inventory (AMPI) assesses workplace behavioral traits like Conscientiousness, Extraversion, and Agreeableness.

    ⚠️
    Answer honestly: Extreme attempts to "fake" an ideal profile can trigger inconsistency flags. Consistency across similar questions is what the inventory checks for.

    Automata — Coding Assessment

    Automata evaluates logical coding, algorithmic thinking, and a debugging mindset. It is not just about writing code, but about writing optimal, error-free code that handles edge cases.

    🧠

    Concept

    Correctness plus optimality — your code must handle hidden and edge-case test inputs, not just the obvious ones.

    ⌨️

    Practice Strategy

    Practice on platforms with hidden test cases. Focus on arrays, strings, basic data structures, and loops.

    # A pattern that shows edge-case awareness
def second_largest(nums):
    if len(nums) < 2:
        return None          # edge case: too few elements
    first = second = float('-inf')
    for n in nums:
        if n > first:
            second, first = first, n
        elif first > n > second:
            second = n
    return second if second != float('-inf') else None

    SVAR — Spoken English

    SVAR is an automated voice assessment evaluating pronunciation, fluency, and spoken comprehension. A machine scores your speech, so clarity matters more than accent.

    • Common mistake: Speaking too fast or mumbling.
    • Preparation: Practice reading passages aloud clearly. Take the test in a quiet room with a good-quality microphone.
    📘
    Setup checklist: Quiet room · tested microphone · steady internet · speak at a natural, even pace.

    Preparation Strategy

    A structured path from a baseline diagnostic to a polished, exam-ready profile.

    The 8-Phase Framework

    Prepare in phases rather than randomly practicing. Each phase builds on the one before it.

    Phase 1 — Diagnose

    Take a baseline test to see where you actually stand.

    Phase 2 — Identify

    Analyze accuracy and error patterns, not just scores.

    Phase 3 — Build

    Learn fundamentals before reaching for shortcuts.

    Phase 4 — Practice

    Solve topic-wise to consolidate each concept.

    Phase 5 — Speed

    Introduce time pressure once fundamentals are solid.

    Phase 6 — Mocks

    Simulate real exam conditions end-to-end.

    Phase 7 — Review

    Maintain an error notebook and revisit mistakes.

    Phase 8 — Polish

    High-impact final revision before the test.

    30-Day Quick Plan

    A compact plan when you have roughly a month. Adjust the ratios to your weakest section.

    1️⃣

    Days 1–7 · Foundation

    Diagnostic test, number systems, grammar basics, logic introductions.

    2️⃣

    Days 8–15 · Topic Practice

    Percentages, algebra, vocabulary, coding-decoding.

    3️⃣

    Days 16–22 · Timed Practice

    Domain topics and reading comprehension under strict time limits.

    4️⃣

    Days 23–30 · Mocks & Revision

    Full mock exams and error-analysis review.

    ✅
    Rule of thumb: Spend the first quarter of your prep window heavily on your weakest section, then blend all sections as the exam approaches.

    Common Preparation Mistakes

    MistakeWhy It HurtsBetter Approach
    Ignoring English because "I speak it well" Spoken fluency ≠ grammatical accuracy or vocabulary depth. Study formal grammar rules and practice RC passages systematically.
    Practicing without a timer A puzzle you solve in 10 minutes at home must be done in 1–2 minutes in the exam. Always practice with a countdown timer after the learning phase.
    Assuming one score guarantees placement Leads to neglecting interview prep and resume building. Treat AMCAT as the first door; prepare for domain interviews in parallel.
    🎓
    Study tip: Keep an "error notebook." Every wrong answer gets one line — the mistake, the correct concept, and how to avoid repeating it. Review it before every mock.

    Scoring & Career

    How your score is read, how recruiters use it, and what happens after the test.

    Percentile vs Percentage

    Percentage is simply (Marks Obtained ÷ Total Marks) × 100. Percentile indicates the percentage of candidates who scored lower than you.

    📊
    Example: A 90th percentile means you performed better than 90% of candidates. Because of adaptive difficulty and scoring algorithms, your percentile depends on how the entire testing population performed — not just your raw marks.

    How Companies Use Your Results

    An AMCAT score is not an automatic job offer. It is a screening tool. You must still clear technical interviews, HR rounds, and meet company-specific eligibility criteria.

    2. Choose the synonym of "Abundant".

    3. A is taller than B, and B is taller than C. Who is the shortest?

    4. Simplify: 12 + 8 × 2 = ?

    5. Fill in the blank: "She ___ to the market every day."

    6. Complete the series: 2, 4, 6, 8, ?

    7. What is 7 × 8?

    8. Choose the antonym of "Increase".

    9. Which number comes next: 5, 10, 15, 20, ?

    10. Convert 3/4 to a percentage.

    11. Which of these words is spelled correctly?

    12. Pointing to a photo, Raj said, "She is the daughter of my mother's only son." How is the girl related to Raj (assume Raj is male)?

    13. What is the square root of 144?

    14. Choose the correct plural of "Child".

    15. A dozen eggs cost ₹60. What is the cost of one egg?

    16. Choose the correct meaning of the idiom "Break the ice".

    17. Which shape completes the pattern: Triangle, Square, Pentagon, ?

    18. If a shirt costs ₹500 and is discounted by 10%, what is the sale price?

    19. Choose the correct article: "___ apple a day keeps the doctor away."

    20. Find the missing number: 3, 6, 9, 12, ?

    Intermediate Level

    A step up — multi-step calculations, coded logic, and applied grammar.

    1. A train travels 360 km in 4 hours. What is its speed in km/hr?

    2. Which sentence is correctly punctuated?

    3. In a certain code, "CAT" is written as "DBU" (each letter shifted forward by one). How is "DOG" written in the same code?

    4. The ratio of boys to girls in a class is 3:2. If there are 30 students total, how many are boys?

    5. Choose the correctly spelled word.

    6. Find the odd one out.

    7. A can complete a work in 12 days and B in 18 days. Working together, how many days will they take?

    8. Choose the correct passive voice of: "The teacher teaches the students."

    9. In a row of children, Raj is 7th from the left and 12th from the right. How many children are in the row?

    10. The average of five numbers is 20. If one number is excluded, the average of the remaining four becomes 18. What is the excluded number?

    11. Choose the word opposite in meaning to "Reluctant".

    12. Statement: "All cats are animals. Some animals are pets." Conclusion: "Some cats are pets." Is this valid?

    13. A sum of money doubles itself in 8 years at simple interest. What is the rate of interest per annum?

    14. Choose the correctly ordered sentence.

    15. Which word does NOT belong with the others?

    16. A man buys an article for ₹800 and sells it for ₹960. What is his profit percentage?

    17. Identify the correct comparative form: "This book is ___ than that one."

    18. Complete the analogy: Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ?

    19. If the perimeter of a square is 48 cm, what is its area?

    20. Five friends sit in a row. A is to the left of B but to the right of C. D is to the right of B. E is at one end. Which of the following is a possible left-to-right order?

    Advanced Level

    Higher-difficulty questions closer to real AMCAT patterns, including a CS/domain item.

    1. A shopkeeper marks an item 40% above cost price and then gives a 15% discount on the marked price. What is his overall profit percentage?

    2. Which sentence shows correct subject-verb agreement?

    3. Statement: "All programmers are logical thinkers. Some logical thinkers are creative." Conclusion: "Some programmers are creative." Is this conclusion valid?

    4. What is the time complexity of binary search on a sorted array of n elements?

    5. What is the compound interest on ₹10,000 at 10% per annum for 2 years, compounded annually?

    6. Data sufficiency: "Is x > 0?" (1) x² > 0 (2) x³ > 0. Which statement(s) alone are sufficient?

    7. Two pipes A and B can fill a tank in 20 and 30 minutes respectively. A third pipe C can empty it in 15 minutes. If all three are opened together, how long will it take to fill the tank?

    8. Choose the sentence with the correct use of the subjunctive mood.

    9. Statement: "All keys are locks. No lock is a door." Which conclusion follows?

    10. Which sorting algorithm has the best average-case time complexity?

    11. A works twice as fast as B. Working together, they complete a job in 12 days. How many days would A alone take?

    12. Choose the sentence demonstrating correct parallel structure.

    13. Data sufficiency: "Is n an even number?" (1) n is divisible by 4. (2) n² is even. Which statement(s) alone are sufficient?

    14. In object-oriented programming, which concept allows a subclass to provide a specific implementation of a method already defined in its superclass?

    15. A boat's speed in still water is 15 km/hr and the speed of the stream is 5 km/hr. What is the time taken to travel 40 km downstream?

    16. Which sentence correctly uses "affect" vs. "effect"?

    17. Statement: "If it rains, the match will be cancelled. It did not rain." Conclusion: "The match was not cancelled." Is this a valid logical deduction?

    18. What is the space complexity of an algorithm that uses a fixed number of variables regardless of input size?

    19. The LCM of two numbers is 180 and their HCF is 6. If one number is 30, what is the other number?

    20. Five people P, Q, R, S, T are ranked by score. T scored higher than R, R scored higher than P, P scored higher than Q, and S scored the lowest. What is the order from highest to lowest?

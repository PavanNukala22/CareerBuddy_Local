    What is App Store Operations?

    Start from zero: what store operations are, the types of stores and distribution, accounts, identifiers and the release pipeline.

    🧭 What is App Store Operations?

    App Store Operations (often shortened to App Store Ops or Release Ops) is everything that happens after the code works on your machine and before the app reaches users: creating the app listing, signing the build so the store trusts it, uploading it, testing it with beta users, passing store review, releasing it gradually and watching it in production.

    🔴 Without App Store Ops skills

    Builds are signed on one laptop. A certificate expires on a Friday and nobody can ship. Metadata is rejected three times. A bad release goes to 100% of users with no way to pause it.

    🟢 With App Store Ops skills

    Signing is automated and documented. Releases follow a checklist and a calendar. Rollouts are staged, monitored and can be halted. Rejections are rare and fixed fast.

    Think of it as the logistics department of mobile development: developers build the product, App Store Ops gets it safely onto millions of phones.

    🔄 The App Release Lifecycle

    Every release, no matter how small, travels through the same pipeline. Most delays happen at the boxes where people forget to plan.

    The release pipeline: each stage has its own owner, tools and failure modes.
    1. Code and freeze: a release branch is cut and only bug fixes are allowed.
    2. Sign: the build is signed with the correct certificate or keystore.
    3. Build: an .ipa (iOS) or .aab (Android) is produced, ideally by CI.
    4. Upload: the build is pushed to App Store Connect or Play Console and processed.
    5. Test: internal, then external testers; the pre-launch report is checked.
    6. Review: store review checks policies, metadata and behaviour.
    7. Release: manual, automatic or scheduled; staged by percentage.
    8. Monitor: crash-free rate, ANR rate, ratings and support tickets decide whether to continue, pause or hotfix.

    🍎 What is App Store Connect?

    App Store Connect (ASC) is Apple's web portal, mobile app and REST API for everything about your apps after the code is written: app records, builds, TestFlight, metadata, pricing, in-app purchases, review submissions, analytics and payments.

    App Privacy ("nutrition labels")

    You must declare which data your app (and any third-party SDK inside it) collects, whether it is linked to the user, and whether it is used for tracking. Categories include contact info, identifiers, usage data, location, purchases and diagnostics.

    ⚠️
    Third-party SDKs count. If your analytics or ads SDK collects the advertising ID, you must declare it. Also include any required Privacy Manifest files and required-reason API declarations that Apple expects for your SDKs.

    Age rating, categories and availability

    • Answer the age-rating questionnaire honestly; Apple derives the rating (4+, 9+, 13+, 16+, 18+).
    • Choose a primary and optional secondary category; it affects ranking and browsing.
    • Choose countries and regions; some need extra documents (for example, local regulatory proof).
    • Set pricing by tier or custom price per storefront; plan pre-orders if you want pre-launch demand.

    🧪 TestFlight

    TestFlight lets you distribute pre-release builds to testers on real devices before App Review of the final version.

    👥 Internal testing

    Up to 100 members of your App Store Connect team. No Beta App Review. Builds appear within minutes after processing. Best for QA and developers.

    🌍 External testing

    Up to 10,000 testers via email or a public link. The first build of each version needs Beta App Review. Best for beta customers and early access.
    • Builds expire after 90 days; upload a new one to keep testers going.
    • Use groups (e.g. QA, Beta customers, Partners) and attach different builds per group.
    • Testers send screenshots, feedback and crash reports from inside TestFlight.
    • A public link with a tester limit lets you invite people without collecting emails.
    • Export compliance (encryption) questions appear on each build; set ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption in Info.plist to skip the prompt.
    Example: a safe beta flow

    Build 2.4.0 (188) → internal group (QA, 1 day) → external group "Beta Customers" (200 testers, 3 days, Beta App Review passes) → promote the same build to App Review. Because the build is identical, what you tested is what ships.

    🔍 App Review and Rejections

    Every app and update is checked against the App Store Review Guidelines. Reviews are mostly 24–48 hours, but plan for longer around holidays and WWDC.

    Typical status flow in App Store Connect. A rejection arrives in the Resolution Center with the guideline number.
    Guideline familyWhat Apple checksCommon rejection
    1. SafetyObjectionable content, user-generated content moderation, kidsNo report / block features in UGC apps
    2. PerformanceCrashes, bugs, completeness, accurate metadata2.1 App Completeness (crash, placeholder content, no demo login)
    3. BusinessIn-app purchase, subscriptions, ads3.1.1 Digital goods sold outside IAP
    4. DesignMinimum functionality, copycat, spam4.3 Spam / duplicate apps; 4.2 Minimum functionality
    5. LegalPrivacy, data, IP, regulated activity5.1.1 Missing privacy policy or account deletion

    How to avoid common rejections

    • Provide a demo account and clear review notes if login is required.
    • Test on a real device with the exact release build, not only the simulator.
    • Remove placeholder text, test URLs and beta wording.
    • If you allow account creation, provide in-app account deletion.
    • Use Apple's IAP for digital goods; explain any permitted external payment flow in review notes.
    • Explain every permission string (camera, location) with a clear, honest purpose.
    • Do not mention other platforms or use trademarks you do not own.

    When you get rejected

    1. Read the Resolution Center message and note the guideline number.
    2. Reply in the Resolution Center with questions or a clear explanation if you disagree.
    3. Fix the issue, upload a new build if code changed, or update metadata only.
    4. Resubmit. If you believe the decision is wrong, use Appeal to the App Review Board.
    5. For critical bugs, request an Expedited Review with a short justification.

    🚀 Release and Staged Rollout

    1. Create a release on a track, upload the .aab, add release notes per language.
    2. Review warnings in Pre-launch report (Google runs your app on real devices and reports crashes, accessibility and security issues).
    3. Choose a rollout percentage for production, for example 5%.
    4. Send changes for review; reviews may take from a few hours to several days.
    5. Increase the percentage (20%, 50%, 100%) while watching Android vitals.
    6. Halt rollout immediately if crashes spike. Halting stops new users from receiving the version; it does not uninstall it for those who already have it.
    OptionMeaning
    Managed publishingApproved changes wait until you press Publish
    Staged rolloutRelease to a % of users and increase over time
    Release notesPer-language text up to 500 characters in the release screen
    CountriesChoose where the release is available
    💡

    Module 04 · Code Signing

    Code Signing & Certificates

    Understand how signing works on iOS and Android, create and rotate certificates and keystores, and fix signing errors fast.

    🔏 What is Code Signing?

    Code signing attaches a digital signature to your app. The signature proves who built it (authenticity) and that nothing was changed after signing (integrity). Phones and stores refuse to install or accept apps with a missing or invalid signature.

    Public-key cryptography in 60 seconds

    • A key pair has a private key (secret, stays with you) and a public key (shared).
    • You sign the app with the private key; anyone can verify it with the public key.
    • A certificate binds your public key to your identity and is itself signed by a trusted authority (Apple, or Google for Play).
    • A CSR (Certificate Signing Request) is the file you send to a certificate authority to request a certificate for your public key.
    • If the private key is lost, you cannot sign updates; if it leaks, others can sign as you.

    🍎 iOS model

    Apple is the certificate authority. You request certificates and profiles from Apple, and the device checks the chain back to Apple's root.

    🤖 Android model

    You generate and own the key. With Play App Signing, Google holds the final app signing key and you keep only an upload key.

    🛠️ iOS Signing Step by Step

    Manual: create and install a distribution certificate

    1. Open Keychain Access → Certificate Assistant → Request a Certificate From a Certificate Authority; save the .certSigningRequest to disk.
    2. In the Developer portal, Certificates → + → Apple Distribution, upload the CSR and download the .cer.
    3. Double-click the .cer to add it to Keychain; it pairs with the private key that was created with the CSR.
    4. Create an App Store provisioning profile linked to your App ID and that certificate; download and double-click it.
    5. In Xcode, set the target's Signing & Capabilities to use that profile, or enable automatic signing.

    🔑 Entitlements, Devices and API Keys

    Entitlements and capabilities

    Special features (Push Notifications, Sign in with Apple, iCloud, Associated Domains, HealthKit, Apple Pay) must be enabled on the App ID and appear in the entitlements file. If the profile and the entitlements disagree, the build fails to sign or is rejected on upload.

    Registering test devices

    • Collect the device UDID (Finder or the Xcode Devices window).
    • Register it in Certificates, IDs & Profiles → Devices, then regenerate the Development and Ad Hoc profiles.
    • Device slots are limited per year and per device type; removing a device frees the slot only at renewal.

    App Store Connect API keys

    Automation (CI, fastlane) authenticates to Apple with an API key: a .p8 private key, a Key ID and an Issuer ID. You can download the .p8 only once, so store it securely. Give it the least role needed (for example App Manager).

    🗝️ Android Keystores and Gradle

    Keystores, keys and aliases

    • A keystore (.jks or .p12) is a password-protected file that holds one or more key entries, each with an alias.
    • Each key entry has its own private key and certificate; the certificate usually is self-signed.
    • The SHA-256 fingerprint of the certificate identifies your signing key and is used by Firebase, Google Sign-In and App Links.
    bash# Generate an upload keystore (valid ~27 years)
keytool -genkeypair -v \
  -keystore upload-keystore.jks \
  -alias upload \
  -keyalg RSA -keysize 2048 -validity 10000

# List the contents and fingerprints
keytool -list -v -keystore upload-keystore.jks -alias upload

# Verify a signed APK
apksigner verify --verbose --print-certs app-release.apk

    Signing in Gradle without hard-coding secrets

    kotlin// app/build.gradle.kts

    val f = rootProject.file("keystore.properties")   // NOT committed to git
    if (f.exists()) load(f.inputStream())
}

        create("release") {
            storeFile = file(props["storeFile"] as String)
            storePassword = System.getenv("STORE_PASSWORD") ?: props["storePassword"] as String
            keyAlias = props["keyAlias"] as String

    Release Management & Rollouts

    Versioning, release trains, checklists, staged rollouts, monitoring, feature flags and hotfixes.

    🗓️ What is Release Management?

    Release management is the discipline of deciding what ships, when, to whom, and how to undo it. Mobile releases are special: you cannot instantly roll back, users update slowly, and every release passes store review.

    🧮
    Versioning
    A clear scheme so everyone knows what is in each build.
    📅
    Cadence
    Fixed release trains (e.g. every 2 weeks) instead of ad hoc launches.
    🎚️
    Gradual rollout
    Expose new code to a small share of users first.
    🛟
    Recovery
    Kill switches, hotfixes and rollback plans ready before launch.

    🔢 Versioning Strategy

    PartExampleRule
    Marketing version2.4.0Semantic: MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH. MAJOR for big or breaking changes, MINOR for features, PATCH for fixes
    Build number187Always increases; never reused; generated by CI from a counter or commit count
    Android versionCode20400187Integer that only goes up; some teams encode version into it (2·4·0·187)

    Example: a compact versionCode scheme

    Use major*1,000,000 + minor*10,000 + patch*100 + hotfix. Version 2.4.0 with no hotfix gives 2,040,000; hotfix 2.4.1 gives 2,040,100. Android allows versionCode up to 2,100,000,000, so leave room to grow.

    🛟 Flags, Kill Switches and Hotfixes

    ToolWhat it gives youWhy mobile needs it
    Feature flagsTurn features on or off per user group without a new releaseDecouples deploy from release
    Remote configChange values (limits, URLs, text) from a dashboardFix problems without store review
    Kill switchDisable a risky feature or force-upgrade a broken versionYour fastest rollback

    Staged exposure by cohortEmployees, then 1%, then everyoneSafe testing in production

    Hotfix procedure

    1. Confirm severity: data loss, security, payments or mass crashes justify a hotfix.
    2. Branch from the shipped tag (hotfix/2.4.1), apply the minimal fix.
    3. Increase patch version and build number; run a focused regression pass.
    4. Submit; on iOS request Expedited Review with a short justification. On Android release to a small % and then raise.
    5. Use a feature flag or kill switch as the bridge while the store review runs.
    6. Merge the fix back into main and the next release branch; write a post-incident review.
    📌
    You can not roll an iOS app back to an earlier binary in the App Store. You can remove a version from sale or submit a new fixed version. On Google Play you can halt a rollout, but you must still ship a higher versionCode to replace a bad build.

    ✍️ Writing Release Notes

    Release notes tell users and testers what changed. Good notes are specific, honest and short.

    ❌ Weak

    "Bug fixes and performance improvements."

    ✅ Strong

    "Faster checkout: saved cards now load instantly. Fixed a crash when opening order history on Android 12. New: dark mode for the Orders tab."
    • Write for users, not engineers: benefit first, no ticket numbers.
    • Localise What's New for your top markets.
    • Keep an internal changelog with ticket links for QA and support.
    • Do not promise features that are not live yet.

    🔐 Shared Signing with match

    fastlane match stores one set of certificates and profiles, encrypted, in a private git repo (or S3 / GCS). Every machine and CI job installs the same identity, which ends the "works on my machine" signing problems.

    bash# One-time setup
bundle exec fastlane match init

# Create or renew (run by a trusted maintainer only)
bundle exec fastlane match appstore
bundle exec fastlane match development

# On CI and developer machines: read-only
bundle exec fastlane match appstore --readonly
    • Protect the encryption passphrase (MATCH_PASSWORD) in your CI secret store.
    • Use readonly: true on CI so a pipeline can never create extra certificates.
    • Use one repo per team, with access limited to people who release.
    • Rotate with match nuke only when you understand the impact; it revokes certificates.

    🩺 Best Practices and Troubleshooting

    • Cache Gradle, CocoaPods and SwiftPM dependencies to shorten builds.
    • Fail early: lint and unit tests run before the expensive signing and upload stages.
    • Upload symbols (dSYMs and ProGuard/R8 mapping files) to your crash tool; otherwise crashes are unreadable.
    • Parallelise iOS and Android jobs.
    • Idempotent lanes: re-running a failed job should be safe.
    • Pin versions of Xcode, Java, Ruby and fastlane to avoid surprise breaks.
    • Version the pipeline in the same repository as the app.
    • Notify: send Slack / Teams messages with build number, track and link to the console.

    🔎 App Store Optimization (ASO)

    App Store Optimization is SEO for app stores. It affects visibility (search and browse ranking) and conversion (the share of page views that become installs).

    LeveriOSAndroid
    TitleName (30) + subtitle (30), both indexedTitle (30), indexed with high weight

    ExperimentsProduct Page Optimization (up to 3 treatments)Store listing experiments
    Campaign pagesCustom Product PagesCustom store listings
    RatingsStrong factor; prompt at success momentsStrong factor; reply to every review
    LocalizationLocalise text and screenshots per storefrontLocalise per language; use machine help but human review

    A simple ASO workflow

    1. Research keywords with a tool and your own search data; note volume, difficulty and relevance.
    2. Hypothesis: "Benefit-led screenshots will increase install rate by 5%."
    3. Experiment: run an A/B test for 2–4 weeks, one change at a time.
    4. Measure impressions, product page views, conversion and downloads.
    5. Rollout winners, repeat every quarter.
    ⚠️
    Never buy fake reviews, use competitor names in keywords or add misleading claims. Both stores remove listings and ban accounts for review manipulation and keyword stuffing.

    📡 Scaling Multi-App Operations

    Large apps, SDK providers and agencies manage many apps and many stores. Typical expert-level concerns:

    • Multiple apps and white-label builds: one codebase, many bundle IDs and listings; automate metadata and signing per brand.
    • Bundle variants: separate Application IDs for debug, staging and production so they can coexist on one phone.
    • Regional releases: stagger by country (Play country targeting; iOS availability) and monitor local issues first.
    • Device and OS coverage: use Firebase Test Lab, AWS Device Farm or BrowserStack for matrices.
    • Dependency risk: track SDK updates, privacy manifests and deprecations; test beta Xcode and Android versions each summer.
    • Release observability: tag crash reports and analytics by version and build; maintain dashboards per release.
    • Compliance evidence: keep screenshots of declarations, review notes and policy approvals for audits.
    • Ownership: if an agency built the app, make sure your company, not the agency, owns the developer account, keys and listing.

    📱 Stores and Basics

    What is App Store Connect and what do you use it for?
    Apple's portal and API for managing apps: app records, metadata, builds, TestFlight, pricing, App Review submissions, analytics and payments. Certificates and profiles are on the separate Developer portal.
    What is the difference between TestFlight internal and external testing?
    Internal: up to 100 team members, no Beta App Review, fast. External: up to 10,000 testers, the first build of each version needs Beta App Review. Builds expire after 90 days.
    What is Google Play Console and what are tracks?
    Google's portal for publishing Android apps. Tracks are release channels: internal, closed, open and production, so one tested bundle can be promoted step by step.
    APK vs AAB?
    APK is an installable package. AAB is a publishing format; Google Play generates optimised APKs per device, giving smaller downloads. New Play apps require AAB.
    Which identifiers can never change after publishing?
    The iOS Bundle ID and the Android Application ID. Changing them means publishing a new app.
    What is the difference between version and build number?
    The version (e.g. 2.4.0) is user-facing. The build number (iOS) or versionCode (Android) is an internal counter that must always increase, even when the version stays the same.

    🔏 Signing and Certificates

    Explain code signing in simple terms.
    You sign the app with a private key. Devices and stores verify the signature with the public key in a certificate, proving who built the app and that it was not modified.
    What is a provisioning profile?
    An Apple file that ties together an App ID, one or more certificates, entitlements and (for development and Ad Hoc) allowed devices. App Store profiles contain no devices.
    What is Play App Signing?
    Google stores the real app signing key and re-signs your releases. You keep only an upload key that can be reset if lost or leaked. It is required for new apps.
    How do you share iOS signing assets across a team and CI?
    Use fastlane match (encrypted git or cloud storage) or Xcode cloud signing, read-only on CI, with the passphrase in a secrets manager.
    What do you do when a distribution certificate expires on release day?
    Create or renew the certificate, regenerate the App Store profile, update CI secrets, run a test build, then add calendar reminders and a signing-health check to prevent a repeat.
    Where do you store keystores and API keys?
    Never in the repository. Use a secrets manager or CI secret store, keep encrypted backups in two places, restrict access and rotate on schedule or after any leak.

    🚀 Releases and Rollouts

    How do you decide whether to roll back or hotfix a bad release?
    Mobile cannot roll back instantly. First contain with a feature flag, kill switch or halted rollout; then ship a hotfix from the release tag with a higher build number, requesting expedited review on iOS if needed.
    How does iOS phased release differ from Android staged rollout?
    iOS: fixed 7-day automatic-update schedule (1% to 100%), pausable for up to 30 days; manual updaters still get it. Android: you choose and change percentages freely and can halt.
    What are your stop conditions for a rollout?
    Pre-agreed thresholds such as crash-free users falling more than 0.3 points below baseline, ANR above the limit, drop in key funnel success or a surge of similar 1★ reviews and tickets.
    How do you handle an App Review rejection?
    Read the guideline in the Resolution Center, reply or fix, resubmit, appeal if needed, and request expedited review only for critical cases. Then update the checklist.
    What are the most common causes of rejection?
    Crashes or incomplete features, missing demo account, privacy policy or label mismatches, digital goods outside IAP, missing account deletion, misleading metadata, and spam or duplicate apps.
    How do you keep the backend compatible with older app versions?
    Version APIs, make changes additive, support the last few app versions and enforce a minimum supported version with a force-update screen when needed.

    🏗️ Automation, Governance and Growth

    How would you automate releases end to end?
    CI builds on merge, runs tests, signs with match or secure keystore, uploads to TestFlight and Play internal, notifies the team, and a protected approval step promotes to production with a low initial rollout.
    What is fastlane and name some actions.
    An open-source automation tool. Key actions: match, gym/build_app, pilot/upload_to_testflight, deliver, supply/upload_to_play_store, increment_build_number, snapshot.
    How do you protect credentials in CI?
    Secret stores, masked logs, least-privilege API keys and service accounts, protected environments with reviewers, rotation, and restricted workflow editing.
    How do you design governance for developer accounts?
    Company-owned accounts on role mailboxes, individual least-privilege users, expiry calendar, documented ownership, quarterly access review, and off-boarding.
    Your Android keystore leaked. What now?
    If Play App Signing is on, request an upload key reset in Play Console, rotate CI secrets, audit recent uploads and communicate internally. Without Play App Signing, you cannot rotate easily, which is why it is mandatory.
    How would you improve an app's conversion on the store?
    Research keywords, improve title and subtitle, run Product Page Optimization / store listing experiments on icon and screenshots, localise, collect ratings at happy moments and reply to reviews.

    Module 01

    PMO Analyst Core Skills: Foundations

    What a PMO does, the vocabulary you need and how the six core skills fit together.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    A PMO Analyst turns scattered project information into one trusted picture. You govern the portfolio, measure delivery with KPIs, consolidate status and risks, audit process compliance and forecast capacity — so leaders can decide early instead of reacting late.

    🧭
    Governance
    Stage gates, decision rights and portfolio control.
    📊
    KPIs & metrics
    Measure schedule, cost, value and quality.
    📑
    Status roll-up
    Consolidate RAG reports into one view.
    🔍
    Audit & compliance
    Check that process is followed, with evidence.
    👥
    Capacity planning
    Match demand to the people available.
    ⚠️
    Risk consolidation
    One portfolio risk picture with owners.
    💡
    The PMO analyst’s real product is trust in the numbers. A late but correct report beats a fast, wrong one.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Explain portfolio, program and project levels and apply stage-gate governance.
    2. Define, calculate and present KPIs such as SPI, CPI and on-time delivery.
    3. Consolidate project status reports into a validated, RAG-based portfolio view.
    4. Plan and run a process audit, rate findings and track corrective actions.
    5. Calculate utilisation and build a demand-versus-capacity forecast.
    6. Merge project risk logs into one scored, de-duplicated portfolio register.
    7. Diagnose common PMO reporting failures and propose fixes.

    Module 02

    Portfolio & Project Governance

    Portfolio, program and project levels, stage gates, decision rights and transparent prioritisation.

    🏢 Basic: Portfolio, Program and Project

    LevelWhat it isTypical question it answers
    PortfolioAll initiatives selected to deliver strategyAre we investing in the right things?
    ProgramRelated projects managed together for combined benefitAre the benefits being delivered together?
    ProjectTemporary effort with a defined outputWill this deliver on time, cost and scope?
    💡
    Think of it as: portfolio = choose, program = coordinate, project = deliver.

    🚦 Intermediate: Stage Gates and Decision Rights

    A governance framework defines who decides what, and when. Gates sit between phases; each has entry criteria, a decision-maker and an outcome.

    Figure 2.1 · A simple stage-gate lifecycle
    GateTypical evidenceDecision maker
    G1 Approve to planBusiness case, sponsor namedSteering committee
    G2 Approve to buildBaseline plan, budget, risksSteering committee
    G3 Approve to go liveTest results, readiness checklistSponsor
    G4 CloseLessons learned, handoverSponsor and PMO

    RACI example

    • Sponsor — A for benefits
    • Project manager — R for delivery
    • PMO — C on standards
    • Steering committee — I on status

    Decision rule

    • One accountable person per decision
    • Escalation path written down
    • Gate outcomes recorded

    ⚖️ Intermediate: Prioritising and Scoring the Portfolio

    Use a weighted scoring model so prioritisation is transparent. Agree criteria and weights with leadership before scoring.

    ProjectValue (40%)Risk (30%)Cost (30%)Weighted score
    Alpha8656.5
    Beta6876.9
    Gamma9446.0
    excel// Weighted score in Excel (row 2)
=B2*0.4 + C2*0.3 + D2*0.3
// Rank projects 1 = best
=RANK(E2, $E$2:$E$4, 0)
    ⚠️
    Keep the model simple. If leaders cannot explain the score, they will not trust the ranking.

    Module 03

    KPI & Metrics Reporting

    Leading and lagging indicators, earned value and how to design KPIs people trust.

    📈 Basic: Leading and Lagging Indicators

    Leading

    • Predicts what will happen
    • Resource forecast, risk trend, backlog age
    • Lets you act early

    Lagging

    • Reports what already happened
    • Budget variance, delivered milestones
    • Confirms results
    KPIFormulaWhat it tells you
    Schedule varianceEV − PVDays or value ahead / behind
    Budget varianceBudget − ActualCost over or under plan
    On-time delivery %On-time milestones ÷ totalReliability of delivery
    Benefits realised %Realised ÷ planned benefitsValue actually delivered
    Status report timelinessReports on time ÷ expectedData discipline

    🧮 Intermediate: Earned Value in Practice

    Earned value compares three numbers: PV (planned value), EV (value of work done) and AC (actual cost).

    MetricFormulaExample (PV 100k, EV 80k, AC 90k)Reading
    SVEV − PV−20kBehind
    CVEV − AC−10kOver cost
    SPIEV ÷ PV0.80Behind
    CPIEV ÷ AC0.89Over cost
    excel// Excel formulas
SPI: =C2/B2      // EV / PV
CPI: =C2/D2      // EV / AC
EAC (simple): =Budget/CPI
    💡
    Both SPI and CPI below 1.0 together is the signal to escalate: the project is late and over cost.

    🛠️ Advanced: Designing KPIs and Dashboard Hygiene

    KPI definition fieldExample
    NameOn-time milestone delivery %
    FormulaMilestones completed by baseline date ÷ milestones due
    SourceProject plan tool, monthly extract
    OwnerPMO analyst
    FrequencyMonthly
    Target / thresholds≥ 90% Green, 75–89% Amber, < 75% Red

    Dashboard pitfalls

    • Too many KPIs
    • No definitions
    • Different date cut-offs
    • Averages hiding outliers

    Good practice

    • 5–8 KPIs per audience
    • Definition footnotes
    • One reporting date
    • Show the distribution and the worst items

    Module 04

    Status Report Aggregation

    Collecting, validating and rolling up project status into one honest portfolio view.

    🚥 Basic: RAG Status and Report Content

    RAGTypical criteriaExpected response
    🟢 GreenOn plan; no issues needing sponsor helpContinue
    🟠 AmberSlipping or a risk is emerging; recovery plan existsPM manages, PMO watches
    🔴 RedOff plan; needs sponsor or steering decisionEscalate with options
    • Overall RAG plus schedule, budget, scope and resource RAG
    • Progress since last report and plan for next period
    • Top risks and issues, decisions needed
    • Milestone dates: baseline vs forecast

    🔄 Intermediate: The Consolidation Workflow

    Figure 4.1 · Status consolidation workflow
    excel// Roll-up with a status table named tblStatus
=COUNTIFS(tblStatus[Overall RAG],"Red")
=COUNTIFS(tblStatus[Overall RAG],"Amber",tblStatus[Program],"Digital")
// Percentage of projects reporting on time
=COUNTIFS(tblStatus[Submitted],"Yes")/ROWS(tblStatus[Project])
    💡
    Keep a submission tracker. Chasing late reports early in the cycle is half the job.

    🍉 Advanced: Data Quality and Honest Reporting

    Weak practice

    • Overall Green despite a Red milestone
    • Narrative copied from last month
    • No dates on actions
    • PMO changes colours silently

    Strong practice

    • Overall RAG cannot be better than worst driver without a note
    • Fresh commentary each period
    • Owner and date on every action
    • Changes challenged openly with the PM
    ⚠️
    If the PMO overrides a project’s RAG, record who, why and when. Silent changes destroy trust on both sides.

    Module 05

    Process Audit & Compliance

    Planning audits, sampling, testing evidence and driving findings to closure.

    🔍 Basic: What a PMO Audits

    AreaExample checkEvidence
    InitiationApproved charter and business caseSigned document, date
    PlanningBaseline plan and risk log existPlan file, version
    ReportingStatus submitted on time monthlySubmission tracker
    Change controlScope changes approved before workChange log entries
    ClosureLessons learned recordedClosure report
    💡
    Audit the process, not the person. The aim is a better system, not blame.

    🔁 Intermediate: The Audit Cycle and Sampling

    Figure 5.1 · The process audit cycle
    excel// Compliance rate for the check “charter approved”
=COUNTIF(F2:F26,"Yes")/COUNTA(F2:F26)
// Compare to target
=IF(G2>=0.9,"Green",IF(G2>=0.75,"Amber","Red"))

    Choose a sample that covers different programs, sizes and project managers, not only the easy ones.

    📝 Advanced: Findings, Severity and Corrective Actions

    SeverityMeaningExpected fix time
    HighControl missing; material impact on cost, schedule or compliance30 days
    MediumControl weak or inconsistently applied60 days
    LowMinor deviation or documentation gap90 days
    • Write each finding as: condition, criteria, cause, effect, recommendation.
    • Agree the finding with the project manager before it goes to leadership.
    • Track actions in a register and re-test after closure.
    • Report trends: repeat findings point to a process or training problem.
    ⚠️
    A finding “closed” without re-testing is only a promise. Verify the fix with fresh evidence.

    Module 06

    Capacity Planning & Resource Forecasting

    Utilisation, demand-versus-capacity heat maps and scenario-based forecasting.

    👥 Basic: Capacity, Demand and Utilisation

    TermDefinitionFormula / example
    CapacityHours people can work on projectsPeople × hours × availability %
    DemandHours projects needSum of project resource requests
    UtilisationShare of available time allocatedAllocated ÷ available
    GapCapacity minus demandNegative means shortfall
    excel// 10 people, 40 h/week, 80% available to projects
=10*40*0.8      // 320 h
// Utilisation for one person
=C2/B2          // allocated / available
    💡
    Plan at 80–85% utilisation. Running everyone at 100% leaves no room for leave, support or surprises.

    🌡️ Intermediate: Demand vs Capacity Heat Map

    Show weeks across the top and roles down the side. Colour each cell by utilisation.

    RoleWk 1Wk 2Wk 3Wk 4
    Business analyst85%95%120%110%
    Developer70%80%90%85%
    Tester60%75%130%100%

    Over-allocated (>100%)

    • Move low-priority work
    • Shift start dates
    • Borrow or hire temporarily

    Under-allocated (<60%)

    • Pull work forward
    • Cross-train
    • Release to other projects

    🔮 Advanced: Forecasting and Scenario Planning

    Combine committed projects (100% weight) with pipeline opportunities weighted by probability, then test scenarios.

    excel// Weighted demand per role and week
=SUMPRODUCT(Demand[Hours], Demand[Probability], --(Demand[Role]=$A2), --(Demand[Week]=B$1))
    ScenarioAssumptionResult to review
    BaseAll committed projects, 50% pipelineGap by role
    UpsidePipeline wins at 80%Hiring need
    DownsideTwo projects delayed 4 weeksIdle capacity
    ⚠️
    A forecast is a range, not a promise. Refresh it monthly and show the assumptions next to the numbers.

    🌐 Advanced: Portfolio Risk Themes and Escalation

    • Group risks by theme (vendor, skills, data, regulatory) to find shared causes.
    • Report the top 10 by score and the trend since last month.

    Module 08

    Scenarios & Troubleshooting

    Four realistic PMO problems with symptoms, diagnosis and resolution.

    🟢 Case: Everything Is Green but Delivery Is Late

    Situation

    The monthly report shows 18 of 20 projects Green, yet three programs missed their quarter-end milestones.

    What you observe

    • Green projects show Red milestones in the plan
    • Commentary is unchanged for three months

    Diagnosis

    RAG is self-assessed without objective thresholds, and nobody checks it against the plan.

    Resolution

    1. Publish RAG criteria with numeric thresholds
    2. Auto-flag Green projects with late milestones
    3. Challenge flagged projects in a review
    4. Record any PMO override

    📊 Case: Two Dashboards, Two Answers

    Situation

    Finance reports 82% on-time delivery; the PMO dashboard shows 91%.

    What you observe

    • Different date cut-offs
    • Finance counts the final go-live; PMO counts any milestone

    Diagnosis

    The KPI has two definitions and two data sources.

    Resolution

    1. Document one KPI definition
    2. Agree the source of truth
    3. Reconcile last quarter to both
    4. Retire the duplicate dashboard

    👥 Case: The 140% Business Analyst

    Situation

    Capacity forecast shows one analyst allocated 56 hours a week for six weeks.

    What you observe

    • Three projects each assume 100% of their time
    • Two tasks are not yet approved

    Diagnosis

    Project managers requested resources independently with no portfolio-level check.

    Resolution

    1. List the analyst’s allocations
    2. Rank projects with the portfolio board
    3. Re-sequence or add cover
    4. Add an allocation check to the planning cycle

    🗃️ Case: The 400-Line Risk Log

    Situation

    The consolidated risk log has 400 rows; leadership cannot see what matters.

    What you observe

    • Same risk entered by several projects
    • Many risks have no owner or review date

    Diagnosis

    No shared template, scale or ownership rules.

    Resolution

    1. Normalise fields and scores
    2. Merge duplicates into portfolio themes
    3. Assign owners; close stale risks
    4. Report the top 10 only

    🌱 Beginner Tasks

    Task 1BeginnerRead the RAG
    A project’s budget is on plan, but its key milestone is three weeks late and no recovery plan exists. It is reported Overall Green. What should the PMO do?
    Check the published RAG criteria for schedule.
    The status is not consistent with the evidence. Under typical criteria it is at least Amber, and Red if there is no recovery plan. Challenge the PM, record the agreed RAG and note any override.
    Task 2BeginnerUtilisation and gap
    Six people work 40 h/week and are 85% available for projects. Demand next week is 230 h. Find capacity, gap and utilisation.
    Capacity = people × hours × availability.
    Capacity = 6 × 40 × 0.85 = 204 h. Gap = 204 − 230 = −26 h (shortfall). Utilisation = 230 ÷ 204 ≈ 113%.

    Task 3IntermediateEarned value check

    SPI = 0.75, CPI ≈ 0.83, EAC ≈ $480k. Late and over cost: escalate with options (re-scope, add resource, or re-baseline).

    Alpha 6.5, Beta 6.9, Gamma 6.0 → Beta, Alpha, Gamma. Note that Gamma has the highest value but weak risk and cost scores; the model is a conversation starter, not a verdict.

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked in PMO Analyst interviews, grouped by level. Draft your answer, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.What does a PMO analyst do day to day?
    Collects and validates project data, builds KPI and status reports, tracks risks and actions, supports governance and audits, and forecasts capacity so leaders can decide early.
    IQ2.What is the difference between a portfolio, a program and a project?
    A project delivers a defined output; a program coordinates related projects for combined benefits; a portfolio is the set of initiatives chosen to deliver strategy.
    IQ3.What does RAG status mean and why do thresholds matter?
    Red, Amber, Green health ratings. Numeric thresholds make ratings consistent and stop self-assessed optimism.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.Explain SPI and CPI.
    SPI = EV ÷ PV measures schedule performance; CPI = EV ÷ AC measures cost efficiency. Below 1.0 means behind schedule or over cost.
    IQ5.How do you consolidate status reports from 20 projects?
    Collect with a standard template, validate gaps and consistency, normalise scales, roll up with COUNTIFS or a pivot, add commentary and publish with data gaps flagged.
    IQ6.A PM disagrees with your RAG override. What do you do?
    Review the evidence together against the published criteria, agree the rating, and document who changed it, why and when.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How would you design a KPI dashboard for executives?
    Pick 5–8 KPIs tied to decisions, define each one, use consistent cut-off dates, show trends and the worst items, and add concise commentary.
    IQ8.How do you sample projects for an audit?
    Cover different programs, sizes and managers, include risk-based picks, document the method and extrapolate carefully.
    IQ9.How do you tell a capacity problem from a prioritisation problem?
    If demand exceeds capacity after realistic availability, it is capacity; if the work is not aligned to strategic priority, it is prioritisation. Often both need a portfolio decision.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you handle risks that appear in many projects?
    Group them into a portfolio theme, assign a single owner, assess the combined exposure and escalate if it passes the threshold.
    IQ11.A KPI changed definition mid-year. What should you do?
    Document the change, restate history on the new basis where possible, annotate the dashboard and tell stakeholders.
    IQ12.Tell me about a time you improved a reporting process.
    Use the STAR format: situation, task, what you changed (template, definitions, automation), and the measured result such as hours saved or fewer corrections.

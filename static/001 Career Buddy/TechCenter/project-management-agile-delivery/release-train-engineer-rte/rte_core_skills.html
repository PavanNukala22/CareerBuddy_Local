    Module 01 · Basic · Foundations

    The RTE Role & SAFe Foundations

    The Release Train Engineer is the servant leader and chief Scrum Master of the Agile Release Train. Start with the ART, its cadence and the mindset behind it.

    🚂 Who is the Release Train Engineer?

    The RTE facilitates ART events and processes, coaches leaders, teams and stakeholders, manages risks and dependencies, and escalates systemic impediments. The RTE does not own content or assign work: teams plan, Product Management prioritises, the RTE optimises flow.

    🧭
    Servant leader
    Removes blockers, enables self-organising teams.
    🗓️
    Cadence owner
    Runs PI Planning, ART Sync, System Demo, I&A.
    ⚠️
    Risk & dependency manager
    Makes risks visible and tracks ROAM.
    🎓
    Coach
    Develops Scrum Masters, POs and leaders.
    📈
    Flow optimiser
    Watches WIP, flow and predictability.
    🚨
    Escalation owner
    Takes systemic impediments upward with data.

    🧱 Anatomy and Cadence of an ART

    ElementDetail
    ART sizeTypically 50–125 people in 5–12 teams, one value stream.
    Program Increment (PI)Time-box of 8–12 weeks; commonly 4 development iterations + 1 Innovation & Planning (IP) iteration.
    IterationUsually 2 weeks, ends with a System Demo.
    Key rolesRTE, Product Management, System Architect, Business Owners, Product Owners, Scrum Masters, System Team.
    PI eventsPI Planning (2 days), ART Sync (weekly), System Demo (each iteration), Inspect & Adapt (end of PI).

    📖 Key Terms

    CadenceFixed rhythm that cuts variability and syncs teams.
    Value streamSteps from idea to value delivered to customer.
    WIP limitCap on work in progress to improve flow.
    LACELean-Agile Center of Excellence driving adoption.
    EnablerArchitecture, infrastructure or exploration work.
    Business OwnerStakeholder accountable for outcomes and BV scoring.
    💡
    The RTE succeeds when the ART runs predictably without the RTE: teaching beats doing.

    Module 02 · Intermediate · Governance

    SAFe Governance

    Lean governance keeps autonomy and alignment in balance: guardrails, cadence and transparency instead of stage-gates and control.

    🏛️ Governance Across Levels

    LevelGovernance mechanism
    Portfolio (LPM)Strategy and investment funding, Agile portfolio operations, Lean governance: spend, audit, compliance.
    ARTCadence, WIP limits, Definition of Done, PI objectives, Program Kanban.
    TeamTeam agreements, built-in quality, iteration goals.

    🛡️ Guardrails, Lean Budgets and Compliance

    Guardrails are policies on spend, investment mix and approvals that let value streams decide fast within limits. Lean budgets fund value streams instead of projects. Regulated products need continuous compliance: evidence produced as a by-product of daily work (traceability, automated tests, audit trail).

    🚨
    Anti-pattern: governance by status reports, phase gates and stage-approvals. They slow flow and hide problems.

    🔁 ART Governance Calendar

    EventPurposeRTE action
    ART Sync (weekly)Scrum of Scrums + PO Sync: progress, dependencies, impedimentsFacilitates; tracks escalations
    Iteration PlanningTeams commit to iteration goalsEnsure cadence alignment
    System DemoIntegrated increment feedbackCoordinates (Module 05)
    Inspect & AdaptMeasure, retrospect, solve systemic problemsFacilitates (Module 06)
    PI PlanningAlign to missionFacilitates (Module 03)

    🧪 Advanced: Governing Flow

    Use Program Kanban with WIP limits and explicit policies (entry/exit criteria). Review flow metrics (velocity, time, load, efficiency, distribution, predictability) rather than activity counts. Small batch size and low utilisation shorten queues: running near 100% utilisation increases delay.

    🧾 Compliance and Audit in Regulated ARTs

    NeedHow the ART meets it
    TraceabilityLink requirement → Feature → Story → test → release record
    Audit evidenceGenerate it from pipeline and tools, not by hand at the end
    Definition of DoneInclude compliance checks per iteration
    Segregation of dutiesApprover differs from author where required
    Change recordsAutomated, immutable history of what changed and why
    💡
    Continuous compliance turns audit from a project into a by-product of normal work.

    Module 03 · Advanced · Facilitation

    PI Planning Facilitation

    Two days that align the ART to a common mission. Preparation determines success.

    🧰 Readiness Checklist

    ReadinessWhat the RTE confirms
    OrganisationalTeams and ART roles defined; Business Owners attend; dates set.
    ContentVision briefing, prioritised top Features, architecture vision, roadmap.
    LogisticsRooms or virtual boards, tools, catering, time zones, facilitators.
    PlanningCapacity and iteration calendar per team, backlog refined, dependency owners known.

    📅 Agenda

    Day 1Day 2
    Business context · Product/solution vision · Architecture vision & dev practices · Planning context & lunchPlanning adjustments from management feedback
    Team breakout #1 (draft plans, objectives, risks)Team breakout #2 (final plans, BV assignment)
    Draft plan review · Management review & problem solvingFinal plan review · Program risks (ROAM) · Confidence vote · Plan rework if needed · Planning retrospective

    🎯 Objectives, Risk and Confidence

    PI Objectives are SMART summaries of intended business value; Business Owners assign value 1–10. Stretch objectives are outside the commitment and not counted in planned value. Risks are classified ROAM: Resolved, Owned, Accepted, Mitigated. The fist-of-five confidence vote must reach ≥3 on average; individual 1–2 votes must voice concerns and the plan is reworked.

    PI Predictability = Actual Business Value ÷ Planned Business Value Reliable ARTs sit around 80–100%.

    ⚖️ Prioritisation with WSJF

    WSJF = Cost of Delay ÷ Job Size where CoD = user/business value + time criticality + risk reduction/opportunity enablement (relative scores).

    FeatureBVTCRRCoDSizeWSJF
    A8531682.0
    B5321025.0
    Example

    Feature B has the higher WSJF (5.0 vs 2.0), so it is sequenced first: shorter jobs with decent value beat big jobs.

    🔁 Pre-PI and Post-PI Planning

    StagePurposeRTE actions
    Pre-PI planningAlign leaders and prepare contentConfirm vision, top Features, capacity, logistics, attendees
    PI PlanningCommit to the planFacilitate agenda, risks, confidence vote
    Post-PI planningConsolidate and communicateMerge plans, publish objectives, set ART Sync dates, update tools

    🧯 PI Planning Failure Modes

    SymptomLikely causeRTE response
    Features not understoodPoor content readinessRun refinement before the event; shorten vision briefing
    Business Owners absentWeak engagementCoach leaders; schedule early; show value of presence
    Over-commitmentIgnoring capacityPlan to capacity with buffer; use stretch objectives
    Hidden dependenciesTeams plan in silosProgram Board time; dependency owners in room
    Remote fatigueLong sessions, poor toolingSplit sessions, rotate facilitators, prepared boards
    Low confidence voteUnclear scope or risksRe-plan; resolve or ROAM risks; vote again

    Module 04 · Advanced · Execution & Coaching

    ART Execution & Coaching

    Keep the train on time and grow the people who run it.

    🔄 Executing the PI

    MechanismHow the RTE uses it
    ART SyncShort, focused: progress against objectives, dependencies, risks; take problems offline.
    Program BoardTrack Features, milestones and dependency lines; re-plan visibly.
    Program KanbanLimit WIP; ensure Features meet Definition of Ready.
    Iteration cadenceAlign iteration dates; verify teams are demoing and learning.
    Risk registerReview ROAM status; escalate unresolved items early.

    📊 Flow Metrics for Execution

    MetricMeaning
    Flow velocityItems completed per time.
    Flow timeElapsed time from start to finish.
    Flow loadItems in process (WIP).
    Flow efficiencyActive time ÷ total flow time.
    Flow distributionMix: Features, enablers, defects, risks.
    Flow predictabilityPlanned vs actual delivery.
    📝
    Rising flow load with falling velocity signals overload: lower WIP before adding people.

    🎓 Coaching Stances and Targets

    Move fluidly between teaching, mentoring, facilitating and coaching depending on need: teach new practices, then step back and ask powerful questions.

    AudienceCoaching focus
    Scrum MastersFacilitation, impediment handling, team health.
    Product OwnersBacklog refinement, WSJF, acceptance.
    ManagersServant leadership, supporting self-organisation, no task-assignment.
    Business OwnersEngagement, objective scoring, trade-offs.

    🔗 Dependencies and Risk

    Identify in PI Planning and continuously in ART Sync. Long-term, reduce coupling: organise teams around value streams and improve architecture, rather than managing ever more dependencies.

    🕒 ART Sync: Agenda and Anti-Patterns

    TimeTopic
    5 minProgress against PI Objectives
    10 minCross-team dependencies and blockers
    5 minRisks (ROAM updates)
    5 minImpediments and escalations
    5 minDecisions, owners and next steps
    📣
    Status theatre
    Reading reports instead of resolving issues.
    🧩
    Problem-solving in the sync
    Take it offline with the people needed.
    🙈
    Absent Product Owners
    Decisions stall; set expectations.
    ⏳
    Overrun
    Time-box and park items.

    Module 05 · Advanced · Integration

    System Demo Coordination

    The System Demo shows the integrated increment of the whole ART at the end of every iteration.

    🎬 Purpose and Principles

    It is the objective measure of progress: working, integrated software/systems in a staging environment, not slides. It provides fast feedback from stakeholders and supports the principle build incrementally with fast, integrated learning cycles.

    🧭 Coordination Checklist

    PhaseActions
    BeforeConfirm the environment, build and test data; agree demo order and time-box (≈60 min); gather stakeholders; ensure Definition of Done applies at system level.
    DuringKeep to time; focus on Feature value; capture feedback; note defects and gaps.
    AfterLog feedback in backlogs; assign owners; track environment/integration issues as impediments.

    🧩 System Team and Environment

    The System Team builds and supports CI/CD, integration and test environments. An unstable demo environment, manual builds or late integration is usually a systemic issue to escalate, not a team issue.

    💡
    End-of-PI Inspect & Adapt starts with a PI System Demo of all PI outcomes to Business Owners and stakeholders.

    🚧 Demo Quality Gates and Anti-Patterns

    Anti-patternEffectFix
    Slides instead of softwareNo real feedbackShow working increment in staging
    Team-by-team silosIntegration surprises lateDemo end-to-end flows across teams
    No feedback captureLearning lostScribe, log to backlog, assign owners
    Unstable environmentFailed demosInvest in environments and pipeline
    Wrong audienceLow value feedbackInvite Business Owners and users

    Module 06 · Expert · Escalation

    Systemic Impediment Escalation

    Make organisational blockers visible, quantified and owned.

    🧱 What Makes an Impediment Systemic?

    It is beyond one team's control, recurs across teams or iterations, and needs ART-level or organisational change: policy, tooling, funding, structure, shared services, or leadership behaviour.

    Team-level

    Resolved by the team or Scrum Master inside the iteration.

    Systemic

    Needs RTE/leadership action; pattern across teams.

    📣 Escalation Path

    Team → Scrum Master → ART Sync → RTE → Management / LACE → Portfolio (LPM) leaders

    Escalation message elementWhy
    Problem statementClear and factual
    Impact (data)Delay, cost of delay, flow time, quality
    Options and recommendationShows ownership
    Decision needed and by whenDrives action
    Owner and follow-up dateCloses the loop

    🛠️ Inspect & Adapt Problem-Solving Workshop

    Agree the problem statement → root-cause analysis (5-Whys / fishbone) → Pareto to select the largest cause → restate the problem → brainstorm solutions → create improvement backlog items with owners → integrate into the next PI Planning.

    💰 Quantify to Prioritise

    Rank impediments by Cost of Delay and flow data (wait time, rework, blocked days), not by seniority or volume of complaint.

    Example

    Environment waits average 3 days per Feature across 8 teams = 24 team-days per iteration; showing this converts "annoying" into a funded fix.

    📨 Escalation Template and Worked Example

    Use one page: Title · Context · Impact (data) · Root cause · Options A/B/C with cost · Recommendation · Decision needed by · Owner.

    Worked example

    8 teams each wait 3 days per iteration for a test environment = 24 team-days per iteration. Over 5 iterations that is 120 team-days per PI of waiting. Option A: dedicated environments; Option B: pipeline automation; Option C: do nothing. Recommend B with a decision requested before next PI Planning.

    Module 07 · Expert · Measurement

    SAFe Metrics and Measure and Grow

    Measure outcomes, flow and competency, and use metrics to learn rather than to judge.

    📏 Measure and Grow

    DomainExamples
    OutcomesBusiness value delivered, customer satisfaction, OKR progress
    FlowFlow time, load, velocity, efficiency, distribution, predictability
    CompetencyBusiness agility and core-competency self-assessments

    The seven core competencies: Team and Technical Agility, Agile Product Delivery, Enterprise Solution Delivery, Lean Portfolio Management, Organizational Agility, Continuous Learning Culture, Lean-Agile Leadership. Assessments are for improvement, not ranking teams.

    🎯 PI Metrics and Worked Predictability

    MetricUse
    PI predictabilityPlanned vs actual business value
    Feature planned vs actualDelivery reliability
    Quality trendEscaped defects, rework
    Objective achievementOutcome focus
    Example · Predictability

    Planned BV: Team A 8, B 7, C 9 = 24. Actual BV: 6, 7, 7 = 20. Predictability = 20 ÷ 24 ≈ 83%.

    🧭 Leading vs Lagging and Goodhart

    🔮
    Leading
    Aging WIP, flow load, dependency age.
    📉
    Lagging
    PI predictability, defects in production.
    ⚠️
    Goodhart law
    When a measure becomes a target it stops being a good measure.
    🗣️
    Use in I&A
    Pair metrics with stories and improvement actions.

    Connect the ART to strategy, funding and large-solution coordination.

    🌊 Value Streams

    TypeMeaning
    Operational value streamSteps that deliver value to the customer (trigger to value)
    Development value streamSteps that build the systems operations use

    Identify the trigger, steps, people, systems and delays; align each ART to a value stream so that most work flows without cross-ART handoffs.

    🏛️ Lean Portfolio Management

    DimensionPractices

    🔗 Connecting ART Work to Strategy

    Each Feature should trace to an Epic and strategic theme. Use WSJF at program and portfolio level, and review capacity allocation across new features, enablers, defects and tech debt.

    Module 09 · Expert · Delivery

    DevOps, Built-in Quality and Release

    The technical foundation that makes ART execution and System Demos reliable.

    🚰 Continuous Delivery Pipeline

    AspectActivities
    Continuous ExplorationHypotheses, research, architecture, Features
    Continuous IntegrationBuild, test, merge and integrate frequently
    Continuous DeploymentDeploy to production-like and production environments
    Release on DemandRelease value when the business wants it

    🧱 Built-in Quality

    PracticePurpose
    Test-driven / behaviour-driven developmentClarify behaviour; test first
    Test automation pyramidMany fast unit tests, fewer service tests, few UI tests
    Shift-left testingFind defects early
    Continuous integrationFrequent merges, fast feedback
    Definition of Done at all levelsTeam, system and release quality

    🚦 Deploy vs Release

    Deploy = code in an environment. Release = value available to users. Decouple them with feature toggles, canary and blue/green strategies, and dark launches. Replace manual approval boards with automated policy checks where possible.

    📊 RTE Role and DORA Metrics

    The RTE works with the System Team on environments and release readiness. The four DORA metrics: deployment frequency, lead time for changes, change failure rate, time to restore service.

    Module 10 · Practice

    Practice: Running an 8-Team ART

    Apply the five core skills to one realistic scenario.

    🏗️ Scenario

    An ART of 8 teams is two weeks from PI Planning. The confidence vote last PI averaged 2.6 and was accepted. Predictability was 61%. Three teams share a flaky staging environment. Managers assign tasks directly to individuals, and a legal-approval policy delays releases by 3 weeks.

    0 / 0 correct

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Governance & PI Planning

    ✍️ What changes would you make to the PI Planning approach?
    Model answer: Enforce the ≥3 confidence rule with rework; prepare content readiness and dependencies earlier; set capacity with buffer and stretch objectives; get Business Owners to score BV; use WSJF; track ROAM.
    Q. Predictability of 61% means…
    • A actual value delivered was far below planned value
    • B all objectives were met
    • C confidence was high
    • D stretch goals were counted

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Execution & Coaching

    ✍️ How do you address managers assigning tasks?
    Model answer: Coach managers on servant leadership, show how task-assignment hurts self-organisation and flow, agree new behaviours, use ART Sync to surface issues, and reinforce through the LACE.
    Q. Best first step with managers?
    • A Publicly blame them
    • B Coach them with evidence on flow and team ownership
    • C Ignore it
    • D Replace the teams

    3️⃣ Task 3 · System Demo & Escalation

    ✍️ Plan the escalation for the environment and legal-approval delays.
    Model answer: Quantify blocked days and CoD; present options (dedicated environments, automated pipeline, pre-approved compliance patterns); escalate with decision and deadline to management/LACE; track in the improvement backlog; verify results next PI.
    Q. Environment instability across 3 teams is…
    • A a team issue only
    • B irrelevant to demos
    • C systemic and needs ART-level action
    • D a PI Planning topic only

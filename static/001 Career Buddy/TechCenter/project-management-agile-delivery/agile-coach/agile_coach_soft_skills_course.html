    Module 01 · Basic · Foundations

    Soft Skills Fundamentals

    Why soft skills decide whether an Agile Coach succeeds, what each of the five skills means and how they work together in a real situation.

    🧭 Why soft skills matter most for an Agile Coach

    An Agile Coach rarely has formal authority. They cannot order a VP to change funding or a team to adopt a practice. Their impact comes from trust, influence and the ability to help people think and work together. Frameworks and tools can be learned in weeks; the soft skills take years and make the real difference.

    🤝
    Influence
    Move leaders and teams without a reporting line.
    🔄
    See the system
    Find the structures behind repeated problems.
    💛
    Handle emotions
    Stay calm and read the emotions of others.
    🎤
    Hold the room
    Be credible and clear with executives.
    EXAMPLE · Same advice, different result

    Two coaches give a CIO the same advice to limit work in progress. The first lectures with slides and is ignored. The second links the advice to the CIO's goal of faster releases, uses the CIO's own data and asks for a one-quarter experiment. The CIO agrees.

    2Systems ThinkingSeeing patterns and structures, not just eventsWhy does this problem keep coming back?

    🏗️ One situation, five skills

    Two department heads, Product and Operations, are blocking each other on a new release process. Releases are delayed and both blame the other in front of the CIO.

    Soft skillWhat the coach does
    Emotional intelligenceNotices both heads feel their status is threatened; stays calm and non-judgemental
    Conflict mediationMeets each separately, then brings them together to focus on interests, not positions
    Systems thinkingShows that the release policy and incentives create the conflict, not bad people
    Influence without authorityBuilds a shared goal both care about: fewer incidents and faster releases
    Executive presencePresents a clear, brief proposal to the CIO and asks for a decision
    💡
    Soft skills work as a set. Systems thinking finds the cause, emotional intelligence keeps trust, mediation resolves the conflict, and influence and presence get the decision.

    🪞 Self-awareness: the foundation

    Every soft skill starts with knowing yourself: your triggers, your biases, your default style under stress, and how others see you.

    PracticeHow
    Reflective journalFive minutes after key meetings: what went well, what triggered me
    360° feedbackAsk peers, clients and leaders for honest input
    Personality and conflict-style toolsFor example Thomas-Kilmann to learn your default conflict mode
    Supervision or peer coachingDiscuss hard cases with another coach
    Notice body signalsTight jaw, fast speech: signs you are being triggered

    🛤️ How soft skills are built

    📝
    Soft skills grow through deliberate practice and feedback, the same inspect-and-adapt loop agile teams use.

    Module 02 · Soft Skill 1 · Influence Without Authority

    Influence Without Authority

    Build trust, understand what others value, frame ideas in their language, build coalitions and influence ethically.

    🤝 Authority versus influence

    AuthorityInfluence
    SourcePosition and reporting linesTrust, expertise, relationships, value exchange
    ResultComplianceCommitment
    LastsWhile the power lastsLong after the conversation
    Coach's positionUsually noneThe main tool
    EXAMPLE · Commitment, not compliance

    An executive orders teams to "do retrospectives". They hold them, but nothing changes. A coach helps one team find a real improvement from its retro and shares the result; other teams ask to learn how.

    🔐 Trust: the base of influence

    David Maister's trust equation explains why some people are trusted quickly and others are not:

    ElementMeaningHow a coach builds it
    CredibilityDo you know what you are talking about?Accurate advice, relevant examples, admitting limits
    ReliabilityDo you do what you say?Small promises kept, follow-up notes, punctuality
    IntimacyIs it safe to share with you?Confidentiality, listening, no gossip
    Self-orientation (divides)Are you focused on yourself?Focus on the client's goals, not your framework or ego
    📝
    Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) ÷ Self-orientation. High self-orientation destroys trust, however credible you are.

    💱 Currencies of influence (Cohen & Bradford)

    Allan Cohen and David Bradford describe influence as an exchange: find what the other person values (their "currencies") and offer something of value in return for what you need.

    Currency typeExamplesOffer to…
    InspirationVision, doing the right thing, excellenceLeaders motivated by purpose
    TaskResources, help, information, faster resultsBusy managers under delivery pressure
    PositionRecognition, visibility, reputationAmbitious leaders
    RelationshipUnderstanding, belonging, supportPeople who value connection
    PersonalGratitude, ownership, comfortPeople who want to feel valued
    EXAMPLE · Finding the currency

    The head of compliance blocks frequent releases. Her currency is risk reduction and reputation with regulators. The coach offers automated audit evidence and a joint presentation to the regulator. She becomes a sponsor.

    🧠 Principles of persuasion

    Robert Cialdini's principles describe why people say yes. Used ethically, they help ideas land.

    PrincipleEthical use by a coach
    ReciprocityHelp first: unblock a problem before asking for something
    Commitment and consistencyStart with a small agreement, e.g. a one-sprint trial
    Social proofShow that similar teams or companies have succeeded
    AuthorityShare real data, research and relevant experience
    LikingBuild genuine rapport and common ground
    ScarcityPoint out a real window of opportunity or cost of delay
    UnityAppeal to shared identity: "we in this bank…"
    ⚠️
    Influence becomes manipulation when it hides the truth or works against the other person's interest. A coach's credibility does not survive manipulation.

    🗣️ Framing ideas in their language

    Coach jargonExecutive framing
    Limit WIPFinish more by starting less: cut time to market
    Cross-functional teamsFewer hand-offs, fewer delays, clearer accountability
    Psychological safetyProblems raised early, fewer expensive surprises
    RetrospectivesContinuous cost and quality improvement built in
    Value stream mappingFind where 80% of our lead time is wasted
    💡
    Before any influence conversation, ask: "What does this person worry about at 2 a.m.?" Link your proposal to that.

    🕸️ Coalitions and pre-wiring

    TacticWhy it works
    Stakeholder map (power, interest, stance)Know whose support you need
    Pre-wiring 1:1 meetingsConcerns are raised privately and addressed before the big meeting
    Co-creating the proposalPeople support what they help build
    Using championsPeers are more persuasive than outside coaches
    Small experimentsLow-risk trials are easier to say yes to

    🚧 Influence traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Arguing harder with more slidesAsk questions and listen for their goals
    Going over someone's head firstTalk to them directly; escalate only with notice
    Using jargonUse their business language
    Only influencing upwardBuild support across and below as well
    Winning the argument, losing the relationshipProtect the relationship for the long game

    Module 03 · Soft Skill 2 · Systems Thinking

    Systems Thinking

    Look beyond events to patterns, structures and mental models; use feedback loops, archetypes and leverage points to change the system.

    🔄 What systems thinking is

    A system is a set of connected parts that produces its own pattern of behaviour. Systems thinking means looking at how the parts interact over time, rather than blaming individual parts. W. Edwards Deming argued that most problems in organisations come from the system, not the people working in it.

    Event thinkingSystems thinking
    "Team B missed the deadline again.""What in our planning and dependencies makes deadlines slip for every team?"
    Fix the symptom quicklyFind the structure that keeps creating the symptom
    Blame a personChange policy, incentives or flow
    Optimise one departmentOptimise the whole

    🧊 The iceberg model

    LevelQuestionExample
    Events (visible)What just happened?A production outage on Friday
    Patterns and trendsWhat has been happening over time?Outages after most Friday releases
    StructuresWhat causes the pattern?Big monthly releases, manual testing, release deadline on Friday
    Mental models (deepest)What beliefs keep the structure in place?"Releasing less often is safer"
    💡
    The deeper the level, the greater the leverage. Changing the belief "less often is safer" unlocks smaller, safer, more frequent releases.

    🔁 Feedback loops and delays

    Loop typeBehaviourExample
    Reinforcing (R)Amplifies change: growth or collapseMore technical debt → slower delivery → more pressure → more shortcuts → more debt
    Balancing (B)Pushes toward a goal or limitLong queue → team limits WIP → queue shrinks
    DelayTime between cause and effectTraining now, results in 3 months; leaders give up too early
    EXAMPLE · Causal loop in words

    Deadline pressure (+) → overtime (+) → fatigue (+) → defects (+) → rework (+) → deadline pressure. This reinforcing loop explains why "working harder" makes the project later.

    🧩 Common system archetypes

    ArchetypePatternAgile example
    Fixes that failA quick fix brings back the problem later, often worseSkipping tests to hit a date leads to more bugs next sprint
    Shifting the burdenRelying on a symptomatic fix weakens the real solutionA hero developer fixes every outage, so the team never learns
    Limits to growthGrowth hits a constraintAdding teams increases velocity until integration testing becomes the bottleneck
    Tragedy of the commonsShared resource overused by allEvery team books the single test environment
    Success to the successfulWinners get more resources and keep winningThe favourite team gets the best people; others fall behind
    Eroding goalsStandards slowly drop under pressureDefinition of Done weakened "just this sprint", every sprint

    🎯 Constraints and leverage points

    Eli Goldratt's Theory of Constraints says a system's output is limited by its constraint. Improving anything else does little. Its five focusing steps:

    Donella Meadows described leverage points, places where a small change has a big effect. Changing goals, rules and mindsets usually has much more leverage than changing numbers such as budgets or targets.

    EXAMPLE · Finding the constraint

    Ten teams produce features faster than the one release team can deploy them. Hiring more developers will not help. The coach focuses on automating deployment, the real constraint.

    🛠️ Systems thinking tools for coaches

    ToolUse
    Causal loop diagramDraw the loops behind a recurring problem with the group
    Iceberg modelMove a discussion from blame to structure and beliefs
    Value stream mapSee the flow and its constraint
    CynefinDecide whether a problem needs analysis or experiments
    Five whysDig from a symptom toward a structural cause
    Little's lawShow how WIP drives lead time
    ⚠️
    Five whys can lead to a single "root cause" that blames a person. In complex systems there are usually several interacting causes; look for structures.

    🚧 Systems thinking traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Local optimisationMeasure and improve end-to-end flow
    Blaming individualsAsk what in the system produced the behaviour
    Ignoring delaysSet expectations for when results will show
    Analysis paralysisRun small experiments to learn how the system responds

    Module 04 · Soft Skill 3 · Emotional Intelligence

    Emotional Intelligence

    Recognise and manage your own emotions, read others accurately, build psychological safety and give feedback that lands.

    💛 The components of emotional intelligence

    Daniel Goleman popularised emotional intelligence (EQ) as a set of skills that can be learned. A common four-part model:

    DomainAboutCoach example
    Self-awarenessKnowing your emotions, strengths and triggersNoticing irritation when a manager dismisses retrospectives
    Self-managementControlling impulses and staying adaptablePausing before replying sharply in a tense meeting
    Social awarenessEmpathy; reading others and the organisationSensing a team is anxious about a reorganisation
    Relationship managementInfluence, conflict handling, teamworkHelping two leads repair trust after a dispute
    📝
    Goleman's original five components are self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy and social skills.

    🧠 Triggers and the amygdala hijack

    Under threat, the brain's alarm system (the amygdala) can take over before the thinking brain responds: an amygdala hijack. People argue, freeze or withdraw. A coach needs to notice it in themselves and others.

    StepWhat to do
    NoticeBody signals: heat, tight chest, fast speech
    NameSilently label it: "I am feeling defensive"
    PauseBreathe, take a sip of water, slow down
    ChooseRespond with intent instead of reacting
    RepairIf you reacted badly, acknowledge it later
    💡
    "Name it to tame it": labelling an emotion helps calm it, in yourself and when you reflect it back to others ("It sounds like this feels unfair").

    🧩 The SCARF model

    David Rock's SCARF model explains five social needs that, when threatened, trigger strong reactions. Change programmes threaten all five.

    DomainThreat during agile changeCoach response
    StatusManagers lose the "boss" roleClarify new, valued roles
    CertaintyUnclear future structureShare what is known, what is not and when you will know
    AutonomyFramework imposed from aboveOffer choices in how to adopt
    RelatednessTeams broken upCreate new team rituals and connections
    FairnessSome areas get resources firstExplain decisions openly and consistently

    🛡️ Psychological safety

    Amy Edmondson defines psychological safety as a shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking. Google's Project Aristotle found it to be the most important factor in effective teams.

    Signs of low safetySigns of high safety
    Silence in retrospectivesPeople raise problems early
    Bad news hidden until lateMistakes discussed openly
    Only the leader speaksEveryone contributes
    "Watermelon" status: green outside, red insideHonest status reports
    Coach practiceExample
    Frame work as learning"This is new; we will learn as we go"
    Model fallibilityLeader says, "I might be missing something"
    Respond productivelyThank people for raising bad news
    Structure participationSilent writing, round-robin, anonymous input

    👂 Empathy in practice

    Type of empathyMeaning
    CognitiveUnderstanding how the other person thinks
    EmotionalFeeling with the other person
    Empathic concernBeing moved to help

    Nonviolent Communication (Marshall Rosenberg) gives a structure for expressing difficult messages with empathy: observation, feeling, need, request.

    EXAMPLE · NVC in a stand-up

    "In the last three stand-ups we ran over 25 minutes (observation). I feel frustrated (feeling) because I need time to focus on coding (need). Could we take detailed discussions offline after the stand-up (request)?"

    💬 Giving and receiving feedback

    The SBI model (Situation, Behaviour, Impact) keeps feedback specific and non-judgemental.

    PartExample
    Situation"In yesterday's PI Planning readout…"
    Behaviour"…you interrupted the team lead three times…"
    Impact"…and the team stopped sharing risks for the rest of the session."
    Receiving feedback wellAvoid
    Listen fully, ask clarifying questionsDefending immediately
    Thank the personDismissing it
    Reflect later and decide what to changeChanging nothing

    🚧 EQ traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Suppressing emotionsAcknowledge them, then choose a response
    Fixing feelings for othersListen and reflect; let them own the solution
    Mistaking niceness for EQEQ includes saying hard things with care
    Ignoring your own burnoutManage your energy; get supervision

    Module 05 · Soft Skill 4 · Executive Presence

    Executive Presence

    Earn credibility with senior leaders: gravitas, concise communication, business language, handling pushback and asking for decisions.

    🎤 What executive presence is

    Executive presence is how senior leaders judge whether you can be trusted with important matters. Sylvia Ann Hewlett's research describes three pillars, with gravitas mattering most.

    PillarIncludesFor an Agile Coach
    GravitasConfidence, calm under pressure, decisiveness, integrityStay steady when an executive challenges agile
    CommunicationClear, concise speaking, reading the room, assertivenessAnswer first, then explain
    AppearancePolished, appropriate to the setting, including on videoProfessional camera, light and sound in virtual meetings
    💡
    Presence is not about being loud or senior. It is about being clear, calm and focused on what matters to the room.

    📌 Answer first: the pyramid principle

    Executives have little time. Barbara Minto's pyramid principle and the military BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front) both say: start with the answer, then give the supporting points, then the detail.

    Weak openingStrong opening
    "So, we started the pilot in March and first we trained the teams, and then…""The pilot cut lead time from 90 to 40 days. I recommend extending it to two more value streams this quarter."
    "There are a lot of factors to consider…""We need a decision today on funding the platform team; here are three reasons."
    EXAMPLE · Pyramid structure

    Answer: Fund a platform team. Reasons: (1) 8 teams wait 2 weeks for environments; (2) the delay costs about two features per quarter; (3) a 6-person team removes most of the wait. Detail: costs and plan in the appendix.

    💼 Speaking the language of the business

    Executive cares aboutTranslate your message into
    Revenue and growthFaster time to market, earlier customer value
    CostLess rework, fewer hand-offs, lower cost of delay
    RiskSmaller releases, earlier feedback, fewer major incidents
    CustomersSatisfaction, retention, ratings
    PeopleEngagement, retention of key talent
    📝
    Quantify where you can, and be honest about uncertainty: "Our estimate is a 30–50% cut in lead time, based on the pilot."

    🧘 Gravitas under pressure

    SituationLow presence responseHigh presence response
    Hard challenge in a board meetingDefensive, long explanationPause, acknowledge, answer briefly, offer follow-up
    You do not know the answerBluff"I do not know yet; I will have it by Thursday"
    Executive is angryMatch the emotion or shrinkStay calm, name the concern, focus on next steps
    Bad newsHide it or bury it in detailSay it early and plainly with a plan
    💡
    Slow down. Pausing before answering signals confidence; rushing signals anxiety.

    📖 Storytelling and data

    Data persuades the head; stories move people. Combine them: one clear number plus one real story.

    EXAMPLE · Story with data

    "Three months ago a customer waited 90 days for a simple fix (context). Seven hand-offs slowed it (challenge). We formed one cross-functional team (action). The same type of fix now ships in 9 days (result). We ask you to fund two more such teams (ask)."

    🗳️ Asking for decisions

    PracticeExample
    State the decision needed upfront"I need your decision on X today."
    Offer clear options with trade-offsOption A: faster, more cost. Option B: slower, less risk.
    Give your recommendation"I recommend A because…"
    Confirm and follow upSend a one-paragraph summary of the decision within 24 hours
    ⚠️
    Leaving an executive meeting without a decision or next step wastes your scarce access. Always know your "ask".

    🚧 Executive presence traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Too much detailLead with the answer; keep detail in reserve
    Agile jargonBusiness language
    Over-apologising, hedgingClear, calm statements
    Trying to look like you know everythingHonest about limits, with a follow-up plan
    Only presenting good newsShare risks early with options

    Module 06 · Soft Skill 5 · Conflict Mediation

    Conflict Mediation

    Understand conflict styles and levels, focus on interests not positions, run a structured mediation and turn conflict into healthy debate.

    ⚡ Healthy and unhealthy conflict

    Conflict is normal in teams doing difficult work. Task conflict, disagreement about ideas, can improve decisions. Relationship conflict, personal friction, usually damages performance. Patrick Lencioni lists fear of conflict as one of the five dysfunctions of a team: absence of trust, fear of conflict, lack of commitment, avoidance of accountability and inattention to results.

    Healthy conflictUnhealthy conflict
    About ideas and approachesAbout people and motives
    Open, in the meetingIn corridors and chat channels
    Ends in a decision and commitmentRepeats without resolution
    Respectful even when heatedSarcasm, blame, silence

    📶 Levels of conflict (Speed Leas)

    LevelNameSignsCoach response
    1Problem to solveInformation shared, focus on the problemFacilitate collaborative problem solving
    2DisagreementSelf-protection, cautious languageBuild safety, clarify interests
    3ContestWinning matters, sides formMediate, find common ground
    4CrusadeProtecting one's group, ideologyStructured mediation; may need leadership help
    5World warDestroy the other sideSeparate parties; formal intervention
    💡
    Identify the level before acting. Tools that work at level 1 make things worse at level 4.

    🎛️ Conflict styles (Thomas-Kilmann)

    The Thomas-Kilmann model maps five conflict modes on two dimensions: assertiveness (my concerns) and cooperativeness (their concerns). No mode is always best.

    ModeAssertive / cooperativeUse when
    CompetingHigh / lowUrgent, critical decision; safety or ethics
    CollaboratingHigh / highBoth concerns too important to compromise; time available
    CompromisingMedium / mediumModerate importance; need a quick workable solution
    AvoidingLow / lowTrivial issue, or time to cool down
    AccommodatingLow / highIssue matters more to the other person; building goodwill

    🎯 Interests, not positions

    Fisher and Ury's Getting to Yes describes principled negotiation, a strong basis for mediation:

    PrincipleMeaning
    Separate the people from the problemBe soft on people, hard on the problem
    Focus on interests, not positionsAsk why they want it
    Invent options for mutual gainBrainstorm before deciding
    Use objective criteriaData, standards, fair rules
    Know your BATNABest Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement
    EXAMPLE · Positions vs interests

    Development wants to release weekly (position) to get fast customer feedback (interest). Operations wants to release quarterly (position) to avoid outages (interest). Option for mutual gain: weekly releases with automated tests, feature toggles and a rollback plan.

    🧑‍⚖️ Running a mediation

    StepCoach actions
    PrepareMeet each party alone; listen; check willingness to meet
    Ground rulesOne speaks at a time, no personal attacks, confidentiality
    StoriesEach describes the situation; the other summarises back before replying
    InterestsAsk "what matters most to you here?"; write shared interests
    OptionsBrainstorm without judging, then evaluate against objective criteria
    AgreementSpecific actions, owners, dates; review in two weeks
    ⚠️
    Stay neutral. If you have a stake in the outcome, or if there is harassment or a legal issue, involve HR or another neutral party.

    🪜 The ladder of inference

    Chris Argyris's ladder of inference shows how people jump from observed data to conclusions: select data → add meaning → make assumptions → draw conclusions → adopt beliefs → act. Conflicts often happen at the top of two different ladders.

    EXAMPLE · Climbing down

    A tester says the developers "don't care about quality". The coach asks, "What did you see that led you there?" The answer is that builds arrive late on Fridays. Developers explain the build server is overloaded. The conflict shifts from people to a shared problem.

    🚧 Conflict traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Forcing harmonyEncourage open task conflict
    Taking sidesRemain neutral; focus on process
    Solving it for themHelp parties find their own agreement
    Ignoring early signsAddress at level 1–2 before it escalates
    No follow-upCheck the agreement holds

    Module 08 · Interview Preparation

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer.

    🤝 Influence Without Authority

    Tell me about a time you influenced a senior leader without authority.
    Use STAR: Situation (CFO blocked product funding), Task (get a pilot approved), Action (learned her concern was cost control, framed the pilot in cost-of-delay terms, pre-wired with finance partners, proposed a one-quarter trial), Result (approved; pilot cut lead time 40% and was extended).
    How do you build trust quickly with a new client?
    Listen first, keep small promises, show relevant expertise without lecturing, keep confidences and focus on their goals, not my framework.
    What is the difference between influence and manipulation?
    Influence is transparent and serves shared interests; manipulation hides intent or works against the other person's interest.

    🔄 Systems Thinking

    Give an example of applying systems thinking.
    Recurring production outages were blamed on testers. Using the iceberg model we found big monthly releases and manual tests behind them, held in place by the belief that "less often is safer". Smaller automated releases cut incidents sharply.
    What is a reinforcing loop?
    A loop where a change amplifies itself, such as technical debt slowing delivery, which increases pressure and shortcuts, which adds more debt.
    How do you find a system's constraint?
    Look where work queues up: value stream maps, cumulative flow diagrams and asking where work waits. Then focus improvement there.

    💛 Emotional Intelligence

    How do you stay calm when a leader attacks agile in public?
    Notice my reaction, pause, acknowledge the concern, ask a question to understand it, and offer to explore it with data later. I do not argue to win in public.
    How do you build psychological safety in a team?
    Frame work as learning, model fallibility, respond well to bad news, use structured participation in meetings and coach leaders to do the same.
    How do you give difficult feedback?
    Use SBI: the situation, the specific behaviour and its impact, then ask for their view and agree next steps.

    🎤 Executive Presence

    How do you present to executives?
    Answer first, three supporting points in business language, a clear decision request with options, and detail held in reserve.
    How do you deliver bad news to a sponsor?
    Early, plainly and with a plan: what happened, the impact, options and my recommendation.
    How do you handle a question you cannot answer?
    Say I do not know yet, explain how I will find out and commit to a time.

    ⚡ Conflict Mediation

    Describe how you would mediate between two team leads.
    Meet each alone, agree ground rules, let each tell their story with the other summarising, surface interests, brainstorm options, agree actions with owners and dates, and follow up.
    What is your default conflict style and when do you change it?
    Name your Thomas-Kilmann default honestly, for example collaborating, and give examples of using competing for safety issues or avoiding to let emotions cool.
    When should a coach not mediate?
    When the coach is not neutral, or the issue involves harassment, legal or HR matters; then involve the right people.

    🏗️ Scenario: Rescue a stalled transformation

    You join a telecom company 9 months into an agile transformation that has stalled. The Head of Engineering and Head of Operations openly blame each other for failed releases. Teams are quiet in retrospectives. The CEO gives you 10 minutes at the next leadership meeting. You have no formal authority.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Influence without authority

    ✍️ How do you gain the support of the two department heads?
    Model answer: Meet each 1:1 and listen for their goals and concerns (their currencies: engineering wants speed, operations wants stability). Build trust by keeping small promises and confidentiality. Frame the change around a shared goal (fewer incidents and faster releases), co-create a small experiment and let them present it together.
    Q. What should come first?
    • A A slide deck explaining agile
    • B 1:1 conversations to understand each leader's goals
    • C An escalation to the CEO
    • D A new framework

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Systems thinking

    ✍️ Failed releases keep happening. Use the iceberg model to analyse them.
    Model answer: Events: failed releases. Patterns: failures after large quarterly releases. Structures: big batches, manual tests, separate dev and ops goals (speed vs stability). Mental model: "releasing less often is safer". Leverage: shared goals and smaller, automated releases.
    Q. Engineering is rewarded for speed and Operations for stability. What does this most directly create?
    • A Natural conflict built into the system
    • B Perfect alignment
    • C Higher psychological safety
    • D Faster releases

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Emotional intelligence

    ✍️ Teams are silent in retrospectives. What do you do?
    Model answer: Treat it as low psychological safety. Use silent writing and anonymous input, have leaders model fallibility, thank people for raising problems, act visibly on one item, and check SCARF threats (status and certainty from the reorganisation).
    Q. Which is the best sign that psychological safety is improving?
    • A Fewer problems are reported
    • B People raise risks and mistakes earlier
    • C Meetings are shorter
    • D Status reports are always green

    4️⃣ Task 4 · Executive presence

    ✍️ You have 10 minutes with the CEO and leadership team. Outline your message.
    Model answer: Answer first: "Releases fail because our structure sets Engineering and Operations against each other. I recommend one shared reliability-and-speed goal and a 90-day joint pilot." Three reasons with data, the ask (approve the pilot and shared goal), options with trade-offs, then follow up in writing within 24 hours.
    Q. Which opening is strongest?
    • A "Let me explain the history of agile…"
    • B "I recommend a shared goal and a 90-day joint pilot; I need your approval today."
    • C "There are many complex factors…"
    • D "Sorry to take your time…"

    5️⃣ Task 5 · Conflict mediation

    ✍️ How do you mediate between the Heads of Engineering and Operations?
    Model answer: Check the conflict level (contest, level 3). Meet each separately, agree ground rules, let each tell their story with the other summarising, surface interests (speed and stability), brainstorm options (automated testing, feature toggles, joint on-call, shared metrics), agree actions with owners and review in two weeks.
    Q. Both heads care deeply and there is time to work it out. Which Thomas-Kilmann mode should you aim for?
    • A Avoiding
    • B Competing
    • C Collaborating
    • D Accommodating

    Module 01 · Basic · Foundations

    Agile Coach Fundamentals

    Start from zero: what an Agile Coach does, how the role differs from a Scrum Master or manager, and how the six core skills fit together in a real enterprise.

    🧭 What is an Agile Coach?

    An Agile Coach helps teams, leaders and whole organisations learn to work in an agile way and keep improving on their own. A Scrum Master usually serves one or two teams. An Enterprise Agile Coach works across many teams, departments and leadership levels, shaping how the organisation plans, funds, organises and delivers work.

    🏢
    Transform the organisation
    Design and guide the move from traditional to agile ways of working.
    🧩
    Scale agile
    Choose and tailor frameworks such as SAFe, LeSS or the Spotify model.
    🎓
    Coach, mentor and train
    Grow agile capability in teams and leaders so it lasts without the coach.
    🔄
    Improve flow and lead change
    Map value streams, remove waste and help people through change.
    EXAMPLE · The role in one sentence

    An insurance company with 40 teams wants to release every month instead of twice a year. The Enterprise Agile Coach assesses where they are, designs the new team and planning structure, coaches leaders, trains staff, maps the claims value stream and guides people through the change.

    ⚔️ Agile Coach versus related roles

    RoleMain focusTypical scope
    Scrum MasterHelps one or a few teams use Scrum well and removes impediments1–3 teams
    Team-level Agile CoachCoaches several teams on practices, flow and collaboration3–8 teams
    Enterprise Agile CoachShapes structure, portfolio, leadership behaviour and culturePrograms, business units, whole enterprise
    Agile Project ManagerPlans across sprints, manages risk, budget and reportingOne project or program
    Line managerPeople development, hiring, performanceA department or chapter
    Management consultantGives expert answers and recommendationsUsually a defined engagement
    💡
    A coach builds the client's ability to find answers. A consultant gives answers. A good Agile Coach knows when to switch between the two.

    🎭 The coaching stances

    The widely used Agile Coaching Competency Framework (Lyssa Adkins and Michael Spayd) describes the stances a coach moves between. Picking the right stance for the moment is a core skill in itself.

    StanceWhat the coach doesUse it when
    TeachingShares knowledge, concepts and practicesThe group lacks basic knowledge
    MentoringShares own experience and advice as a more experienced practitionerSomeone is growing into a role the coach has done
    Professional coachingAsks powerful questions; the client finds the answerThe client has the knowledge and needs clarity or commitment
    FacilitatingRuns a neutral process so a group reaches its own decisionA group needs to plan, decide or resolve something together
    Domain masteryBrings technical, business or transformation expertiseSpecific expert input is needed (e.g. DevOps, Lean portfolio)
    EXAMPLE · Switching stance

    In a workshop a director asks, "What is WIP?" The coach briefly teaches it, then facilitates the group to set their own WIP limits, and later coaches the director one-to-one on why she keeps adding work.

    🏗️ One enterprise, six skills

    Take a retail bank with 300 people in IT across 30 teams. Releases happen twice a year, and the mobile app is losing customers to competitors.

    Core skillWhat the Agile Coach does at the bank
    TransformationRuns an agility assessment, agrees a vision with executives and plans a pilot-then-scale roadmap
    Scaling frameworksRecommends SAFe for the regulated payments area and a lighter LeSS-style setup for the mobile product
    Enterprise coachingCoaches the CIO and product leaders on decentralised decisions and outcome-based funding
    Mentoring and trainingTrains 300 people by role, mentors new Scrum Masters and starts communities of practice
    Value stream mappingMaps "idea to app store" and finds 70 days of waiting in approvals and manual testing
    Change managementBuilds a guiding coalition, celebrates early wins and handles resistance from middle managers
    EXAMPLE · Which skill first?

    The CIO says, "Just roll out SAFe to everyone next month." The coach starts with transformation (assess and agree outcomes) and change management (readiness), before choosing a scaling framework.

    StepWhat happensWho leads

    📖 Key terms (what is what)

    Agile transformationOrganisation-wide change in structure, process and culture toward agility.
    Scaling frameworkA defined way to coordinate many agile teams, e.g. SAFe or LeSS.
    Value streamAll steps that deliver value to a customer, from request to delivery.
    Lead timeTotal time from request to delivery, including waiting.
    Flow efficiencyActive work time divided by total lead time.
    Coaching stanceThe mode a coach takes: teaching, mentoring, coaching, facilitating.
    Guiding coalitionA group of influential leaders who drive a change together.
    ResistanceNatural pushback to change; information to understand, not a fault.
    Community of practiceVolunteers with a shared skill who learn together.
    Agile Release TrainIn SAFe, a team of agile teams (50–125 people) that plans and delivers together.
    Shu-Ha-RiLearning stages: follow the rules, adapt the rules, go beyond the rules.
    Psychological safetyShared belief that it is safe to speak up and take risks.

    Module 02 · Core Skill 1 · Organizational Agile Transformation

    Organizational Agile Transformation

    Assess where the organisation is, agree outcomes, design the target operating model and roll out change in waves that last.

    🎯 What transformation really means

    An agile transformation changes how the whole organisation works: how teams are formed, how work is funded and prioritised, how decisions are made and how leaders behave. Installing Jira and renaming project managers as Scrum Masters is not a transformation. Real transformation changes structure, process, technology and culture together.

    LayerOld wayAgile way
    StructureTeams by function (dev, test, ops)Long-lived cross-functional teams aligned to value
    FundingAnnual project budgetsFunding value streams or products with quarterly review
    PlanningBig plan up frontRolling plans, regular re-prioritisation
    DecisionsEscalated to the topPushed to the people with the information
    MeasurementOn time, on budget, on scopeOutcomes, flow and customer value
    LeadershipCommand and controlSet direction, remove obstacles, serve
    ⚠️
    "Doing agile" without changing funding, structure and leadership leads to agile theatre: new ceremonies, same results.

    🔍 Assessing the current state

    Before changing anything, the coach builds a clear picture of today. Good assessments mix several sources so no single opinion dominates.

    MethodWhat it showsExample
    InterviewsPain points, beliefs, politics30-minute talks with 25 people across levels
    SurveysPatterns across many peopleTeam health or agility survey for 300 staff
    Delivery metricsActual speed and qualityLead time 120 days, 2 releases a year, 18% change failure rate
    Value stream mappingWhere work waits70 of 90 days spent waiting for approvals
    ObservationWhat really happensSitting in on planning and status meetings
    Culture modelHow information flowsWestrum: pathological, bureaucratic or generative
    EXAMPLE · Assessment summary

    Findings for the bank: teams are organised by technology layer, each feature needs 6 hand-offs, release approval takes 3 weeks, and managers say "agile" but still assign tasks daily. The top three improvement themes are team structure, release automation and leadership behaviour.

    🧭 Vision, outcomes and the case for change

    People support change they understand. The coach helps executives write a short case for change and a set of measurable outcomes. "Become agile" is not an outcome; "cut time-to-market from 6 months to 6 weeks" is.

    Weak goalStrong outcomeMeasure
    Adopt Scrum everywhereDeliver customer features fasterLead time from idea to production
    Train everyone in SAFeImprove predictability of commitmentsProgram predictability measure (planned vs actual business value)
    Use JiraMake work and flow visibleAll work on shared boards; flow metrics reviewed monthly
    Be more collaborativeRaise engagement and retentionEngagement score, attrition rate
    💡
    Tie every outcome to a business problem the executives already feel. That is the fuel for the whole change.

    🏛️ Designing the target operating model

    The target operating model describes how the organisation will work after the change. The coach co-designs it with leaders rather than handing it over.

    Design areaKey questionsCommon choices
    Team structureHow do we organise teams around value?Stream-aligned, platform, enabling and complicated-subsystem teams (Team Topologies)
    RolesWho decides what?Product Owner/Manager, Scrum Master, RTE, chapter leads
    CadenceHow often do we plan and sync?2-week sprints, quarterly big-room planning
    PortfolioHow is work funded and prioritised?Lean portfolio management, value-stream budgets, WSJF
    GovernanceHow do we stay compliant without slowing down?Built-in quality, automated controls, lighter stage gates
    TechnologyCan we release safely and often?CI/CD, test automation, DevOps practices
    📝
    Conway's law: systems mirror the communication structure of the organisation that builds them. If you want a modular product, design teams around those modules.

    🌊 Rollout strategy: pilot, then scale

    ApproachHow it worksRisk
    Big bangEveryone changes on one dateHigh disruption, no time to learn
    Pilot then wavesStart with one value stream, learn, then expandSlower, but lessons reduce later risk
    Grassroots onlyTeams adopt agile without leadership changeHits a ceiling at the management layer
    Top-down mandate onlyLeaders order agile adoptionCompliance without belief; agile theatre
    EXAMPLE · Choosing a pilot

    The coach picks the mobile-app value stream: high business visibility, a sponsor who wants change, about 60 people and few hard dependencies. Success there creates a story others want to follow.

    📊 Measuring transformation progress

    Measure outcomes, not activity. Counting trained people or teams "using Scrum" says little about real results.

    CategoryExample metrics
    Speed and flowLead time, cycle time, flow efficiency, deployment frequency
    QualityChange failure rate, escaped defects, time to restore service
    PredictabilityPlanned vs delivered business value, forecast accuracy
    CustomerNPS, adoption of new features, customer complaints
    PeopleEngagement, psychological safety, attrition
    BusinessRevenue from new features, cost of delay avoided
    📝
    The four DORA metrics are a useful delivery baseline: deployment frequency, lead time for changes, change failure rate and time to restore service.

    🚧 Transformation anti-patterns

    Anti-patternSymptomCoach response
    Agile theatreNew ceremonies, same resultsRefocus on outcomes and remove the real constraints
    Copy-paste frameworkFramework installed without contextTailor to the business and start from real problems
    Frozen middleMiddle managers block changeGive them a clear new role and coach them early
    Training equals transformationEveryone certified, nothing changedFollow training with on-the-job coaching
    Metrics as weaponsVelocity used to compare teamsUse metrics for learning, never for punishment
    No exit planPermanent dependence on external coachesBuild internal coaches and a clear hand-over plan

    Module 03 · Core Skill 2 · Scaling Frameworks (SAFe, LeSS, Spotify Model)

    Scaling Frameworks: SAFe, LeSS & Spotify Model

    Understand how each scaling approach coordinates many teams, when to use which, and how to tailor rather than copy.

    🧩 Why scaling is hard

    One Scrum team is simple. Twenty teams on one product create new problems: dependencies, integration, shared priorities, architecture and funding. Scaling frameworks give a structure for these. None of them fixes a weak team-level practice; scaling bad agile just produces bigger bad agile.

    🔗
    Dependencies
    Team A waits for Team B, which waits for a shared platform team.
    🧪
    Integration
    Each team is "done", but the product does not work end to end.
    📋
    Priorities
    Twenty Product Owners, twenty different top priorities.
    🏗️
    Architecture
    Without shared direction, design drifts and debt grows.
    💡
    First ask, "Can we de-scale?" Fewer teams with clearer ownership often beat a heavier framework.

    🚆 SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework)

    SAFe is the most widely adopted scaling framework. It organises 5–12 teams (about 50–125 people) into an Agile Release Train (ART) that plans together every Planning Interval (PI), usually 8–12 weeks (formerly called Program Increment).

    ElementWhat it is
    ConfigurationsEssential, Large Solution, Portfolio and Full SAFe
    Agile Release TrainLong-lived team of agile teams aligned to a value stream
    PI PlanningTwo-day, all-hands planning event; teams commit to PI objectives and map dependencies on a program board
    Release Train Engineer (RTE)Servant leader and chief Scrum Master of the train
    Product ManagementOwns the ART backlog of features
    System ArchitectGuides architecture across the train
    Business OwnersKey stakeholders who assign business value to PI objectives
    Innovation & Planning (IP) iterationLast iteration of the PI for innovation, planning and Inspect & Adapt
    Inspect & Adapt (I&A)PI demo, quantitative review and problem-solving workshop
    Lean Portfolio ManagementStrategy, funding and governance by value stream
    EXAMPLE · WSJF

    SAFe ranks features with Weighted Shortest Job First: WSJF = Cost of Delay ÷ Job Size, where Cost of Delay = user-business value + time criticality + risk reduction/opportunity enablement. A feature with CoD 21 and size 3 (WSJF 7) goes before one with CoD 30 and size 10 (WSJF 3).

    🧱 LeSS (Large-Scale Scrum)

    LeSS is "Scrum applied to many teams working together on one product." It adds few roles and focuses on de-scaling organisational complexity.

    RuleMeaning
    One Product OwnerA single PO for the whole product
    One Product BacklogOne ordered backlog shared by all teams
    One Definition of DoneCommon quality bar across teams
    One shippable incrementAll teams integrate into one product every sprint
    Common sprintAll teams start and end the sprint together
    Feature teamsCross-functional, cross-component teams that deliver end-to-end features
    Sprint Planning One & TwoPart one: which items each team takes; part two: each team plans how
    Overall RetrospectiveImprove the whole product system, not only each team
    VariantSize
    LeSS2–8 teams
    LeSS Huge8+ teams, split into Requirement Areas each with an Area Product Owner
    📝
    LeSS deliberately avoids extra layers like a program manager role. Coordination happens by teams talking directly, shared sprint reviews and "just talk" norms.

    🎵 The Spotify model

    The Spotify model comes from 2012 papers and videos describing how Spotify organised engineering. It is a set of ideas about autonomy with alignment, not a prescribed framework. Spotify itself has said it kept changing and others should not copy it as-is.

    UnitWhat it isClosest equivalent
    SquadSmall, autonomous, cross-functional team with a long-term missionScrum team
    TribeCollection of squads in a related area, kept under about 100–150 peopleAgile Release Train / business unit
    ChapterPeople with the same skill within a tribe; chapter lead is often the line managerFunctional competency group
    GuildVoluntary community of interest across tribesCommunity of practice
    ⚠️
    Copying squads, tribes and chapters as a reorganisation without Spotify's engineering culture, trust and release automation usually produces renamed silos.

    ⚖️ Comparing the approaches

    AspectSAFeLeSSSpotify model
    PhilosophyComprehensive, prescriptive guidanceMinimal: more with lessCulture and autonomy, not a framework
    Roles addedMany (RTE, Product Management, Architect…)Very fewChapter lead, tribe lead, agile coach
    PlanningPI Planning every 8–12 weeksShared sprint planning every sprintSquads decide; light alignment
    Best fitLarge, regulated, many dependencies, need for portfolio governanceOne product, leadership willing to restructure deeplyStrong engineering culture and high trust
    Main riskBecomes heavy and bureaucraticRequires big organisational changeCopied without the culture
    Other optionsNexus (3–9 Scrum teams), Scrum@Scale, Disciplined Agile
    EXAMPLE · Choosing

    A government agency with strict audit needs and 10 teams on a benefits system chooses SAFe Essential. A start-up scale-up with 6 teams on one app chooses LeSS. Neither copies the Spotify org chart.

    🛠️ Tailoring and launching a scaled setup

    StepCoach focus
    Identify value streamsOrganise around how value reaches customers, not current departments
    Define the product or ARTClear boundaries, one backlog, a named product owner or product manager
    Form teamsCross-functional, stable, minimise hand-offs
    Train rolesLeaders, POs, Scrum Masters and teams trained before launch
    First planning eventFacilitate dependencies, risks (ROAM: Resolved, Owned, Accepted, Mitigated) and objectives
    Inspect and adaptUse real data to tune the model each cycle

    🚧 Scaling traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Scaling before team basics workStabilise team-level agility first
    Component teams with heavy hand-offsMove toward feature teams
    Treating PI Planning as a top-down commitment eventTeams estimate their own capacity and commit to objectives
    Framework as the goalFramework serves business outcomes
    Adding roles instead of removing complexityDe-scale: simplify architecture and ownership

    Module 04 · Core Skill 3 · Enterprise Agile Coaching

    Enterprise Agile Coaching

    Coach leaders, teams and the system itself: contracting, powerful questions, coaching models and coaching at the portfolio level.

    🏢 Levels of enterprise coaching

    LevelClientTypical coaching topics
    TeamA team and its Scrum Master/POFlow, collaboration, quality, retrospectives
    Team of teamsAn ART, program or product groupDependencies, integration, big-room planning
    PortfolioPortfolio and product leadersFunding, prioritisation, WIP limits on initiatives
    LeadershipExecutives and managersMindset, decision-making, behaviour, culture
    Organisation (system)The enterprise as a wholeStructure, policies, incentives, HR and finance practices
    💡
    At enterprise level, the client is often the system, not one person. Ask: "What in our structure produces this behaviour?"

    🤝 Contracting and the coaching agreement

    Every engagement starts with a coaching agreement (sometimes called a coaching alliance). It sets expectations so the coach is not seen as a spy for management or an extra pair of hands.

    Agreement elementExample
    Purpose and goalsHelp the payments ART improve predictability from 55% to 80%
    Sponsor and clientSponsor: VP Payments. Client: the ART leadership team
    Scope and boundariesCoach does not report on individuals
    ConfidentialityWhat is shared in 1:1 coaching stays private unless agreed
    Ways of workingWeekly leadership session, fortnightly retrospective review
    Success measures and durationReview after 3 months; plan hand-over to internal coach
    ⚠️
    A "triangle" exists: sponsor, client and coach. Make expectations explicit with all three, or the coach ends up caught in the middle.

    ❓ Powerful questions and active listening

    Powerful questions are open, short and focused on the client's thinking. They start with "what" or "how" more than "why" (which can sound like blame).

    Instead of…Ask…
    Why did the release fail?What did we learn from this release?
    Don't you think you should limit WIP?What would happen if the team finished work before starting more?
    Have you tried pair programming?What options have you considered?
    Is that a good idea?How will you know if it is working?
    Listening levelFocus
    Level 1 – InternalListening to your own thoughts and reactions
    Level 2 – FocusedFully on the other person's words and meaning
    Level 3 – GlobalWords plus tone, energy, body language and what is not said
    EXAMPLE · Level 3 listening

    A manager says the reorganisation is "fine" while crossing his arms and changing the subject. The coach notices and asks, "What part of it feels less fine?"

    🧭 Coaching models

    ModelStepsUse for
    GROWGoal, Reality, Options, Will (way forward)Structured 1:1 coaching conversations
    Shu-Ha-RiFollow, adapt, transcendChoosing how prescriptive to be with a team
    TuckmanForming, storming, norming, performingReading team development stage
    CynefinClear, complicated, complex, chaotic (plus confusion)Choosing a response to the type of problem
    Solution-focusedLook for what already works and do more of itQuick, positive progress
    EXAMPLE · GROW in practice

    Goal: "I want my teams to deliver without me approving every story." Reality: "I review all 40 stories weekly." Options: clearer acceptance criteria, PO authority, spot checks. Will: "For the next sprint I will review only 5 random stories."

    👔 Coaching leaders

    Leaders shape the system, so leadership coaching often has the highest impact. The coach helps leaders move from controlling work to creating conditions for teams to succeed.

    FromToCoaching move
    Assigning tasksSetting clear outcomes and contextPractise writing intent: "what and why", not "how"
    Status meetingsGoing to see the work (gemba)Attend a sprint review instead of a status report
    Utilisation targetsFlow and outcomesShow WIP and lead-time data
    Blame for failureLearning from experimentsRun a blameless review together
    Deciding everythingDelegating with clear levelsUse a delegation board (Management 3.0: 7 levels from tell to delegate)
    💡
    Model the behaviour you coach: be curious, admit what you do not know and ask for feedback on your own coaching.

    🧪 Coaching the system: observations to experiments

    StepExample
    ObserveFeatures wait 5 weeks for architecture review
    HypothesisEmbedding an architect in planning will cut wait time
    ExperimentFor one PI, the architect attends refinement for two teams
    MeasureReview wait drops from 5 weeks to 4 days
    DecideScale to all teams; update the governance policy
    📝
    Frame changes as safe-to-fail experiments. Leaders accept a small trial far more easily than a permanent policy change.

    🚧 Coaching traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Becoming the team's Scrum MasterCoach the Scrum Master to do it
    Reporting on people to managementKeep confidentiality; report on system patterns
    Always teaching, never coachingCheck whether the client already knows; ask first
    Pushing your favourite frameworkStart from the client's problem and context
    Creating dependencePlan for the coach to become unnecessary

    Module 05 · Core Skill 4 · Mentoring & Training

    Mentoring & Training

    Design learning that changes behaviour: training by role, brain-friendly delivery, mentoring programmes, communities of practice and measuring impact.

    🎓 Training, mentoring and coaching compared

    TrainingMentoringCoaching
    GoalTransfer knowledge and skills to a groupGrow a person through shared experienceUnlock the client's own thinking
    Who knows the answerTrainerMentor shares from experienceClient
    FormatWorkshops, courses1:1 over months1:1 or team sessions
    Example2-day Product Owner courseSenior coach guides a new Scrum MasterHelping a manager decide how to delegate
    💡
    Training alone rarely changes behaviour. The rule of thumb is train, then coach on the job.

    🧠 How adults learn

    Adults learn best when learning is relevant, active and connected to what they already know.

    IdeaWhat it means for agile training
    Training from the Back of the Room (Sharon Bowman)Learners do most of the talking and doing; the 4Cs: Connections, Concepts, Concrete practice, Conclusions
    70-20-10Roughly 70% of learning on the job, 20% from others, 10% formal courses
    Bloom's taxonomyMove from remember and understand to apply, analyse, evaluate and create
    Spaced practiceShort sessions over weeks beat one long event
    Shu-Ha-RiBeginners need rules; experts need principles and freedom
    EXAMPLE · 4Cs session plan (60 minutes)

    Connections (10 min): pairs share their worst planning meeting. Concepts (10 min): short input on sprint goals. Concrete practice (30 min): teams write sprint goals for a real backlog. Conclusions (10 min): each person writes one thing they will try next sprint.

    🗺️ Designing a role-based learning plan

    AudienceLearning needFormat
    ExecutivesWhy agile, their role, funding and governance changesHalf-day leadership workshop + 1:1 coaching
    Middle managersNew role, servant leadership, removing impedimentsWorkshop series + peer circles
    Product Owners/ManagersValue, backlog, stakeholder management, outcomes2–3 day course + mentoring
    Scrum Masters/RTEsFacilitation, coaching, metrics, flowCourse + mentoring + community of practice
    Team membersAgile basics, practices, tools, engineering qualityTeam-based training before team launch
    Support functions (HR, finance)Agile funding, roles and performanceTargeted workshops
    📝
    Train intact teams together where possible. People who learn together form shared language and agreements.

    🌱 Mentoring programmes

    Mentoring grows internal capability so the organisation does not depend on external coaches.

    ElementGood practice
    MatchingPair by growth goal, not just seniority
    AgreementGoals, meeting rhythm (e.g. fortnightly), confidentiality
    Growth pathClear skill levels, e.g. from new Scrum Master to team coach to enterprise coach
    ShadowingMentee observes mentor, then mentor observes mentee and gives feedback
    ReflectionShort reflective log after each session
    EndingPlanned close with review of progress
    EXAMPLE · Shadowing ladder

    Week 1–2: new Scrum Master watches the mentor facilitate a retrospective. Week 3–4: they co-facilitate. Week 5: the mentee leads while the mentor observes and gives feedback afterwards.

    👥 Communities of practice

    A community of practice (CoP) is a voluntary group that shares a skill and learns together, such as a Scrum Master CoP or a Product CoP. It spreads learning across teams and keeps it alive after formal training ends.

    Success factorExample
    A clear purpose"Help every Scrum Master run better retrospectives"
    A coordinator with time10% of a person's time to organise sessions
    Regular rhythmMonthly lean coffee plus a quarterly mini-conference
    Practical contentReal problems from members, not slides from management
    Leadership supportTime protected; leaders attend as learners

    📏 Measuring learning impact

    The Kirkpatrick model evaluates training at four levels. Most organisations stop at level 1; agile coaches aim for levels 3 and 4.

    LevelQuestionExample measure
    1. ReactionDid they like it?Feedback score 4.5/5
    2. LearningDid they learn it?Quiz score, skill demonstration
    3. BehaviourDo they apply it at work?Sprint goals now written for 90% of sprints
    4. ResultsDid business results improve?Fewer carried-over stories; predictability up from 60% to 80%
    ⚠️
    A happy-sheet score of 4.8 does not prove anything changed. Always plan how you will check behaviour 4–8 weeks later.

    🚧 Training traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Slide-heavy lecturesActive, practice-based sessions
    One-size-fits-all curriculumRole-based paths
    Certification as the goalCapability shown in real work
    No follow-upCoaching and CoP after training
    Mentor does the work for the menteeMentor guides; mentee does

    Module 06 · Core Skill 5 · Value Stream Mapping

    Value Stream Mapping

    Map how value flows from request to customer, calculate lead time and flow efficiency, find waste and design a better future state.

    🌊 What is a value stream?

    A value stream is the full sequence of steps an organisation uses to deliver value to a customer, from the first request to value in the customer's hands. Value stream mapping (VSM) is a Lean technique to make that flow visible, including the waiting between steps.

    Type (SAFe terms)Example
    Operational value streamCustomer applies for a loan → loan is approved and paid out
    Development value streamIdea for a mobile feature → feature live in the app store
    💡
    Most delay is between steps, not inside them. VSM makes that waiting visible.

    📐 Key VSM measures

    MeasureMeaningExample
    Process time (PT)Time actively working on the itemCoding takes 3 days
    Lead time (LT)Elapsed time for the step, including waitingCoding step lasts 10 days end to end
    %C&A (percent complete and accurate)Share of work received that needs no reworkTesters say 70% of builds arrive usable
    Total lead timeSum of all step lead times90 days idea to production
    Total process timeSum of all process times12 days of real work
    Flow efficiency (activity ratio)Total process time ÷ total lead time12 ÷ 90 = 13%
    📝
    A flow efficiency of 5–15% is common in knowledge work. Speeding up the work itself helps little; cutting waiting helps a lot.

    🗺️ Running a VSM workshop

    TipWhy
    Map one value stream, end to endPartial maps hide the biggest delays
    Invite people who do the work at each stepManagers often do not know the real flow
    Use real data where possibleTickets and timestamps beat guesses
    Map current state honestlyA pretty map of the ideal process teaches nothing

    🔢 Worked example: idea to production

    StepProcess timeLead time%C&A
    Business case approval1 day20 days80%
    Requirements4 days15 days60%
    Development5 days20 days90%
    Testing3 days15 days75%
    Change approval board0.5 day14 days95%
    Deployment0.5 day6 days90%
    Total14 days90 days
    EXAMPLE · Reading the map

    Flow efficiency = 14 ÷ 90 ≈ 16%. Rolled %C&A = 0.8 × 0.6 × 0.9 × 0.75 × 0.95 × 0.9 ≈ 0.28, so only about 28% of items pass through with no rework. The biggest gains are cutting the 19 days of waiting in approval and fixing requirements quality (60% C&A).

    🗑️ Finding waste

    Lean software waste (Poppendieck)Example
    Partially done work15 stories coded but not tested
    Extra featuresBuilding reports nobody uses
    RelearningRediscovering a fix because it was not documented
    Hand-offsAnalyst → developer → tester → ops
    Task switchingDevelopers on 4 projects at once
    DelaysWaiting 2 weeks for an environment
    DefectsBugs found in production
    💡
    Ask the team: "Where does work wait, and why?" The answer usually points to approvals, hand-offs, shared specialists or environments.

    🎯 Future state and flow metrics

    The future-state map shows the target flow, usually for 6–12 months ahead, and an improvement plan with owners. The Flow Framework (Mik Kersten) offers metrics to track progress after VSM.

    Flow metricMeaning
    Flow velocityNumber of items completed per period
    Flow timeTime from work start to done
    Flow loadNumber of items in progress (WIP)
    Flow efficiencyActive time ÷ flow time
    Flow distributionMix of features, defects, risks and debt
    EXAMPLE · Future state

    Replace the weekly change board with automated checks for standard changes (cuts 14 days), add BDD-style acceptance criteria (raises requirements C&A to 85%), and automate regression tests. Target lead time: 35 days.

    🚧 VSM traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Mapping the "official" processMap what really happens
    Optimising one stepOptimise the whole flow
    No follow-up after the workshopAssign owners and review monthly
    Too much detailKeep 5–12 major steps
    Blaming a departmentFocus on the system and hand-offs

    Enterprise Change Management

    Use proven change models, map stakeholders, work with resistance and sustain the change after the coaches leave.

    🔄 Why change management matters

    Structures and processes can be redesigned on paper in weeks. People take longer. Change management is the discipline of helping individuals and the organisation move from the current state to the future state and stay there.

    Without change managementWith change management
    Announcement by email, then silenceClear story, repeated in many channels
    People hear about role changes lastAffected people involved early
    Resistance treated as a problemResistance treated as information
    Old habits return in 6 monthsNew ways reinforced through structures and rewards

    📚 Key change models

    ModelStepsBest for
    Kotter's 8 steps1 Create urgency, 2 Build a guiding coalition, 3 Form a strategic vision, 4 Enlist a volunteer army, 5 Remove barriers, 6 Generate short-term wins, 7 Sustain acceleration, 8 Institute changeOrganisation-level change planning
    ADKAR (Prosci)Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, ReinforcementIndividual change; diagnosing where a person is stuck
    LewinUnfreeze, Change, RefreezeSimple explanation of change phases
    Satir change modelLate status quo, Foreign element, Chaos, Transforming idea, Practice and integration, New status quoExpecting the dip in performance
    Bridges' transition modelEnding, Neutral zone, New beginningThe emotional side of change
    EXAMPLE · ADKAR diagnosis

    A test manager knows why the change is happening (Awareness) and has been trained (Knowledge), but does not want it because her team is being split. Her gap is Desire, so more training will not help; a conversation about her future role will.

    📉 The performance dip

    Almost every change causes a temporary drop in performance before improvement: the J-curve, or the "chaos" stage in the Satir model. Leaders who are not warned may panic and cancel the change at the worst moment.

    💡
    Tell sponsors in advance: "Expect 1–2 sprints or one PI of lower predictability while teams reform. We will track it and report openly."

    🗺️ Stakeholder mapping and engagement

    Quadrant (power/interest grid)StrategyExample
    High power, high interestManage closely, co-createCIO, business unit heads
    High power, low interestKeep satisfiedCFO, head of compliance
    Low power, high interestKeep informed, involveTeam members, Scrum Masters
    Low power, low interestMonitorUnaffected departments
    Commitment levelMeaning
    ChampionActively promotes the change
    SupporterAgrees and helps when asked
    NeutralWaiting to see
    ScepticDoubts it will work
    BlockerActively works against it
    📝
    Plot where each key stakeholder is today and where you need them to be. The gap shows where to spend coaching time.

    🛡️ Working with resistance

    Resistance is usually a rational response from the person's point of view: fear of losing status, competence or certainty. Treat it as information.

    Source of resistanceWhat you might hearResponse
    Loss of status"So I am not a manager any more?"Clarify the new role and its value
    Loss of competence"I have never worked like this."Training, mentoring, time to practise
    Bad past experience"We tried agile in 2019, it failed."Ask what went wrong; show what is different
    Real concern about risk"How will audit approve this?"Involve them in designing the control
    Overload"I have no time for this."Stop other work; reduce WIP of initiatives
    EXAMPLE · Turning a blocker

    The head of operations blocks continuous delivery over stability fears. The coach invites him to design the deployment safety checks. Three months later he presents the results at the town hall.

    📣 Communication and short-term wins

    PracticeExample
    Repeat the vision oftenOne clear story told by leaders at every forum
    Two-way channelsAsk-me-anything sessions, open Q&A boards
    Show progress visiblyMonthly demo of improved lead time and customer feedback
    Celebrate early winsThe pilot ART cut release time from 3 months to 3 weeks
    Leaders model the changeExecutives attend PI Planning and sprint reviews
    💡
    Plan short-term wins within the first 3–6 months. Without visible results, support for change fades.

    🔒 Sustaining the change

    LeverHow it anchors the change
    HR and performanceTeam goals and behaviours replace individual output targets
    FinanceFunding value streams or products, not projects
    Career pathsRoles such as Product Owner and agile coach have clear growth paths
    Internal coachesCoaching capability built inside the organisation
    GovernancePolicies updated to match the new way of working
    Continuous improvementRegular inspect-and-adapt at every level
    ⚠️
    If performance reviews still reward individual heroics, people will go back to old habits, no matter how good the training was.

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer.

    🧭 Fundamentals

    What does an Enterprise Agile Coach do?
    Helps the organisation adopt and sustain agile ways of working at every level: assessing the current state, designing the operating model, coaching leaders and teams, building capability through training and mentoring, improving flow with value stream mapping and leading people through change.
    How is an Agile Coach different from a Scrum Master?
    A Scrum Master serves one or a few teams on Scrum. An Enterprise Agile Coach works across many teams, leadership and the system: structure, funding, governance and culture.
    Describe the coaching stances you use.
    Teaching, mentoring, professional coaching and facilitating, plus domain expertise. I choose based on what the client needs: teach when knowledge is missing, coach when they have the knowledge but need clarity, facilitate when a group must decide.
    How do you know your coaching is working?
    Agreed outcome measures improve (e.g. lead time, predictability, engagement), behaviour changes are visible, and the client relies less on me over time.

    🏢 Organizational Agile Transformation

    How would you start a transformation in a new organisation?
    Listen first: interviews, surveys, metrics and a value stream map. Then agree a case for change and measurable outcomes with the sponsor, co-design a target model, pilot in one value stream, measure, adjust and scale in waves.
    What is agile theatre and how do you avoid it?
    Agile ceremonies without real change in results. Avoid it by focusing on outcomes, changing funding, structure and leadership behaviour, and removing real constraints such as approval boards.
    What metrics would you use to track a transformation?
    Outcome and flow metrics: lead time, deployment frequency, change failure rate, predictability, customer satisfaction and engagement. Not the number of people trained.
    How do you handle a CEO who wants agile everywhere in three months?
    Acknowledge the urgency, explain the risks of a big bang, propose a fast pilot with visible wins in 90 days and a wave plan, and agree outcome measures so progress is clear.

    🚆 Scaling Frameworks

    Compare SAFe and LeSS.
    SAFe gives detailed guidance with extra roles (RTE, Product Management), PI Planning every 8–12 weeks and portfolio governance; it suits large, regulated settings. LeSS keeps Scrum roles, one PO and one backlog with a common sprint and aims to de-scale; it suits one product where leadership will restructure deeply.
    What is the Spotify model and should we copy it?
    Squads, tribes, chapters and guilds describing autonomy with alignment at Spotify around 2012. It should not be copied; use its principles and fit them to your culture and engineering maturity.
    How do you prepare for the first PI Planning?
    Ready the backlog with Product Management, train all roles, confirm capacity, set business context and vision talks, prepare the program board and facilitation, and plan Business Owner involvement for scoring objectives.
    When would you not use a scaling framework?
    When teams could be decoupled instead, when team-level agility is weak, or when the product is small enough for a few independent teams.

    🧭 Enterprise Coaching and Mentoring & Training

    Walk through a coaching conversation using GROW.
    Goal: what they want; Reality: what is happening now; Options: what they could do; Will: what they will commit to and when. I ask open questions and let the client choose.
    How do you coach a command-and-control manager?
    Build trust, find their goals, use data to show the cost of current behaviour, and agree small experiments such as delegating one decision type, then reflect on results.
    How do you design an agile training programme?
    Role-based learning paths, active brain-friendly sessions, intact team training, then on-the-job coaching and communities of practice. Measure at Kirkpatrick levels 3 and 4.
    How do you build internal coaching capability?
    Select candidates, define a growth path, mentor through shadowing and co-facilitation, give feedback, form a coach community and plan the external coaches' exit.

    🌊 Value Stream Mapping

    Explain value stream mapping.
    A Lean method to map every step from request to delivery, with process time, lead time and %C&A, to find waiting and waste and design a better future state.
    What is flow efficiency?
    Total process time divided by total lead time. If work takes 10 days of effort over 100 days, flow efficiency is 10%.
    A map shows 90 days lead time and 14 days of work. What do you do?
    Focus on the biggest waits, for example approvals and test environments, use %C&A to find rework sources, design a future state and agree owners and dates for improvements.

    🔄 Enterprise Change Management

    Which change models do you use?
    Kotter for organisation-level planning, ADKAR to diagnose individuals, Satir and Bridges for the emotional journey and to prepare leaders for the dip.
    How do you deal with resistance?
    Treat it as information. Find the cause, such as loss of status or competence, involve resisters in solutions, provide support and show quick wins.
    How do you make change stick?
    Align HR, finance, governance and career paths with the new ways of working, build internal coaches and keep inspect-and-adapt at every level.

    🏗️ Scenario: Transform a retail bank

    You join a retail bank as Enterprise Agile Coach. It has 300 people in 30 teams organised by technology layer, releases twice a year, and its mobile app rating has fallen to 3.1 stars. The CIO wants "SAFe for everyone next quarter". Lead time from idea to production is about 120 days, middle managers fear losing their roles, and the risk department worries about compliance.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Transformation

    ✍️ The CIO wants SAFe for everyone next quarter. What do you propose instead, and what outcomes do you agree?
    Model answer: Agree the urgency, then propose a 4–6 week assessment, a pilot on the mobile value stream within the quarter and waves after that. Agree outcomes: cut lead time from 120 to 45 days within 12 months, release monthly, raise the app rating above 4.0 and keep compliance findings at zero.
    Q. What should the coach do first?
    • A Launch SAFe for all 30 teams
    • B Assess the current state and agree measurable outcomes with the sponsor
    • C Buy a new tool
    • D Rename roles

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Scaling

    ✍️ About 70 people work on the mobile app across 7 teams, with dependencies on core banking. Which scaling approach do you suggest and why?
    Model answer: Form one Agile Release Train (7 teams, about 70 people) around the mobile value stream with PI Planning, an RTE and Product Management, because there are real dependencies and compliance needs. Move teams from technology layers to feature teams over time, and include the core banking contact in PI Planning.
    Q. About how many people does a SAFe Agile Release Train usually include?
    • A 5–10
    • B 50–125
    • C 300–500
    • D 1,000+

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Enterprise coaching

    ✍️ The head of mobile reviews every story and assigns tasks daily. How do you coach him?
    Model answer: Agree a coaching agreement. Use GROW: his goal (faster, reliable delivery), reality (he is a bottleneck: 40 reviews a week), options (clear acceptance criteria, PO authority, delegation levels) and will (review only 5 random stories next sprint). Review the impact together after the sprint.
    Q. Which question best fits professional coaching?
    • A Why are you micromanaging?
    • B What would your team need to make these decisions without you?
    • C You should stop reviewing stories.
    • D Have you read about delegation?

    4️⃣ Task 4 · Mentoring & training

    ✍️ Design the learning plan for the pilot ART launch.
    Model answer: Executives: half-day leadership workshop. Managers: workshop series on their new role. Product Managers/POs: 2-day course plus mentoring. Scrum Masters/RTE: course plus mentoring and a CoP. Teams: train as intact teams the week before PI Planning. Follow up with coaching and check behaviour at 6 weeks (Kirkpatrick level 3).
    Q. Which evidence best shows the training worked?
    • A Feedback score of 4.7/5
    • B Everyone got a certificate
    • C Teams now write and meet clear sprint goals and predictability improved
    • D The course finished on time

    5️⃣ Task 5 · Value stream mapping

    ✍️ The mobile value stream has 120 days lead time and 15 days of process time. Change approval takes 20 days, waiting for test environments 25 days. What do you recommend?
    Model answer: Flow efficiency = 15 ÷ 120 = 12.5%. Target the biggest waits: automate standard change approval with built-in controls (agreed with risk), provide on-demand test environments, and improve requirements quality. Future-state target: about 45 days lead time, with owners and monthly review.
    Q. Process time is 15 days and lead time 120 days. What is flow efficiency?
    • A 8%
    • B 12.5%
    • C 15%
    • D 80%

    6️⃣ Task 6 · Change management

    ✍️ Middle managers fear losing their roles and are quietly blocking. How do you handle it?
    Model answer: Diagnose with ADKAR: they lack Desire because of loss of status. Clarify new roles (chapter leads, product leaders, coaches), involve them in designing the model, give them early training and coaching, make them visible champions of early wins, and align HR career paths.
    Q. Managers understand the change but do not want it because of status loss. Which ADKAR element is missing?
    • A Awareness
    • B Desire
    • C Knowledge
    • D Ability

    Module 01

    Product Owner — Technical Skills

    Learn the tools a Product Owner uses every day: product management platforms (Aha!, ProdPad), Jira, user analytics (Mixpanel, Google Analytics) and wireframing (Figma, Miro).

    🎯
    Role focus: Product Management Tools (Aha!, ProdPad, Jira) · User Analytics (Mixpanel, Google Analytics) · Wireframing Basics (Figma, Miro).

    📘 Course overview

    A Product Owner does not need to code, but must be fluent with tools that capture ideas, plan work, measure behavior and visualize solutions. Tools do not replace thinking; they make decisions faster, clearer and traceable.

    🧭
    Learning path: capture ideas and feedback → plan roadmap and delivery → measure usage → sketch solutions → feed learning back.
    🗓️
    Plan
    Roadmaps, ideas and feedback in Aha! and ProdPad.
    📋
    Deliver
    Epics, stories and boards in Jira.
    📈
    Measure
    Events, funnels and retention in Mixpanel and GA.
    🖼️
    Visualize
    Wireframes and flows in Figma and Miro.

    🎯 Learning objectives

    • Choose the right tool for planning, delivery, measurement or design.
    • Capture and organize ideas and customer feedback in a product management tool.
    • Build a roadmap that links goals, themes and releases.
    • Configure Jira boards, workflows and filters for a product team.
    • Define events and read funnels, cohorts and retention reports.
    • Compare what Mixpanel and Google Analytics measure best.
    • Sketch low-fidelity wireframes and user flows in Figma and Miro.
    • Connect all tools into one product feedback loop.

    Module 02

    Aha! Essentials

    Learn how Aha! connects strategy, ideas and releases in one traceable product workspace.

    🗓️ Aha! · Why dedicated product tools

    Spreadsheets and slides struggle with links between goals, ideas and releases. Product management platforms keep strategy, ideas, feedback and roadmaps in one traceable place.

    🧰 Aha! · Overview

    • Strategy: goals, initiatives and visual strategy boards.
    • Ideas: portals to collect and vote on ideas.
    • Roadmaps: timeline, release and theme views.
    • Scoring: custom score cards on ideas and features.
    • Integrations: two-way sync with Jira.

    🪜 Aha! · Strategy to features hierarchy

    Aha! links levels so every feature traces upward.

    Goals → Initiatives → Releases → Epics → Features
    Typical Aha! hierarchy

    ⚖️ Aha! · Scoring ideas

    • Create a custom score card such as customer value, effort and strategic fit.
    • Score each idea with the team.
    • Sort by total score and review outliers.
    • Keep scores as input; decide with judgement.

    🛠️ Aha! · Hands-on practice

    • Create a free trial workspace.
    • Add three goals and link five ideas to them.
    • Build a release with two features.

    Module 03

    ProdPad Essentials

    Learn ProdPad's feedback-to-idea workflow, objectives and flexible roadmap views.

    🧰 ProdPad · Overview

    • Ideas and feedback inbox linked to customer requests.
    • Objectives and key results tied to roadmap items.
    • Now / Next / Later roadmap that avoids fixed dates.
    • Shared roadmap views for teams and stakeholders.
    • Integrations with Jira and support tools.

    📥 ProdPad · Feedback to ideas workflow

    Capture customer feedback in the inbox, link each item to a matching idea, then review ideas against objectives. Demand becomes visible without losing the voices behind it.

    🔗 ProdPad · Roadmap formats

    FormatBest for
    TimelineStakeholders needing dates
    Now / Next / LaterOutcome-based, flexible planning
    Theme-basedStrategy communication
    ReleaseLaunch coordination

    ⚖️ Tools · Choosing between Aha! and ProdPad

    Aha!

    Strong in strategy, detailed scoring and date-based release planning.

    Fits larger organizations.

    ProdPad

    Strong in lean roadmaps, feedback to idea linking and outcome focus.

    Fits fast-moving product teams.

    🧪 ProdPad · Practical example

    Support logs 30 requests for CSV export. The PO tags them to one idea, scores it on reach and strategy fit, links it to the objective improve reporting, and places it in Next on the roadmap with a note on why.

    ⚠️ ProdPad · Common mistakes

    ⚠️
    Using the roadmap as a dumping ground. Every roadmap item should trace to an objective.

    Module 04

    Jira Setup & Workflow

    Set up a clean Jira project with the right hierarchy, workflow, board and fields.

    📋 Jira · Issue hierarchy

    LevelMeaningExample
    EpicLarge body of workWishlist feature
    StoryUser-valued sliceAdd item to wishlist
    Task / Sub-taskWork stepCreate API endpoint
    BugDefectHeart icon not saving

    🔄 Jira · Workflow and statuses

    A workflow is the set of statuses an item moves through. Keep it simple, for example To Do → In Progress → In Review → Done. Align the Done status with your team Definition of Done.

    🧭 Jira · Boards

    • Scrum board: sprints, backlog and burndown.
    • Kanban board: continuous flow with WIP limits.
    • Backlog view: drag to rank; top is next.

    🧩 Jira · Epics and versions

    Use epics for features and versions for releases. Fill descriptions, acceptance criteria and links so each item is self-explanatory.

    🏷️ Jira · Fields, labels and components

    • Priority: use few levels with clear meaning.
    • Labels: lightweight tags for themes.
    • Components: parts of the product.
    • Avoid custom fields nobody reads.

    Module 05

    Jira Filters, JQL & Reports

    Find work fast with JQL and use dashboards and reports to support decisions.

    🔎 Jira · Filters and JQL

    JQL is the Jira Query Language for finding issues.

    GoalExample JQL
    Open bugstype = Bug AND resolution = Unresolved
    My team sprint itemssprint in openSprints() AND assignee = currentUser()
    Stale storiesstatus = "To Do" AND updated <= -30d

    💾 Jira · Saved filters and subscriptions

    Save frequently used filters, share them with the team and subscribe to a daily or weekly email, for example unresolved bugs on the current release.

    📊 Jira · Dashboards and reports

    • Burndown: remaining work in a sprint.
    • Velocity: delivered points per sprint, used for forecasting.
    • Cumulative flow: reveals bottlenecks.
    • Release burndown: progress to a version.

    📝 Jira · Reporting in practice

    Use the burndown in standups, velocity for forecasting and a release burndown for stakeholder updates. Pair charts with a short written summary.

    ⚠️ Jira · Common mistakes

    ⚠️
    Hundreds of unread tickets, too many custom fields, and using velocity as a performance score. Keep Jira lean and use it to support conversation, not replace it.

    Module 06

    Analytics Foundations

    Understand the concepts, metrics and tracking plan behind trustworthy product analytics.

    📈 Analytics · Core concepts

    TermMeaning
    EventA user action such as Signup Completed
    PropertyDetail on an event or user, such as plan type
    FunnelOrdered steps and drop-off between them
    CohortGroup of users sharing a trait or start date
    RetentionShare of users who return over time

    🏴‍☠️ Analytics · AARRR and North Star

    AARRR (Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue, Referral) is a simple lens across the user lifecycle. A North Star metric is the one number that best shows delivered customer value, such as weekly active teams.

    🧩 Analytics · Tracking plan

    • Start from a business question.
    • Name events as Object + Action in past tense.
    • Define required properties for each event.
    • Document the plan so engineering and analytics match.
    • Avoid tracking everything; track what supports decisions.

    🔐 Analytics · Data quality and privacy

    • Validate events in a test environment before release.
    • Keep naming consistent.
    • Respect consent and privacy rules in your region.
    • Avoid collecting personal data you do not need.

    Module 07

    Mixpanel & Google Analytics

    Use Mixpanel for in-product behavior and Google Analytics for acquisition, and read funnels and cohorts.

    🧰 Mixpanel · Overview

    • Event-based product analytics.
    • Funnels, flows, retention and segmentation.
    • Tracks identified users across sessions and devices.
    • Best for questions about in-product behavior.

    🧰 Google Analytics · Overview

    • GA4 is event-based, with strong acquisition and web reporting.
    • Shows traffic sources, campaigns, pages and conversions.
    • Best for marketing and website performance.
    • Integrates with Google Ads and Search Console.

    Mixpanel asks

    Which feature do retained users use?

    In-product behavior.

    Google Analytics asks

    Which channel brings converting visitors?

    Acquisition and web.

    🔻 Funnels · Reading a funnel

    Sign-up funnel: Visit 1000 → Start form 400 → Submit 200 → Verify 150. The largest relative drop is Visit to Start (60%). Investigate there first, with qualitative research to learn why.

    📅 Retention · Cohorts in practice

    Compare cohorts by sign-up week. If recent cohorts keep more users after day 7 than older ones, your onboarding changes are working. Always check sample size.

    🧪 Analytics · Practical example

    After an onboarding change, week-1 retention for the new cohort is 38% against 30% before. The PO checks sample size and seasonality, then keeps the change and plans the next experiment.

    ⚠️ Analytics · Common mistakes

    ⚠️
    Vanity metrics such as page views, tracking without a plan, and confusing correlation with causation. Pair numbers with user research and run controlled tests when possible.

    Module 08

    Wireframing & Figma

    Learn what wireframes are, when to use them and how to build one in Figma.

    🖼️ Wireframes · What and why

    A wireframe is a simple layout of a screen showing structure, content and actions without final visual design.

    FidelityLookUse
    LowBoxes and labelsEarly ideas and quick feedback
    MidReal text and spacingReview with the team
    HighNear-final visualsUsability tests and handoff

    🧰 Figma · Basics

    • Frames represent screens.
    • Shapes, text and auto layout build structure.
    • Components keep repeated elements consistent.
    • Prototype links connect screens into a clickable flow.
    • Comments let stakeholders give feedback in place.

    ✍️ Wireframes · Principles

    • One primary action per screen.
    • Clear hierarchy: what is seen first.
    • Consistent patterns and spacing.
    • Design empty, loading and error states.
    • Annotate behavior, not just layout.

    📐 Figma · Auto layout and components

    Auto layout makes frames resize with their content. Components let you edit one button and update every use, which keeps wireframes consistent.

    ⚠️ Wireframes · Common mistakes

    ⚠️
    Polishing visuals too early, skipping flows, and designing alone. Keep fidelity low until the idea is validated.

    Module 09

    Miro, Flows & Handoff

    Map user flows in Miro and hand wireframes to the team through Jira stories.

    🧰 Miro · Basics

    • Infinite board for brainstorming and mapping.
    • Sticky notes, shapes and connectors for user flows.
    • Templates for journey maps and story maps.
    • Voting and timer tools for workshops.

    Use Figma for

    Screens, components and clickable prototypes.

    Design detail.

    Use Miro for

    Workshops, flows, maps and early sketches.

    Thinking together.

    🧭 Flows · User flows

    A user flow shows the path from entry to goal. Draw start, steps, decisions and end. Check error and empty states.

    Open app → Search → Select item → Add to cart → Pay → Confirmation
    Simple purchase flow

    🤝 Handoff · From wireframe to Jira story

    • Attach the Figma frame link to the story.
    • Write acceptance criteria that match the screen states.
    • Review the design with engineers before the sprint.
    • Update the wireframe when decisions change.

    🛠️ Wireframes · Hands-on practice

    • Sketch a login screen in Figma with two states.
    • Map a sign-up flow in Miro with one decision point.
    • Share both for feedback and record changes.

    Final Preparation

    Interview Preparation

    Prepare to explain tool choices, analytics reasoning and design collaboration with practical examples.

    🎤 Interview preparation

    Interviewers want to see that tools support your decisions. Name the tool, the task and the result.

    💡
    Answer principle: situation → tool → action → result.

    💬 Common interview questions

    Tools
    How do you choose between Aha!, ProdPad and a simple spreadsheet?
    Jira
    How do you keep a Jira backlog clean and useful?
    Analytics
    What is the difference between Mixpanel and Google Analytics?
    Metrics
    How would you investigate a drop in sign-up conversion?
    Funnels
    What does a retention curve tell you?
    Design
    How do you use wireframes to align stakeholders?

    🧠 Answer framework

    StepWhat to explain
    1. SituationThe product problem.
    2. ToolWhich tool and why.
    3. ActionWhat you configured or analyzed.
    4. ResultDecision or metric change.
    5. LearningWhat you would do differently.

    🎯 Interview practice

    💡
    Practice question: “Activation dropped 10% after a release. Walk me through your investigation.”

    Strong answers check the release timeline, segment by platform and cohort, inspect funnel steps in Mixpanel, review session recordings, then verify with the team and test a fix.

    Module 01 · Basic

    Technical Skills Fundamentals — Role, Responsibilities & Core Concepts

    Build a practical foundation for planning, scheduling, digital work management, financial control and technical decision-making.

    🧭 What Are Technical Skills?

    Technical skills are the practical abilities used to plan, organise, analyse, control and improve technical work. In this role, technical capability is not limited to knowing software; it means turning requirements and data into a controlled plan, maintaining accurate information, analysing performance and supporting sound technical decisions.

    💡
    Role principle: tools are only useful when the underlying data, logic, assumptions and decisions are correct.

    🛠️ Technical Skills Role Overview

    A technical-skills professional works across the flow from requirement → plan → schedule → execution → measurement → analysis → decision → improvement. The role naturally combines project planning, scheduling, digital work management and financial control.

    📋
    Translate requirements
    Convert scope, specifications, milestones and expectations into actionable work.
    🗺️
    Build the plan
    Structure deliverables, activities, resources, dependencies and dates.
    📅
    Control time
    Maintain schedules, Gantt views, baselines, milestones and forecasts.
    💻
    Use digital tools
    Apply MS Project, Primavera P6 and Smartsheet appropriately.
    💰
    Control cost
    Connect planned work with budgets, actuals, commitments and forecasts.
    📊
    Support decisions
    Turn project information into clear reports, trends and actions.

    🎯 Responsibilities & Work Standards

    Technical work should be accurate, traceable, timely and decision-oriented. The professional is expected to maintain reliable data while coordinating with stakeholders and delivery teams.

    ResponsibilityExpected practice
    PlanningBuild realistic plans from approved scope and assumptions.
    SchedulingMaintain logic, dates, calendars, milestones and baselines.
    Data managementKeep source information consistent, current and traceable.
    Cost controlCompare plan, actuals, commitments and forecast.
    ReportingPresent exceptions, trends, risks and recommended actions.
    CoordinationWork with delivery, finance, procurement and leadership teams.
    GovernanceFollow approved change, approval and document-control processes.

    🧩 Types of Technical Work

    Technical work can be grouped by the decision it supports rather than by the software used.

    Work typeTypical output
    PlanningWBS, activity list, resource plan and assumptions
    SchedulingGantt chart, network logic, milestones and baseline
    MonitoringProgress update, variance report and forecast
    Financial controlBudget, cost report, commitments and estimate at completion
    CollaborationAction register, workflow, dashboard and status information
    AnalysisTrend, root cause, scenario comparison and recommendation
    GovernanceChange record, approval trail and controlled reporting

    🔄 Core Work Process

    A repeatable technical process reduces errors and makes work easier to review.

    StepPractical question
    1. UnderstandWhat exactly is required and what is the source of truth?
    2. StructureWhat deliverables and work packages are needed?
    3. PlanWho will do the work, with what resources and assumptions?
    4. ScheduleWhen will activities happen and what depends on what?
    5. Execute / updateWhat actually happened?
    6. AnalyseWhat changed, why, and what is the forecast?
    7. DecideWhat action, approval or escalation is required?
    8. ImproveWhat should be changed in the next planning cycle?

    📖 Key Terms & Fundamentals

    WBSHierarchical breakdown of project scope into manageable work packages.
    ActivityA scheduled unit of work with attributes such as duration, dates and relationships.
    MilestoneA significant checkpoint or event with no planned duration.
    BaselineAn approved reference plan used to compare actual performance.
    DependencyA logical relationship that controls sequence between activities.
    FloatThe amount of schedule flexibility available before a date constraint is affected.
    ActualWhat has happened or been incurred to date.
    ForecastThe current expectation of future or final performance.
    VarianceThe difference between planned/baselined and actual or forecast values.
    DashboardA concise visual view of selected performance information.
    CommitmentA cost obligation that may not yet have become an actual expense.
    EACEstimate at Completion: the current expected final cost.

    Module 02 · Intermediate

    Planning & Work Breakdown

    Turn requirements into structured deliverables, resources, dependencies and actionable work packages.

    📋 Understanding Work Requirements

    Good technical planning begins by identifying the approved source of requirements. Requirements may come from scope statements, contracts, specifications, approved drawings, schedules, budgets, stakeholder decisions or controlled change requests.

    📌
    Never build a detailed plan from an unverified assumption when an approved source is available.
    Requirement checkWhat to confirm
    ScopeWhat is included and excluded?
    DeliverablesWhat must be produced or completed?
    AcceptanceHow will completion be recognised?
    ConstraintsWhat budget, date, resource or technical limits apply?
    DependenciesWhat must happen before or after the work?
    AuthorityWho can approve changes or decisions?

    🧱 Work Breakdown & Deliverables

    Break work down until each work package is clear enough to estimate, schedule, assign and monitor. Avoid creating hundreds of tiny activities that add administration without improving control.

    LevelExample
    OutcomeNew production facility ready for operation
    DeliverableProduction line installed
    Work packageEquipment installation
    ActivityPosition equipment and complete mechanical connections
    Task detailVerify anchor positions against approved layout

    👥 Resource & Capacity Planning

    Resource planning matches workload with available people, equipment, specialist capability and time. A technically correct schedule can still fail if the assigned resource is unavailable or overloaded.

    🧑‍🔧
    People
    Consider skill, availability, workload and accountability.
    🏗️
    Equipment
    Check capacity, access, utilisation and maintenance constraints.
    🏢
    Suppliers
    Consider lead times, commitments and external dependencies.
    ⏱️
    Capacity
    Identify overloads before they become schedule delays.

    ⛓️ Priorities, Dependencies & Constraints

    Priority tells the team what deserves attention first; dependency tells the team what must happen in relation to other work; a constraint limits the available options.

    ConceptExample
    PriorityCritical commissioning activity receives attention before a low-impact improvement.
    DependencyTesting starts only after installation is complete.
    ConstraintA specialist is available only for two days.
    AssumptionA supplier is expected to deliver by an agreed date.
    RiskThe supplier may miss the date and affect downstream work.

    ✅ Planning Best Practices

    • Use consistent activity naming and coding.
    • Document major assumptions.
    • Assign accountable owners.
    • Use realistic durations rather than desired dates.
    • Review dependencies with the people doing the work.
    • Protect approved baselines from informal changes.
    • Keep source data and reporting definitions consistent.
    ✓
    Practice activity: Take a familiar project and create five deliverables, three work packages under each, and identify the top five dependencies.

    Module 03 · Intermediate

    Scheduling & Gantt Charting

    Build and control time-based plans using logic, milestones, baselines, float and recovery methods.

    📅 Scheduling Fundamentals

    Scheduling converts structured work into a time-based execution model. A useful schedule answers what, who, when, in what sequence and with what flexibility.

    Schedule elementPurpose
    DurationHow long the activity is expected to take.
    Start / finishPlanned timing.
    RelationshipLogical link to another activity.
    CalendarDefines working time and exceptions.
    MilestoneMarks an important checkpoint.
    ConstraintLimits when an activity can occur.
    BaselineApproved reference for performance comparison.

    📊 Gantt Charts

    A Gantt chart shows activities against time. It is useful for communicating the plan, spotting overlap, seeing milestones and comparing planned dates with current dates.

    📌
    Task bars
    Show planned activity duration.
    🔗
    Links
    Show predecessor and successor relationships.
    🏁
    Milestones
    Highlight major checkpoints.
    📏
    Baseline
    Make schedule movement visible.
    📈
    Progress
    Show actual or remaining work.

    ⛓️ Dependencies, Milestones & Critical Path

    Most schedules use logical relationships such as Finish-to-Start. The critical path is the sequence that controls the earliest possible completion date under the current schedule logic.

    RelationshipMeaning
    Finish-to-StartSuccessor starts after predecessor finishes.
    Start-to-StartSuccessor starts after predecessor starts.
    Finish-to-FinishSuccessor finishes after predecessor finishes.
    Start-to-FinishSuccessor cannot finish until predecessor starts.
    💡
    Do not label an activity “critical” because it feels important. Use schedule logic and calculated float to determine criticality.

    📏 Progress Tracking & Baselines

    Updating a schedule means recording what actually happened and revising the remaining work based on evidence. A baseline lets you distinguish plan movement from the current forecast.

    Update fieldExample
    Actual startWork genuinely began on 8 June.
    Actual finishTesting completed on 15 June.
    Remaining durationTwo days remain for close-out.
    Forecast finishCurrent logic predicts 19 June.
    Baseline finishApproved plan was 16 June.

    🚀 Schedule Recovery

    When a schedule slips, first identify the cause and affected path. Recovery options should be evaluated for cost, risk, quality and feasibility.

    Recovery methodTypical effect
    ResequenceChange activity order where logic allows.
    Fast-trackOverlap work that was previously sequential, increasing coordination risk.
    CrashAdd resources or capacity, increasing cost.
    Scope decisionRemove, defer or redesign nonessential work through approved change control.
    Constraint removalResolve a supplier, access, approval or resource blocker.
    🧪
    Practice activity: Build a 10-activity Gantt plan and identify the critical path. Then delay one critical activity by three days and propose two recovery options.

    Module 04 · Advanced

    MS Project & Digital Planning

    Use Microsoft Project to build, baseline, update and analyse practical project schedules.

    🖥️ MS Project Fundamentals

    Microsoft Project can support task planning, dependencies, calendars, resources, baselines, progress updates and schedule analysis. Its value depends on disciplined project data rather than simply creating attractive Gantt charts.

    CapabilityPractical use
    Task hierarchyRepresent WBS and work packages.
    LinksCreate schedule logic.
    CalendarsRepresent working time.
    ResourcesAssign people or other capacity.
    BaselineCapture approved schedule.
    TrackingRecord actual and remaining work.

    🧱 Building a Practical Schedule

    A practical MS Project schedule should begin with the WBS and then add activities, durations, relationships and milestones. Avoid hard-coding dates when logic can calculate them.

    1. Create the work breakdown.
    2. Enter activities and milestones.
    3. Estimate realistic durations.
    4. Set logical predecessors and successors.
    5. Apply appropriate calendars.
    6. Review the resulting dates and critical path.
    7. Save the approved baseline.

    ⚙️ Resources, Calendars & Baselines

    Resource-aware planning helps expose capacity conflicts. Calendars should reflect actual working conditions such as shifts, weekends, holidays or planned shutdowns.

    ControlCommon mistakeBetter practice
    ResourcesAssuming unlimited capacityCheck availability and overloads.
    CalendarUsing a generic five-day weekReflect actual working time.
    BaselineChanging the plan without preserving the referenceRebaseline only through approved governance.
    DatesTyping dates to force the desired resultLet dependencies drive dates where possible.

    📈 Updating & Analysing Performance

    During execution, update actual starts, actual finishes, remaining duration and progress. Then analyse variance rather than simply moving future dates until the report looks healthy.

    QuestionEvidence
    Are milestones late?Baseline vs forecast milestone dates
    Why is the project late?Critical path, blockers and dependency changes
    Is a resource overloaded?Resource utilisation and assignments
    What changed?Actuals, approved changes and schedule history

    🧪 Practical Activity

    🖥️
    Exercise: Create a small project in MS Project with 15 activities, at least 3 milestones, 10 logical dependencies, two resource constraints and a baseline. Update five activities as actuals and explain the resulting variance.

    Module 05 · Advanced

    Primavera P6 & Integrated Scheduling

    Develop advanced scheduling capability with structured networks, calendars, float, critical paths and forecasting.

    📐 Primavera P6 Fundamentals

    Primavera P6 is used for structured planning and scheduling, especially where projects contain complex activity networks, multiple calendars, resources and detailed progress updates.

    ConceptPractical meaning
    EPSEnterprise Project Structure used to organise projects.
    WBSBreaks project scope into manageable hierarchy.
    ActivityBasic schedulable unit of work.
    RelationshipLogical connection between activities.
    CalendarDefines working and non-working periods.
    FloatSchedule flexibility.
    BaselineApproved reference schedule.

    🗂️ EPS, WBS & Activity Structures

    A strong P6 structure makes it possible to filter, summarise and report work at the right level. The WBS should represent how the project is managed, not merely how a spreadsheet happens to be organised.

    📌
    Use coding and consistent naming so activities can be grouped by discipline, area, contractor, work package or reporting need.

    ⛓️ Logic, Calendars, Float & Critical Path

    P6 schedule quality depends heavily on network logic and realistic calendars. Review open ends, excessive constraints, negative float and unexpected critical paths before relying on the forecast.

    ReviewWhy it matters
    Logic completenessReduces activities that float independently of the network.
    Calendar correctnessPrevents unrealistic working dates.
    FloatHighlights flexibility or schedule pressure.
    Critical pathShows the chain controlling completion.
    ConstraintsIdentifies imposed dates that may distort logic.

    📈 Updating & Forecasting

    Progress updates should be evidence-based. Record actual dates, physical progress or remaining duration according to the project's agreed update method, then recalculate the schedule.

    Update practiceGood approach
    Data dateUse a clearly defined status date.
    ActualsEnter what actually occurred.
    Remaining workEstimate from current evidence.
    ForecastReview milestone and completion movement.
    NarrativeExplain major changes, causes and actions.

    🧠 Advanced Schedule Control

    At advanced level, schedule control includes logic quality checks, baseline comparison, critical-path analysis, scenario planning and recovery evaluation.

    • Review activities with unusually high float or excessive constraints.
    • Investigate negative float and impossible dates.
    • Use what-if scenarios before committing recovery actions.
    • Compare current forecast with approved baseline.
    • Connect schedule risks to cost and resource consequences.
    🧪
    Practice: Create two recovery scenarios for a delayed commissioning sequence and compare completion date, additional cost and execution risk.

    Module 06 · Advanced

    Smartsheet, Collaboration & Reporting

    Manage shared work, workflows, dashboards, status reporting and data governance.

    🤝 Collaborative Work Management

    Technical work increasingly crosses teams. Collaborative work management provides a shared place to assign ownership, track status, collect updates and surface exceptions.

    NeedUseful mechanism
    OwnershipNamed owner and due date
    StatusControlled status values
    EvidenceAttachments, links or comments
    EscalationFlag, alert or exception workflow
    VisibilityDashboard or filtered report

    🗂️ Smartsheet Fundamentals

    Smartsheet can support collaborative task tracking, structured sheets, forms, workflows, dashboards and cross-team reporting. It is particularly useful when many people need to contribute updates without directly editing complex scheduling models.

    📋
    Work tracker
    Track actions, owners, dates and status.
    🔄
    Workflow
    Route approvals, reminders or status changes.
    📊
    Dashboard
    Summarise key project information for stakeholders.
    📝
    Forms
    Collect standardised information from contributors.

    ⚙️ Dashboards, Workflows & Automation

    Automation should reduce repetitive administration without hiding important decisions. Before automating, define the source data, trigger, owner, exception condition and expected action.

    Automation elementExample
    TriggerStatus changes to “Ready for Approval”.
    ActionNotify the assigned approver.
    ConditionOnly items above a defined threshold are escalated.
    EvidenceApproval is recorded with date and user.
    ExceptionOverdue items appear on an escalation dashboard.

    📊 Status Reporting

    A strong technical report is concise but decision-ready. It should separate facts from interpretation and highlight what management needs to know.

    Report areaContent
    Overall statusCurrent health and material change.
    ScheduleMilestones, forecast and major variance.
    CostBudget, actual, commitment and forecast.
    Risks / issuesExposure, owner and action.
    DecisionsApproval or escalation required.
    Next periodImmediate priorities.

    🛡️ Data Quality & Governance

    Digital tools amplify bad data as efficiently as good data. Governance establishes who owns fields, how values are defined, when data is updated and how changes are controlled.

    • Use controlled status and category values.
    • Define mandatory fields.
    • Remove duplicate records.
    • Use consistent date and currency conventions.
    • Protect formulas and reference data.
    • Keep a clear owner for every critical dataset.

    Module 07 · Advanced

    Financial Management & Cost Control

    Connect technical work to budgets, actuals, commitments, forecasts and evidence-based financial decisions.

    💰 Financial Management Fundamentals

    Financial management connects technical work to money. A technical professional should understand enough financial control to explain what was planned, what has been spent or committed, what remains and where the final result is heading.

    TermMeaning
    EstimateExpected cost based on assumptions and information.
    BudgetApproved financial plan.
    Actual costCost incurred to date.
    CommitmentObligation that may not yet be actual spend.
    ForecastCurrent expectation of future/final cost.
    VarianceDifference between plan and actual/forecast.
    EACCurrent estimate of final cost.

    📈 Estimating, Budgeting & Baselines

    A good estimate is transparent about assumptions, quantities, rates, timing and uncertainty. Once approved, the budget becomes a control reference.

    PracticeWhy it matters
    Document assumptionsMakes the estimate reviewable.
    Use work breakdownConnects cost to scope.
    Identify uncertaintyImproves contingency and decision quality.
    Approve baselineCreates a stable reference.
    Control changesPrevents hidden budget growth.

    🧮 Actuals, Commitments & Forecasts

    Looking only at actual cost can be misleading when major commitments or future work remain. A complete view considers actuals, commitments and the cost still required to finish.

    ViewQuestion
    ActualWhat has already been incurred?
    CommitmentWhat have we already obligated ourselves to pay?
    Remaining estimateWhat additional cost is needed?
    EACWhere do we now expect to finish?
    VarianceHow far are we from the approved plan?

    🔎 Variance & Performance Analysis

    Variance is useful only when it leads to understanding and action. Investigate material deviations by asking what changed, why it changed, whether it is temporary or structural, and what happens if nothing is done.

    Variance typePossible cause
    Labour costOvertime, productivity, rate change or resource mix.
    Material costPrice change, quantity change or scope change.
    Schedule costDelay causing extended labour or equipment use.
    Forecast growthNew information or previously missed work.
    Favourable variancePotentially genuine saving or simply delayed spend.

    ⚖️ Financial Decision Making

    Technical decisions often involve trade-offs. A cheaper option is not automatically better if it increases schedule, quality, operational or risk exposure.

    DecisionCompare
    Add resourcesCost vs schedule recovery and execution risk.
    Change supplierPrice vs lead time, capability and risk.
    Defer scopeSavings vs business value and downstream impact.
    Invest in automationUpfront cost vs recurring benefit and payback.
    Accept varianceShort-term impact vs final forecast and strategic need.
    💡
    Practice activity: Given a project with a budget of ₹10 lakh, actual cost of ₹6 lakh and ₹3 lakh of committed work, prepare three possible final-cost scenarios and explain the management action for each.

    Module 08 · Expertise

    Advanced Technical Practice & Expertise

    Integrate tools, manage uncertainty, communicate decisions and build repeatable expert-level control.

    🔗 Integrated Planning Across Tools

    Expert practice is not about using every tool for everything. It is about assigning each tool a clear purpose and maintaining consistent information between them.

    Tool / methodBest-fit purpose
    MS ProjectDetailed project scheduling and task/resource control.
    Primavera P6Complex, logic-rich project scheduling and integrated planning.
    SmartsheetCollaborative tracking, workflows, forms and dashboards.
    Gantt chartCommunication and visual time-based planning.
    Financial toolsBudget, actual, commitment, forecast and variance control.
    📌
    Avoid maintaining multiple independent “sources of truth” for the same critical date or cost value. Define the system of record and the reporting interface.

    🚨 Risk, Crisis & Decision Making

    Advanced technical work requires judgement under uncertainty. When a major risk becomes an issue, stabilise the situation, establish reliable facts, identify decision authority and compare practical options.

    StepAction
    StabiliseProtect safety, critical operations and decision quality.
    AssessDetermine impact on scope, schedule, cost and stakeholders.
    OptionsDevelop realistic responses and trade-offs.
    DecideUse the correct approval authority.
    ActAssign owners and deadlines.
    VerifyCheck whether the response produced the intended result.

    🤝 Leadership & Stakeholder Engagement

    Technical expertise becomes valuable when others can understand and act on it. Leadership means creating clarity, accountability and trust while communicating technical information at the right level.

    🎯
    Executive audience
    Focus on outcome, risk, decision and consequence.
    🧑‍🔧
    Delivery team
    Focus on tasks, dependencies, blockers and evidence.
    💰
    Finance
    Focus on budget, actuals, commitments and forecast.
    🤝
    Client / stakeholder
    Focus on milestones, expectations, changes and decisions.

    📊 Advanced Reporting & Governance

    At expert level, reporting should expose patterns rather than merely describe status. Use trend data, thresholds, exception reporting and controlled decision records.

    Expert practiceExample
    Trend analysisThree consecutive weeks of milestone slippage.
    Threshold reportingEscalate when forecast cost exceeds an agreed limit.
    Scenario analysisCompare recovery options before committing.
    Decision logRecord decision, rationale, owner and date.
    Data governanceDefine source, owner, refresh cycle and validation rules.

    🏆 Expert Practice & Continuous Improvement

    Expertise is demonstrated through repeatable results, not tool familiarity alone. Strong practitioners improve the system that produces the work.

    • Standardise templates and naming conventions.
    • Review recurring planning and reporting errors.
    • Automate repetitive low-value administration.
    • Measure forecast accuracy over time.
    • Capture lessons and feed them into future estimates.
    • Coach other users on planning discipline and data quality.
    🧪
    Capstone activity: Choose a realistic project. Build a WBS, 20-activity schedule, Gantt view, resource plan, cost baseline, Smartsheet-style action tracker and one-page executive dashboard. Identify three risks and provide two recovery scenarios.

    Module 01 · Basic · Foundations

    Agile Project Manager Fundamentals

    Start from zero: what an Agile Project Manager does, how agile differs from traditional management, and how the six core skills fit together on a real project.

    🧭 What is an Agile Project Manager?

    An Agile Project Manager helps a team deliver value in short cycles called iterations or sprints. Instead of planning everything up front and hoping nothing changes, they plan a little, deliver a little, learn, and adjust. They protect the team, keep stakeholders informed and make sure the project stays on course for its goals, dates and budget.

    🔁
    Plan iteratively
    Break work into small cycles and re-plan as you learn.
    🛡️
    Manage risk
    Spot threats early and keep a live risk register.
    ⚖️
    Control scope and capacity
    Match what is asked to what the team can really deliver.
    📣
    Track and report
    Show true progress to sponsors and stakeholders.
    EXAMPLE · A project in one sentence

    A bank wants a customer portal in six months. The Agile Project Manager plans two-week sprints, watches risks like a late payment API, keeps the backlog realistic for the team, tracks milestones and sends clear updates to executives.

    ⚔️ Agile versus traditional (waterfall)

    AspectTraditional (waterfall)Agile
    PlanningDetailed plan up frontRolling plan, refined every iteration
    DeliveryOne big delivery at the endWorking increments every sprint
    ScopeFixed, changes need formal controlFlexible, reprioritised in the backlog
    Time and costEstimated from fixed scopeOften fixed, so scope flexes
    Customer feedbackLate, at testing or handoverEarly and frequent, at every review
    RiskFound late, often at integrationFound early through short cycles
    Manager styleDirects and controlsFacilitates and removes blockers
    💡
    Agile does not mean no plan. It means planning at the right level of detail for the time horizon.

    📜 Agile values and principles

    The Agile Manifesto states four values. It says the items on the right still matter, but the items on the left matter more.

    We value more……thanWhat it means in practice
    Individuals and interactionsprocesses and toolsTalk to people first; tools support the team
    Working softwarecomprehensive documentationShow a working increment; write only useful documents
    Customer collaborationcontract negotiationWork with the customer, not against the contract
    Responding to changefollowing a planAdapt the plan when facts change
    EXAMPLE · Values in action

    A stakeholder asks for a change in sprint 4. Following a plan rigidly says no. Responding to change adds it to the backlog, weighs it against other items and agrees what moves down.

    👥 Where the Agile PM fits in Scrum

    Scrum has three accountabilities: Product Owner, Scrum Master and Developers. There is no “project manager” in the Scrum Guide, but many organisations still need one to manage budget, cross-team dependencies, governance and reporting. The Agile PM covers that gap without taking decisions that belong to the team.

    WhoOwnsDoes not own
    Product OwnerProduct Goal, backlog order, value decisionsHow the work is done
    Scrum MasterScrum is understood and used well; removes impedimentsDelivery commitments
    DevelopersSprint plan, how work is built, qualityBacklog priority
    Agile Project ManagerPlan across sprints, risks, budget, milestones, reporting, cross-team dependenciesTelling the team how to build or what order the Product Owner sets
    StakeholdersNeeds and feedbackDay-to-day team work
    ⚠️
    A common mistake is the Agile PM acting as a command-and-control boss. The role serves the team and the sponsor, it does not assign tasks.

    Take a customer self-service portal: 6 months, one Scrum team of 6, two-week sprints, fixed go-live date.

    🛤️ How an agile project flows

    StepWhat happensWho leads
    Vision and roadmapWhy we are doing this and the big milestonesProduct Owner, Agile PM
    Release planWhich features go in which releaseProduct Owner, Agile PM
    Sprint planningTeam picks backlog items for the sprint goalWhole team

    EXAMPLE · One cycle

    Monday: planning picks 30 points. Daily: 15-minute check-ins. Last Friday: review with users, then a retrospective. Next Monday the cycle starts again with what was learned.

    📖 Key terms (what is what)

    SprintA fixed-length iteration, one month or less, that produces a usable increment.
    Product backlogOrdered list of everything that might be needed in the product.
    Sprint backlogItems chosen for the sprint plus the plan to deliver them.
    IncrementA usable, done piece of the product.
    User storyA short need: “As a user I want … so that …”.
    Story pointRelative size of an item, not hours.
    VelocityPoints a team completes per sprint.
    Definition of DoneShared checklist that says when work is truly finished.
    Sprint goalThe single purpose of the sprint.
    ImpedimentSomething blocking the team that needs removing.
    StakeholderAnyone affected by or interested in the project.
    RetrospectiveTeam meeting to improve the way of working.
    EXAMPLE · Reading a status line

    “Sprint 5 goal met, 28 of 30 points done, velocity 29, one impediment open.” Decoded: the sprint purpose was achieved, almost all planned work is done, the average pace is steady and one blocker still needs action.

    Module 02 · Core Skill 1 · Iterative Project Planning

    Iterative Project Planning

    Plan at several levels, size work with story points, forecast with velocity and re-plan every sprint.

    🪜 Planning at five levels

    Agile plans are made at different levels. The further away the date, the less detail.

    LevelHorizonMain ownerOutput
    Product visionYear or moreProduct OwnerWhy the product exists
    RoadmapQuartersProduct Owner with Agile PMThemes and key milestones
    Release planMonthsAgile PM and teamWhich features in which release
    Sprint plan1 to 4 weeksWhole teamSprint goal and sprint backlog
    Daily planOne dayDevelopersHow to move toward the sprint goal
    EXAMPLE · Zoom levels

    The vision says “customers can self-serve”. The roadmap shows login in Q1 and payments in Q2. The sprint plan this fortnight is “login with password reset”.

    📝 Backlog and user stories

    A user story is written as: As a [user], I want [goal], so that [benefit]. Good stories follow INVEST.

    LetterMeaningCheck
    IIndependentCan it be built without waiting on another story?
    NNegotiableIs the detail open to discussion?
    VValuableDoes a user or the business gain?
    EEstimableCan the team size it?
    SSmallFits within one sprint?
    TTestableAre acceptance criteria clear?
    TermMeaningUsed when
    Definition of ReadyStory is clear enough to startBefore sprint planning
    Acceptance criteriaConditions that must be true for the story to passDuring build and review
    Definition of DoneShared quality standard for every itemBefore calling work finished

    🎯 Estimating work

    TechniqueHow it worksGood for
    Story pointsRelative size using a scale such as 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13Sprint and release forecasting
    Planning pokerTeam shows cards together, discusses gaps, re-votesReducing anchoring bias
    T-shirt sizesXS, S, M, L, XL for rough early sizingRoadmap level
    Three-point (PERT)(Optimistic + 4 × Most likely + Pessimistic) ÷ 6Work with real uncertainty
    Reference storyCompare new items to a known oneKeeping points consistent
    EXAMPLE · PERT

    Optimistic 4 days, most likely 6, pessimistic 14. Estimate = (4 + 4 × 6 + 14) ÷ 6 = 42 ÷ 6 = 7 days.

    📝
    Story points measure relative effort and uncertainty. They are not hours and must not be used to compare teams.

    🏃 Velocity and forecasting

    ItemFormulaExample
    VelocityPoints completed per sprint (done only)28, 32, 30
    Average velocitySum ÷ number of sprints(28 + 32 + 30) ÷ 3 = 30
    Sprints remainingRemaining points ÷ velocity240 ÷ 30 = 8 sprints
    Forecast rangeRemaining ÷ high and low velocity240 ÷ 36 = 6.7 and 240 ÷ 24 = 10
    EXAMPLE · Give a range

    With 240 points left and velocity between 24 and 36, say “between 7 and 10 sprints”, not “exactly 8”. A range is more honest than a single date.

    💡
    Only count items that meet the Definition of Done. Partly finished items score zero.

    🗓️ Sprint planning step by step

    StepWhat happens
    1. WhyProduct Owner proposes a sprint goal
    2. WhatTeam selects backlog items that fit capacity and the goal
    3. HowDevelopers break items into tasks and plan the work
    4. CommitTeam forecasts what it can deliver and agrees the sprint backlog
    EXAMPLE · Goal first

    Goal: “Customers can log in and reset a password.” Items outside that goal wait, even if they are easy.

    🌊 Rolling wave planning

    Rolling wave planning plans near-term work in detail and far-term work at a high level, then adds detail as the date nears. This is also called progressive elaboration.

    HorizonDetail levelExample
    This sprintTasks and hoursBuild login form
    Next 2 sprintsRefined stories with pointsPassword reset, session timeout
    Next quarterEpics and themesPayments
    BeyondVision onlyMobile app

    🪤 Planning traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Treating velocity as a target to push upUse it as a forecast tool, not a performance goal
    Planning every sprint for monthsDetail only the next few sprints
    Counting “almost done” pointsCount only items that are Done
    Committing 100% of capacityLeave room for bugs, support and the unexpected
    Skipping the sprint goalAlways have one clear sprint goal
    Giving a single date with no rangeState a forecast range and confidence

    🧪 Check your understanding

    Q. Remaining backlog is 240 points. The last three sprints delivered 28, 32 and 30. About how many sprints remain?
    • A 6 sprints
    • B 8 sprints
    • C 10 sprints
    • D 12 sprints

    Module 03 · Core Skill 2 · Agile Risk Management

    Agile Risk Management

    Find risks early, score them, choose a response and track exposure sprint by sprint.

    🔦 Why agile lowers risk, and key terms

    Short iterations expose problems early, so risk is discovered when it is cheap to fix. The Agile PM still keeps a visible, living risk process.

    TermMeaningExample
    RiskUncertain event that may affect the goalThe payment vendor may deliver late
    IssueA risk that has happenedThe vendor API is two sprints late
    ImpedimentBlocker that slows the team nowTest environment is down
    AssumptionSomething believed true but not provenThe users have modern browsers
    DependencyReliance on another team or supplierNeeds the identity service
    OpportunityUncertain event with a positive effectA reusable component exists
    💡
    A risk has not happened yet. If it has, it is an issue or an impediment.

    🔍 Identifying risks

    TechniqueHow it worksWhen
    Risk workshopTeam lists what could go wrongProject start and each release
    Pre-mortemImagine the project failed, then ask whyBefore commitment
    RetrospectiveLook for repeating problemsEvery sprint
    SpikeTime-boxed research on an unknownBefore large, uncertain work
    Dependency mappingMap who and what the plan relies onRelease planning
    Assumption logRecord and test assumptionsThroughout
    EXAMPLE · Pre-mortem

    The team imagines the portal launch failed. Reasons: payments not ready, security audit failed, key developer left. Each reason becomes a candidate risk.

    🎲 Assessing risks

    MeasureFormulaExample
    ExposureProbability × Impact40% × $50,000 = $20,000
    Score (1 to 5 scales)Probability score × Impact score4 × 3 = 12
    Total exposureSum of all risk exposures$20,000 + $15,000 = $35,000

    Rank risks by exposure or score, then address the highest first. Add risk-reduction items to the backlog so the work is visible and sized.

    EXAMPLE · Risk-adjusted backlog

    A spike to test the vendor API is put at the top of the backlog because it reduces the largest risk early.

    🧭 Responding to risks

    Response (threat)MeaningExample
    AvoidRemove the causeDrop the risky feature
    MitigateReduce probability or impactSpike plus a fallback payment provider
    TransferShift impact to another partyFixed-price clause or insurance
    AcceptDo nothing but monitorLow-impact browser quirk
    Response (opportunity)Meaning
    ExploitMake sure it happens
    EnhanceRaise probability or benefit
    SharePartner to capture it
    AcceptTake it if it comes

    ROAM is a quick way to classify risks in a group: Resolved, Owned, Accepted, Mitigated.

    📒 Risk register and risk burndown

    ColumnWhy it is there
    ID and descriptionClear, specific cause and effect
    Probability and impactTo rank the risk
    ExposureTo compare risks
    OwnerOne named person responsible
    Response and triggerWhat we do and when
    Status and review dateTo keep it alive

    A risk burndown chart plots total exposure sprint by sprint. A falling line shows risk is being reduced, a flat line shows it is being ignored.

    EXAMPLE · Burndown

    Exposure was $120,000 at sprint 1, $90,000 at sprint 3 and $55,000 at sprint 6. The trend shows risk is being retired.

    🔄 Risk in the sprint rhythm

    EventRisk activity
    Release planningRun a risk workshop and a pre-mortem
    Sprint planningAdd risk-reducing items and spikes
    Daily check-inRaise blockers and new risks
    Sprint reviewTest assumptions with stakeholders
    RetrospectiveReview top risks and update the register

    🪤 Common agile risks

    RiskEarly signResponse
    Unclear product goalBacklog keeps changing directionWorkshop with the Product Owner to restate the goal
    Key-person dependencyOnly one person can do a taskPair work and cross-training
    Technical debtBugs and slowdowns increaseReserve capacity for refactoring
    Scope volatilityLarge mid-sprint changesProtect the sprint, reprioritise at the next one
    External dependencyVendor dates slipEarly spike, fallback, regular contact
    Unavailable stakeholdersReviews get missedAgree a calendar with the sponsor

    🧪 Check your understanding

    Module 04 · Core Skill 3 · Agile Scope and Capacity Management

    Agile Scope & Capacity Management

    Hold time and cost steady, flex scope wisely, and match the plan to the capacity the team really has.

    🔺 Flexible scope inside fixed time and cost

    Traditional projects fix scope and let time and cost move. Agile often fixes time and cost and lets scope flex, delivering the most valuable items first.

    ApproachFixedFlexes
    TraditionalScopeTime and cost
    AgileTime and costScope
    💡
    If the date is fixed and new work arrives, something else of similar size must leave or the date must move.

    🥇 Prioritising the backlog

    MethodHow it worksBest for
    MoSCoWMust, Should, Could, Won’t (this time)Fixed deadline scope control
    Value vs effortPlot items, do high value and low effort firstQuick ranking
    WSJFCost of Delay ÷ Job sizeEconomic ordering
    KanoBasic, performance and delight featuresProduct strategy
    Cost of delayWhat waiting costs per weekTime-critical items
    EXAMPLE · WSJF

    Item A: value 8 + time 5 + risk 3 = 16, size 4, WSJF = 4. Item B: 13 + 8 + 5 = 26, size 13, WSJF = 2. Do A first.

    📝
    A common MoSCoW guide: about 60% of effort Musts, 20% Shoulds, 20% Coulds, so there is a buffer.

    🔀 Scope change versus scope creep

    TermMeaningHandling
    Scope changeAgreed, visible change to the backlogProduct Owner reprioritises, trade-off is shown
    Scope creepUnplanned additions with no trade-offSpot early, route through the backlog and the sponsor
    StepChange request flow
    1Capture the request in the backlog
    2Size it and assess value and risk
    3Compare to what it displaces
    4Product Owner and sponsor decide
    5Update the roadmap and tell stakeholders

    🧮 Calculating capacity

    ItemFormulaExample
    Available daysPeople × working days − leave6 × 10 − 4 = 56
    Effective capacityAvailable days × focus factor56 × 0.75 = 42 person-days
    Capacity vs velocityCapacity is time available, velocity is points delivered42 person-days may give 30 points
    EXAMPLE · Sprint capacity

    Team of 6, 10-day sprint, two people each away 2 days. Available = 60 − 4 = 56 days. Focus factor 0.75 gives 42 effective person-days.

    ⚠️
    Capacity changes with leave, holidays and meetings. Never reuse last sprint’s number without checking.

    📈 Forecasting with burnup and burndown

    ChartShowsStrength
    BurndownWork remaining over timeSimple view of what is left
    BurnupWork done and total scope over timeShows scope change clearly
    EXAMPLE · Burnup forecast

    Scope 200 points, done 80 after 4 sprints, so velocity 20. Remaining 120 ÷ 20 = 6 more sprints.

    💡
    Prefer burnup when scope changes. The scope line moves and the chart shows why the finish date moved.

    🛡️ Protecting the team

    ToolPurpose
    WIP limits (Kanban)Cap work in progress to improve flow
    Little’s LawAverage cycle time = WIP ÷ throughput
    Capacity bufferLeave room for bugs and support
    Tech-debt allowanceReserve time for maintenance
    Sprint goal protectionAvoid mid-sprint scope injection
    EXAMPLE · Little’s Law

    WIP 12 items and throughput 4 items per week gives average cycle time 12 ÷ 4 = 3 weeks.

    🪤 Scope and capacity traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Saying yes to every requestShow the trade-off and let the Product Owner choose
    Planning at 100% of capacityPlan at the realistic focus factor
    Using velocity to compare teamsUse it only for the team’s own forecasting
    Ignoring leave and holidaysRecalculate capacity every sprint
    Everything is a MustChallenge: what if we only had half the time?

    🧪 Check your understanding

    Q. A team of 6 has a 10-day sprint. Two people are each away for 2 days and the focus factor is 0.75. What is the effective capacity?
    • A 36 person-days
    • B 42 person-days
    • C 45 person-days
    • D 56 person-days

    Module 05 · Core Skill 4 · Milestone Tracking

    Milestone Tracking

    Define milestones as outcomes, measure progress honestly and spot slippage before the date is lost.

    🚩 Milestones in an agile project

    A milestone is a significant outcome tied to a date or decision. In agile it should describe a result, such as “login live for pilot users”, not an activity such as “coding finished”.

    Milestone typeExampleTracked by
    Sprint goalCustomers can reset passwordsSprint review
    Release milestoneBeta to 500 pilot usersRelease burnup
    Fixed external dateRegulatory deadline, trade showRoadmap and buffer
    Decision gateSecurity audit sign-offGate checklist
    Funding checkpointStage 2 budget releaseSponsor review
    EXAMPLE · Outcome not activity

    “Payments coded” is an activity. “Customers can pay by card in the pilot” is a milestone.

    🔎 Tracking progress honestly

    RuleWhy
    Count only Done workPartly done work gives false comfort
    Measure working softwareDocuments and meetings are not progress
    Use the same scale each sprintTrends need consistency
    Show scope changesA moving target must be visible
    Report forecast rangesSingle dates imply false certainty
    ⚠️
    “90% complete” is a trap. Items are either done or not done.

    📊 Key progress metrics

    MetricWhat it tellsHow
    VelocityPace of deliveryDone points per sprint
    BurnupProgress against total scopeDone and scope lines
    Sprint goal hit rateReliabilityGoals met ÷ sprints
    Cycle timeHow long work takes once startedDone date − start date
    Lead timeHow long from request to doneDone date − request date
    ThroughputItems finished per periodCount of done items
    Cumulative flowWhere work is piling upStacked status chart
    Escaped defectsQuality leaking outDefects found after release
    EXAMPLE · Cycle versus lead time

    A story is requested on 1 May, started on 8 May and finished on 12 May. Lead time = 11 days. Cycle time = 4 days.

    📉 Spotting slippage early

    SignalMeaningAction
    Velocity falling for 3 sprintsCapacity or quality problemInvestigate at the retrospective
    Burnup scope line risingScope growthReprioritise with the Product Owner
    Sprint goals missed twicePlan too ambitiousPlan at lower commitment
    Cumulative flow band wideningWork piling in one stageAdd WIP limits
    EXAMPLE · Date check

    After sprint 6 the team has done 150 of 360 points at velocity 25. Remaining 210 ÷ 25 = 8.4, so 9 more sprints. Total 15, but only 12 are planned. The milestone is at risk now, not in sprint 12.

    🧰 Milestone tracking tools

    ToolUse
    Roadmap with milestonesShows key dates and themes
    Release burnupForecasts the release date
    Milestone trend chartShows how a date has moved across reports
    Dependency boardTracks cross-team links
    Jira, Azure DevOps, AsanaHold backlog, sprints and dashboards
    💡
    Use the milestone trend to ask: has this date moved in each report? If so, why?

    🪤 Tracking traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Tracking tasks done instead of outcomesTrack Done items and goals met
    Hiding slippage until lateRaise it as soon as the forecast shifts
    Moving the baseline silentlyRecord the change and the reason
    Treating velocity as a performance scoreUse it to forecast, not to judge people

    Module 06 · Core Skill 5 · Stakeholder Reporting

    Stakeholder Reporting

    Know your audience, report real progress and use sprint reviews, dashboards and clear status colours.

    👥 Know your stakeholders

    GroupPowerInterestApproach
    SponsorHighHighManage closely: regular one-page update
    ExecutivesHighLowKeep satisfied: short summary
    End usersLowHighKeep informed: sprint reviews
    Support teamsLowLowMonitor: occasional update
    RegulatorsHighMediumFormal evidence at gates
    💡
    The power and interest grid helps decide how often and in what depth to communicate.

    🗒️ What a good status report contains

    SectionContent
    Headline statusOverall RAG and one-sentence reason
    ProgressGoals met, milestones, burnup forecast
    Next periodSprint goals and planned demos
    Risks and issuesTop three with owner and action
    Decisions neededSpecific asks with a date
    BudgetSpend versus plan and forecast
    EXAMPLE · Headline

    Amber: “Forecast finish is one sprint late because the payment API is delayed. Decision needed by 14 June on scope trade-off.”

    🚦 RAG status

    ColourMeaningExample
    GreenOn track within toleranceForecast inside the date range
    AmberAt risk, recovery plan existsOne sprint late, action agreed
    RedOff track, help neededDate cannot be met without a decision
    ⚠️
    A “watermelon” report is green outside and red inside. Honest amber early is better than red late.

    🎤 Sprint review versus status report

    ItemSprint reviewStatus report
    PurposeInspect the increment and gather feedbackInform about overall health
    AudienceTeam and stakeholdersSponsors and executives
    FormatLive demo and discussionOne-page written update
    FrequencyEvery sprintWeekly or fortnightly
    FocusWhat was built and what nextProgress, risks, decisions

    📈 Dashboards and information radiators

    ElementWhy
    Burnup chartForecast and scope change
    Top risksVisible exposure
    Milestone timelineKey dates at a glance
    Sprint goal statusCurrent focus
    BlockersWhere help is needed
    EXAMPLE · Radiator

    A wall board and a live dashboard show the same data so no one needs a meeting to find out where the project stands.

    🪤 Reporting traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Vanity metrics (hours worked, lines of code)Report outcomes and forecasts
    Same report for everyoneTailor depth to audience
    Hiding bad newsShare early with a plan
    Too many numbersThree to five key measures
    Reporting only statusAdd the decisions you need

    🧪 Check your understanding

    Q. The sponsor has high power and high interest. Which approach fits best?

    • B Keep informed with occasional email

    Module 07 · Core Skill 6 · Hybrid Agile/Waterfall Methodologies

    Hybrid Agile/Waterfall Methodologies

    Combine agile delivery with fixed gates, contracts and milestones, and report on both views.

    🧩 Why hybrid exists

    Many organisations have fixed budgets, regulation, hardware lead times and governance gates, yet want agile speed and feedback. Hybrid uses agile where change is likely and plan-driven methods where the constraint is fixed.

    FactorPoints toward agilePoints toward waterfall
    RequirementsUnclear or changingStable and well known
    RegulationLightHeavy evidence needed
    DependenciesFewMany, with long lead times
    CustomerAvailable oftenAvailable only at gates
    ContractFlexibleFixed scope and price

    🧱 Common hybrid patterns

    PatternPlan-driven partAgile partExample
    Water-Scrum-FallUp-front requirements and end releaseScrum build in the middleBank release process
    Gated agileStage gates for fundingSprints between gatesPublic-sector project
    Fixed milestonesRegulatory deadlineBacklog flexes insideCompliance launch
    Parallel tracksHardware planSoftware sprintsDevice with firmware
    Agile discovery, plan buildBuild to a fixed planDiscovery sprints firstLarge system integration
    EXAMPLE · Water-Scrum-Fall

    Business case and regulatory sign-off are done up front. The team builds in sprints. Release, training and handover follow a fixed plan.

    🎚️ Choosing and tailoring the mix

    QuestionIf yes
    Is the date legally fixed?Fix the milestone, flex scope within it
    Are requirements still being discovered?Use iterations and spikes
    Does hardware have a long lead time?Plan that track early, in a plan-driven way
    Are auditors expecting documents?Add document outputs to the Definition of Done
    Do many teams depend on each other?Use an integrated roadmap and regular syncs
    💡
    Tailor to the project. Start with the constraints, then pick the lightest process that respects them.

    🏛️ Governance in a hybrid project

    ElementHow it works in hybrid
    Stage gatesReview outcomes and evidence, not just documents
    Definition of DoneIncludes compliance and test evidence
    Change controlBacklog changes inside tolerance, formal control outside it
    TraceabilityLink requirements to stories and tests
    Decision rightsProduct Owner on scope, sponsor on budget and dates
    EXAMPLE · Gate evidence

    The security gate needs a penetration test report. The team adds it to the Definition of Done for the final three sprints.

    🗺️ Planning and reporting both views

    Sponsors may ask for a Gantt or milestone view while the team works from a backlog. The Agile PM links both with an integrated roadmap and maps work packages to epics.

    MetricFormulaExample
    Planned Value (PV)Budgeted cost of work planned to date$400,000
    Earned Value (EV)Budgeted cost of work actually done$360,000
    SPIEV ÷ PV360 ÷ 400 = 0.90
    CPIEV ÷ Actual cost360 ÷ 375 = 0.96
    📝
    SPI below 1.0 means behind schedule. Here SPI is 0.90, so about 10% behind plan.

    💼 Contracts and budgets

    ModelHow it worksTrade-off
    Fixed price, fixed scopeEverything specified up frontChange is costly
    Fixed price, variable scopePrice fixed, backlog flexesNeeds trust and priorities
    Capped time and materialsPay for effort up to a capCost control with flexibility
    Stage fundingRelease money per stage or releaseGovernance each stage
    Change for freeSwap items of equal size at no costNeeds good sizing

    🪤 Hybrid traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Agile in name only (old process with new words)Check feedback loops and working increments
    Gates that wait for big-bang documentsDefine gates around evidence from increments
    Two plans that disagreeKeep one integrated roadmap
    Team told the date and scope are both fixedFlex scope inside the milestone

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer.

    🧭 Fundamentals

    What does an Agile Project Manager do?
    Helps the team deliver value in short cycles by planning iteratively, managing risk, balancing scope and capacity, tracking milestones, reporting to stakeholders and handling governance. They facilitate and remove blockers rather than command.
    How is the role different from a Scrum Master and Product Owner?
    The Product Owner owns value and backlog order. The Scrum Master coaches the team on Scrum and removes impediments. The Agile PM covers budget, cross-team dependencies, milestones, risk and reporting across the project.
    Name the four Agile Manifesto values.
    Individuals and interactions, working software, customer collaboration, and responding to change, valued more than processes and tools, comprehensive documentation, contract negotiation and following a plan.
    When is agile a poor fit?
    When requirements are fixed and well understood, regulation demands heavy up-front evidence, or customers cannot take part. Even then a hybrid approach often helps.

    🔁 Iterative Project Planning

    How do you plan an agile project?
    Start with a vision and roadmap, break into a release plan, then plan each sprint in detail. Refine the backlog continuously and re-forecast with the latest velocity.
    How do you forecast a release date?
    Remaining points ÷ average velocity, then give a range using the lowest and highest recent velocity. For 240 points and velocity 24 to 36 the range is 7 to 10 sprints.
    What are story points and why not hours?
    Relative size that includes effort, complexity and uncertainty. They avoid false precision and arguments about individual speed.
    What is rolling wave planning?
    Detail the near term and keep the far term high level, adding detail as it approaches.
    What is the difference between Definition of Ready and Definition of Done?
    Ready says a story is clear enough to start. Done says the work meets the shared quality standard.

    ⚠️ Agile Risk Management

    How do you manage risk in an agile project?
    Run a risk workshop and pre-mortem, keep a register with owners, score by exposure, add risk-reducing items to the backlog, review top risks every sprint and track a risk burndown.
    Risk, issue or impediment?
    A risk may happen, an issue has happened, an impediment is blocking the team now. Each has a different owner and response.
    What is a spike?
    A time-boxed research story to reduce uncertainty, such as testing a vendor API before committing to a design.
    Calculate exposure for a 30% risk with $60,000 impact.
    0.30 × $60,000 = $18,000.
    What is ROAM?
    Resolved, Owned, Accepted, Mitigated. A quick way to classify risks in a group session.

    ⚖️ Agile Scope and Capacity Management

    How do you handle a scope request mid-project?
    Capture it in the backlog, size it, compare value and cost with what it displaces, let the Product Owner decide, and update the roadmap and stakeholders.
    How do you calculate sprint capacity?
    (People × working days − leave) × focus factor. For 6 people, 10 days, 4 days leave and 0.75 focus, capacity is 42 person-days.
    Explain MoSCoW and WSJF.
    MoSCoW sorts into Must, Should, Could, Won’t. WSJF ranks by cost of delay divided by job size, so high value, small items go first.
    What is the difference between scope change and scope creep?
    Scope change is visible and agreed with a trade-off. Scope creep is unplanned growth with no decision.
    Why prefer a burnup chart?
    It shows both work done and total scope, so scope growth is visible.

    🚩 Milestone Tracking and Stakeholder Reporting

    How do you track milestones in agile?
    Define milestones as outcomes, use a release burnup, track sprint goal hit rate and velocity, and review the forecast every sprint.
    What do you do when a milestone is at risk?
    Update the forecast, find the cause, present options (reduce scope, add capacity, move date), and ask the sponsor to decide early.
    How do you tailor reports for different stakeholders?
    Use the power and interest grid. Sponsors get a one-page report with decisions needed, users see demos at sprint review, executives get a short summary.
    What is a watermelon report?
    A report that shows green status while real progress is red. It destroys trust, so report amber early.
    Which metrics do you report?
    Burnup forecast, velocity trend, sprint goal hit rate, top risks, cycle time and budget versus forecast. Avoid vanity metrics.

    🧱 Hybrid Agile/Waterfall Methodologies

    What is a hybrid approach and when do you use it?
    Agile delivery combined with plan-driven elements such as gates, fixed contracts or regulatory deadlines. Use it when constraints are fixed but requirements still evolve.
    How do you run agile with a fixed regulatory date?
    Fix the milestone, flex the scope inside it using MoSCoW, protect the Musts, and add compliance evidence to the Definition of Done.
    How do you report to a sponsor who wants a Gantt chart?
    Keep an integrated roadmap, map epics to work packages and show milestones plus earned value, while the team works from the backlog.
    Compute SPI for PV $400,000 and EV $360,000.
    SPI = 360 ÷ 400 = 0.90, so about 10% behind schedule.
    What contract models suit agile?
    Fixed price with variable scope, capped time and materials, stage funding, and change-for-free swaps.

    🏗️ Scenario: Launch a customer self-service portal

    Your company must launch a customer self-service portal in 24 weeks for a fixed marketing and regulatory date. One Scrum team of 6 works in 2-week sprints (12 sprints). The backlog is 360 story points and recent velocity is about 30. A payment vendor is a key dependency, a security audit gate is required before go-live, and the sponsor wants clear updates. In week 12 the team is behind, the vendor slips, and a director asks for extra features.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Iterative planning

    ✍️ Backlog is 360 points, velocity ranges from 26 to 34. Can you commit to 12 sprints and what do you tell the sponsor?
    Model answer: Average velocity 30 gives 360 ÷ 30 = 12 sprints with no buffer. At velocity 26 it takes 13.8, so 14 sprints. At 34 it takes 10.6, so 11 sprints. Tell the sponsor the range is 11 to 14 sprints and that the fixed date needs a scope buffer, for example only Musts committed.
    Q. Remaining backlog is 360 points and velocity is 30. How many sprints are needed?
    • A 10
    • B 12
    • C 14
    • D 16

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Risk management

    ✍️ The payment vendor has a 50% chance of delivering two sprints late, costing $80,000. What do you do?
    Model answer: Exposure = 0.5 × $80,000 = $40,000. Make it a top risk with an owner. Add a spike to test the API early, agree a fallback provider, review it every sprint and track it on the risk burndown. Tell the sponsor because it threatens the fixed date.
    Q. What is the best early action for the vendor risk?
    • A Accept and do nothing
    • B Run a spike and arrange a fallback provider
    • C Wait until the vendor is late
    • D Remove the risk from the register

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Scope and capacity

    ✍️ A director wants 40 extra points. Next sprint 2 of 6 people are away 2 days each, 10-day sprint, focus factor 0.75. What do you do?
    Model answer: Capacity = (60 − 4) × 0.75 = 42 person-days. The team is already at full load, so extra scope needs a trade-off. Size the request, show the Product Owner and sponsor what it displaces (for example Could items), and decide. Do not add 40 points without removing 40 or moving the date.
    Q. Team of 6, 10-day sprint, 4 days leave, focus 0.75. What is the effective capacity?
    • A 42 person-days
    • B 45 person-days
    • C 56 person-days
    • D 60 person-days

    4️⃣ Task 4 · Milestone tracking

    ✍️ After sprint 6, 150 of 360 points are done and velocity is 25. Will you hit 12 sprints?
    Model answer: Remaining 210 ÷ 25 = 8.4, so 9 more sprints and 15 in total. Only 12 are planned, so the milestone is at risk. Options: cut scope to Musts, add capacity carefully, or move the date. Raise it now with a forecast and options.
    Q. 150 of 360 points are done and velocity is 25. How many sprints remain?

    • B 8.4 so 9
    • C 10
    • D 12

    5️⃣ Task 5 · Stakeholder reporting

    ✍️ Write the headline status for the sponsor.
    Model answer: Amber: “Forecast go-live is three sprints late (15 versus 12) because velocity is 25 and the payment vendor is delayed. Decision needed by Friday: cut Could and Should items to protect the date, or approve a move.” Add top risks and the next demo date.
    Q. Which headline is the most useful to the sponsor?
    • A All green, team is working hard
    • B Amber: forecast three sprints late, decision needed on scope by Friday
    • C Red: project failing
    • D Sprint 6 had 12 meetings

    6️⃣ Task 6 · Hybrid governance

    ✍️ The security audit gate needs evidence in sprint 10. PV is $400,000 and EV $360,000. How do you handle both?
    Model answer: Add the penetration test and security documents to the Definition of Done for the final sprints and book the audit early. SPI = 360 ÷ 400 = 0.90, so about 10% behind. Report SPI beside the burnup forecast and keep one integrated roadmap showing the gate date.

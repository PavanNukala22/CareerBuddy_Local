    Soft Skills Fundamentals for the Agile PM

    Start from zero: why soft skills decide whether an agile project succeeds, what the five core soft skills are and how they work together.

    🧭 Why soft skills matter

    Tools and methods set the structure, but people deliver the project. An Agile Project Manager leads without command authority, so influence, clarity and judgement matter as much as plans. This course covers five soft skills: Negotiation, Priority Alignment, Adaptability, Clear Communication and Problem Solving.

    🤝
    Negotiation
    Reach agreements that protect the project and the relationship.
    🎯
    Priority Alignment
    Get everyone pulling toward the same most valuable work.
    🌱
    Adaptability
    Respond to change without losing direction.
    💬
    Clear Communication
    Make sure the right people understand the right thing.
    EXAMPLE · One day, five skills

    Morning: negotiate scope with a sponsor. Midday: align two directors on priority. Afternoon: a vendor drops out, so adapt the plan, explain it clearly, and solve the root cause with the team.

    Module 02 · Soft Skill 1 · Negotiation

    Negotiation

    Prepare, understand interests, trade fairly and confirm agreements so both the project and the relationship stay strong.

    🤝 What negotiation is and where PMs use it

    Negotiation is a conversation to reach agreement when parties want different things. Agile PMs negotiate scope, deadlines, budgets, resources, vendor terms and team commitments.

    SituationTypical partiesWhat is traded
    Extra scope near the deadlineSponsor and Product OwnerScope, date, budget
    Vendor delivery dateVendorPrice, dates, penalties
    Borrowing a specialistAnother managerTime, priority, favours
    Sprint commitmentTeam and Product OwnerScope and quality
    Budget increaseFinanceValue and risk

    ⚔️ Two styles: distributive and integrative

    StyleIdeaResultUse when
    Distributive (win-lose)Fixed pie, each side takes moreOne side gains, other losesOne-off price haggling
    Integrative (win-win)Grow the pie by finding shared interestsBoth gainOngoing relationships
    💡
    On projects, relationships continue after the deal, so aim for integrative outcomes.

    🧭 Principled negotiation: four rules

    RuleMeaningExample
    Separate people from the problemBe soft on people, hard on the issue“We both want the launch to succeed.”
    Focus on interests, not positionsAsk whyDeadline, not feature, is the interest
    Invent options for mutual gainBrainstorm before decidingPhased release
    Use objective criteriaAgree fair standardsVelocity data, market rates
    EXAMPLE · Interests

    Position: “Add all 40 points.” Interest: “Be ready for the regulator’s date.” Option: release the regulated part first and the rest after.

    🛠️ Preparing: BATNA, ZOPA and limits

    TermMeaningExample
    BATNAYour best alternative if no dealUse a second vendor
    Reservation pointWorst deal you will accept$120,000 maximum
    ZOPAZone of possible agreementOverlap of both limits
    AnchorFirst number or offerOpening price
    ConcessionSomething you giveLonger contract
    EXAMPLE · ZOPA

    Sponsor’s maximum budget is $120,000. Vendor’s minimum price is $100,000. The ZOPA is $100,000 to $120,000. If the vendor’s minimum were $130,000, there would be no ZOPA.

    🪜 The negotiation process

    StepWhat you do
    1. PrepareKnow interests, BATNA, limits and data
    2. OpenSet tone and purpose
    3. ExploreAsk questions, listen, find interests
    4. ProposeOffer options and packages
    5. BargainTrade concessions, use “if … then …”
    6. CloseSummarise and agree next steps
    7. ConfirmWrite it down and share
    ⚠️
    Verbal agreements fade. Always send a short written summary within a day.

    🎯 Techniques that work

    TechniqueHow it worksExample
    If-then tradeLink a give to a get“If we add this, then we drop that.”
    Package dealNegotiate several issues togetherScope, date and budget together
    SilencePause after an offerLet them respond
    LabellingName feelings“It seems you are worried about the date.”
    AnchoringOpen first with a reasoned numberExplain with data
    Break timePause when tension rises“Let us take ten minutes.”
    EXAMPLE · If-then

    “If you add 40 points, then we move 40 points of Could items out, or the release moves by about one sprint.”

    🪤 Negotiation traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Splitting the difference automaticallyCheck it is fair against criteria
    Giving concessions for freeTrade each concession
    Arguing positionsAsk about interests
    No BATNAPrepare alternatives
    Letting emotion leadPause and label feelings
    No follow-up in writingConfirm by email

    Module 03 · Soft Skill 2 · Priority Alignment

    Priority Alignment

    Agree what matters most, resolve conflicts between stakeholders and keep one ordered list the whole team can follow.

    🎯 What priority alignment is

    Priority alignment means stakeholders and the team agree on what is most important and in what order. Without it, the team gets conflicting asks and effort is wasted.

    Sign of misalignmentEffect
    Everything is “high priority”Nothing is first
    Two directors give different ordersTeam stalls or picks sides
    Backlog order changes dailyLow throughput and morale
    Goals unknown to the teamWork with no purpose

    🧰 Prioritisation methods

    MethodHow it worksBest for
    MoSCoWMust, Should, Could, Won’tFixed scope boundaries
    Value vs effortPlot items on two axesQuick ranking
    WSJFCost of delay ÷ job sizeEconomic ordering
    RICEReach × Impact × Confidence ÷ EffortProduct features
    Eisenhower matrixUrgent and important quadrantsPersonal and team tasks
    Dot votingEach person has a few votesGroup workshops
    EXAMPLE · RICE

    Feature A: Reach 2,000, Impact 2, Confidence 80%, Effort 4. RICE = 2,000 × 2 × 0.8 ÷ 4 = 800. Feature B: Reach 500, Impact 3, Confidence 100%, Effort 2. RICE = 500 × 3 × 1.0 ÷ 2 = 750. A ranks first.

    🧮 The Eisenhower matrix

    QuadrantUrgent?Important?Action
    1YesYesDo now
    2NoYesSchedule
    3YesNoDelegate or reduce
    4NoNoDrop
    💡
    Most project value sits in quadrant 2: important but not yet urgent, such as risk reduction and refactoring.

    👥 Aligning stakeholders

    StepWhat you do
    1. Map stakeholdersPower and interest grid
    2. Surface goalsAsk each what success looks like
    3. Link to the product goalUse shared goals or OKRs
    4. Score togetherRun a workshop with agreed criteria
    5. DecideProduct Owner orders the backlog
    6. PublishOne visible ordered list
    7. ReviewRe-check each sprint or release
    EXAMPLE · Workshop

    Two directors each list their top five items. The group scores all ten using value and cost of delay. The outcome is one ordered list that both signed off.

    ⚖️ Decision rights and escalation

    DecisionOwner
    Backlog orderProduct Owner
    How to buildDevelopers
    Budget and date changeSponsor
    Cross-team conflict not solvedSteering group or sponsor
    ⚠️
    Escalate with a clear question and options, not a complaint. State the impact and the decision needed by a date.

    📊 Keeping alignment alive

    PracticePurpose
    Sprint goalOne shared purpose for each sprint
    Visible roadmapEveryone sees the order and dates
    Regular backlog refinementOrder stays current
    Sprint reviewStakeholders see results and re-check priority
    Decision logRecord why priorities were set

    🪤 Priority traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Everything is MustForce-rank: if only half the time, what stays?
    Loudest voice winsUse criteria and data
    HiPPO (highest paid opinion)Ask for evidence and score together
    Priorities change mid-sprintProtect the sprint goal and reorder next sprint
    No one owns the orderName the Product Owner as decider

    Module 04 · Soft Skill 3 · Adaptability

    Adaptability

    Respond to change with a clear head: assess, decide, communicate and adjust without losing direction.

    🌱 What adaptability is

    Adaptability is adjusting plans, behaviour and approach when conditions change. In agile it is built in: inspect and adapt. It is not chaos. It is planned flexibility with a clear goal.

    Type of changeExample
    ScopeSponsor adds a regulatory feature
    PeopleA key developer leaves
    TechnologyA tool is retired
    SupplierA vendor withdraws
    MarketA competitor launches first
    OrganisationA reorganisation moves the sponsor

    🔄 Responding to change: a simple loop

    StepWhat you doExample
    1. PauseDo not react at onceTake an hour to gather facts
    2. Assess impactScope, time, cost, risk, peopleTwo sprints lost
    3. Generate optionsAt least twoFallback vendor, cut scope
    4. DecideWith the right ownerSponsor picks
    5. CommunicateTell everyone affectedShort update with reason
    6. Adjust the planUpdate backlog, roadmap, risksRe-forecast
    7. LearnRetrospectiveRecord lesson
    EXAMPLE · Vendor withdraws

    The payment vendor withdraws. Impact: two sprints. Options: a second vendor or reduced payment scope. Sponsor picks the second vendor. The plan, risk log and roadmap are updated and the team hears the reason the same day.

    🛣️ Adaptability inside agile events

    EventHow it supports adaptation
    Sprint planningAdjusts the plan to the latest facts
    Daily ScrumAdjusts the next day’s work
    Sprint reviewAdapts the backlog to feedback
    RetrospectiveAdapts how the team works
    Backlog refinementRe-orders as value changes
    📝
    In Scrum only the Product Owner can cancel a sprint, for example when the sprint goal becomes obsolete. It is rare.

    🧠 Helping people through change

    Plans adapt faster than people. The ADKAR model describes five steps to personal change.

    LetterStepWhat helps
    AAwarenessExplain why the change is needed
    DDesireShow benefits and listen to concerns
    KKnowledgeTrain and give information
    AAbilityPractice and coach
    RReinforcementRecognise and embed the change
    EXAMPLE · Tool change

    A team moves from spreadsheets to Jira. The PM explains why, runs a short training, pairs people with a helper and reviews usage after two sprints.

    🧪 Small experiments and learning

    ToolMeaningExample
    PDCAPlan, Do, Check, ActTry a 10-minute daily check-in for two sprints
    ExperimentSmall, time-boxed test with a measurePair review on one story type
    Retrospective actionOne or two changes per sprintAdd a Definition of Done line
    Growth mindsetSkills grow with practice“We have not mastered this yet.”
    💡
    Change one thing at a time, set a measure and a review date, and keep or drop the change based on evidence.

    🧱 Flexible with guardrails

    FlexibleFixed
    Backlog orderSprint goal during the sprint
    How work is doneDefinition of Done
    Scope within the dateLegal and safety rules
    Tools and tacticsTeam values
    ⚠️
    Too much change harms focus. Adapt when evidence supports it, not at every new request.

    🪤 Adaptability traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Reacting before facts are knownPause and assess
    Clinging to the original planRe-plan when facts change
    Changing direction every weekUse criteria and protect the sprint goal
    Not explaining changesShare the reason
    Ignoring how people feelListen, then support

    • C Ignore it until the next review

    Module 05 · Soft Skill 4 · Clear Communication

    Clear Communication

    Say the right thing to the right person in the right way, and check that it was understood.

    💬 What clear communication is

    Clear communication means the receiver understands the message as the sender meant it and can act on it. Sending information is not enough. Understanding must be confirmed.

    ElementQuestion to ask
    AudienceWho needs this and what do they already know?
    PurposeInform, decide, ask or persuade?
    MessageWhat is the one key point?
    ChannelMeeting, email, chat or board?
    TimingWhen do they need it?
    Follow-upHow will I know it was understood?

    🧱 Structuring messages

    ModelMeaningBest for
    BLUFBottom line up frontExecutive emails
    SBARSituation, Background, Assessment, RecommendationEscalations and urgent updates
    SBISituation, Behaviour, ImpactFeedback
    Pyramid principleAnswer first, then reasonsProposals
    EXAMPLE · SBAR

    S: Forecast go-live is three sprints late. B: Velocity is 25 and the payment vendor is delayed. A: The fixed date cannot be met with all scope. R: Cut Could and Should items, decision needed by Friday.

    👂 Listening and questioning

    SkillHowExample
    Open questionStarts with what, how, why“What would success look like?”
    Closed questionYes or no“Is the date fixed?”
    ParaphraseRepeat in your words“So you need it by June.”
    Read-backConfirm instructions“I will send the plan by Monday. Correct?”
    SilenceAllow thinking timeCount to five
    💡
    Ask open questions to explore and closed questions to confirm.

    ✍️ Written communication

    TypeGood practice
    Status emailHeadline first, then details, then the ask
    One-page updateStatus, progress, risks, decisions
    Chat messageOne topic, clear action, a date
    Meeting notesDecisions and actions at the top
    Release notePlain words for users
    EXAMPLE · Rewrite

    Weak: “Some issues with the vendor, will keep you posted.” Strong: “Vendor API is two weeks late. Impact: one sprint. Decision needed by Thursday: use the fallback provider.”

    🗣️ Meetings, remote and async

    PracticeWhy
    Purpose and agenda in the invitePeople prepare
    Decide the decision-makerAvoid endless debate
    Record decisions and ownersNothing is lost
    Camera on for key sessionsBetter signals when remote
    Use async updates for statusSaves meeting time
    Time zones in mindFairness
    📝
    If a meeting has no purpose or decision, replace it with a written update.

    SponsorOne-page status and decisionsWeeklyEmail and callAgile PM
    TeamSprint goal, blockersDailyStand-upScrum Master
    UsersDemo and feedbackEach sprintSprint reviewProduct Owner

    EXAMPLE · Channels grow fast

    A team of 8 has 8 × 7 ÷ 2 = 28 channels. Adding 2 people gives 10 × 9 ÷ 2 = 45. Larger teams need clear structure.

    🎭 Difficult conversations and feedback

    StepWhat to do
    PrepareKnow the facts and goal
    Open with intent“I want us to solve this together.”
    Describe with SBISituation, behaviour, impact
    ListenLet them respond
    Agree actionsWho does what by when
    Follow upCheck progress
    EXAMPLE · SBI

    “In yesterday’s review (situation) you interrupted the tester twice (behaviour), so she stopped sharing findings (impact). Can we agree how to give everyone space?”

    Too many recipientsSend to those who must act

    Module 06 · Soft Skill 5 · Problem Solving

    Problem Solving

    Define the real problem, find root causes with simple tools, choose between options fairly and follow through.

    🧩 What problem solving is

    Problem solving is finding what is really wrong, choosing a good fix and checking that it worked. Agile PMs solve problems with the team, not for the team.

    StepQuestion
    1. DefineWhat exactly is the problem?
    2. Gather factsWhat do we know and see?
    3. Find root causeWhy is it happening?
    4. Generate optionsWhat could we do?
    5. DecideWhich option is best?
    6. ActWho does what by when?
    7. CheckDid it work?

    📝 Defining the problem

    Weak statementStrong statement
    The team is slowVelocity dropped from 30 to 22 in three sprints
    Quality is bad12 critical bugs escaped to production in May
    Stakeholders are unhappySponsor has asked for scope changes five times since sprint 3
    💡
    A good problem statement has a measure, a time and no blame or solution inside it.

    🔎 Root cause tools

    ToolHow it worksBest for
    5 WhysAsk why repeatedlySingle-thread causes
    Fishbone (Ishikawa)Group causes by categoryMany possible causes
    Pareto chartRank causes by frequencyChoosing what to fix first
    Fault tree or timelineMap events in orderIncidents
    EXAMPLE · 5 Whys

    Build failed. Why? A test broke. Why? Data changed. Why? Shared test data was edited. Why? No rules for editing. Why? Never agreed. Root cause: no test-data rule. Fix: create the rule and isolate test data.

    📊 Pareto: fix the vital few

    Bug areaCountShare
    Login4040%
    Payments3030%
    Profile1515%
    Other1515%
    EXAMPLE · Pareto

    Login and payments together are 70 of 100 bugs, so 70%. Fix those first for the largest effect.

    ⚖️ Choosing between options

    OptionCost (40%)Risk (35%)Speed (25%)Weighted score
    A4350.4×4 + 0.35×3 + 0.25×5 = 3.9
    B3530.4×3 + 0.35×5 + 0.25×3 = 3.7
    📝
    Higher score means better. Option A scores 3.9 and beats B at 3.7. Agree the weights before scoring to avoid bias.

    🧠 Thinking and deciding well

    PracticeWhy
    Diverge then convergeGenerate many ideas first, then choose
    Use data over opinionReduces bias
    Pre-mortemSpot failure before it happens
    Involve the teamBetter ideas and buy-in
    Time-box decisionsAvoid endless analysis
    Decide and reviewCheck results later
    EXAMPLE · Time-box

    Give the team 30 minutes to list options and score them. If still unclear after one more spike, escalate with the options and a recommendation.

    🪜 Removing impediments and escalating

    StepAction
    Log itRecord the impediment and owner
    Try to solve in the teamUse the right expertise
    Escalate if out of reachDescribe impact, options and decision needed
    Follow upConfirm it is fixed
    ReviewLook for patterns in the retrospective

    🪤 Problem solving traps

    TrapBetter approach
    Jumping to solutionsDefine the problem first
    Fixing symptomsFind root cause
    Blaming peopleLook at the process
    Solving aloneInvolve the team
    Not checking resultsMeasure after the fix

    Module 08 · Interview Preparation

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer. Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for behavioural questions.

    🧭 Fundamentals

    Why are soft skills important for an Agile PM?
    An Agile PM leads without command authority. Influence, clear communication and judgement turn plans into results and keep stakeholders and the team aligned.
    How do you answer behavioural questions?
    Use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Be specific, use numbers and say what you learned.
    Which soft skill is your strongest and how do you grow the others?
    Name one with a real example, then describe how you seek feedback, practise and reflect in retrospectives.

    🤝 Negotiation

    Tell me about a time you negotiated scope or deadline.
    Describe the situation, the interests behind each side’s position, the options you created and the trade you agreed. Give the result and what you confirmed in writing.
    What is BATNA and why does it matter?
    Best alternative to a negotiated agreement. It sets your walk-away point and gives you confidence not to accept a poor deal.
    A sponsor wants 40 more points with no date change. What do you say?
    Acknowledge the need, ask about the interest, show velocity and capacity, then offer if-then trades: swap Could items, phase the release or move the date.
    What is the difference between a position and an interest?
    A position is what someone says they want. An interest is why they want it. Solving the interest gives more options.
    How do you close a negotiation?
    Summarise the agreement, confirm owners and dates, and send it in writing the same day.

    🎯 Priority Alignment

    Two directors give conflicting priorities. What do you do?
    Meet each to learn goals, bring them together with shared criteria such as value and cost of delay, produce one ordered list and have the Product Owner confirm it. Escalate with options if still stuck.
    How do you use RICE?
    Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort. For Reach 2,000, Impact 2, Confidence 80% and Effort 4 the score is 800.
    What if everything is a Must?
    Force-rank with a constraint: if we had half the time, what stays? Use MoSCoW with a limit on Musts.
    Who decides backlog order?
    The Product Owner. The Agile PM facilitates alignment and makes trade-offs visible.

    🌱 Adaptability

    Tell me about a time a plan changed suddenly.
    Explain the change, how you paused and assessed impact, the options you raised, who decided, how you communicated and what the result and lesson were.
    How do you balance flexibility with focus?
    Protect the sprint goal and Definition of Done, change backlog order between sprints, and adapt when evidence supports it.
    How do you support a team through a tool change?
    Use ADKAR: explain why, show benefits, train, coach and reinforce. Review after two sprints.
    What is a small experiment?
    A time-boxed change with a measure, such as a 10-minute check-in for two sprints, reviewed in the retrospective.

    💬 Clear Communication

    How do you escalate a delay?
    Use SBAR: situation, background, assessment, recommendation, with the decision needed and a date.
    How do you tailor communication to different audiences?
    Use a communication plan. Sponsors get a one-page update, executives a short summary, users live demos and the team daily stand-ups.
    How do you give difficult feedback?
    Use SBI: situation, behaviour, impact. Listen, agree actions and follow up.
    Why do large teams struggle to communicate?
    Channels grow with n(n−1)÷2. Eight people have 28, ten have 45. Structure and clear channels help.
    How do you make sure a message was understood?
    Ask for read-back or a short summary and agree next steps.

    🧩 Problem Solving

    Describe how you find a root cause.
    Define the problem with a measure, gather data, use 5 Whys or a fishbone, confirm with evidence and agree corrective and preventive actions.
    What is a Pareto chart?
    A ranking of causes by frequency. If Login and Payments are 70 of 100 bugs, fix those first.
    How do you choose between two options?
    Agree criteria and weights, score each option and discuss. For example 0.4×4 + 0.35×3 + 0.25×5 = 3.9 beats 3.7.
    When do you escalate a problem?
    When the team cannot solve it within its authority or time-box. Bring impact, options and a recommendation.
    How do you avoid blaming people?
    Focus on the process and facts, use blameless retrospectives and agree actions on the system.

    🏗️ Scenario: The customer portal in week 10

    The customer portal has a fixed go-live date. Velocity is 30 and the team is at capacity. The sponsor asks for 40 extra points, two directors give conflicting priorities, the payment vendor withdraws, the team of 8 is about to grow to 10, and bugs in Login and Payments keep escaping to production.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Negotiation

    ✍️ The sponsor wants 40 extra points with no date change. How do you respond?
    Model answer: Ask why, to find the interest. Show velocity 30 and that the team is at capacity. Offer if-then trades: swap 40 points of Could items, phase the release or move the date by about one sprint. Agree and send the summary in writing.
    Q. Which response is the strongest?
    • A Say yes and hope
    • B Say no and end the talk
    • C Ask about the interest and offer if-then trades
    • D Escalate immediately without data

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Priority alignment

    ✍️ Feature A: Reach 2,000, Impact 2, Confidence 80%, Effort 4. Feature B: Reach 500, Impact 3, Confidence 100%, Effort 2. Which first and how do you align the directors?
    Model answer: A = 800, B = 750, so A first. Run a workshop where both directors agree the criteria, score together and sign off one ordered list. The Product Owner confirms the order.
    Q. What is Feature A’s RICE score?
    • A 400
    • B 800
    • C 1,600
    • D 3,200

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Adaptability

    ✍️ The payment vendor withdraws mid-sprint. What do you do?
    Model answer: Pause and gather facts. Assess impact (for example two sprints). Generate options such as a second vendor or reduced payment scope. The sponsor decides. Update the plan, risks and roadmap. Tell the team and stakeholders the same day with the reason. Learn in the retrospective.
    Q. What is the best first step?
    • A Cancel the project
    • B Pause, assess impact and list options
    • C Wait for next review
    • D Blame the vendor

    4️⃣ Task 4 · Clear communication

    ✍️ The team grows from 8 to 10. Write the SBAR for the sponsor on the delay and say how many channels the team has.
    Model answer: Channels: 8 gives 28, 10 gives 45. SBAR: Situation: forecast is three sprints late. Background: velocity is 25 and the vendor withdrew. Assessment: the date cannot be met with all scope. Recommendation: cut Could and Should items, decision needed by Friday.

    • B 36
    • C 45
    • D 55

    5️⃣ Task 5 · Problem solving

    ✍️ Login has 40 bugs, Payments 30, Profile 15 and Other 15. How do you tackle escaped bugs?

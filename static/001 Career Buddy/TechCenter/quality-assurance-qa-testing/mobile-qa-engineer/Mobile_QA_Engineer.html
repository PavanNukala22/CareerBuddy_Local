    Course Overview & Fundamentals

    Understand what a mobile QA engineer does, what you will learn, what you need first and how mobile apps and platforms work.

    📘 1. Course Overview

    Mobile apps live in the most unpredictable environment in software: thousands of device models, two main operating systems, changing networks, interruptions, small batteries and app stores that review every release. A Mobile QA Engineer makes sure an app works for real people on real devices, in real conditions, before and after release.

    App lifecycle and states

    Many mobile bugs appear when the app changes state. Know the states and test the transitions.

    StateMeaningTypical test
    Not runningProcess not started or killed by the OSCold start time and restored state
    ForegroundVisible and interactiveNormal functional tests
    BackgroundHidden, may run limited tasksReturn after minutes or hours; session still valid
    Suspended / killedOS reclaimed memoryReopen after the OS killed the app; no data loss
    ⚠️
    Remember: the OS can kill your app at any time to free memory. Unsaved form data and in-flight uploads must survive this.

    The mobile test strategy

    Keep most checks fast and automated at low levels, and reserve real-device sessions for what only they can reveal.

    LevelExamplesWhere it runs
    UnitBusiness logic, view models, validationDeveloper machine and CI
    Integration and APIBackend contracts, local database, auth flowsCI with mocks or a test backend
    UI automationLogin, search, purchase, syncEmulators in CI and a real-device farm
    Exploratory on real devicesGestures, interruptions, network changes, visual polishPhysical devices in the team device lab

    💻 7. Practical Examples

    Example 1: Login with biometrics

    IDScenarioExpected result
    L1Valid email and passwordUser lands on home; session stored securely
    L2Wrong password three timesClear error; account lock message follows the rule
    L3Enable fingerprint or Face ID after loginNext launch offers biometric prompt
    L4Biometric fails or is cancelledFallback to password; no crash
    L5Biometric enrolment removed in device settingsApp asks for full login again
    L6App killed and relaunchedSession restored or login requested as designed

    Example 2: Push notification

    IDScenarioExpected result
    P1Notification permission allowed, app in backgroundNotification appears with correct title and text
    P2Tap notification when app is closedApp opens at the right screen (deep link)
    P3Tap notification when app is openNavigates without losing current state
    P4Permission deniedApp works; settings screen explains how to enable
    P5Device in Do Not Disturb or offline then onlineDelivered or queued as designed; no duplicates

    Example 3: Offline mode and sync

    • Add an item while offline: it appears with a pending indicator.
    • Reconnect: the item syncs once, with no duplicate.
    • Edit the same record on two devices while offline: conflict rule is applied and explained.
    • Kill the app during sync: nothing is lost or corrupted.
    • Switch from Wi-Fi to cellular mid-upload: upload resumes or restarts cleanly.

    driver.find_element(AppiumBy.ACCESSIBILITY_ID, "login").click()
    assert driver.find_element(AppiumBy.ACCESSIBILITY_ID, "welcome").is_displayed()
finally:
    driver.quit()

    📈 9. Intermediate Concepts

    Building a device matrix

    You cannot test everything. Choose devices from your own analytics, then add a few risk-based extras.

    Choose byExamples
    Usage shareTop 10–15 device models and OS versions in your analytics
    OS rangeLatest, latest minus one, and the oldest version you officially support
    ScreenSmall phone, large phone, tablet, a foldable if supported
    Manufacturer riskVendors with aggressive battery or background restrictions
    HardwareLow-memory device, device without biometrics, device with a notch

    Network conditions

    • Use the emulator network settings, Network Link Conditioner on iOS, or a proxy such as Charles to throttle speed and add latency.
    • Test 3G-like speed, high packet loss and complete loss mid-request.
    • Check timeouts, retries, loading states and error messages.
    • Confirm the app does not repeat a payment or order when a request is retried.

    Logs and crash analysis

    PlatformWhere to lookWhat to capture
    Androidadb logcat, ANR traces, Firebase CrashlyticsStack trace, device model, OS version, app version, steps
    iOSXcode device logs, Console app, crash reports, CrashlyticsSymbolicated stack trace, device, iOS version, build number

    Device farms

    Cloud services such as BrowserStack, Sauce Labs, AWS Device Farm and Firebase Test Lab give you access to many real devices without buying them. Use them for broad compatibitity runs and keep a small local lab for exploratory work.

    Mobile accessibility

    • Turn on TalkBack (Android) or VoiceOver (iOS) and complete the main flow by ear.
    • Check that every control has a clear label and role.
    • Touch targets should be large enough (around 44 pt on iOS, 48 dp on Android).
    • Increase the system font size and check that text is not clipped.
    • Check colour contrast and do not rely on colour alone to convey meaning.

    🚀 10. Advanced Concepts

    How Appium works

    Your test (the client) sends commands over HTTP to the Appium server. The server uses a platform driver to control the app on the device.

    PartRole
    Test clientYour script in Python, Java, JavaScript or another language
    Appium serverReceives WebDriver commands and routes them
    DriverUiAutomator2 for Android, XCUITest for iOS
    Device or emulatorRuns the app under test

    Native frameworks: Espresso and XCUITest

    Espresso (Android) and XCUITest (iOS) run close to the app, so they are faster and more stable than black-box tools, but they are platform-specific and usually written by developers or SDETs.

    kotlin@Test
fun loginShowsHome() {
    onView(withId(R.id.email)).perform(typeText("qa@example.com"), closeSoftKeyboard())
    onView(withId(R.id.password)).perform(typeText("Secret123"), closeSoftKeyboard())
    onView(withId(R.id.login)).perform(click())
    onView(withText("Welcome")).check(matches(isDisplayed()))
}

    Performance profiling

    MetricHow to checkRule of thumb
    Cold start timeAndroid Studio Profiler, Xcode Instruments, store vitalsUnder 2 seconds for the first screen
    Frame rateProfile GPU rendering, Instruments Core AnimationSteady 60 fps scrolling; no long frames
    MemoryAndroid Profiler, Instruments Allocations and LeaksNo growth after repeating a flow
    BatteryBattery Historian, Instruments Energy LogNo heavy work or wake locks in background
    App sizeBuild output and store listingKeep downloads small, especially on slow networks

    Mobile security basics

    The OWASP Mobile Application Security Verification Standard (MASVS) and the Mobile Security Testing Guide (MASTG) describe what to verify.

    • Sensitive data is not stored in plain text (shared preferences, files, logs, backups).
    • All traffic uses TLS; consider certificate pinning for high-risk apps.
    • Tokens live in the Keystore or Keychain, not in code or local storage.
    • No secrets are hard-coded in the app package.
    • Deep links validate input and do not expose private screens.
    • Behaviour on rooted or jailbroken devices follows the risk policy.

    A mobile delivery workflow

    1. Developer opens a pull request; unit tests and lint run.
    2. CI builds a debug app and runs UI smoke tests on an emulator.
    3. On merge, CI builds a signed beta and uploads it to TestFlight and the Play internal track.
    4. Testers run exploratory and regression checks on real devices.
    5. Release candidate goes to a staged rollout (for example 5%, then 25%, then 100%).
    6. The team watches crash-free rate and reviews before widening the rollout.
    yaml# GitHub Actions: Android build and unit tests
name: android
on: [pull_request]
jobs:
  build:
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    steps:
      - uses: actions/checkout@v4
      - uses: actions/setup-java@v4
        with: { distribution: temurin, java-version: 17 }
      - run: ./gradlew testDebugUnitTest assembleDebug
      - uses: actions/upload-artifact@v4
        with: { name: app-debug, path: app/build/outputs/apk/debug/*.apk }

    ✅ 12. Testing & Quality

    Release checklist

    • Smoke test passes on the newest and oldest supported OS and on two real devices per platform.
    • Install, upgrade from the previous version and uninstall are verified.
    • Critical flows pass: sign-up, login, core action, payment (with sandbox accounts).
    • Permissions, notifications and deep links work.
    • Accessibility, localisation and dark mode checked on the main screens.
    • No open blocker or critical bugs; accepted risks are recorded.
    • Store listing, privacy details and screenshots are accurate.
    • Crash reporting and analytics are enabled in the release build.

    Quality metrics to track

    MetricWhy it mattersTypical target
    Crash-free sessionsDirect measure of stability99.5% or higher
    ANR rate (Android)Freezes damage ratings and store rankingBelow store thresholds
    Cold start timeFirst impression and retentionUnder 2 seconds
    Store rating and reviewsReveals problems in the fieldStable or rising
    Escaped defectsQuality of testing before releaseFalling over releases
    Update adoptionHow long old versions stay aliveMajority on latest within weeks

    ⭐ 14. Best Practices

    Mobile testing best practices

    PracticeWhy it matters
    Build the device matrix from real analyticsSpend effort where your users are
    Test on real devices as well as emulatorsHardware, sensors, vendor changes and performance only show up on real devices
    Start every test run from a known clean statePrevents leftover data and permissions from hiding bugs
    Always test interruptions and state changesCalls, notifications, background and kill are daily events for users
    Test on slow and unreliable networksMany users are not on fast Wi-Fi
    Use accessibility identifiers for locatorsStable automation and better accessibility at once
    Include device, OS and build in every bugDevelopers cannot reproduce mobile bugs without them
    Use sandbox accounts for payments and dedicated test accountsProtects real money and real customer data
    Roll out gradually and watch crash-free rateLimits the damage if a bad build slips through
    Re-test after every OS release in betaCatches breaking OS changes before users upgrade

    🩺 15. Common Errors & Troubleshooting

    Symptom, likely cause and fix

    SymptomLikely causeFix
    Device not listed by adb devicesUSB debugging off, bad cable or missing driverEnable developer options and USB debugging, accept the prompt, try another cable and run adb kill-server then adb start-server
    Appium cannot create a sessionWrong capabilities, driver not installed, device not readyCheck the Appium log, confirm driver installation with appium driver list and verify device name and platform version
    Element not found in AppiumScreen not loaded, wrong locator or different WebView contextWait for a condition, inspect with Appium Inspector and switch to the correct context
    Test passes on emulator but fails on a real devicePerformance, permissions dialogs or vendor behaviour differRun on the device with logs, handle permission prompts explicitly and check manufacturer battery settings
    App crashes on launch after an updateDatabase migration or removed settingTest upgrade from older versions; capture the stack trace and review the migration
    Push notification never arrivesPermission denied, token not registered, battery optimisationCheck permission, token registration, notification channel settings and device power-saving mode
    Login works on Wi-Fi but fails on cellularCertificate, proxy or timeout issueReproduce with network throttling, inspect failed requests and check server timeouts
    iOS build will not install on a deviceProvisioning or signing problemCheck the certificate, provisioning profile and registered device, or use TestFlight
    ⚠️
    Beginner mistake: testing only on the latest flagship phone. Test on an older, slower, low-memory device too: that is where many users experience your app.

    Troubleshooting routine

    1. Reproduce on a second device and note the model, OS and app version.
    2. Capture logs (logcat or Xcode) and a screen recording.
    3. Change one variable: network, permission, state, data.
    4. Check whether it is new by installing the previous build.
    5. Log the bug with all of the above and the exact steps.

    🎤 16. Interview Preparation

    Questions and model answers

    Q1. How is mobile testing different from web testing?

    Answer: More environments (devices, OS versions, vendors), touch and sensors, changing networks, interruptions, battery, store release cycles and the fact that users may not update.

    Q2. How do you decide which devices to test on?

    Answer: Start from analytics for usage share, cover newest and oldest supported OS, different screen sizes, and add risky manufacturers and low-memory devices.

    Q3. Emulator or real device?

    Answer: Emulators for fast, cheap, repeatable checks and CI. Real devices for performance, sensors, interruptions, vendor behaviour and final verification.

    Q4. What interruptions would you test?

    Answer: Incoming call or SMS, notifications, alarms, low battery, charger connection, switching apps, locking the screen and the OS killing the app in the background.

    Q5. Explain how Appium works.

    Answer: Tests send WebDriver commands to the Appium server, which uses a platform driver such as UiAutomator2 or XCUITest to control the app on the device or emulator.

    Q6. Appium versus Espresso or XCUITest?

    Answer: Appium is cross-platform and language-flexible; Espresso and XCUITest are native, faster and more stable but platform-specific.

    Q7. How do you test offline behaviour?

    Answer: Turn on airplane mode, perform actions, reconnect and verify sync, conflicts, duplicates and error messages; also test connection loss mid-request.

    Q8. What would you check before releasing an app?

    Answer: Smoke and regression on supported OS versions and real devices, upgrade path, permissions, notifications, accessibility, crash reporting enabled, no blockers, and a staged rollout plan.

    Q9. How do you investigate a crash reported from the field?

    Answer: Get the stack trace and device details from Crashlytics or store vitals, reproduce on a similar device and OS, check recent changes, write a regression test after the fix.

    Q10. What metrics define app quality?

    Answer: Crash-free sessions, ANR rate, startup time, store rating, escaped defects and how quickly users adopt updates.

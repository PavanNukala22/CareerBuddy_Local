    Course Overview & Fundamentals

    Understand what a QA Manager / Director of Quality does, what you will learn, what you need first, and the quality-management foundations behind the role.

    📘 1. Course Overview

    A QA Lead decides how a release is tested. A QA Manager / Director of Quality decides how the whole company builds, tests and proves quality: who is on the team, how much money is spent and on what, which numbers leadership watches, and which standards every product team must meet. You move from running a testing effort to running a quality function that is accountable to the business.

    QA Lead or QA Manager / Director: what changes?

    🧭 QA Lead / Test Lead

    Plans and runs testing for a team or release. Works with a sprint or release horizon. Measured on release quality and team predictability.

    🏛️ QA Manager / Director of Quality

    Designs the quality organisation, budget and standards across many teams. Works with a quarter-to-three-year horizon. Measured on business outcomes, cost and risk.

    The director's operating loop

    The loop runs every quarter and is reviewed with finance, engineering and product leadership.

    Typical career ladder

    LevelScopeMain question they answer
    QA Lead / Test LeadOne team or releaseIs this release safe to ship?
    QA ManagerSeveral teams and people managers or leadsDo my teams have the people, tools and process to deliver quality?

    🧱 4. Fundamentals

    A director needs a shared language with finance, HR, engineering and executives. These foundations are the base for everything else in the course.

    Quality assurance, quality control and testing

    TermFocusExample
    Quality assurance (QA)Preventing defects by improving the processCode review standards, definition of done, release criteria
    Quality control (QC)Detecting defects in the productInspections, test execution, acceptance checks
    TestingEvaluating the software by running it or analysing itUnit, integration, system and exploratory tests
    Quality managementDirecting and controlling an organisation for qualityPolicy, objectives, planning, improvement, audits

    Cost of quality

    Cost of quality splits spending into the cost of getting it right and the cost of getting it wrong (the prevention, appraisal and failure model).

    CategoryMeaningExamples
    PreventionStop defects from happeningTraining, standards, design reviews, static analysis rules
    AppraisalFind defects before customers doTest execution, inspections, audits, test tools and environments
    Internal failureDefects found before releaseRework, retests, delayed releases
    External failureDefects found by customersHotfixes, support load, refunds, lost customers, penalties
    💡
    Failures found later usually cost more to fix, so a modest rise in prevention and appraisal spending is often repaid by lower failure cost. Use your own incident and rework data to show the trade.

    Organisation models for QA

    ModelHow it worksStrengthsRisks
    CentralisedOne QA department serves all teamsConsistent standards, flexible staffingDistance from product teams, queues and bottlenecks
    EmbeddedTesters sit inside product teamsDeep product knowledge, fast feedbackUneven practices, isolated people, harder career growth
    Hybrid / centre of excellenceEmbedded testers plus a small central group for standards, tools and coachingLocal speed with shared standardsNeeds clear roles and good communication
    Outsourced / managed serviceA vendor provides testing capacity or a full serviceElastic capacity, lower unit costKnowledge loss, vendor dependence, communication overhead

    Quality maturity

    Maturity models such as TMMi describe five levels of test process maturity. Use them as a conversation tool, not a score to chase.

    TMMi levels: from ad hoc testing to continuously improving, data-driven testing.

    From business goals to quality actions

    Every quality metric should trace back to a business objective; if it does not, ask why you report it.
    Business objectiveQuality goalMetricInitiative
    Grow revenue from the checkout flowFewer failed purchasesProduction defects in checkout; checkout error rateRisk-based regression and monitoring for checkout
    Reduce support costFewer customer-reported defectsDefect escape rate; support tickets per releaseRoot cause programme for top escape sources
    Ship fasterShorter feedback timePipeline duration; change failure rateParallel test execution; flaky test reduction
    Win enterprise customersProve controls to customersSOC 2 report status; audit findingsEvidence automation and process standards
    💡
    Agree the exact definition of each metric and each cost with finance and engineering before you present numbers. Most executive disagreements are about definitions, not about quality.

    ⚙️ 5. Core Concepts

    People and organisational leadership

    ConceptWhat it meansWhy a director cares
    Hiring planRoles, timing and skills needed to meet demandHiring takes months; plan one or two quarters ahead
    Performance managementGoals, regular feedback, reviews and calibrationFair, consistent standards across managers
    Career pathingDefined levels with expectations for technical and management tracksRetention and clear growth
    Capacity planningProductive time available versus testing demandPrevents overload and surprise hiring
    Resource allocationDeciding which people work on which projectsPuts scarce skills where risk and value are highest

    Productive days per person = (working days − leave and holidays − training and admin) × focus factor. FTE needed = demand in person-days × (1 + buffer) ÷ productive days per person.

    Allocation approachHow it worksBest when
    DedicatedPeople stay on one projectLarge, long-running, high-risk products
    Shared poolPeople move between projects as demand shiftsMany small projects with uneven demand
    HybridCore dedicated people plus a flexible pool for peaksMost mid-sized organisations
    ⚠️
    Splitting one person across many projects is costly: context switching reduces focus. Limit anyone to two projects where you can.

    Budget and tool procurement

    Budget lineExamplesTypical decisions
    PeopleSalaries, contractors, recruitingHeadcount, contractor mix, location
    Tools and licencesTest management, automation, performance, security toolsBuy, build or consolidate; licence model
    InfrastructureCloud test environments, device farms, CI runnersScheduling, sizing, spot capacity, reserved discounts
    Training and certificationCourses, conferences, certificationsSkills gaps and retention
    Vendors and servicesManaged testing, penetration testing, auditsInsource or outsource; contract terms

    TCO = licence + implementation + training + infrastructure + support + migration and exit costs, over a stated period (usually three years).

    Macro quality metrics

    MetricDefinitionCaution
    Defect escape rateProduction defects ÷ (pre-release + production defects) × 100Depends on what counts as a defect and when the quarter ends
    Defect removal efficiency (DRE)100% − escape rateSame data, opposite view
    MTTDMean time to detect: average of (detected − started) for incidents or defectsNeeds an agreed rule for the start time
    MTTR (resolve)Mean time to resolve: average of (resolved − detected)The R is used for repair, recovery, respond and resolve; write down which one you mean
    Change failure rateDeployments that cause a failure ÷ total deploymentsNeeds a clear definition of "failure"
    Automation ROI(Benefit − cost) ÷ cost × 100Benefits are estimates; state assumptions
    Cost of qualityPrevention + appraisal + internal failure + external failureFailure costs are often under-counted

    📉 Lagging indicators

    Escape rate, production incidents, customer-reported defects. They show results after the fact.

    📈 Leading indicators

    Requirement coverage, risk coverage, flaky test rate, time to feedback. They warn you before customers do.

    Compliance and process standards

    Standard / frameworkWhat it isQA relevance
    ISO 9001International standard for a quality management system (QMS)Documented processes, internal audits, corrective actions, continual improvement
    ISO/IEC 27001Standard for an information security management system (ISMS)Secure development and testing, test data protection, separate test and production environments
    SOC 2Attestation report from an independent auditor against the AICPA Trust Services CriteriaEvidence that changes are tested, reviewed and approved before release
    ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119Series of software testing standardsReference for test processes and documentation
    ISO/IEC 25010Software product quality modelCommon vocabulary for quality characteristics such as reliability and security
    📝
    ISO standards are certified by accredited certification bodies. SOC 2 is an attestation report issued by a licensed CPA firm; it is not a certification. Clause and control numbers change between editions, so check the current text with your compliance team.

    Enterprise release criteria by risk tier

    TierTypical servicesExample criteria
    Tier 1Revenue-critical, customer-facing, regulatedNo open Critical or Major; pass rate ≥ 98%; all must-have requirements covered; no high vulnerabilities; rollback tested
    Tier 2Important business servicesNo open Critical; ≤ 2 Major with workaround; pass rate ≥ 95%; rollback tested

    Thresholds are examples. Agree yours with engineering, product, security and risk owners and review them at least once a year.

    Performance management

    Feedback should be continuous; the formal review should hold no surprises.
    PracticeHow
    GoalsSpecific, measurable and tied to outcomes (for example, reduce flaky tests from 8% to 3% by Q3)
    One-to-onesWeekly or fortnightly: blockers, feedback, growth, workload
    FeedbackTimely, specific, about behaviour and impact; praise as well as concerns
    CalibrationManagers compare evidence across teams to apply the same standard
    UnderperformanceClear expectations in writing, regular check-ins, support, and early involvement of HR if there is no improvement
    ⚠️
    Never rank individuals by raw metrics such as test case counts or defects logged. They are easy to game and punish people who take on hard, valuable work.

    Capacity planning and allocation

    • Forecast demand per project from release plans and risk, in person-days.
    • Calculate productive days per person after leave, training and a focus factor.
    • Add a buffer for attrition, sickness and urgent work.
    • Compare FTE needed with current FTE; list options for any gap (reprioritise, contract, automate, hire).
    • Review quarterly; update when scope or release dates move.

    Licence management

    • Keep a licence register: product, owner, model, count, cost, renewal date.
    • Review usage every quarter and reclaim inactive licences before renewal.
    • Calendar renewal dates 90 to 120 days ahead so you can negotiate.
    • Consolidate overlapping tools where teams do the same job with different products.

    Example 1: Automation ROI and payback

    python: automation_roi.py# Annual model for an automation programme. Every input is an assumption to replace with your own data.
build_hours = 1200            # one-off effort to build the suite
rate = 50                     # loaded cost per hour (USD)
maintenance_hours = 400       # per year
tools_and_infra = 18000       # per year (licences, cloud)
manual_hours_saved_per_run = 120
runs_per_year = 12
escapes_avoided = 6           # production defects avoided per year (estimate)
cost_per_escape = 4000        # average cost of one escaped defect (estimate)

build_cost = build_hours * rate
annual_cost = maintenance_hours * rate + tools_and_infra
annual_benefit = manual_hours_saved_per_run * runs_per_year * rate + escapes_avoided * cost_per_escape
annual_net = annual_benefit - annual_cost

print(f"Build cost            : ${build_cost:,.0f}")
print(f"Annual running cost   : ${annual_cost:,.0f}")
print(f"Annual benefit        : ${annual_benefit:,.0f}")
print(f"Annual net (year 2+)  : ${annual_net:,.0f}")
for years in (1, 2, 3):
    benefit = annual_benefit * years
    cost = build_cost + annual_cost * years
    roi = 100 * (benefit - cost) / cost
    net = benefit - cost
    sign = "-" if net < 0 else ""
    print(f"{years}-year ROI           : {roi:6.1f}%   (net {sign}${abs(net):,.0f})")
print(f"Payback               : {build_cost / (annual_net / 12):.1f} months")

def weighted(vals, w):
    return sum(v * x for v, x in zip(vals, w)) / sum(w)


w = list(weights.values())

for name, vals in scores.items():

w2[1] *= 2

for name, vals in scores.items():

    Blameless incident reviews

    After a significant escape, QA takes part in a review that asks how did our system allow this? rather than who made a mistake?

    1. Build a timeline: start, detection, response, resolution.
    2. Identify contributing causes in requirements, tests, environments, monitoring and process.
    3. Agree a small number of actions with owners and dates.
    4. Track the actions and review the effect on escape rate and MTTD.

    pythondef npv(rate, cashflows):
    # cashflows[0] is year 0 (usually negative), then one value per year
    return sum(cf / (1 + rate) ** t for t, cf in enumerate(cashflows))

# Build cost 60k in year 0, then 58k net benefit a year for 3 years, discount rate 10%

    ⭐ 14. Best Practices

    • Start from the business. Link every goal, metric and budget line to a business objective.
    • Define terms once. Write down every metric with its start and end event and its data source.
    • Show trends and ranges. Report medians as well as means and four quarters rather than one snapshot.
    • Pair metrics. Every headline metric gets a balancing metric to discourage gaming.
    • Plan capacity with a buffer. Build in time for leave, training, attrition and urgent work.
    • Hire with structure. Same questions, written rubric, independent scoring before the debrief.
    • Evaluate vendors on evidence. Agree weights first, test with your own scenarios, calculate TCO and run a sensitivity check.
    • Review spend monthly. Licence usage, cloud cost and renewals need a regular owner.
    • Tier your release criteria. Match rigour to risk and require named, time-limited waivers.
    • Automate evidence. Collect proof of controls from your tools continuously.
    • Share ownership of quality. Quality is built by everyone; QA enables and verifies.
    • Invest in your managers. Coach them, build successors and protect time for their people.

    Do and don't

    ✅ Do

    Say "Escape rate fell from 14.0% to 4.4% in four quarters; here is the cost and the next target."

    ❌ Don't

    Say "Quality is better" without numbers, definitions or a comparison.

    ✅ Do

    Present the budget request with the outcome, the cost, the alternatives and the risk of doing nothing.

    ❌ Don't

    Ask for headcount by saying the team is busy.

    ✅ Do

    Set a waiver expiry date and review each waiver in the monthly quality review.

    ❌ Don't

    Let exceptions pile up with no owner or end date.

    💡
    Before any report or meeting, ask: what decision does this help someone make? If there is none, shorten it or drop it.

    🎤 16. Interview Preparation

    Core questions

    QuestionShort answer
    How does a Director of Quality differ from a QA Lead?A lead runs testing for a team or release; a director sets quality strategy, budget, standards and organisation across the company and is accountable for business outcomes.
    How do you plan QA capacity?Estimate demand per project in person-days, compute productive days per person after leave, training and a focus factor, add a buffer, and compare FTE needed with current FTE.
    How do you hire well?Define outcomes and a scorecard first, use structured interviews with a rubric and a practical exercise, score independently and decide in a debrief.
    How do you manage performance fairly?Clear goals, regular feedback, evidence-based reviews and calibration across managers using written level expectations.
    How do you evaluate a vendor?Agree weighted criteria first, run a proof of concept with your own scenarios, calculate three-year TCO, check references and security, and test sensitivity to weights.
    How do you control cloud costs for test environments?Tag everything, schedule non-production environments, use ephemeral and interruptible capacity where safe, right-size, set budgets and alerts, and report showback.
    How do you calculate automation ROI?(Benefit − cost) ÷ cost, where cost includes build, maintenance, tools and infrastructure, and benefits include saved effort and avoided failures with stated assumptions.
    Define defect escape rate.Production defects divided by all defects found before and after release, as a percentage; the inverse is defect removal efficiency.
    What are MTTD and MTTR?Mean time to detect is the average from problem start to detection; mean time to resolve is the average from detection to resolution. Define the R explicitly because it is used for several meanings.
    Which quality metrics do you show executives?Escape rate, MTTD, MTTR, change failure rate, automation ROI, cost of quality and compliance status, with trends, targets and balancing metrics.
    What is the difference between SOC 2 and ISO 27001?SOC 2 is an auditor's attestation report against the Trust Services Criteria; ISO 27001 is a certification of an information security management system by an accredited body.
    What does QA contribute to a SOC 2 audit?Evidence that changes are tested, reviewed and approved before release, plus documented exceptions and access controls on test environments.
    How do you define enterprise release criteria?Tier services by risk, set measurable criteria for each tier, require named time-limited waivers and enforce the rules in the pipeline.
    How do you align QA with business objectives?Cascade company objectives into quality objectives, key results and metrics, and review them in a regular business review.
    How do you decide between building and buying a tool?Build when it differentiates you or constraints demand it and you can maintain it; otherwise buy, comparing the full life cost of each.

    Scenario questions

    ScenarioHow to approach it
    The CFO asks for a 15% cut to the QA budget.Show the current spend by category, find waste (unused licences, always-on environments, overlapping tools), propose options with the risk of each, and protect the controls and capacity that reduce escape rate.
    Escape rate rose from 5% to 9% in one quarter.Check definitions and data first, classify the escapes by cause and service, find the top patterns, agree targeted actions and review progress monthly.
    Engineering wants to skip release criteria for a deadline.Use the tiered policy: show which criteria fail and the risk, offer options, and if the business accepts the risk require a named, time-limited waiver.
    A key vendor announces a 40% price increase at renewal.Check contract caps, gather usage data, evaluate alternatives with a proof of concept, negotiate using the alternatives, and plan a migration or phased reduction.
    Two strong testers threaten to leave.Talk to them, understand the cause (growth, workload, pay, manager), offer a growth path and workload fix where possible, and build cross-training to reduce key-person risk.

    Structure your answers

    Use STAR for experience questions; quote numbers for the result.

    Prepare three stories

    • A time you built a business case and won budget, with the figures.
    • A time you improved a macro metric (escape rate, MTTD, MTTR, cost) and how you proved it.
    • A time you led a difficult people decision or reorganisation and how you kept trust.
    💡
    Quote real numbers: "escape rate fell from 14% to 4.4% in four quarters" is stronger than "quality improved".

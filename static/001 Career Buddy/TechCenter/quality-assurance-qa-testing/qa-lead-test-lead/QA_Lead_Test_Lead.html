    📘 1. Course Overview

    Every release depends on decisions that are not about a single test case: what to test first, how much testing is enough, which defect to fix today, and whether the product is safe to ship. A QA Lead / Test Lead owns those decisions for a team or project. You move from executing tests to planning the testing, steering the team, measuring the process and keeping everyone informed.

    📋
    Test Planning & Governance
    Test strategy drafting, risk-based testing analysis, sprint estimation and requirement traceability matrices (RTM).
    🧭
    Team Execution & Triage
    Bug triage facilitation, defect prioritisation, blocker escalation and daily workload balancing across manual and automated tracks.
    📊
    Process Metrics
    Test velocity, defect leakage, test pass/fail rates and mean time to detect (MTTD).
    🤝
    Cross-Functional Communication
    Daily coordination with product managers, engineering leads and DevOps teams.

    QA Engineer or QA Lead: what changes?

    🧪 QA Engineer

    Designs and runs test cases, logs defects, automates scenarios and owns the quality of their stories. Success is measured by the quality of the work delivered.

    🧭 QA Lead / Test Lead

    Plans and estimates testing, assigns work, runs triage, reports status and risk, and improves the process. Success is measured by the team's predictability and the quality of the release.

    The lead's working loop

    The loop repeats every sprint; each pass should make the next plan more accurate.

    🎯 2. Learning Objectives

    After this course you will be able to run the testing for a sprint or a release from planning to sign-off.

    • Draft a test strategy and a test plan with scope, approach, entry and exit criteria.
    • Perform risk-based testing analysis and use it to decide test depth and order.
    • Estimate testing effort for a sprint using historical velocity, three-point estimates and capacity planning.
    • Build and maintain a requirement traceability matrix (RTM) and measure requirement coverage.
    • Facilitate bug triage, separate severity from priority and agree on clear defect decisions.
    • Escalate blockers early with the impact and a recommendation.
    • Balance daily workload across manual and automated tracks using capacity and work-in-progress limits.
    • Track test velocity, defect leakage, pass/fail rates and MTTD and explain what the numbers mean.

    🧱 4. Fundamentals

    A lead needs a shared vocabulary with developers, product managers and DevOps. These are the foundations you will use every day.

    Software Testing Life Cycle (STLC)

    Each stage has entry and exit criteria; the lead decides when a stage is complete.
    StageLead's responsibilityOutput
    Requirement analysisReview for testability; raise gaps; start the RTMQuestions log, RTM draft
    Test planningScope, approach, estimates, risks, criteriaTest plan
    Test designReview test cases for coverage of risk and requirementsReviewed test suite
    Environment setupConfirm environments, data and access with DevOpsReady environment, smoke check
    Test executionAssign work, track progress, run triageResults, defect log, daily status
    Test closureExit criteria check, lessons learnedTest summary report

    Waterfall and Agile: what the lead does differently

    🏗️ Waterfall / phased

    One detailed plan up front, a long test phase near the end, formal sign-off documents and a single big release decision.

    🔄 Agile / iterative

    A lightweight plan per release and per sprint, testing in every sprint, continuous risk review and frequent small release decisions.

    Test levels and types

    TermMeaningTypical owner
    Unit testChecks a small piece of code in isolationDeveloper
    Integration testChecks that components work togetherDeveloper and QA
    System testChecks the whole product against requirementsQA
    UATBusiness users confirm the product meets their needsBusiness with QA support
    Smoke testQuick check that a build is stable enough to test furtherQA or pipeline
    Sanity testNarrow check that a specific fix or change worksQA
    Regression testChecks that existing behaviour still works after a changeQA, mostly automated
    Exploratory testSimultaneous learning, test design and executionQA

    Verification and validation

    🔎 Verification

    Are we building the product right? Reviews, walkthroughs and static checks against the specification.

    ✅ Validation

    Are we building the right product? Running the software to check it meets user needs.

    Defect life cycle

    Side paths: Reopened (retest fails), Deferred, Duplicate, Rejected, Cannot reproduce.
    💡
    Agree the exact meaning of each status with the team in one meeting. Most reporting problems later come from statuses that mean different things to different people.

    Entry, exit and suspension criteria

    TypeExample criteria
    EntryBuild deployed and smoke test passed; test environment and data ready; requirements reviewed; test cases approved
    Exit100% of high-priority tests executed; no open Blocker or Critical defects; overall pass rate of at least 95%; RTM shows every must-have requirement covered; known issues accepted by the product owner
    SuspensionSmoke test fails; environment is down for more than 4 hours; more than 30% of tests blocked by one defect
    ResumptionBlocking defect fixed and verified; environment restored and smoke test passed

    The numbers above are examples. Agree thresholds with the product owner and engineering lead before testing starts.

    Risk-based testing analysis

    You never have time to test everything, so test the riskiest things first and deepest. Risk is scored as likelihood × impact, each on a 1 to 5 scale.

    ScoreBandWhat it means for testing
    15 to 25CriticalFull coverage with several techniques, automated regression, review by a second tester
    10 to 14HighThorough coverage of main and failure paths, automated smoke checks
    5 to 9MediumMain paths and key boundaries; exploratory time
    1 to 4LowSmoke check or light exploratory test; accept the residual risk

    1Payment checkout3515 (Critical)API and UI tests, negative and failure paths, security and load checks, automated regression
    2Login and SSO2510 (High)Main flows, lockout and session expiry, automated smoke
    4Order history339 (Medium)Main paths, pagination and filters
    3Search ranking428 (Medium)Key queries and boundaries, exploratory session
    5Promo banner text212 (Low)Visual smoke check

    📦 Product risk

    The product may not work as needed. Example: tax is calculated incorrectly at checkout.

    🗓️ Project risk

    The project may not deliver testing on time. Example: the only performance tester leaves in the last sprint.

    Requirement traceability matrix (RTM)

    An RTM links each requirement to the tests that verify it and to the defects found. It answers two questions: is everything tested? and why does this test exist?

    Req IDRequirementPriorityTest casesTest statusDefects
    REQ-01User can log in with email and passwordHighTC-101, TC-102Passed-
    REQ-02Account locks after 5 failed attemptsHighTC-103FailedD-105
    REQ-03User can reset the passwordMediumnoneNot covered-
    REQ-04Session expires after 30 minutes of inactivityMediumTC-104Passed-
    TypeDirectionUsed for
    Forward traceabilityRequirement to testsFinding requirements with no tests
    Backward traceabilityTest to requirementFinding tests that verify nothing needed (waste)
    BidirectionalBoth directionsFull coverage and change impact analysis
    💡
    Requirement coverage = requirements with at least one linked test ÷ total requirements × 100. Coverage says a test exists, not that it passed, so also report how many requirements are fully verified.

    Bug triage facilitation

    Triage is a short, regular meeting where the team decides what happens to each new or disputed defect. The lead facilitates; product, engineering and QA decide together.

    Aim for 15 to 30 minutes, two or three times a week, or daily near a release.
    OutcomeWhen to use it
    Fix nowBlocks testing or release, or high priority and low effort
    Fix in this sprintImportant and fits in capacity
    BacklogReal but not urgent; product owner agrees to schedule later
    Defer / won't fixLow impact, high cost or out of scope; record the reason and owner of the decision
    DuplicateLink to the original and close this one
    Cannot reproduceAsk for steps, build and environment; retry; close with a note after a set time
    Works as designedConfirm with product; raise a requirement change if users would be surprised

    Triage meeting tips

    • Share the filter before the meeting so people arrive prepared.
    • Time-box each defect to two minutes; move long debates offline.
    • Never discuss solutions in depth; decide the outcome and the owner.
    • Record every decision in the tracker, not only in meeting notes.

    Use SBAR to keep the message short: Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation.

    textS: The staging environment has been down since 09:30. 14 of 20 planned tests for Sprint 12 cannot run.
B: A configuration change was deployed last night. DevOps is aware; no ETA yet.
A: If it is not fixed by 14:00 we lose one test day and the Friday release is at risk.
R: Ask DevOps for an ETA by 11:00. If none, roll back the change and re-run the smoke test.

    Daily workload balancing across manual and automated tracks

    TrackTypical workHow to balance
    ManualNew feature testing, exploratory sessions, usability, UAT supportAssign by feature and skill; keep a buffer for retests
    AutomationNew scripts, maintenance, flaky test fixes, CI healthReserve fixed capacity every sprint, not "when free"
    SharedDefect retests, test data, environment checksRotate ownership daily so it does not fall on one person
    • Look at the board every morning: who is blocked, who is overloaded, what is about to breach its target.
    • Limit work in progress per person (for example, two items) to finish work before starting more.
    • Hold back 10 to 20% of capacity for retests and surprises.
    • Keep a skills matrix so you can move people when one track is behind.

    Process metrics: quick definitions

    MetricDefinition
    Test velocityTest cases executed (or designed, or automated) per person per day or per sprint
    Defect leakageDefects found after release ÷ (defects found before release + after release) × 100
    Defect removal efficiency (DRE)100% minus leakage; how many defects testing caught
    Test pass ratePassed ÷ executed × 100
    Execution rateExecuted ÷ planned × 100
    MTTDMean time to detect: average time from a defect being introduced to being found
    Defect densityDefects per size unit (per thousand lines of code, per story point or per feature)
    Reopen rateDefects reopened ÷ defects fixed; shows fix and retest quality

    💻 7. Practical Examples

    Example 1: Sprint estimate with three-point estimation

    Three-point (PERT) estimation uses an optimistic (O), most likely (M) and pessimistic (P) value. Expected effort E = (O + 4M + P) ÷ 6 and standard deviation SD = (P − O) ÷ 6.

    python: pert.pyfrom math import sqrt

tasks = {
    "Test design":       (3, 5, 10),
    "Execution cycle 1": (4, 6, 14),
    "Regression":        (2, 3, 8),
    "Automation":        (5, 8, 17),
}

total, var = 0.0, 0.0
for name, (o, m, p) in tasks.items():
    e = (o + 4 * m + p) / 6
    sd = (p - o) / 6
    total += e
    var += sd ** 2
    print(f"{name:<18} E={e:5.2f}  SD={sd:4.2f}")

sd_total = sqrt(var)
print(f"Total expected     : {total:.1f} person-days")
print(f"Total SD           : {sd_total:.2f}")
print(f"95% confidence     : {total + 2 * sd_total:.1f} person-days")
    outputTest design        E= 5.50  SD=1.17
Execution cycle 1  E= 7.00  SD=1.67
Regression         E= 3.67  SD=1.00
Automation         E= 9.00  SD=2.00
Total expected     : 25.2 person-days
Total SD           : 3.02
95% confidence     : 31.2 person-days
    💡
    Share the range, not a single number: "about 25 person-days, up to 31 with high confidence". It starts the right conversation about scope and risk.

    python: rtm_check.pyimport csv
from collections import defaultdict

tests_by_req = defaultdict(list)
priority = {}

with open("rtm.csv", newline="") as f:
    for row in csv.DictReader(f):
        priority[row["req_id"]] = row["priority"]
        if row["test_id"]:
            tests_by_req[row["req_id"]].append(row["status"])
        else:
            tests_by_req.setdefault(row["req_id"], [])

total = len(tests_by_req)
covered = [r for r, t in tests_by_req.items() if t]
uncovered = [r for r, t in tests_by_req.items() if not t]
verified = [r for r, t in tests_by_req.items() if t and all(s == "PASS" for s in t)]

print(f"Requirements         : {total}")
print(f"Coverage             : {100 * len(covered) / total:.1f}%")
print(f"Verified (all pass)  : {len(verified)}")
print("Uncovered            :", ", ".join(uncovered))
print("High priority gaps   :", ", ".join(r for r in uncovered if priority[r] == "High"))

    python: metrics.pyimport csv
from datetime import datetime
from statistics import mean

FMT = "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M"


def load(path):
    with open(path, newline="") as f:
        return list(csv.DictReader(f))


def hours(start, end):
    delta = datetime.strptime(end, FMT) - datetime.strptime(start, FMT)
    return delta.total_seconds() / 3600


def leakage(defects):
    prod = sum(1 for d in defects if d["found_in"] == "PROD")
    return 100 * prod / len(defects) if defects else 0.0


def mttd(defects):
    gaps = [hours(d["introduced_at"], d["detected_at"]) for d in defects]
    return mean(gaps) if gaps else 0.0


def pass_rate(results):
    executed = [r for r in results if r["status"] in ("PASS", "FAIL")]
    passed = sum(1 for r in executed if r["status"] == "PASS")
    return 100 * passed / len(executed) if executed else 0.0


def execution_rate(results):
    executed = sum(1 for r in results if r["status"] in ("PASS", "FAIL"))
    return 100 * executed / len(results) if results else 0.0


if __name__ == "__main__":
    defects = load("defects.csv")
    results = load("results.csv")
    print(f"Defect leakage : {leakage(defects):.1f}%")
    print(f"DRE            : {100 - leakage(defects):.1f}%")
    print(f"MTTD           : {mttd(defects):.1f} hours")
    print(f"Pass rate      : {pass_rate(results):.1f}%")
    print(f"Execution rate : {execution_rate(results):.1f}%")

    Lab 1: Draft a test plan

    1. Pick a feature or release you know.

    Lab 2: Risk-based analysis

    1. List 8 to 10 features.
    2. Score likelihood and impact (1 to 5) with the team.
    3. Calculate the score and band for each feature.
    4. Map each band to a test depth and reorder the test design list.

    🔎 Expected result

    A scored risk register and a prioritised test list.

    🛠️ If it goes wrong

    If every item scores High, make the scale stricter or compare features against each other.

    Lab 3: Estimate and capacity

    1. Break the sprint testing into 4 to 6 tasks.
    2. Give O, M and P for each task and run the PERT script.
    3. Build the capacity table with a focus factor.
    4. List two options if demand exceeds capacity.

    🔎 Expected result

    An estimate with a range and a capacity comparison.

    🛠️ If it goes wrong

    If demand is far above capacity, return to Lab 2 and cut low-risk scope first.

    Lab 4: Build an RTM

    1. Take 10 requirements or user stories.
    2. Link each to test cases (or note that none exist).
    3. Run the coverage script or use spreadsheet formulas.
    4. Create test design tasks for uncovered high-priority items.

    🔎 Expected result

    An RTM with a coverage percentage and a gap list.

    🛠️ If it goes wrong

    Tests with no requirement are candidates for review; ask why they exist.

    Lab 5: Run a mock triage

    1. Take 10 defects from your tracker (or invent them).
    2. Assign severity first, then agree priority with a colleague acting as product owner.
    3. Decide an outcome, owner and date for each.
    4. Time the session and keep it under 30 minutes.

    🔎 Expected result

    A triage log with decisions and owners.

    🛠️ If it goes wrong

    If debates run long, park them and set a follow-up with only the people involved.

    Lab 6: Metrics dashboard

    2. Add a trend: the same metrics for the last 3 sprints.

    Definition of Ready and Definition of Done: QA items

    📥 Definition of Ready

    • Acceptance criteria are specific and testable
    • Test data and environment needs are known
    • Dependencies and risks are listed
    • Estimate includes testing

    📤 Definition of Done

    • Acceptance tests executed and passed
    • No open Critical or Major defects for the story
    • Regression tests updated or automated
    • RTM and test results are linked in the tracker

    Estimation techniques

    TechniqueHow it worksBest for
    Planning pokerTeam members estimate independently and discuss differencesStories in refinement
    Historical velocityUse test effort or throughput from past sprintsStable teams with history
    Three-point (PERT)Optimistic, most likely and pessimistic values give an expected value and rangeUncertain or larger tasks
    Work breakdownSplit into small tasks, estimate each, add contingencyReleases and test cycles
    Ratio to development effortTesting effort as a share of development effort, calibrated from your own historyEarly rough estimates
    ⚠️
    Any ratio you quote must come from your own history. Industry rules of thumb vary widely by product, risk and automation level.

    Capacity = people × available days × focus factor. Subtract planned leave and holidays first, then apply a realistic focus factor (often 0.6 to 0.8) for meetings, retests and support.

    ✅ Good candidates

    Stable, repetitive, high-risk, data-driven and frequently run checks: smoke, regression of core flows, API contracts.

    🚫 Poor candidates

    Features that change weekly, one-off checks, usability judgement, and tests that need complex physical setup.

    Forecasting completion

    Days left = remaining tests ÷ recent daily execution rate. Example: 120 tests remain and the last three days averaged 20 executions per day, so about 6 working days remain. If the release is in 4 days, raise it now with options.

    Automation governance and return on investment

    Break-even runs = cost to automate ÷ (manual cost per run − automated maintenance cost per run).

    pythondef break_even_runs(build_hours, manual_hours_per_run, maintenance_hours_per_run):
    saving = manual_hours_per_run - maintenance_hours_per_run
    return build_hours / saving if saving > 0 else float("inf")

print(break_even_runs(8, 1.5, 0.25))   # 6.4 runs

    A test that takes 8 hours to automate, costs 1.5 hours to run manually and 0.25 hours a run to maintain pays back after about 6.4 runs. Tests run only once or twice rarely justify automation.

    ⭐ 14. Best Practices

    • Plan by risk. Spend the most effort where likelihood and impact are highest.
    • Review requirements early. A defect found in refinement is the cheapest one.
    • Define terms once. Severity, priority, statuses and every metric need written definitions.
    • Time-box triage. Decide the outcome and owner; move solutions offline.
    • Escalate early with options. State the impact and recommend an action.
    • Protect capacity. Keep a buffer and limit work in progress; overtime is not a plan.
    • Pair metrics. Show pass rate with execution rate, and speed with quality.
    • Show trends. One number says little; four sprints of numbers show direction.
    • Keep automation healthy. Fix or remove flaky tests; reserve capacity for maintenance.
    • Communicate in the audience's language. Business risk for product, evidence for engineering, dates for delivery.
    • Record decisions and accepted risks with names and dates.
    • Run retrospectives with data and follow up on actions.

    Do and don't

    ✅ Do

    Say "Here is what we tested, what we did not, and the risk of each. Here are three options."

    ❌ Don't

    Say "QA has signed off" without evidence, or "the testing is not finished" without options.

    ✅ Do

    Ask a developer to confirm a defect's impact before arguing about severity.

    ❌ Don't

    Turn triage into blame; focus on the defect and the decision.

    💡
    When in doubt, ask: what decision does this report or meeting help someone make? If none, shorten or drop it.

    🎤 16. Interview Preparation

    Core questions

    QuestionShort answer
    What is the difference between a test strategy and a test plan?The strategy is long-lived and describes how we test in general; the plan covers one project or release with scope, schedule, resources and risks.
    How do you do risk-based testing?List features, score likelihood and impact, calculate a risk score, and use it to decide test depth and order. Revisit it when scope or risk changes.
    How do you estimate testing effort?Break work into tasks, use three-point estimates and historical data, compare with capacity (people × days × focus factor) and present a range with assumptions.
    What is an RTM and why use it?It links requirements to tests and defects. It shows coverage gaps, justifies each test and supports impact analysis when requirements change.
    Severity versus priority?Severity is technical impact; priority is business urgency. QA proposes severity; the product owner decides priority.
    How do you run an effective bug triage?Share the list first, time-box each defect, confirm severity, set priority with product, decide the outcome and owner, and record it in the tracker.
    How do you handle a release blocker?Raise it immediately, contact the owner, and if unresolved escalate with impact and a recommendation (SBAR). Keep stakeholders updated until it is resolved.
    How do you balance manual and automation work?Plan by capacity, reserve a fixed share for automation and maintenance, limit work in progress, and rebalance daily from the board.
    Define defect leakage and how you reduce it.Defects found after release divided by all defects found, times 100. Reduce it with risk-based planning, RTM reviews, stronger regression and root cause analysis of each escape.
    What is MTTD?Mean time to detect: the average time between a defect being introduced and being found. Lower is better; improve it with earlier testing and faster pipelines.
    Which metrics do you report weekly?Execution and pass rate, open defects by severity and age, leakage trend, MTTD, and the top risks with a forecast.
    How do you communicate with product, engineering and DevOps?Daily short updates in their language: risk and readiness for product, reproducible defects for engineering, environment and pipeline needs for DevOps.
    How do you decide whether to release?Use agreed exit criteria and a scorecard. QA presents evidence and risks; the product owner or release manager makes and records the decision.
    What do you do when asked to cut testing time?Show coverage and remaining risk, rank by risk, and offer options: reduce scope, add capacity, move the date or accept a named risk.
    How do you improve a weak process?Measure, find the biggest problem, try one change, check the effect and standardise or stop.

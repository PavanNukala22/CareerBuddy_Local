    Module 01 · Research & Elicitation

    Interviewing SMEs

    Interviewing Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) and translating complex technical architectures into plain language. Build practical skills through examples, visual explanations, and a 50-question focused quiz.

    🎯 Prepare the objective

    Define the information gap before the interview. Identify what the writer needs to know, what evidence already exists, and which questions require SME input.

    🎯
    User goal
    What the reader needs to accomplish.
    🧭
    Context
    What must be known before acting.
    ✅
    Outcome
    What successful completion looks like.

    ❓ Ask effective questions

    Start with open questions when discovering a workflow, then narrow into decisions, exceptions, terminology, and examples. Avoid asking questions that embed an unverified assumption.

    Good practiceWhy it helps
    Use precise, task-focused wordingReaders can act without guessing.
    Show dependencies before actionsPrevents avoidable failure.
    Test against the real product or processKeeps documentation accurate.

    👂 Listen and verify

    Paraphrase ambiguous answers, confirm terminology, and distinguish facts from assumptions. Important claims should be checked against authoritative sources.

    ✕ BAD

    Select Settings, choose Notifications, and enable Email alerts.

    🗂️ Capture structured notes

    Separate confirmed facts, open questions, decisions, sources, and follow-up owners. Record terminology that needs consistent use.

    💡
    Practical tip: A structured note set is more useful than an unfiltered transcript.

    🧪 Worked example

    Objective: document rejected access requests. Questions: What triggers rejection? Which role reviews it? What happens next? What is logged? What is the escalation path?

    🧪
    Worked example: After the meeting, verify the workflow in the current product or approved process documentation.

    🔬 🧠 Advanced SME elicitation

    Advanced interviews uncover assumptions, decision criteria, exceptions, and undocumented tribal knowledge.

    🚀
    Advanced practice: Treat the interview as evidence gathering: distinguish observed behavior from opinion and verify high-impact claims with artifacts.
    🎯
    Design for reuse
    Build content structures that are easy to review, update, and reuse.
    🔎
    Validate with evidence
    Verify important claims, procedures, and outputs against the real source.
    🛡️
    Control risk
    Expose prerequisites, dependencies, exceptions, and recovery paths.

    🧠 🔎 Evidence triangulation

    If an SME says “the service always retries,” ask where that behavior is configured, what happens after the retry limit, and which log or metric proves it. Compare the answer with diagrams, tickets, code comments, or product behavior.

    Module 02 · Research & Elicitation

    Translating Complex Technical Architectures into Plain Language

    Interviewing Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) and translating complex technical architectures into plain language. Build practical skills through examples, visual explanations, and a 50-question focused quiz.

    🎯 Choose the right abstraction

    Explain architecture at the level required by the audience. A beginner may need major components and flow, while an engineer may need interfaces and dependencies.

    🎯
    User goal
    What the reader needs to accomplish.
    🧭
    Context
    What must be known before acting.
    ✅
    Outcome
    What successful completion looks like.

    🔄 Explain data flow

    Anchor the explanation to a realistic request or transaction. Describe the source, major processing steps, destination, and important boundaries.

    Good practiceWhy it helps
    Use precise, task-focused wordingReaders can act without guessing.
    Show dependencies before actionsPrevents avoidable failure.
    Test against the real product or processKeeps documentation accurate.

    🧠 Use analogies carefully

    Analogies can create a mental model, but state the actual technical behavior and avoid comparisons that imply false capabilities.

    ✕ BAD

    Configure the settings as appropriate and continue.

    ✓ GOOD

    Select Settings, choose Notifications, and enable Email alerts.

    🗺️ Narrate diagrams

    When a diagram is dense, describe the primary flow and the role of the major components. Use the narrative to direct attention to the important path.

    💡
    Practical tip: Readers should not have to decode every box before understanding the main story.

    🧪 Worked example

    Engineering text: “The service asynchronously performs idempotent reconciliation against the upstream ledger.” Plain-language version: “The service compares records with the external ledger in the background and can safely repeat the operation without creating duplicates.”

    🧪
    Worked example: Have a technical reviewer confirm that the simplified wording preserves the constraints.

    🔬 🏗️ Advanced architecture storytelling

    Advanced writers preserve technical truth while controlling abstraction so different audiences understand the same system at different depths.

    🚀
    Advanced practice: Use layered explanations: purpose first, major components second, data flow third, and implementation detail only when needed.
    🎯
    Design for reuse
    Build content structures that are easy to review, update, and reuse.
    🔎
    Validate with evidence
    Verify important claims, procedures, and outputs against the real source.
    🛡️
    Control risk
    Expose prerequisites, dependencies, exceptions, and recovery paths.

    🧠 🔄 Layered architecture example

    A beginner explanation can say “the API receives a request and sends work to a processing service.” An advanced layer can add authentication, queueing, retries, persistence, and observability without forcing every reader to absorb every layer.

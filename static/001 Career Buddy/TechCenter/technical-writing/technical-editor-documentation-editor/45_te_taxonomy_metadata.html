    Module 01

    Taxonomy and Metadata for Technical Editors

    Tagging, categorization, index management and search optimization: making documentation findable, filterable and consistent for readers and search engines.

    🏛️ Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    Taxonomy and metadata turn a pile of pages into a navigable knowledge base. Disciplined tagging, good indexes and tuned search make content findable and measurable.

    🏷️
    Tagging
    Consistent, controlled labels.
    🗂️
    Categories
    Facets and hierarchies.
    📇
    Indexes
    Entries readers use.
    🔎
    Search
    Findable content.
    💡
    Taxonomy and metadata are the information architecture behind findability: they decide whether a reader finds the right page in seconds or gives up.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Design taxonomies with facets, hierarchies and controlled vocabularies.
    2. Define metadata schemas and tagging rules for documentation.
    3. Build and maintain indexes, glossaries and cross-references.
    4. Optimize pages for site search and web search engines.
    5. Analyze search queries and fix zero-result problems.
    6. Govern tags: prevent sprawl, merge duplicates, retire stale values.
    7. Measure findability with search and navigation metrics.

    🗂️ Basic: Hierarchies and Facets

    ApproachHow it worksUse when
    Hierarchy (tree)Parent and child categoriesStable, mutually exclusive topics
    FacetsIndependent filters: product, task, audience, versionContent fits several dimensions
    Flat tagsFree labelsInformal, small sets (risky at scale)
    Controlled vocabularyApproved list of termsConsistency and automation
    ThesaurusTerms plus synonyms and relationsSearch and indexing

Audience: Admin | Developer | End user

    RuleExample
    Use nouns for topics and verbs for tasksBilling; Configure alerts

    Change requests, tag audits, merging and retiring tags, ownership, versioning of the taxonomy and communication.

    🧹 Governing the Taxonomy

    PracticeDetail
    Taxonomy ownerNamed person or small council
    Request processForm with term, definition, example, reason
    Decision criteriaUsed by many pages; user language; no duplicate
    Merge and retireMap old tag to new; redirect filters
    VersioningDated releases with change notes
    TrainingAuthors know the vocabulary
    Quarterly auditUsage, duplicates, orphan tags
    textTAG AUDIT
Tags in use: 600 | Used on only 1 page: 240 -> 240 / 600 = 40% (target under 15%)
Near-duplicates found: "api-key", "api-keys", "apikey" -> merge into "api-keys".
Action: retire 180 single-use tags; map 60 to approved terms; leave 0 unowned.
Result target: 600 - 180 - 60 + 0 = 360 tags (-40%).

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked in Technical Editor interviews about taxonomy and metadata, grouped by level. Draft your answer, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.What is the difference between a taxonomy and tags?
    A taxonomy is an organized, governed system; tags are labels, ideally drawn from a controlled vocabulary within it.
    IQ2.What is a facet?
    An independent dimension, such as product, task or version, used to filter content.
    IQ3.How do you create a taxonomy?
    Research users, card sort, draft, tree test, tag content and review regularly.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.What metadata do docs need?
    Title, description, product, task, audience, version, owner and review date.
    IQ5.How do you enforce metadata quality?
    Templates, required fields, enumerated values and CI validation.
    IQ6.How do you build a good index?
    Index concepts in user terms with subentries, see and see-also references and tested locators.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you improve site search?
    Analyze queries, rewrite titles, add synonyms, boost key pages and monitor metrics.
    IQ8.What are precision and recall?
    Precision is the share of results that are relevant; recall is the share of relevant items found.
    IQ9.What do zero-result queries tell you?
    Missing content, missing synonyms or naming mismatches.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How do you stop tag sprawl?
    A controlled vocabulary, request process, audits, merging and retiring tags.
    IQ11.How do you handle versions in a taxonomy?
    A version facet, canonical latest pages and clear notices on old versions.
    IQ12.Tell me about improving findability.
    Describe the problem, data, changes and measured improvement.

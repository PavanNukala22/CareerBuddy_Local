      Market & Competitive Research

      About market research

      Research gives technical copy a defensible direction. This module teaches audience segmentation, competitor matrixing and positioning so that the message reflects the reader’s task and supported product facts.

      Learning Objectives

      By the end of this module, you should be able to:

      • Build evidence-led segment cards instead of relying on job titles alone.
      • Compare alternatives with consistent criteria and visible unknowns.
      • Create positioning and message pillars with proof and boundaries.

      Prerequisites

      Understand the classroom product and distinguish a source claim from your interpretation. No live customer interviews or competitor accounts are needed for the exercises.

      Fundamentals

      Audience evidence explains relevance; competitor evidence explains alternatives; positioning connects the offer to a particular decision. Each research conclusion should make its evidence and uncertainty visible.

      Core Concepts

      Use the following distinctions when reviewing your draft:

      ConceptWhat to check
      Evidence and assumptionKeep observations and research gaps separate.
      Comparison and scopeUse consistent criteria and comparable context.
      Position and expressionThe internal decision guides channel-specific copy.

      Market & Competitive Research

      Audience segmentation

      Segmentation divides an audience into useful groups based on needs, context or behaviour. A job title may help locate a reader, but it rarely explains the whole decision. Consider what the person is trying to accomplish, their technical knowledge, buying role and the constraints affecting them.

      Segment by the problem and decision

      Collect evidence from interviews, sales questions, support conversations and available research. Separate observations from assumptions. Build a segment card with the reader’s task, trigger, objection, evidence need and next action. Use a persona as a working summary, not an invented biography that hides uncertainty.

      ElementHow to use it
      TaskWhat the reader needs to accomplish.
      TriggerWhat prompts them to look for a solution now.
      Decision roleUser, technical evaluator, approver or another role.
      ObjectionA specific concern such as integration effort or evidence scope.
      Content needWhat helps them make the next decision.

      Worked example

      SegmentMain concernUseful content
      Engineer evaluating integrationWill it fit the documented interface and workflow?Versioned data sheet and integration checklist.
      Operations manager evaluating effortDoes the workflow release useful review capacity?Pilot method and a clearly scoped efficiency example.
      Security reviewerWhat controls and approved evidence can be reviewed?Precise control description and current assurance scope.

      Why this works. The groups are distinguished by a decision task. They can exist within the same organisation and read different parts of one content programme.

      Go deeper

      When evidence contradicts a persona, update the segment rather than forcing the reader into it. Check whether a claimed preference is observed in several conversations or is only one stakeholder’s assumption.

      Practice

      Write two segment cards for the classroom product. Include one observed fact, one assumption to validate and an objection-specific content recommendation.

      Segment by the decision and the operating context

      Audience segments should explain differences that change the message, evidence or next action. Role, task, buying stage and environment can matter more than a broad industry label. Record why a distinction affects the content rather than dividing people into categories that all receive the same generic pitch. Validate the segment against research and revise it when the evidence changes.

      Additional worked example

      Audience segmentation

      A fictional incident-platform campaign separates a technical evaluator checking integration fit from an executive reviewing operational priorities. The first receives an interface checklist, while the second receives a scoped business-outcome explanation. Both messages describe the same product without pretending their decisions are identical.

      Review the reasoning

      CheckWhat to record
      PurposeWhich reader decision and claim this topic addresses.
      EvidenceDoes the segment change the message or evidence meaningfully?
      BoundaryWhich result is supported and what still needs factual, product or publication review.

      Practice: change one assumption

      Rewrite the worked example for a changed audience, evidence basis or next action. State which conclusion remains supported, which must be revised and what the next reviewer should check. Compare your answer with the decision flow below.

      Practice scenario: Audience segmentation

      Fictional review situation

      A campaign labels every person in healthcare as one audience, even though some evaluate integration and others approve budgets. What segmentation would help the writer?

      Explained review answer

      Segment by the relevant decision, role, context and stage. A technical evaluator may need requirements and implementation evidence, while a budget reviewer may need a scoped business explanation. Use research to justify the distinction, and avoid creating segments that receive identical generic content with only a changed label.

      Your task: write a short response explaining the supported result, the missing evidence or condition, and the next review action. Then introduce one change to the situation and explain which part of the answer must be revised.

      Competitor matrixing

      Market & Competitive Research

      Competitor matrixing

      A competitor matrix compares alternatives using consistent criteria and traceable evidence. Its purpose is to understand the market and support a reader’s decision. A blank cell means information is missing; it does not establish that the competitor lacks the capability.

      Compare like with like

      Choose alternatives relevant to the segment, including the current manual approach where appropriate. Define the criterion before collecting information, record the source and date, and distinguish an observed claim from your interpretation. Use “unknown” when the evidence is insufficient. Verify material comparison claims before publishing them.

      ElementHow to use it
      CriterionA defined, decision-relevant attribute.
      EvidenceThe source, date and context supporting the cell.
      StatusKnown, unknown, inferred or pending review.
      Like-for-like scopeComparable version, plan or scenario.
      Reader implicationThe trade-off, without inventing superiority.

      Worked example

      CriterionTraceFlow (fictional)Alternative B (fictional)Manual workflow
      Related-alert reviewSupported in classroom packet.Unknown: no verified reference supplied.Depends on the documented human process.
      API limitsHypothetical 60 requests/minute in v1.Unknown; request evidence.Not applicable to a software API.
      Evidence noteTraining packet v1.Research gap, not proven absence.Confirm actual process with team.

      Why this works. The matrix makes incomplete research visible. It does not label Alternative B inferior solely because the exercise provides no documentation.

      Go deeper

      A comparison can become misleading when different product plans or measurement conditions are treated as equivalent. Maintain a review date and change log. A competitor’s marketing claim is evidence of their message, not necessarily independent proof of performance.

      Practice

      Create a matrix with five criteria, source/date fields and an unknown status. Write one fair conclusion and one statement the evidence does not support.

      Keep competitive comparisons comparable and dated

      A competitor matrix needs defined criteria, identifiable evidence and a current scope. Compare equivalent plans, versions and deployment contexts where possible. An absent public statement does not prove that a capability is absent. Label unknowns and retain the date and source behind each cell so a later reviewer can update the comparison without inheriting an unsupported claim.

      Additional worked example

      Competitor matrixing

      A fictional matrix finds a documented capability for one vendor but no comparable public evidence for another. The second cell says “not verified from the reviewed source.” It does not say “unsupported” until an appropriate source establishes that conclusion.

      Review the reasoning

      CheckWhat to record
      PurposeWhich reader decision and claim this topic addresses.
      EvidenceAre the comparison criteria and evidence genuinely comparable?
      BoundaryWhich result is supported and what still needs factual, product or publication review.

      Practice: change one assumption

      Rewrite the worked example for a changed audience, evidence basis or next action. State which conclusion remains supported, which must be revised and what the next reviewer should check. Compare your answer with the decision flow below.

      Practice scenario: Competitor matrixing

      Fictional review situation

      A competitor comparison uses one vendor’s enterprise plan and another vendor’s entry plan without stating the difference. How should the matrix be reviewed?

      Explained review answer

      Clarify the comparison boundary and use equivalent contexts where possible. Label plan-dependent facts, unknowns, dates and sources. If the plans cannot be compared directly, explain the limitation rather than presenting the difference as a universal product disadvantage. Retain the evidence behind every published cell.

      Your task: write a short response explaining the supported result, the missing evidence or condition, and the next review action. Then introduce one change to the situation and explain which part of the answer must be revised.

      Messaging and positioning

      Positioning identifies who the offer is for, what problem it helps with, what alternatives the reader considers and why the offer fits. Messaging turns that position into words for particular channels. A position should be specific enough to guide choices; “innovative solution for everyone” rarely does.

      Build a message house

      Start with the intended segment and job. Define a central promise, then add a small set of supporting pillars with approved proof. Positioning is the internal decision framework; a landing-page headline is one expression of it. Adapt vocabulary and emphasis for a channel while preserving the product facts.

      ElementHow to use it
      AudienceA defined reader and decision context.
      Problem/categoryWhat the offer helps evaluate or perform.
      Main promiseA useful outcome stated within the evidence boundary.
      PillarsMechanisms or reasons supporting the promise.
      Proof and exclusionsEvidence and conditions limiting the claim.

      Worked example

      Internal positioning draft: For engineering teams evaluating incident-review workflows, TraceFlow brings related alerts and recorded review events into an evaluation-ready workflow. Its documented grouping, API and export capabilities support technical-fit review; time benefits should be tested in a scoped pilot.
      Message pillarProof required
      Review related signals togetherApproved grouping capability description.
      Assess integration fitVersioned interface requirements and limits.
      Review recorded eventsApproved export scope and configuration context.

      Why this works. The statement identifies the audience and evaluation task. The pillars rely on documented capabilities rather than unsupported “best” or “fastest” comparisons.

      Go deeper

      Stress-test a message against the strongest relevant alternative and a real objection. If the claim only sounds distinctive because the alternatives were described unfairly, revise the positioning. A good position can acknowledge a case where the offer is not the right fit.

      Practice

      Write an internal positioning statement, one central promise, three pillars and the evidence each needs. Then adapt the promise into an engineer-facing headline and a manager-facing email opening.

      Make positioning specific without inventing differentiation

      Positioning identifies the audience, problem, approach and relevant reason to consider the offer. A useful message links a documented difference to the reader’s decision. Avoid superlatives that require evidence the team does not have. When two alternatives share a capability, explain the relevant implementation or service context instead of claiming exclusivity.

      Additional worked example

      Messaging and positioning

      A fictional platform serves teams coordinating alert review across multiple groups. The draft explains its documented grouping workflow and supported handover context. It does not call the product the “only” solution unless a reliable comparison supports that broad claim.

      Review the reasoning

      CheckWhat to record
      PurposeWhich reader decision and claim this topic addresses.
      EvidenceIs the proposed difference documented and relevant to the reader?
      BoundaryWhich result is supported and what still needs factual, product or publication review.

      Practice: change one assumption

      Rewrite the worked example for a changed audience, evidence basis or next action. State which conclusion remains supported, which must be revised and what the next reviewer should check. Compare your answer with the decision flow below.

      Practice scenario: Messaging and positioning

      Fictional review situation

      A positioning draft says “the only tool for engineering teams,” but the research has not checked alternatives. What is a stronger message?

      Explained review answer

      Use the documented audience, problem and relevant approach. Explain why the supported capability matters in that context without the unsupported exclusivity claim. If a genuine difference is proposed, identify the evidence and review owner. Clear positioning does not require a superlative that the team cannot substantiate.

      Your task: write a short response explaining the supported result, the missing evidence or condition, and the next review action. Then introduce one change to the situation and explain which part of the answer must be revised.

      Audience and competitor research worked examples

      Research note: Two fictional engineers ask about API limits before discussing a pilot. One manager asks whether review time can be released for other work. Treat these as evidence of different questions; do not conclude that every engineer or manager behaves identically.

      Convert the note into segment hypotheses, then seek additional evidence. In the competitor matrix, write “unknown” wherever the supplied material does not establish a capability.

      Tool or working documentUse in this module

      Competitor evidence matrixKeep source, date, plan/context and unknown status.

      Workflow: brief → evidence collection → draft → subject-matter review → editorial/measurement checks → approval → release → learning. A document and spreadsheet are sufficient for the exercises; use the actual approved tools and owners on a real project.

      Audience and competitor research best practices

      • Start from the reader’s decision and the accepted product facts.
      • Write a clear first draft before polishing phrasing.
      • Put essential conditions close to the claim they qualify.
      • Use consistent terms, units and evidence across channels.
      • Version substantive changes and ask the right owner to resolve a fact or measurement gap.

      Testing & Quality

      Market & Competitive Research

      Audience and competitor research quality checks

      CheckHow to verify it
      Audience and taskAsk a reader to state the asset’s intended decision after scanning the opening.
      Technical accuracyCompare every feature, unit and limitation with the approved source.
      Claim supportTrace quantitative or assurance wording to evidence and conditions.
      Calculation and measurementRecalculate figures and verify the event, denominator and period.
      Experience and handoverCheck mobile readability, headings, links, CTA destination and owner approval.

      Worked quality check: a draft says “60 requests/second” while the packet says “60 requests/minute”. The draft fails accuracy even if it reads smoothly. Correct the unit and every other asset that repeats the error.

      Audience and competitor research errors and troubleshooting

      Common errorCorrection or next check
      A persona is an invented biography.Record actual task evidence and assumptions separately.
      Unknown is labelled “not supported”.Retain unknown until the criterion has been verified.
      Positioning promises superiority without a comparison.Define the audience and supported fit instead.

      If reviewers disagree, identify whether the gap concerns strategy, technical truth, evidence or measurement. Resolve it with the responsible owner and revise the affected outputs consistently.

      Intermediate & Advanced Concepts

      Market & Competitive Research

      Market research intermediate techniques

      Use one criterion consistently across alternatives, then explain how different segments weigh that criterion. Missing information should lead to a research question, not an invented weakness.

      Practice comparison

      Choose two plausible drafts or approaches from this module. Compare their fit to the same reader and evidence. Explain which difference changes meaning and which is only an editorial preference.

      Write research questions before collecting evidence

      Research should answer a decision that can change the message or content plan. Define which audience problem, criterion or objection you need to understand. Record the source, context and limitation of each observation. This reduces the risk of collecting only examples that confirm the draft already preferred by the team.

      Worked example

      A fictional team asks whether integration effort or alert-review organisation is the evaluator’s main concern. Interview notes and approved sales questions are organised by that question. The copywriter changes the evidence order when the research supports a different primary decision.

      Compare competitor evidence within a defined boundary

      Specify the comparison criteria, plan or version context and evidence date. Separate confirmed facts from unknowns. A missing public statement is not proof of a missing capability. Keep a source note for every matrix cell so a later reviewer can update the comparison without inheriting an unsupported conclusion.

      Worked example

      A fictional matrix verifies a capability for vendor A but finds no equivalent source for vendor B. The B entry says “not verified from the reviewed evidence.” The writer requests a suitable source before turning that unknown into a published disadvantage.

      Turn segment evidence into a message hypothesis

      A useful segment changes the message, proof or next action. Record the reader’s role, decision and operating context, then write a hypothesis about what information would help. Validate it against evidence instead of changing only the industry label on an otherwise identical generic pitch.

      Worked example

      A fictional technical evaluator receives an interface checklist, while a business sponsor receives a scoped operational-outcome explanation. Both use approved product facts. The content differs because the readers must make different decisions, not because one group is assumed to be universally less technical.

      Market research advanced techniques

      Stress-test positioning with a real objection and a relevant alternative. Update assumptions when new evidence contradicts a segment or previously used comparison.

      Change scenario

      A relevant alternative turns out to support the previously unknown capability. Update the matrix and review any positioning dependent on supposed absence.

      Separate a positioning hypothesis from verified differentiation

      A research insight can suggest a useful message without proving that the product is unique. State the audience problem and documented approach, then identify what evidence would be needed for a broader competitive assertion. Keep the strength of the published difference proportional to the reviewed comparison.

      Worked example

      A fictional team finds that evaluators value a particular handover workflow. The positioning highlights the product’s documented workflow for that audience. It does not call the capability exclusive until reliable, comparable evidence supports the claim about alternatives.

      Maintain a research-to-message audit trail

      Record how a research observation changed the message, which source supports it and which reviewer accepted the interpretation. If a source or product condition changes, this trail helps the writer locate affected assets. It also makes disagreements reviewable instead of leaving positioning decisions dependent on memory or the loudest stakeholder.

      Worked example

      A fictional matrix entry influences a landing-page comparison. When the reviewed vendor plan changes, the writer follows the source note to the affected copy and rechecks the claim. The update is scoped to the changed evidence rather than rewriting the complete message without a reason.

      Challenge contradictory evidence deliberately

      Advanced research looks for evidence that could change the proposed segment or message. Record conflicting observations and compare their context before choosing a conclusion. A small or biased set of interviews may reveal useful hypotheses while remaining insufficient for a broad market claim. Explain that limitation to the message owner.

      Worked example

      A fictional team’s interviews suggest integration effort is important, while another segment prioritises review ownership. The writer keeps both contexts visible and proposes separate messages for review. The draft does not convert one group’s feedback into a claim about every buyer.

      Interview Preparation

      Market research interview preparation

      Scenario question: A competitor page does not mention audit export. What can you conclude?

      Example answer. I can conclude that the reviewed page does not establish the capability. I cannot conclude that the competitor lacks it. I would mark the matrix unknown and record the source/date and next verification step.

      Portfolio explanation: describe the reader and task, show the original evidence, explain one writing decision and one revision, then state what the result does and does not establish.

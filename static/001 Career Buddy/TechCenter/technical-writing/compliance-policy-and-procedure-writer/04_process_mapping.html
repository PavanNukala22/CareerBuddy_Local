    Module 01

    Process Mapping Foundations

    Why compliance writers map processes, the three main notations — BPMN business process models, workflow flowcharts and swimlane diagrams — and how maps connect to SOPs, controls and audits.

    🗺️ Course Overview: Why Writers Map Processes

    Before you can write a reliable procedure, you must understand the process. Process maps make the sequence, decisions, hand-offs and controls visible — revealing gaps, duplicated steps and missing approvals that text hides. For a Compliance / Policy and Procedure Writer, maps are both a discovery tool and a communication tool inside SOPs.

    🧩
    BPMN
    Standard notation for detailed, unambiguous business process models.
    🔀
    Workflow flowcharts
    Simple boxes, diamonds and arrows for quick understanding.
    🏊
    Swimlane diagrams
    Show who does each step and every hand-off between roles.
    🛡️
    Control points
    Where checks, approvals and records occur — key for audits.

    🔺 Choosing a Notation

    NeedBest notation
    Quick explanation in an SOP for operatorsSimple flowchart
    Show responsibilities and hand-offsSwimlane diagram
    Precise model for analysis, automation or regulatorsBPMN 2.0
    High-level overview for executivesValue chain or SIPOC

    Module 02

    Workflow Flowcharts

    Drawing clear flowcharts — standard symbols, flow direction, decision logic, connectors, levels of detail and common mistakes.

    🔣 Basic: Standard Symbols

    SymbolShapeMeaning
    TerminatorRounded rectangle (oval)Start or end
    ProcessRectangleAn action or activity
    DecisionDiamondA yes/no or multi-way question
    Input/OutputParallelogramData in or out
    DocumentRectangle with wavy baseA document or record produced
    ConnectorSmall circleContinuation to another part of the chart
    ArrowLine with arrowheadDirection of flow

    ➡️ Intermediate: Drawing a Flowchart

    Figure 2.1 · Purchase requisition approval
    • Flow top-to-bottom or left-to-right consistently.
    • Every decision has labelled exits (Yes/No) that all lead somewhere.
    • One start; one or more clearly labelled ends.
    • Label activities with verb + object ("Approve requisition").

    ⚖️ Intermediate: Decision Logic

    ✅ Clear decision

    "Amount > ₹5,00,000?" with Yes and No both labelled and both paths ending.

    ❌ Ambiguous decision

    "Check amount" in a diamond with only one exit — readers can't tell what happens otherwise.

    💡
    Decisions should be questions with defined answers. If you need judgement ("Is it acceptable?"), define the criteria in the SOP text.

    🏗️ Advanced: Levels of Detail

    LevelPurposeSteps
    Level 0Context: process in the value chain1 box
    Level 1Major phases5–9 boxes
    Level 2Detailed workflow for the SOP10–30 steps
    Level 3Task detail (work instruction)As needed
    📌
    If a flowchart exceeds one page, split it into sub-processes linked by connectors rather than shrinking the font.

    🏆 Expert: Common Flowchart Mistakes

    • Dead ends (paths that never reach an end).
    • Loops without exit criteria.
    • Mixing levels of detail on one chart.
    • Missing records and approvals that the SOP text mentions.
    • Map and SOP text out of sync after a revision.

    ✅ Best Practices & Common Errors

    ✅ Best practices

    Standard symbols; one start and clear ends; decisions as questions with labelled exits; verb + object labels; one level of detail per chart; split large charts into subprocesses.

    ❌ Common errors

    Dead ends; unlabelled decision exits; loops without exit criteria; tiny fonts to fit one page; charts out of sync with the SOP.

    Common errorWhy it happensFix
    Dead-end pathsExceptions added lateTrace every path to an end
    Ambiguous decisionsJudgement words in diamondsDefine criteria in the question
    Map and SOP differSeparate updatesVersion map and SOP together

    Module 03

    Swimlane Diagrams

    Mapping responsibilities and hand-offs — lane design, cross-functional flows, control points, timing, and using swimlanes to find delays and compliance gaps.

    🏊 Basic: What Swimlanes Show

    A swimlane diagram divides the map into lanes, one per role, department or system. Each activity sits in the lane of the role that performs it, so hand-offs between roles become visible.

    Figure 3.1 · Employee leaver process (swimlanes)

    🔁 Intermediate: Designing Lanes

    • Use roles, not people's names ("Payroll Officer", not "Priya").
    • Order lanes by first involvement or by organisational proximity.
    • Include systems as lanes when they perform automated steps.
    • Keep 3–7 lanes; more becomes unreadable — split the process.
    💡
    Every arrow crossing a lane boundary is a hand-off. Hand-offs are where delays and errors happen — and where controls often belong.

    🛡️ Intermediate: Marking Control Points

    MarkerMeaningExample
    C1, C2…Control stepManager approval of access
    📄Record producedSigned removal checklist
    ⏱️Time requirementWithin 24 hours
    ⚠️Risk pointManual re-keying of bank details

    📉 Advanced: Analysing Swimlanes

    AnalysisQuestionTypical finding
    Hand-off countHow many lane crossings?Too many → delays
    Wait timeWhere does work sit idle?Approvals pending 3 days
    Control coverageAre risks matched by controls?No check after manual entry
    SoDDoes one role perform conflicting steps?Same person requests and approves

    🏆 Expert: From Swimlane to RACI and SOP

    Figure 3.2 · Swimlane to documentation

    The swimlane becomes the backbone of the SOP: lanes → Responsibilities section, activities → numbered steps, control markers → controls table, documents → Records section.

    ✅ Best Practices & Common Errors

    ✅ Best practices

    Lanes named by role or system; 3–7 lanes; mark controls, records and time requirements; analyse hand-offs and waits; derive RACI from lanes.

    ❌ Common errors

    People's names as lanes; 14+ lanes; activities in the wrong lane; hidden SoD conflicts; no control markers.

    Common errorWhy it happensFix
    Unreadable diagramToo many rolesOverview plus detailed maps
    Wrong lane placementMapped from manager's viewValidate with performers
    Missing controlsFocus only on flowAsk "what could go wrong here?"

    Module 04

    Business Process Modeling with BPMN 2.0

    The international standard for process models — events, activities, gateways, flows, pools and lanes, data objects, subprocesses, and how BPMN supports compliance and automation.

    🧩 Basic: Core BPMN Elements

    CategoryElementsShape
    EventsStart, intermediate, endCircles (thin, double, thick border)
    ActivitiesTask, subprocessRounded rectangles
    GatewaysExclusive (XOR), parallel (AND), inclusive (OR), event-basedDiamonds with markers
    FlowsSequence flow, message flow, associationSolid arrow, dashed arrow, dotted line
    SwimlanesPool (participant), lane (role)Large rectangles
    Data & artifactsData object, data store, annotationDocument shape, cylinder, bracket

    🔀 Intermediate: Gateways

    GatewayMarkerBehaviour
    Exclusive (XOR)✕ or emptyExactly one path taken based on condition
    Parallel (AND)＋All paths taken at once; join waits for all
    Inclusive (OR)◯One or more paths taken
    Event-basedPentagon in circlesPath chosen by which event occurs first
    ⚠️
    A frequent modelling error: splitting with a parallel gateway and joining with an exclusive gateway — the process will trigger downstream steps multiple times. Use matching gateway types.

    ⏱️ Intermediate: Events

    • Start events: none, message (e.g., "complaint received"), timer ("1st of each month").
    • Intermediate events: timers ("wait 24 h"), messages, escalations.
    • Boundary events on tasks: e.g., a timer boundary on "Approve" for SLA escalation.
    • End events: none, message, error, terminate.
    Figure 4.1 · Complaint handling in BPMN terms

    🏊 Advanced: Pools, Lanes & Message Flows

    A pool represents a participant (organisation or system); lanes subdivide it by role. Sequence flows stay inside one pool; communication between pools uses message flows (dashed). Example: your company pool and a regulator pool exchanging a notification message.

    xml<bpmn:process id="BreachNotification" isExecutable="false">
  <bpmn:startEvent id="Start" name="Incident confirmed"/>
  <bpmn:task id="Assess" name="Assess risk to individuals"/>
  <bpmn:exclusiveGateway id="NotifyNeeded" name="Notification required?"/>
  <bpmn:task id="Notify" name="Notify supervisory authority"/>
  <bpmn:endEvent id="End" name="Incident closed"/>
  <bpmn:sequenceFlow sourceRef="Start" targetRef="Assess"/>
  <bpmn:sequenceFlow sourceRef="Assess" targetRef="NotifyNeeded"/>
  <bpmn:sequenceFlow sourceRef="NotifyNeeded" targetRef="Notify" name="Yes"/>
  <bpmn:sequenceFlow sourceRef="NotifyNeeded" targetRef="End" name="No"/>
  <bpmn:sequenceFlow sourceRef="Notify" targetRef="End"/>
</bpmn:process>

    🏆 Expert: BPMN for Compliance & Automation

    • Model timer boundary events for regulatory deadlines (72 h, 60 days) to make SLAs explicit.
    • Use data objects to show records produced — mapping directly to evidence.
    • Executable BPMN (Camunda, Flowable) can enforce the process, generating audit trails automatically.
    • Keep a descriptive model for documentation and a technical model for automation; align them.

    ✅ Best Practices & Common Errors

    ✅ Best practices

    Matching split and join gateways; message flows only between pools; timer events for deadlines; data objects for records; validate models in the modeler.

    ❌ Common errors

    Parallel split with exclusive join; message flows inside a pool; missing end events; unlabelled gateway conditions; mixing descriptive and executable details.

    Common errorWhy it happensFix
    Gateway mismatchCopying shapesPair gateway types and test paths
    Wrong flow typeNotation unfamiliaritySequence inside pools, message between
    Deadlines not visibleTimers omittedTimer boundary events for SLAs

    From Process Maps to Procedures & Controls

    Running discovery workshops, validating maps, converting maps into SOP steps, linking control points to traceability, and keeping maps and text synchronised.

    🗣️ Intermediate: Discovery Workshops

    Figure 5.1 · Process discovery workshop

    🔁 Advanced: Map-to-SOP Conversion

    Map elementSOP element
    LanesResponsibilities section
    ActivitiesNumbered steps (actor + verb)
    Decisions"If…, go to step X" or decision table
    Data objects / documentsRecords section
    Control markersControls table with IDs (traceability)
    Timer eventsTime requirements in steps

    🧵 Advanced: Linking Controls to Traceability

    textControl ID   Map element            SOP step   Requirement           Evidence

    🏆 Expert: Keeping Maps and Text in Sync

    • Store maps as controlled documents (or embedded in the SOP) with the same version as the text.
    • Change control requires updating both; review checklist includes "map matches steps".
    • Maintain a process library (process architecture) so maps link level 1 → level 2 → SOPs.

    ✅ Best Practices & Common Errors

    ✅ Best practices

    Interview performers; start with the happy path then exceptions; convert lanes to responsibilities and activities to steps; link controls to traceability IDs; keep maps and text in one controlled version.

    ❌ Common errors

    Mapping only from managers' descriptions; losing exceptions during conversion; control markers not linked to evidence; maps stored outside the DMS.

    Common errorWhy it happensFix
    Idealised as-is mapWrong participantsInclude frontline performers
    Steps missing in SOPManual conversionConversion checklist (lane, activity, decision, record)
    Orphan mapsSeparate storageEmbed or co-version maps with SOPs

    Module 07

    Testing & Quality of Process Maps

    Validating maps for correctness, completeness, readability and compliance — walk-throughs, syntax checks, control coverage and metrics.

    ✅ Map Quality Checklist

    • One start; every path reaches an end.
    • Every decision is a question with labelled exits.
    • Every activity is in the correct lane; labelled verb + object.
    • Gateways split and join with matching types (BPMN).
    • Controls and records marked and linked to IDs.
    • Map matches the SOP text and current practice.

    🚶 Walk-Through Validation

    Ask a performer to trace a recent real case through the map. Every deviation is either a map error or an undocumented variant to add.

    🤖 Automated Checks (BPMN XML)

    pythonimport xml.etree.ElementTree as ET
ns = {"bpmn": "http://www.omg.org/spec/BPMN/20100524/MODEL"}
root = ET.parse("breach_notification.bpmn").getroot()
ids_with_out = {f.get("sourceRef") for f in root.iter(f"{{{ns['bpmn']}}}sequenceFlow")}
for el in root.iter():
    tag = el.tag.split("}")[-1]
    if tag in ("task", "exclusiveGateway", "startEvent") and el.get("id") not in ids_with_out:
        print("Dead end:", tag, el.get("name"))

    Real-World Scenarios & Troubleshooting

    Process mapping situations compliance writers encounter.

    🏭 Scenario: Deviation Investigation at a Pharma Plant

    Repeated batch record deviations. Mapping the as-is process in swimlanes reveals that QA review happens after packaging starts. To-be map moves QA release before packaging; SOP and controls updated.

    🔁 Troubleshooting: Map Says One Thing, People Do Another

    FindingCauseFix
    Map shows manager approval; practice skips it for urgent ordersUndocumented "urgent" variantModel urgent path with post-approval within 24 h, or enforce the control

    🧩 Scenario: BPMN Model Rejected by Automation Team

    Gateways mismatched and message flows used inside a pool. Fix: correct gateway pairs, convert internal message flows to sequence flows, add data objects for records, re-validate in the modeler.

    🏊 Troubleshooting: Swimlane With 14 Lanes

    • Unreadable and nobody uses it.
    • Fix: create a level-1 overview with 5 phases, then separate level-2 swimlanes per phase with 4–6 lanes each.

    (a) Parallel (AND) split and join; (b) Exclusive (XOR) gateway based on employment country.
    Task 6AdvancedMap to SOP
    Convert a 3-lane swimlane (HR, IT, Payroll) leaver map into SOP section headings.

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions on process mapping for compliance writing roles.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.Why map a process before writing an SOP?
    To understand sequence, decisions, roles and controls; to find gaps and agree reality with performers before writing.
    IQ2.What are the basic flowchart symbols?
    Terminator, process, decision, input/output, document, connector and arrows.
    IQ3.What does a swimlane diagram show?
    Who performs each activity and where hand-offs occur between roles.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you run a process discovery workshop?
    Scope with SIPOC, involve performers, map the happy path, add exceptions, mark controls and records, validate.
    IQ5.Explain BPMN gateways.
    Exclusive chooses one path, parallel takes all, inclusive takes one or more, event-based chooses by first event.
    IQ6.How do you show controls on a map?
    Mark control points with IDs and record symbols, linked to the traceability matrix.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you convert a map into an SOP?
    Lanes become responsibilities, activities become steps, decisions become conditional steps or tables, data objects become records, controls become a controls table.
    IQ8.What is the difference between sequence and message flows?
    Sequence flows connect activities within one pool; message flows show communication between pools.
    IQ9.How do you analyse a swimlane for improvement?
    Count hand-offs, measure waits, check control coverage and SoD, remove non-value steps.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How would you build a process library?
    Define process architecture levels, standard notation and style, link maps to SOPs and controls, govern versions and owners.
    IQ11.When would you choose BPMN over a simple flowchart?
    When precision, multiple participants, events and automation or regulator review matter.
    IQ12.A map and practice disagree. What do you do?
    Investigate with performers, decide whether practice or map is correct with the owner, update the document or fix the practice, retrain.

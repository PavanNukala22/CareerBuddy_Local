    Document Management Systems Foundations

    How controlled documents live inside a Document Management System (DMS) — the document lifecycle, metadata, versioning, workflows and audit trails, and the three platforms compliance writers meet most: SharePoint, MasterControl and Veeva Vault.

    🗄️ Course Overview: What a DMS Does for Compliance Writers

    A Document Management System (DMS) stores controlled documents and enforces the rules around them: who can edit, review and approve; which version is effective; who has been trained; and a tamper-evident history of every action. For a Compliance / Policy and Procedure Writer, the DMS is where your documents are born, approved, published, reviewed and retired.

    🟦

    🟧

    Life-sciences cloud platform — QualityDocs, QMS, RIM, eTMF.
    🔄
    Lifecycle tracking
    Draft → review → approval → effective → periodic review → superseded/obsolete.

    Module 02

    SharePoint for Document Control

    Turning SharePoint document libraries into a controlled document system — content types, metadata columns, versioning, approvals with Power Automate, permissions, retention labels and audit logs.

    🟦 Basic: Libraries, Columns & Content Types

    • Document library — a container for documents with shared settings.
    • Columns (metadata) — Document ID, Document Type, Owner, Department, Status, Effective Date, Next Review Date.
    • Content types — reusable templates + metadata for "Policy", "SOP", "Work Instruction", "Form".
    • Views — filtered lists, e.g., "Effective SOPs", "Reviews due in 60 days".
    💡
    Use metadata and views instead of deep folder trees. Folders hide documents; metadata lets people find them by type, department or status.

    🔢 Intermediate: Versioning & Approval

    SettingRecommended for controlled documents
    VersioningMajor and minor versions (drafts as minor, published as major)
    Draft item securityOnly users who can edit see drafts
    Require check-outOn, to prevent concurrent edits
    Content approvalOn, or approvals via Power Automate
    Document ID serviceOn, for permanent IDs
    Figure 2.1 · SharePoint approval flow

    🔐 Intermediate: Permissions

    GroupPermission
    Document ControlFull control of controlled libraries
    AuthorsEdit (drafts)
    Reviewers / approversRead + approval tasks
    All employeesRead effective (major) versions only
    ⚠️
    Broken permission inheritance on individual files becomes unmanageable. Manage access at library or folder level with groups.

    🗃️ Advanced: Retention & Records

    • Microsoft Purview retention labels apply retention periods (e.g., "SOP – 10 years after obsolete").
    • Labels can declare records, locking documents from editing or deletion.
    • Use event-based retention ("retain 6 years after superseded").
    • The unified audit log captures views, edits, deletions and sharing.

    🏆 Expert: SharePoint in Regulated (GxP) Environments

    SharePoint can support GxP use, but it requires a validated configuration, controlled workflows, e-signature solutions meeting Part 11, audit-trail review procedures and change control on the configuration itself. Many life-sciences companies use a purpose-built system (MasterControl, Veeva) instead, and keep SharePoint for non-GxP documents.

    ✅ Best Practices & Common Errors

    ✅ Best practices

    Content types and metadata over deep folders; major/minor versioning with draft security; required check-out; Document IDs; group-based permissions; retention labels.

    ❌ Common errors

    Drafts visible to all; broken permission inheritance on single files; folders as the only organisation; no retention configured; approvals done outside SharePoint.

    Common errorWhy it happensFix
    Staff follow draftsDraft security too broadRestrict drafts to editors and test
    Permission chaosFile-level sharingManage by library and groups
    Documents hard to findFolder-only structureMetadata and views

    Veeva Vault (QualityDocs & QMS)

    Document control on the Veeva Vault life-sciences platform — document types and classifications, lifecycles and states, workflows, e-signatures, binders, and connections to QMS and training.

    🟧 Basic: Vault Applications

    ApplicationPurpose
    Vault QualityDocsControlled GxP documents (SOPs, policies, specifications)
    Vault QMSDeviations, CAPA, change control, audits
    Vault TrainingTraining assignments and records
    Vault RIMRegulatory submissions and registrations
    Vault eTMF / ClinicalClinical trial master files

    🧬 Intermediate: Document Types & Lifecycles

    • Document type → subtype → classification hierarchy (e.g., Governance & Procedure → SOP → Quality SOP).
    • Each type follows a lifecycle with states such as Draft, In Review, Approved, Effective, Superseded, Obsolete.
    • Entry criteria and actions on states (e.g., cannot enter Approved without required fields).
    Figure 4.1 · Vault lifecycle example

    ✍️ Intermediate: Workflows & E-signatures

    • Review and approval workflows assign tasks to users or roles.
    • E-signatures capture signature meaning ("Approved", "Reviewed") with re-authentication.
    • Overlays and watermarks on rendered PDFs indicate status ("UNCONTROLLED IF PRINTED").

    🔗 Advanced: Connections Across Vault

    A deviation in Vault QMS can trigger a change control, which creates a new draft version of an SOP in QualityDocs, which on approval issues training in Vault Training — a fully traceable chain.

    Figure 4.2 · Veeva quality chain

    🏆 Expert: Configuration & Validation

    • Vault releases occur several times a year; regulated customers assess release impact and validate configurations.
    • Configuration changes (new document types, lifecycle states) go through change control.
    • Admins review audit trails periodically per data integrity SOPs.

    ✅ Best Practices & Common Errors

    ✅ Best practices

    Clear type/subtype/classification hierarchy; lifecycle entry criteria; signature meanings; watermarks on rendered copies; release-impact assessment and change control for configuration.

    ❌ Common errors

    Too many document types; lifecycles without entry criteria; configuration changed without validation; printed copies used as controlled.

    Common errorWhy it happensFix
    Type sprawlEach team adds typesGoverned type catalogue
    Documents approved incompleteNo entry criteriaRequired fields on state entry
    Unvalidated config changeAdmin shortcutsChange control for configuration

    Monitoring documents across their life — status reporting, periodic review scheduling, overdue management, effective-date control, obsolescence, retention and lifecycle KPIs.

    📊 Intermediate: Lifecycle Status Reporting

    ReportPurpose
    Documents by statusHow many drafts, in review, effective
    Reviews due next 90 daysPlan workload
    Overdue periodic reviewsCompliance risk
    Documents in review > 30 daysBottlenecks
    Training compliance per new revisionReadiness before effective date
    pythonimport pandas as pd
docs = pd.read_csv("dms_export.csv", parse_dates=["next_review", "effective_date"])
today = pd.Timestamp("2026-10-05")
overdue = docs[(docs.status == "Effective") & (docs.next_review < today)]
due90 = docs[(docs.status == "Effective") & docs.next_review.between(today, today + pd.Timedelta(days=90))]
print("Overdue reviews:", len(overdue), "| Due in 90 days:", len(due90))
print(overdue.groupby("owner").size().sort_values(ascending=False).head())

    Real-World Scenarios & Troubleshooting

    DMS problems compliance writers help solve.

    📄 Scenario: Operators Using Printed Old SOPs

    An auditor finds an outdated printed SOP at a workstation. Fixes: "UNCONTROLLED IF PRINTED" watermark with print date, controlled print process for necessary paper copies, periodic shop-floor checks, and access to effective versions on kiosks.

    🔐 Troubleshooting: Drafts Visible to All Staff

    FindingCauseFix
    Employees followed a draft SOPSharePoint draft item security set to "any user who can read"Restrict drafts to editors; test visibility; CAPA

    ⏳ Scenario: 300 Overdue Periodic Reviews

    After a reorganisation, many owners left. Fix: reassign ownership via metadata update, prioritise by risk, bulk-review low-risk documents, add an "owner left" trigger from HR leaver process, track via dashboard.

    🔁 Troubleshooting: Migration Lost Approval History

    • Approvals in the legacy system weren't migrated; auditors ask for evidence.
    • Fix: retrieve legacy audit trails from the read-only archive, link them to migrated documents, document the migration validation.

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions about document management systems for compliance writing roles.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.What is a DMS?
    A system that stores controlled documents and enforces versioning, workflows, permissions, lifecycle states and audit trails.
    IQ2.What are typical lifecycle states?
    Draft, in review, approved, effective, superseded and obsolete.
    IQ3.Why use metadata instead of folders?
    Metadata makes documents findable and reportable by type, owner and status; folders hide content.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How would you set up SharePoint for document control?
    Content types, metadata, major/minor versioning, draft security, check-out, approval flows, Document IDs, group permissions, retention labels.
    IQ5.How does training link to documents?
    Documents are linked to roles; revisions trigger read-and-understand tasks; release can depend on training completion.
    IQ6.What is a periodic review?
    Scheduled review of an effective document with a recorded outcome (no change, revise, retire) and next review date.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.What makes a DMS Part 11 compliant?
    Validated system, unique e-signatures with meaning, audit trails, access controls, record retention and procedures — compliance is system plus process.
    IQ8.How do you report on document lifecycle health?
    Status counts, overdue reviews, cycle times, training readiness, owners missing, via dashboards.
    IQ9.Compare MasterControl and Veeva Vault.
    Both are regulated QMS/document platforms with lifecycles, e-signatures and training; Veeva focuses on life sciences with connected Vault apps; MasterControl spans regulated manufacturing with integrated quality modules. Choice depends on industry, existing ecosystem and needs.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How would you migrate documents to a new DMS?
    Inventory, cleanse, map metadata and lifecycles, pilot, verify, migrate or archive history, validate, train, decommission legacy read-only.
    IQ11.How do you prevent use of outdated documents?
    Single source in DMS, watermarks, controlled printing, supersession automation, training on new versions, shop-floor checks.
    IQ12.What governance does a DMS need?
    Ownership model, document types and lifecycles under change control, admin roles, audit-trail reviews, KPIs and periodic system review.

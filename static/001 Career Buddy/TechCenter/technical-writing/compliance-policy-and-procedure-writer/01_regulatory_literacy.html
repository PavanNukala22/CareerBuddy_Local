    Module 01

    Regulatory Literacy — Why Writers Must Read the Rules

    What a Compliance / Policy and Procedure Writer needs to understand about standards and regulations — ISO 9001/27001, SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR, OSHA and FDA — and how that knowledge turns into accurate, audit-ready documents.

    📜 Course Overview: What Regulatory Literacy Means

    A Compliance / Policy and Procedure Writer turns regulatory requirements into policies, SOPs and work instructions that people can follow and auditors can verify. Regulatory literacy is the ability to read a standard or regulation, understand what it requires, identify which requirements apply to your organisation, and express them correctly in your documents — without inventing obligations or missing mandatory ones.

    🏭
    ISO 9001 / 27001
    International management-system standards for quality and information security.
    🛡️
    SOC 2
    US attestation framework for service organisations' security controls.
    🏥
    HIPAA / GDPR
    Privacy and security of health data (US) and personal data (EU).
    ⚙️
    OSHA / FDA
    Workplace safety rules and regulated products like drugs and devices.
    💡
    You are not expected to be a lawyer. You are expected to read precisely, ask the right questions, cite sources and write requirements without distortion.

    📖 Core Vocabulary

    TermMeaning for a writer
    RequirementSomething that must be satisfied — usually phrased with "shall"
    ClauseA numbered section of a standard, e.g., ISO 9001 clause 7.5
    ControlA safeguard that addresses a risk or requirement
    Documented informationISO term for documents and records the organisation must keep
    RecordEvidence that something happened (signed form, log entry)
    ApplicabilityWhether a requirement applies to your organisation or process
    ScopeBoundaries of what a standard or programme covers
    CitationExact reference to the source, e.g., 45 CFR 164.308(a)(1)

    Module 02

    ISO 9001 & ISO 27001

    Reading the two most common ISO management-system standards — the shared clause structure, required documented information, Annex A controls and how they shape policies and procedures.

    🏗️ Basic: The Shared Structure (Clauses 4–10)

    ISO management-system standards share a common high-level structure, so ISO 9001 (quality) and ISO 27001 (information security) look alike.

    ClauseTitleTypical documents
    4Context of the organisationScope statement, interested parties
    5LeadershipQuality / information security policy, roles
    6PlanningRisk assessment method, objectives
    7SupportCompetence records, awareness, documented information control (7.5)
    8OperationProcedures for core processes; ISO 27001 risk treatment
    9Performance evaluationInternal audit programme, management review records
    10ImprovementNonconformity and corrective action records
    Figure 2.1 · Plan-Do-Check-Act

    🏭 Intermediate: ISO 9001 — What Writers Produce

    • Quality policy (clause 5.2) — short, signed by top management, communicated.
    • Scope (4.3) — what products, sites and processes are covered; justify any non-applicable requirements.
    • Control of documented information (7.5) — your document control SOP: approval, review, versions, distribution, retention.
    • Operational procedures (clause 8) — e.g., design control, purchasing, production, release.
    • Records — training (7.2), calibration (7.1.5), audits (9.2), management review (9.3), corrective actions (10.2).
    📌
    ISO 9001:2015 does not require a "quality manual" by name. It requires the documented information needed for effective processes — decide based on size and complexity.

    🔐 Intermediate: ISO 27001:2022 — Required Documents

    RequirementClauseWriter's output
    ISMS scope4.3Scope document
    Information security policy5.2Top-level policy
    Risk assessment & treatment process6.1.2, 6.1.3Risk methodology
    Statement of Applicability (SoA)6.1.3 dList of Annex A controls, applicability and justification
    Objectives6.2Objectives with measures
    Operational planning & control8.1Procedures and evidence
    Internal audit & management review9.2, 9.3Programme and records
    Figure 2.2 · ISO 27001:2022 Annex A themes

    🧩 Advanced: From Clause to Policy Statement

    ✅ Accurate translation

    Clause 7.5.3 requires control of documents. Policy: "All controlled documents shall be approved before release, reviewed at least every 24 months, and retained per the Records Retention Schedule."

    ❌ Distortion

    "ISO requires all documents to be reviewed every 12 months." — ISO does not set this frequency; the organisation decides.

    ⚠️
    The most common writer error is attributing your organisation's choices to the standard. Separate "required by ISO" from "our decision".

    🏆 Expert: Integrated Management Systems

    Many organisations certify to several standards. Because of the shared structure, one document set can serve ISO 9001 and ISO 27001: a single document control SOP, one internal audit procedure, one management review — with standard-specific annexes.

    Shared processServes
    Document control9001 7.5 and 27001 7.5
    Internal audit9001 9.2 and 27001 9.2
    Corrective action9001 10.2 and 27001 10.2
    Competence & awareness9001 7.2–7.3 and 27001 7.2–7.3

    ✅ Best Practices & Common Errors

    ✅ Best practices

    Cite the edition (ISO 27001:2022) and clause; separate "required by ISO" from "our decision"; reuse one document set for shared clauses 4–10; keep the SoA aligned with the risk treatment plan.

    ❌ Common errors

    Citing 2013 Annex A numbers; claiming ISO sets review frequencies; writing a "quality manual" nobody uses; excluding Annex A controls without justification.

    Common errorWhy it happensFix
    Outdated clause referencesCopying old templatesCitation-version check in every review
    Over-attributing to ISOConfusing standard with internal policyLabel organisational choices explicitly
    SoA out of sync with risksUpdated separatelyReview SoA whenever risk treatment changes

    Module 03

    SOC 2 for Policy Writers

    The AICPA Trust Services Criteria, Type I vs Type II reports, and the policies, procedures and evidence a SOC 2 audit expects.

    🛡️ Basic: What SOC 2 Is

    SOC 2 is an attestation report by an independent CPA firm on a service organisation's controls, based on the AICPA Trust Services Criteria (TSC). SaaS and service companies provide it to customers to prove security practices.

    CategoryFocusRequired?
    Security (Common Criteria)Protection against unauthorised accessAlways
    AvailabilitySystem uptime commitmentsOptional
    Processing IntegrityComplete, accurate processingOptional
    ConfidentialityProtection of confidential informationOptional
    PrivacyPersonal information lifecycleOptional

    ✅ Type I

    Controls are suitably designed at a point in time.

    ❌ Not enough for many buyers

    Type II tests operating effectiveness over a period (typically 3–12 months) — most enterprise customers ask for Type II.

    📑 Intermediate: Typical SOC 2 Policy Set

    PolicyCommon Criteria area
    Information Security PolicyCC1, CC2 governance & communication
    Access Control PolicyCC6.1–CC6.3 logical access
    Change Management PolicyCC8.1
    Incident Response PlanCC7.3–CC7.5
    Risk Assessment PolicyCC3
    Vendor Management PolicyCC9.2
    Business Continuity & DR PlanA1 (Availability)
    Acceptable Use, HR SecurityCC1.4, CC1.5
    💡
    SOC 2 auditors test what your policies say you do. Writing "quarterly access reviews" creates a control the auditor will sample every quarter.

    🧪 Advanced: Writing Testable Controls

    textWeak:   "Access is reviewed regularly."
Strong: "The IT Security Manager reviews user access to production systems quarterly.
         Reviews are documented in the Access Review Log, exceptions are remediated
         within 10 business days, and the review is approved by the CTO."
Testable elements: owner · frequency · scope · evidence · timeline · approver

    🏆 Expert: Policy-to-Evidence Alignment

    Figure 3.1 · SOC 2 readiness for documents
    ⚠️
    Over-promising is dangerous: a policy promising "real-time monitoring of all logs" that isn't true becomes an audit exception. Write what is done, then improve.

    ✅ Best Practices & Common Errors

    ✅ Best practices

    Write controls with owner, frequency, scope, evidence and approver; promise only what is operated; map each policy to Common Criteria; prepare evidence before the Type II period starts.

    ❌ Common errors

    Aspirational policies ("real-time monitoring of everything"); vague frequencies ("regularly"); evidence stored in personal inboxes; confusing Type I with Type II.

    Common errorWhy it happensFix
    Untestable control wordingWriting goals, not controlsUse the testable-control checklist
    Promised control not operatedCopying vendor templatesValidate each statement with the owner
    Missing evidence for a quarterNo remindersCalendar and GRC tasks per control

    Module 04

    HIPAA & GDPR

    Privacy and security regulations that drive many policies — HIPAA's Privacy, Security and Breach Notification Rules, GDPR's principles, rights and accountability documents, and how to write policies that satisfy both.

    🏥 Basic: HIPAA at a Glance

    HIPAA (US) protects protected health information (PHI) held by covered entities (health plans, providers, clearinghouses) and their business associates.

    RuleCore focusWriter outputs
    Privacy RuleUses and disclosures of PHI; patient rightsNotice of Privacy Practices, use/disclosure policies
    Security RuleSafeguards for electronic PHIAdministrative, physical, technical safeguard policies
    Breach Notification RuleNotifying individuals, HHS, mediaBreach response procedure
    Figure 4.1 · HIPAA Security Rule safeguards
    📌
    HIPAA requires policies and procedures and their documentation to be retained for 6 years from creation or last effective date (45 CFR 164.316).

    🇪🇺 Intermediate: GDPR Essentials for Writers

    GDPR elementArticleDocument
    PrinciplesArt. 5Data protection policy
    Lawful basisArt. 6, 9Processing register, notices
    TransparencyArt. 13–14Privacy notices
    Data subject rightsArt. 15–22DSAR procedure
    Records of processingArt. 30RoPA
    SecurityArt. 32Information security policy
    Breach notificationArt. 33–34Breach procedure (72 hours to authority)
    DPIAArt. 35DPIA template and procedure

    ⚖️ Advanced: Writing One Policy for Both

    ✅ Harmonised breach procedure

    Single procedure with jurisdiction-specific timelines: GDPR authority within 72 hours where feasible; HIPAA individuals without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 days after discovery.

    ❌ Merged incorrectly

    "All breaches must be reported within 60 days" — violates GDPR's 72-hour rule.

    textSection 5.3 Notification timelines
5.3.1 EU/EEA personal data: The DPO shall notify the competent supervisory authority
      without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware (GDPR Art. 33).
5.3.2 US PHI: The Privacy Officer shall notify affected individuals without unreasonable delay
      and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery (45 CFR 164.404).

    🏆 Expert: Interpretation & Escalation

    • When a requirement is ambiguous (e.g., "appropriate technical measures"), document the organisation's chosen interpretation and who approved it.
    • Track regulator guidance (EDPB guidelines, HHS OCR guidance) and update interpretations.
    • Keep a jurisdiction matrix for multi-country organisations.

    ✅ Best Practices & Common Errors

    ✅ Best practices

    Keep jurisdiction-specific timelines side by side; cite articles and CFR sections; route ambiguous terms to the DPO/Privacy Officer and record the interpretation; maintain RoPA and HIPAA documentation retention.

    ❌ Common errors

    Merging timelines into one wrong deadline; treating consent as the default lawful basis for employees; forgetting business associates; "fully compliant" marketing claims in policies.

    Common errorWhy it happensFix
    One breach deadline for all dataSimplifyingNotification matrix by jurisdiction and contract
    Missing BAA processSaaS team unaware of HIPAAAdd BA onboarding to vendor procedure
    No record of interpretationDecisions made in meetingsInterpretation log with approver

    OSHA & FDA (Industry-Specific Regulations)

    Writing for regulated operations — OSHA written safety programmes and recordkeeping, FDA cGMP and quality-system documentation, 21 CFR Part 11 electronic records, and data integrity (ALCOA+).

    ⚙️ Basic: OSHA Written Programmes

    OSHA regulations (29 CFR 1910 general industry, 1926 construction) require employers to protect workers. Many standards require written programmes that writers draft and maintain.

    StandardWritten requirement
    Hazard Communication 1910.1200Written hazard communication programme, labels, SDS access
    Lockout/Tagout 1910.147Energy control procedures and periodic inspection
    Respiratory Protection 1910.134Written respiratory protection programme
    Emergency Action Plans 1910.38Written emergency action plan (oral allowed for ≤ 10 employees)
    Recordkeeping Part 1904OSHA 300/300A/301 logs, retained 5 years

    💊 Intermediate: FDA Quality Documentation

    RegulationApplies toKey documents
    21 CFR 211 (cGMP)Finished pharmaceuticalsSOPs, batch records, deviations, change control
    21 CFR 820 (QMSR)Medical devices — aligned with ISO 13485 since Feb 2026Quality manual, design controls, CAPA
    21 CFR Part 11Electronic records & signaturesValidation, audit trails, e-signature controls
    21 CFR 58 (GLP)Nonclinical lab studiesStudy protocols, SOPs
    📌
    In FDA-regulated work, the rule is "if it isn't documented, it didn't happen". SOPs must be followed exactly; deviations must be recorded and investigated.

    Module 08

    Real-World Scenarios & Troubleshooting

    Situations compliance writers face when regulations meet real organisations.

    🏥 Scenario: SaaS Company Starts Serving Hospitals

    A SaaS company with SOC 2 begins processing hospital data and becomes a HIPAA business associate. Writer actions: add HIPAA requirements to the register, update the security policy for ePHI, add a BAA process, retention of documentation for 6 years, and breach procedure timelines for PHI.

    🔁 Troubleshooting: Conflicting Timelines Across Policies

    DocumentStated timeline
    Incident Response Plan"Notify within 30 days"
    Privacy Policy"Notify within 72 hours"
    Customer contract"Notify within 48 hours"

    Fix: build a single notification matrix with the strictest applicable timeline per data type and contract, reference it from all documents, and remove duplicated timelines.

    🧾 Scenario: Auditor Challenges a Citation

    The auditor notes the policy cites "ISO 27001 A.12.4" — a 2013 control number. Fix: update citations to ISO 27001:2022 (A.8.15 Logging), add a citation-version check to the review checklist.

    ⚙️ Scenario: Plant Adds a New Chemical Process

    • Identify OSHA impacts: Hazard Communication, PPE assessment, possibly Process Safety Management (1910.119) depending on chemicals and quantities.
    • Update written programmes and SDS index; schedule training records.

    Module 11

    Interview Preparation

    Questions asked for compliance writer and policy writer roles about regulatory knowledge.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.What is the difference between a standard and a regulation?
    Regulations are legally binding rules issued by government agencies; standards are voluntary documents from bodies like ISO that become obligatory when you certify or a contract requires them.
    IQ2.What does "shall" mean in a policy?
    A mandatory requirement. "Should" indicates a recommendation and "may" a permission.
    IQ3.What is a requirements register?
    A table linking each requirement to its source citation, applicability, owner and the document that addresses it.

    🛠️ Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.What documents does ISO 27001 require?
    Scope, policy, risk assessment and treatment processes, SoA, objectives, evidence of competence, operational planning, monitoring results, internal audit and management review records, nonconformities and corrective actions.
    IQ5.How do you write a control for SOC 2?
    Specify owner, activity, frequency, scope, evidence and approval so the auditor can test it; avoid promising more than is done.
    IQ6.How do HIPAA and GDPR breach rules differ?
    GDPR requires notifying the authority without undue delay and within 72 hours where feasible; HIPAA requires notifying individuals without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 days after discovery, with HHS reporting based on breach size.

    🚀 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you handle an ambiguous requirement?
    Document possible interpretations, consult the owner and legal, record the chosen interpretation, rationale and approver, and revisit when guidance appears.
    IQ8.How do you keep up with regulatory change?
    Subscribe to official sources and alerts, triage impact, update the register and documents before effective dates, train staff, and keep evidence.
    IQ9.What is ALCOA+?
    Data integrity principles: attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, plus complete, consistent, enduring and available.

    🏆 Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.How would you build one document set for ISO 9001, ISO 27001 and SOC 2?
    Use the shared ISO structure for common processes (document control, audit, corrective action), a crosswalk to SOC 2 criteria, and framework-specific annexes; maintain one register.
    IQ11.An executive wants the policy to say "fully GDPR compliant". Response?
    Explain risk of unverifiable claims; propose precise statements about specific measures and processes; route marketing claims through legal.
    IQ12.How do you prepare documentation for an FDA inspection?
    Ensure SOPs are current and followed, training records complete, deviations and CAPAs closed, change control trail intact, Part 11 controls validated, and rapid document retrieval.

    Module 01 · Foundations

    What are Mixed Methods?

    Start from zero: why combine qualitative and quantitative evidence, and the main designs.

    🧩 What are Mixed Methods?

    Mixed methods research deliberately combines qualitative (why, how, meaning) and quantitative (how many, how much, how often) evidence in one study or programme so each compensates for the other's weakness.

    🔴 One method only

    Survey says 62% are dissatisfied, but nobody knows why. Or interviews give rich stories, but nobody knows how common they are.

    🟢 Mixed methods

    Telemetry shows where users struggle, interviews explain why, and a survey sizes how widespread it is.

    ⚖️ Qualitative vs Quantitative

    QualitativeQuantitative
    AnswersWhy and howHow many and how much
    DataWords, observations, imagesNumbers, counts, rates
    SampleSmall (5–30), purposiveLarge (100s–1000s), representative
    StrengthDepth, context, discoveryGeneralisation, comparison, tracking
    WeaknessNot statistically generalisableLacks explanation
    MethodsInterviews, diaries, usability testsSurveys, analytics, A/B tests

    🗂️ Core Mixed-Methods Designs

    DesignOrderUse
    Explanatory sequentialQUANT → qualExplain surprising numbers
    Exploratory sequentialQUAL → quantDiscover themes, then measure them
    Convergent parallelQUAL + QUANT at the same timeCross-check findings
    EmbeddedOne method supports the otherOpen questions inside a survey
    Multiphase / continuousRepeated cycles over timeProduct discovery programmes
    TelemetryAutomatically collected usage data (clicks, sessions, errors).
    TriangulationConfirming a finding with different methods.
    ComplementarityMethods add different perspectives.
    IntegrationDeliberately combining findings, not just reporting side by side.
    Joint displayTable or chart showing qual and quant results together.
    Q. Which design explains surprising numbers with interviews?
    • A Parallel only
    • B None
    • C Exploratory sequential
    • D Explanatory sequential

    🤝 Reliability

    • Create a codebook with definitions and example quotes.
    • Have two coders tag a sample; compute inter-rater agreement (Cohen's kappa: 0.6–0.8 good, >0.8 very good).
    • Discuss disagreements and refine the codebook.
    • Tools: Dovetail, Condens, NVivo, ATLAS.ti, spreadsheets, AI-assisted clustering (always human-verify).
    💡
    Turning qual into numbers ("34% of comments mention buffering") is a classic bridge between the two worlds.

    Module 03 · Surveys & Telemetry

    Statistical Surveys and Telemetry Data

    Design valid surveys and read behavioural data with its limits.

    📋 Survey Basics

    TopicGuideline
    Question typesClosed (scales, multiple choice), open-text, ranking, matrix (use sparingly)
    Standard scalesSUS (usability), NPS (loyalty), CSAT (satisfaction), CES (effort), UEQ, SEQ
    WordingNeutral, single-barrelled, plain language, balanced scales
    LengthUnder 10 minutes; fewer questions = better completion
    SamplingRandom or quota-based; watch non-response bias
    Sample sizeAbout 385 for ±5% margin at 95% confidence (large population)

    🔴 Bad question

    "How satisfied are you with our fast, friendly and affordable support?"

    🟢 Better question

    "How satisfied are you with the speed of our support?" (1 = Very dissatisfied … 5 = Very satisfied)

    📡 Telemetry (Behavioural Data)

    EventA recorded action: lesson_started, video_paused, search_used.
    FunnelSteps toward a goal with drop-off at each step.
    CohortGroup of users sharing a start date or trait.
    Retention curveShare of a cohort still active over time.
    Session replay / heatmapsVisual playback of behaviour; mind privacy.
    Tool typeExamples
    Product analyticsMixpanel, Amplitude, GA4, PostHog
    Replay and heatmapsHotjar, FullStory, Clarity
    Data warehouse and BIBigQuery, Snowflake, Looker, Power BI
    Survey platformsQualtrics, SurveyMonkey, Typeform

    ⚠️ Limits of Each Data Type

    DataLimit
    TelemetryShows what, not why; depends on correct event tracking
    SurveysSelf-report bias, social desirability, non-response
    BothCorrelation is not causation
    ⚠️
    Respect privacy: get consent, anonymise, follow GDPR/DPDP, and avoid collecting sensitive data without need.
    Q. Which question is a good survey question?
    • A "How satisfied are you with the speed of our support?" (1–5)
    • B "How satisfied are you with our fast and friendly support?"
    • C "Rate everything."
    • D "You love our app, right?"

    Module 05 · Expert Practice

    Expert Mixed-Methods Programmes

    Quality criteria, causal thinking, programmes and AI-assisted analysis.

    🧠 Quality in Mixed Methods

    Inference qualityAre conclusions justified by both strands?
    Integration qualityAre findings truly combined?
    Sequencing logicWhy this order of methods?
    TransparencyDocument sampling, coding, analysis.
    ReflexivityAcknowledge researcher influence.

    🔁 Towards Causal Insight

    ApproachStrength
    Randomised experimentsStrongest causal claim
    Difference-in-differencesCompare change vs a similar group
    Interviews to trace mechanismsExplain how the effect happens
    Natural experiments / matched cohortsWhen randomisation is not possible
    📌
    Quantitative methods show that an effect exists; qualitative methods reveal how and why.

    🏗️ Continuous Research Programme

    • Run a research cadence: weekly telemetry review, monthly interview wave, quarterly large survey.
    • Maintain a research repository with tagged evidence linking quotes, metrics and decisions.
    • Create dashboards that pair metrics with verbatim quotes.
    • Use AI to summarise and cluster comments, but verify with human review and keep raw data accessible.

    ⚠️ Pitfalls

    🔴 Side-by-side only

    Qual and quant reported separately in two chapters; no one connects them.

    🟢 Truly integrated

    Findings merged by theme with a joint display, conflicts explored, and one set of recommendations.

    Q. What is a hallmark of strong mixed methods?
    • A Reporting strands separately
    • B Skipping documentation
    • C Using only surveys
    • D Deliberate integration of findings

    Module 06 · Practice: Why Do Learners Skip Quizzes?

    Practice: Why Do Learners Skip Quizzes?

    Design and integrate a mixed-methods study.

    0 / 0 correct

    📝 Scenario

    Telemetry shows only 35% of learners attempt module quizzes, though the team believes they help learning. You must explain why and recommend changes.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Choose a design

    Q. Which design fits best?
    • A Convergent parallel only
    • B Only a survey
    • C Explanatory sequential: telemetry → interviews → survey
    • D Only a prototype test
    ✍️ Justify your choice in two lines.
    Model answer: Telemetry reveals the pattern; interviews explain reasons; a survey measures how common each reason is, giving both depth and scale.

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Interview plan

    ✍️ Write 3 interview questions to learn why people skip quizzes.
    Model answer: 1) "Tell me about the last time you saw a quiz. What did you do?" 2) "What goes through your mind when you see a quiz?" 3) "What would make you more likely to try it?"

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Survey item

    Q. Pick the best survey item.
    • A "I skip quizzes because I'm short on time." (Strongly disagree … Strongly agree)
    • B "Why are you lazy?"
    • C "Rate the platform."
    • D "Don't you agree quizzes waste time?"

    4️⃣ Task 4 · Integrate

    ✍️ Interviews: 8/12 fear failing publicly. Survey: 47% worry about scores. Telemetry: skip rate 82% when the score is shown to peers. Write an integrated insight and recommendation.
    Model answer: Insight: Fear of public scoring, not lack of interest, drives quiz avoidance (qual theme confirmed by 47% survey agreement and 82% skip in telemetry). Recommendation: make scores private by default, add practice quizzes with no score, then A/B test completion rates.

    5️⃣ Task 5 · Limits

    Q. Which statement is correct?
    • A Survey proves causation
    • B Telemetry explains why
    • C Triangulated evidence strengthens but does not prove causation; an experiment tests it
    • D Interviews show how many

    Module 07 · Interview Preparation

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer.

    🧩 Fundamentals

    Why use mixed methods?
    Qualitative methods explain why and discover; quantitative methods size and generalise. Together they give confident, explainable decisions.
    Explanatory vs exploratory sequential?
    Explanatory: quantitative first, then qualitative to explain. Exploratory: qualitative first to discover themes, then quantitative to measure them.
    What is triangulation?
    Confirming a finding from independent methods or sources to reduce the blind spots of each.

    📋 Methods

    How do you code open-text feedback?
    Read, open-code, group into categories, name themes, count and quote; use a codebook and test inter-rater agreement.
    What makes a good survey question?
    Neutral, single-barrelled, simple language, balanced scale, tested in a pilot.
    What are the limits of telemetry?
    It shows what happened, not why; relies on correct tracking; may miss offline behaviour; privacy considerations.

    🏛️ Integration

    What is a joint display?
    A table or chart that places qualitative themes, survey figures and telemetry side by side by theme.
    Survey and analytics disagree — what next?
    Check sampling, segments, bias and definitions, then add targeted qualitative work to understand the gap.
    How do you use AI in analysis safely?
    For first-pass clustering and summaries, then human verification against raw data; protect personal data and document the process.

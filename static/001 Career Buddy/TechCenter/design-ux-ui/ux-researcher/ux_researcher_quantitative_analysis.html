    Module 01 · Foundations

    What is Quantitative UX Research?

    Start from zero: what quantitative research measures, types of data and scales, and core statistical vocabulary.

    🧭 What is Quantitative UX Research?

    Quantitative UX research uses numbers from larger samples to measure how many, how much and how often. It tells you how widespread a problem is, tracks experience over time and helps prioritise with confidence.

    📏
    Measure
    Satisfaction, ease and loyalty scores.
    📊
    Size
    How many users face a problem.
    📈
    Track
    Change across releases and cohorts.
    🎯
    Prioritise
    Back decisions with representative evidence.

    🗂️ Types of Quantitative Methods

    MethodMeasuresExample
    SurveysAttitudes, preferences, self-reported behaviourSatisfaction survey to 5,000 users
    Standardised UX metricsSUS, CSAT, NPS, CES, UMUX-LiteQuarterly SUS benchmark
    Behavioural analyticsWhat users actually doFunnels, retention cohorts
    Quantitative usability testingSuccess rates and times on larger samples40 users × 5 tasks
    A/B testingCausal effect of a changeNew onboarding vs old

    📐 Types of Data & Scales

    ScaleMeaningExampleValid summaries
    NominalCategories, no orderDevice: Android / iOSCounts, %
    OrdinalOrdered, gaps not equalVery unsatisfied → very satisfiedMedian, % top-2-box
    IntervalOrdered, equal gaps, no true zeroSUS score (treated as interval)Mean, SD
    RatioEqual gaps + true zeroTime on task, number of ordersMean, median, ratios

    📖 Key Terms (What is What)

    PopulationEveryone you want to learn about (all active users).
    SampleThe subset you actually measure.
    Mean / medianAverage / middle value. Prefer median for skewed data like time.
    Standard deviationHow spread out values are.
    Margin of errorThe ± range around a survey result, e.g. 62% ± 4%.
    Confidence levelHow sure you are the true value is inside that range (usually 95%).
    Statistical significanceA difference unlikely to be due to chance alone.
    Top-2-box% choosing the top two options on a scale.

    Module 02 · Survey Design

    Survey Design

    Write surveys that give clean, unbiased, decision-ready data.

    📝 What Makes a Good Survey?

    • Starts from a research question and decision, not a list of "nice to know" questions.
    • Short: aim for 5–10 minutes; completion drops with length.
    • Targets the right people at the right moment (e.g. right after a task).
    • Uses clear, neutral, single-topic questions.

    🗂️ Types of Survey Questions

    TypeExampleUse for
    Single choice"Which device do you mainly use?"Categories
    Multiple choice"Which features have you used? (select all)"Usage breadth
    Likert scale"The app is easy to use." Strongly disagree → Strongly agreeAttitudes
    Rating scale"How satisfied are you? 1–5"Satisfaction
    Ranking"Rank these 5 features by importance"Priorities (max 5–7 items)
    MatrixSeveral items on the same scaleCompact, but causes fatigue
    Open-ended"What is the main reason for your score?"Explaining numbers

    🧲 Common Question Mistakes

    MistakeBadBetter
    Leading"How much do you love our new design?""How would you rate the new design?"
    Double-barrelled"Was checkout fast and easy?"Split into two questions
    Jargon"Rate the IA of the PDP.""How easy was it to find product details?"
    Unbalanced scaleGood / Very good / ExcellentVery poor → Very good, with a midpoint
    Missing optionNo "Other" or "Not applicable"Add them
    Hypothetical"Would you pay for premium?""Have you paid for any app subscription in the last 3 months?"

    🎯 Sampling & Sample Size

    Sampling typeHowRisk
    RandomEvery user has equal chanceBest for representativeness
    StratifiedRandom within segments (plan, region)Ensures small segments are included
    ConvenienceWhoever responds (social media)High bias
    InterceptIn-app at a key momentCaptures only active users
    📌
    Rule of thumb: about 385 responses gives ±5% margin of error at 95% confidence for a large population; about 1,070 gives ±3%.
    ⚠️
    Non-response bias: people with strong feelings respond more. Compare respondent profiles with your user base.

    Module 03 · Benchmark Metrics

    Benchmark Metrics: SUS, CSAT & NPS

    Calculate and interpret the standard UX metrics used to track experience quality.

    📏 What is a Benchmark Metric?

    A benchmark metric is a standardised measure collected the same way over time, so you can compare releases, segments or competitors. Using validated questionnaires makes results comparable with industry norms.

    MetricMeasuresScale
    SUSPerceived usability of a system10 items → 0–100 score
    CSATSatisfaction with an interaction1–5 (or 1–7)
    NPSLoyalty / likelihood to recommend0–10 → −100 to +100
    CESEffort to get something done1–7 "easy to handle my issue"
    SEQEase of a single task1–7
    UMUX-LiteShort usability measure2 items

    🧮 SUS — System Usability Scale

    10 statements rated 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree), alternating positive and negative wording.

    1. Odd items (positive): score − 1.
    2. Even items (negative): 5 − score.
    3. Add all 10 contributions (0–40).
    4. Multiply by 2.5 → SUS score 0–100.
    EXAMPLE · One participant

    Odd items 4, 5, 4, 4, 5 → 3+4+3+3+4 = 17. Even items 2, 1, 2, 2, 1 → 3+4+3+3+4 = 17. Total 34 × 2.5 = SUS 85.

    SUS scoreInterpretation
    > 80.3Excellent (top ~10%)
    68Average
    < 51Poor — serious usability problems
    ⚠️
    SUS is not a percentage. A score of 68 is the 50th percentile, not "68% usable".

    😊 CSAT — Customer Satisfaction

    "How satisfied were you with [interaction]?" on 1–5. CSAT = % of responses that are 4 or 5.

    EXAMPLE · Support chat CSAT

    200 responses: 90 gave 5, 60 gave 4 → (90 + 60) ÷ 200 = 75% CSAT.

    📣 NPS — Net Promoter Score

    "How likely are you to recommend [product] to a friend or colleague?" 0–10.

    ScoreGroup
    9–10Promoters
    7–8Passives (not counted)
    0–6Detractors
    📌
    NPS = % Promoters − % Detractors, ranging from −100 to +100.
    EXAMPLE · Calculation

    1,000 responses: 450 promoters (45%), 350 passives, 200 detractors (20%) → NPS = 45 − 20 = +25.

    💡
    NPS is a relationship/loyalty signal, best for trends and segments. Always ask a follow-up "Why?" — the score alone tells you nothing about what to fix.

    🧠 Check Yourself

    Q. 30% promoters, 40% passives, 30% detractors. NPS?

    • D −30

    Module 04 · Cohort Behavioural Tracking

    Cohort Behavioural Tracking

    Measure what groups of users actually do over time, and connect behaviour to attitudes.

    👥 What is a Cohort?

    A cohort is a group of users who share a characteristic or start point. Cohort analysis tracks how each group behaves over time, so you can see whether changes help new users more than old ones.

    🗂️ Types of Cohorts

    TypeGrouped byExample
    Acquisition / time-basedWhen users startedSigned up in Week 12
    BehaviouralAn action they tookUsers who completed onboarding checklist
    Attribute / segmentA propertyAndroid users in Tier-2 cities
    ExperimentTest groupVariant B of new onboarding

    📊 Reading a Retention Cohort

    CohortUsersWeek 1Week 2Week 4Week 8
    Jan wk 13,20040%29%21%17%
    Jan wk 23,05041%30%22%17%
    Jan wk 3 (new tutorial)3,30049%36%27%22%
    Jan wk 43,15050%37%27%—
    ✅
    The tutorial lifted retention at every week, and the curve still flattens (around 22%) — evidence of a lasting, not novelty, effect.

    🔗 Connecting Behaviour and Attitude

    Behavioural cohortSUSNPS90-day retention
    Used "Saved lists"79+3864%
    Never used it66+1231%
    ⚠️
    This is correlation. Engaged users may both like the app more and use Saved lists. Use an experiment or qualitative follow-up to test cause.

    📈 Behavioural Metrics to Track

    🚀
    Activation
    % reaching the key first action within N days.
    🔁
    Retention
    % still active at day/week N.
    📆
    Frequency
    Active days per week per user.
    🧩
    Feature adoption
    % of active users using a feature.

    Module 05 · Analysis & Statistics

    Analysing Quantitative Data

    Clean data, compare groups correctly and report numbers honestly with confidence intervals.

    🧹 Data Cleaning

    • Remove speeders (finished far faster than possible) and straight-liners (same answer every row).
    • Add an attention check ("Select 'Agree' for this item").
    • Handle missing values consistently; report how many were excluded.
    • Check for duplicate responses.

    📊 Descriptive Statistics

    StatisticUseExample
    Count / %Categorical answers62% use Android
    Mean ± SDInterval data, roughly symmetricSUS 71 ± 14
    Median, IQRSkewed data (time)Median 48 s (IQR 32–80)
    Top-2-boxLikert items58% agree or strongly agree

    🎯 Confidence Intervals

    A 95% confidence interval shows the range the true value likely sits in. Always show it for small samples.

    EXAMPLE · Task success with 20 users

    16/20 succeeded = 80%. The 95% CI (adjusted-Wald) is roughly 58%–93%. You can say "most users succeed", not "exactly 80% succeed".

    ⚖️ Comparing Groups

    ComparisonCommon test
    Two means (SUS old vs new, different users)Independent t-test
    Same users, before vs afterPaired t-test
    Two proportions (success rates)Chi-square or N-1 two-proportion test
    3+ groupsANOVA
    Ordinal data / non-normalMann–Whitney U
    💡
    You don't need to calculate by hand — tools like Excel, Google Sheets, R, Python or MeasuringU calculators do it. You do need to choose the right test and explain the result plainly.

    Module 06 · Interview Preparation

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer.

    📝 Survey Design Questions

    What makes a good survey question?
    Clear, neutral, about one topic, with balanced scales and options like "Other" or "Not applicable". Avoid leading, double-barrelled and hypothetical questions.
    How many responses do you need?
    About 385 gives ±5% margin of error at 95% confidence for a large population; about 1,070 gives ±3%.
    What is non-response bias?
    People with strong opinions respond more, so results may not represent all users. Compare respondent profiles with the user base.
    Why pilot a survey?
    To catch confusing wording, broken logic and length problems with 5–10 people before sending to thousands.

    📏 Benchmark Metrics Questions

    How is SUS calculated and what is a good score?
    Odd items: score − 1; even items: 5 − score; sum and multiply by 2.5 for 0–100. Average is about 68; above 80 is excellent. It is not a percentage.
    How is NPS calculated?
    % promoters (9–10) minus % detractors (0–6), ranging from −100 to +100. Passives (7–8) aren't counted.
    CSAT vs NPS vs CES?
    CSAT measures satisfaction with an interaction, NPS measures loyalty/likelihood to recommend, and CES measures effort to get something done.
    What are the limitations of NPS?
    It measures loyalty, not usability, says nothing about why, and is sensitive to timing and culture. Always add a "why" follow-up and trend it over time.

    👥 Cohorts & Analysis Questions

    What is cohort analysis?
    Tracking groups of users who share a start point or behaviour over time, e.g. weekly sign-up cohorts, to see whether changes improve retention.
    Mean or median for time on task?
    Median, because time data is skewed by a few very slow participants.
    What is a confidence interval?
    The range in which the true value likely lies, e.g. 80% success (95% CI 58–93%) with 20 users. It shows how precise a result is.
    How do you combine quant and qual?
    Explanatory (quant first, then qual explains why) or exploratory (qual first, then quant sizes the finding), triangulating for confidence.
    What is the HEART framework?
    Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention and Task success — a way to choose UX metrics linked to goals.

    💳 Scenario: WalletWise Expense App

    WalletWise redesigned its expense-tracking flow in April. Leadership asks: "Did the redesign improve the experience, and is it keeping people around?" You have survey access to all users and analytics data.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Design the Survey

    ✍️ Write 4 survey questions (include one standardised metric and one open question).
    Model answer: Q1 SUS (10 items) about the expense flow. Q2 SEQ after adding an expense: "Overall, how easy or difficult was adding this expense?" 1–7. Q3 "How often did you log expenses in the past 2 weeks?" (Never / 1–2 / 3–6 / Daily). Q4 open: "What, if anything, makes logging expenses difficult?"
    Q. Which is the biggest problem: "How easy and fast was adding an expense?"
    • A It is leading
    • B It is double-barrelled
    • C It is too short
    • D Nothing

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Calculate the Metrics

    MetricBefore (March)After (May)
    SUS (mean, n=420 / 410)6374
    Promoters / Passives / Detractors32% / 38% / 30%41% / 37% / 22%
    CSAT (4–5 on 1–5)58%71%
    Q. What is NPS after the redesign?
    • A +41
    • B +19
    • C +22
    • D +63
    ✍️ Interpret the SUS change.
    Model answer: SUS rose from 63 (below the 68 average) to 74 (above average, approaching "good"). With n≈400 per wave the difference is likely significant (confirm with a t-test); this suggests the redesign improved perceived usability.

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Read the Cohort Data

    Sign-up cohortWeek 1Week 4Week 8
    March (old flow)38%20%15%
    April (new flow)47%28%23%
    May (new flow)48%29%—
    ✍️ What do you conclude, and what would make you more confident it was the redesign?
    Model answer: Retention improved ~8–9 points at every week and holds in May, suggesting a durable effect. To strengthen causality: check no marketing or seasonal changes in April, compare with a holdout or A/B test if available, and segment by platform to ensure the effect is consistent.

    4️⃣ Task 4 · Report to Leadership

    ✍️ Write a 3-sentence summary for leadership.
    Model answer: "The April redesign made expense tracking easier and more habit-forming. Usability rose from below average (SUS 63) to above average (74), NPS improved from +2 to +19, and week-8 retention rose from 15% to 23%. Next, we'll address the top remaining complaint — receipt scanning accuracy — and re-measure in Q3."

    Module 01 · Foundations

    What is Research Synthesis?

    Start from zero: what synthesis means, the difference between data, findings and insights, and the main synthesis artefacts.

    🧭 What is Synthesis?

    Synthesis is turning raw research data — notes, quotes, recordings, numbers — into meaning the team can act on. Storytelling is communicating that meaning so people remember it, believe it and act on it.

    Figure 1.1 · The synthesis ladder

    ⚖️ Data vs Finding vs Insight

    LevelExample
    DataP4: "I just screenshot the bill and send it on WhatsApp."
    Finding6 of 8 participants share bills outside the app.
    InsightUsers split bills socially, in the chat where the group already is — the app forces them to switch context, so they skip it.
    RecommendationLet users share a split request directly into WhatsApp with a payment link.
    💡
    A good insight is non-obvious, evidence-backed, explains why, and suggests action.

    🗂️ Types of Synthesis Artefacts

    ArtefactWhat it showsBest for
    Affinity diagramNotes clustered into themesMaking sense of messy qualitative data
    PersonaAn archetype of a user groupKeeping teams focused on real user needs
    Journey mapSteps, thoughts, emotions and pain points over timeSeeing the end-to-end experience
    Empathy mapSays / thinks / does / feelsQuick alignment on one user type
    Service blueprintJourney + front-stage + back-stage processesComplex services with many teams
    Insight report / readoutKey findings and recommendationsDecision-making

    📖 Key Terms

    CodingTagging pieces of data with labels (codes) to find patterns.
    ThemeA pattern found across many data points.
    ArchetypeA representative pattern of behaviour, not a real individual.
    TouchpointAny interaction between user and product/service.
    Moment of truthA make-or-break point in the journey.
    StakeholderAnyone who uses or acts on research.
    Q. "6 of 8 participants abandoned at the KYC step" is a…
    • A Raw data point
    • B Finding
    • C Insight
    • D Recommendation

    Module 02 · Affinity Diagramming

    Affinity Diagramming

    Cluster research notes into themes, then turn themes into insights.

    🗃️ What is an Affinity Diagram?

    An affinity diagram organises many individual observations (usually one per sticky note) into groups based on natural relationships. It is bottom-up: themes emerge from the data instead of being decided in advance.

    🔄 Step by Step

    Figure 2.1 · Affinity diagramming process
    • Write notes as observations with participant ID: "P3 checks balance before every purchase."
    • Do it with the team (PM, designer, engineer) to build shared understanding.
    • Tools: Miro, FigJam, Dovetail, or physical sticky notes.

    🗂️ Types of Coding

    ApproachHowWhen
    Inductive (bottom-up)Codes emerge from dataExploratory research
    Deductive (top-down)Predefined codes from research questions or a frameworkEvaluating known topics
    HybridStart with some codes, add new onesMost practical studies

    🧩 Example

    EXAMPLE · Food-delivery interviews, 10 participants
    Theme (group label)Example notes
    "I don't trust the delivery time"P1 adds 15 min to estimate · P4 calls restaurant · P7 orders earlier "just in case"
    "Extra charges surprise me"P2 abandoned at packaging fee · P5 compares totals across apps · P9 "hidden fees feel like a trick"
    "I reorder the same few things"P3 orders same thali weekly · P6 uses past orders tab · P10 wants "usual" button

    Insight: Uncertainty (time and cost) drives the most frustration; users build their own buffers. → Show reliable time ranges and total cost upfront.

    Q. Why cluster silently first?
    • A To save paper
    • B So loud voices don't dominate and themes emerge from the data
    • C Because talking is banned
    • D To speed up coding

    Module 03 · Persona Creation

    Persona Creation

    Create evidence-based personas that teams actually use — and avoid stereotypes.

    👤 What is a Persona?

    A persona is a fictional but research-based archetype representing a group of users with similar goals, behaviours and pain points. It helps teams design for real needs instead of "the average user" or themselves.

    🗂️ Types of Personas

    TypeBased onUse
    Research (qualitative) personaInterviews and observationMost common; rich goals and behaviours
    Data-driven / statistical personaSegmentation of survey + analytics dataSizing segments; marketing alignment
    Proto-personaTeam assumptions (to be validated)Early alignment; must be replaced by research
    Jobs-based personaJobs-to-be-done rather than demographicsProduct strategy
    Anti-personaUsers you are NOT designing forScope decisions

    🧱 Anatomy of a Good Persona

    Name & quote"Ravi, the busy branch manager" — "I need answers in 2 minutes, not 2 hours."
    ContextManages 3 pharmacy branches in Vijayawada; mostly on mobile between visits.
    GoalsKeep stock available; avoid expired medicine losses.
    BehavioursChecks reports at 7 am and 9 pm; relies on WhatsApp updates from staff.
    Pain pointsReports are desktop-only; stock data is a day old.
    NeedsMobile alerts for low/expiring stock; trusted real-time numbers.
    EvidenceBased on 9 interviews, 2 site visits, 1,200 survey responses (segment = 23%).
    ⚠️
    Avoid decorative details (favourite colour, hobbies) that don't affect design, and avoid stereotypes by age, gender or region. Behaviour and goals define personas, not demographics.

    Module 04 · Journey Mapping

    Journey Mapping

    Visualise the end-to-end experience over time to find pain points and opportunities.

    🗺️ What is a Journey Map?

    A customer/user journey map shows the stages a person goes through to reach a goal, with their actions, thoughts, emotions, touchpoints and pain points at each stage — including moments outside your product.

    🗂️ Types of Journey Maps

    TypeShowsUse
    Current-stateHow the experience works todayFinding pain points
    Future-stateThe intended experienceVision and alignment
    Day-in-the-lifeA whole day, beyond one productContext and opportunities
    Service blueprintJourney + staff actions + systems behind the scenesFixing operational causes
    Experience mapA general human experience, not product-specificBroad strategy

    🧱 Journey Map Layers

    LayerSearchCompareApplyWaitGet approved
    ActionsGoogles "personal loan"Checks 4 appsFills form, uploads docsChecks status dailyGets SMS
    Thoughts"Which is safe?""Why are rates unclear?""Why so many documents?""Is it stuck?""Finally!"
    Emotion😐😟😫😟😊
    TouchpointsAds, reviewsWebsitesAppApp, call centreSMS, email
    OpportunitiesTrust signalsClear total costAuto-fetch docsStatus timelineNext steps
    ✅
    The emotion curve highlights where to focus: here, the Apply and Wait stages.

    🔄 Building a Journey Map

    1. Choose one persona and one scenario (e.g. first loan application).
    2. Gather evidence: interviews, diary studies, support tickets, analytics.
    3. Define stages from the user's point of view.
    4. Fill layers with evidence, not guesses — cite sources.
    5. Highlight moments of truth and opportunities.
    6. Workshop with the team; assign owners to opportunities.
    Q. Which journey map type shows staff actions and back-end systems?

    • B Future-state
    • C Service blueprint
    • D Empathy map

    Module 05 · Storytelling

    Executive-Level Insight Presentations

    Present research so senior leaders understand it in minutes and make decisions.

    🎤 What Executives Need

    ⏱️
    Brevity
    The answer first — often in 1 slide.
    💰
    Business impact
    Revenue, cost, risk, growth.
    🎯
    A decision
    What do you need from them?
    🧾
    Credible evidence
    Numbers + a short user clip.

    🔺 Pyramid Principle

    Figure 5.1 · Lead with the answer, then support it

    🔴 Chronological

    "We interviewed 12 people… then we did a survey… then we found…" — the point arrives on slide 18.

    🟢 Answer-first

    "First-time borrowers abandon because they can't see the total cost. Fixing it could recover ~₹1.2 Cr/year. We need 1 sprint and Finance approval."

    📖 Story Structures

    StructureShapeUse
    SCRSituation → Complication → ResolutionMost exec readouts
    What / So what / Now whatFinding → meaning → actionShort updates
    Hero's journey (user story)A persona's struggle and resolutionBuilding empathy
    Before / afterToday vs future statePitching a change

    🎞️ Evidence That Lands

    • A 30–60 second video clip of a user struggling beats 10 slides.
    • Pair every quote with a number: "6 of 8 users" or "41% of 1,200 respondents".
    • State confidence (high/medium/low) and limitations honestly.
    • One chart per message; title the chart with the takeaway, not "Survey results".
    EXAMPLE · Takeaway chart title

    Instead of "Q3 NPS by segment" → "New users are 3× more likely to be detractors — onboarding is the problem".

    Module 06 · Interview Preparation

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer.

    🧩 Synthesis Questions

    Data vs finding vs insight?
    Data is raw (a quote). A finding is a pattern (6 of 8 did X). An insight explains why it happens and what it means, suggesting action.
    How do you run an affinity diagram?
    Atomise observations onto notes, cluster silently, name groups in the user's voice, group into higher themes, then write evidence-backed insights — ideally with the team.
    Inductive vs deductive coding?
    Inductive: codes emerge from the data (exploratory). Deductive: predefined codes from research questions or frameworks. Most studies use a hybrid.
    How do you make synthesis rigorous?
    Link every insight to evidence, involve more than one person, look for counter-examples and state confidence and limitations.

    👤 Personas & Journey Map Questions

    What makes a persona useful?
    It is based on research and focuses on goals, behaviours, context and pain points — not decorative demographics — and is actively used in decisions.
    What is a proto-persona?
    A persona based on team assumptions, useful for early alignment but which must be validated with research.
    What layers does a journey map include?
    Stages, actions, thoughts, emotions, touchpoints, pain points and opportunities for one persona and scenario.
    Journey map vs service blueprint?
    A journey map shows the user's experience; a service blueprint adds front-stage and back-stage staff actions and supporting systems.

    🎤 Storytelling & Impact Questions

    How do you present research to executives?
    Answer first (Pyramid Principle), business impact, two or three supporting points, a short user clip and a clear ask, with confidence levels.
    What is the SCR structure?
    Situation, Complication, Resolution — a concise way to frame a problem and recommendation.
    How do you make research findings stick?
    Short clips, numbers paired with quotes, takeaway chart titles, involving stakeholders in sessions and synthesis, and assigning owners to actions.
    What is atomic research?
    Storing research as small linked units — experiments, facts, insights, recommendations — in a searchable repository so insights are reusable.
    How do you measure research impact?
    Decisions influenced, product metrics moved, insight reuse, stakeholder engagement and costs avoided by stopping the wrong builds.

    🏥 Scenario: CareConnect Telehealth App

    You interviewed 10 patients (aged 45–70) who booked video consultations on CareConnect. 38% of booked video consults fail to start on time. Notes:

    • P1: "I joined 10 minutes early but the doctor never appeared."
    • P2: Son sets up the app; patient can't join alone.
    • P3: "The link came on email; I only check WhatsApp."
    • P4: Camera permission popup confused her; tapped "Don't allow".
    • P5: "No one told me the doctor was running late."
    • P6: Bought data pack just before call; low network in village.
    • P7: Couldn't find the "Join" button; called helpline.
    • P8: "I didn't know if the payment went through, so I didn't join."
    • P9: Uses daughter's phone; notifications go to her.
    • P10: Doctor joined late; patient left after 5 minutes.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Affinity Diagram

    ✍️ Group the notes into 3–4 themes and name each theme in the user's voice.
    Model answer: "I don't know what's happening" (P1, P5, P8, P10) — no status of doctor or payment. "I can't do this alone" (P2, P9) — depends on family. "The app talks to me in the wrong place" (P3, P9) — links/notifications on email or someone else's phone. "The tech gets in my way" (P4, P6, P7) — permissions, network, finding Join.

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Insight

    Q. Which is the strongest insight?
    • A Patients are bad with technology
    • B Patients miss consults because they lack real-time status and reminders in channels they actually use, so uncertainty makes them give up
    • C Users want a new logo
    • D Doctors are lazy

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Persona

    ✍️ Write a short behaviour-based persona.
    Model answer: Lakshmi, the family-assisted patient — "My son set it up; I just want the doctor to call me." Context: 62, uses a shared phone, mostly WhatsApp. Goals: consult without travelling. Pain points: email links, permission pop-ups, no idea if the doctor is late. Needs: WhatsApp reminders with one-tap join, clear "doctor is 5 minutes late" status, family member can be added as helper. Evidence: P2, P3, P4, P5, P9.

    4️⃣ Task 4 · Journey Stage

    ✍️ Describe the "Joining the call" stage: actions, thoughts, emotion, opportunity.
    Model answer: Actions: searches for link, opens app, denies camera permission, waits. Thoughts: "Is the doctor coming? Did I pay?" Emotion: anxious → frustrated. Opportunities: pre-call check (camera, network) 1 hour before, live doctor status, payment confirmation on the join screen.

    5️⃣ Task 5 · Executive Story

    ✍️ Write a 3-sentence SCR summary for the COO.
    Model answer: Situation: Video consults are our fastest-growing service. Complication: 38% fail to start on time, mainly because patients get no real-time status and links arrive where they don't look — each failed consult costs a refund and a doctor's slot. Resolution: We recommend WhatsApp reminders with one-tap join, live doctor status and a pre-call check; a 4-week pilot could cut failures by half. We need Engineering for 2 sprints and approval for WhatsApp Business messaging costs.

    Module 01 · Foundations

    What is Evaluative Research?

    Start from zero: what evaluative research and usability mean, the types of usability tests, and the core vocabulary.

    🧭 What is Evaluative Research?

    Evaluative research checks how well a design, prototype or live product works for its users. It answers: Can people use it? Where do they struggle? Does the new version work better than the old?

    🌱 Generative

    Discovers needs and opportunities before a solution exists. "What problems do freelancers have with invoicing?"

    🧪 Evaluative

    Assesses a specific solution. "Can freelancers create and send an invoice on this prototype?"

    📏 What is Usability?

    ISO 9241-11 defines usability as the extent to which a product can be used by specified users to achieve specified goals with effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction in a specified context.

    🎯
    Effectiveness
    Can users complete the task? (success rate, errors)
    ⏱️
    Efficiency
    How much time and effort does it take?
    😊
    Satisfaction
    How do users feel? (SUS, SEQ ratings)
    📚
    Learnability
    How quickly do new users become proficient?

    🗂️ Types of Evaluative Methods

    MethodWhat it evaluatesStage
    Moderated usability testTask success, problems and reasons, with a live facilitatorPrototype or live
    Unmoderated usability testTask success at scale without a facilitatorPrototype or live
    Tree testingFindability in a navigation structure (no visuals)Before visual design
    Card sortingHow users group and label contentInformation architecture planning
    First-click testWhere users click first for a taskWireframes and mockups
    Heuristic evaluationExpert review against usability principlesAny stage, no users
    Benchmark studyUsability metrics compared over time or vs competitorsLive product

    📖 Key Terms (What is What)

    TaskA realistic goal given to a participant, e.g. "Find a flight to Pune next Friday."
    ScenarioShort story giving context and motivation for the task.
    Think-aloudParticipants say what they think while doing tasks.
    Task successWhether the participant completed the task correctly.
    Severity ratingHow serious a usability problem is (e.g. 1–4).
    Information architecture (IA)How content is organised, structured and labelled.
    Facilitator / moderatorThe researcher who runs the session.
    Q. Which is an evaluative research question?
    • A What are freelancers' biggest money worries?
    • B Can users find the "Download invoice" option in the new design?
    • C How do families plan holidays?
    • D What tools do nurses use?

    Module 02 · Usability Testing

    Moderated & Unmoderated Task-Based Testing

    Plan tasks, run sessions, and choose between moderated and unmoderated testing.

    🧪 What is Task-Based Usability Testing?

    Participants attempt realistic tasks on a product or prototype while the researcher observes. The goal is to find where and why people struggle, not whether they "like" the design.

    📌
    Five users typically uncover most of the major usability problems in one round (Nielsen). Test, fix, then test again with another 5.

    ⚖️ Moderated vs Unmoderated

    ModeratedUnmoderated
    FacilitatorPresent live (in person or remote)Absent; tool gives instructions
    DepthHigh — can probe "why?"Lower — relies on think-aloud
    Scale & speed5–10 sessions over days20–100+ sessions in hours
    Cost per participantHigherLower
    Best forComplex flows, early prototypes, sensitive topicsSimple tasks, quick validation, benchmarks
    ToolsZoom, Lookback, in-person labUserTesting, Maze, Lyssna, Useberry

    ✍️ Writing Good Tasks

    🔴 Weak task

    "Click the Accounts tab, then Statements, and download March." (gives away the path)

    🟢 Strong task

    "Your landlord needs proof of rent paid in March. Get a copy of your bank statement for that month."

    • Use the user's goal and language, not UI labels.
    • One clear end point so success is measurable.
    • Order tasks realistically; start easy.
    • Define success criteria in advance.

    Module 03 · Tree Testing

    Tree Testing

    Test whether people can find things in your navigation structure — before any visual design exists.

    🌳 What is Tree Testing?

    Tree testing (reverse card sorting) gives participants a text-only version of your navigation hierarchy (the "tree") and asks them to find where they would go to complete tasks. With no visuals, it isolates labels and structure.

    EXAMPLE · A text-only tree

    Home → Accounts (Savings, Current, Statements) · Payments (UPI, Bills, Scheduled) · Cards (Debit, Credit, Limits) · Help

    Task: "You want to stop a payment that runs every month. Where would you go?" Correct: Payments → Scheduled.

    📊 Tree Test Metrics

    MetricMeaning
    Success rate% who chose a correct destination
    Directness% who went straight there without backtracking
    Time takenHow long it took
    First clickWhich top-level category they chose first — strong predictor of success
    Path analysisThe routes taken, including wrong turns (pietree)
    Figure 3.1 · First-click distribution for "stop a monthly payment"

    🔄 Running a Tree Test

    1. Build the tree from your proposed IA (3–4 levels deep is common).
    2. Write 8–10 tasks covering key and risky content.
    3. Recruit 50+ participants per tree for reliable percentages.
    4. Run unmoderated (Optimal Workshop Treejack, UXtweak, Maze).
    5. Analyse success, directness and first clicks; fix labels; retest.
    💡
    Test the current tree and the proposed tree with the same tasks to compare.
    Q. 31% first clicked "Accounts" to stop a recurring payment. What does this suggest?
    • A Users are careless
    • B Many users expect recurring payments to live under Accounts — consider cross-linking or relabelling
    • C Delete the Accounts menu
    • D Nothing

    Module 04 · Card Sorting

    Card Sorting

    Learn how users group and name content, so your information architecture matches their mental models.

    🗃️ What is Card Sorting?

    Participants sort content items written on cards into groups that make sense to them. It reveals mental models: how users expect content to be organised and labelled.

    🗂️ Types of Card Sorting

    TypeHow it worksUse when
    Open sortUsers create and name their own groupsEarly IA; learning users' vocabulary
    Closed sortUsers place cards into predefined categoriesValidating existing categories
    Hybrid sortPredefined categories + users can add new onesRefining an IA
    ModeratedIn person / live, with think-aloudUnderstanding reasoning
    UnmoderatedOnline tool, many participantsStatistical patterns (30–50+ users)

    📐 Reading the Results

    • Category labels: collect the names users invent — they become navigation labels.

    Module 05 · Analysis

    Analysing & Reporting Usability Findings

    Turn observations into prioritised, actionable findings that teams fix.

    🗂️ From Observations to Issues

    Figure 5.1 · Usability analysis pipeline

    🔥 Severity Ratings

    SeverityMeaningExample
    4 · CatastrophicPrevents task completionUsers can't find how to pay
    3 · MajorSignificant delay or frustrationAddress form rejects valid PIN codes
    2 · MinorSmall delay, users recoverAmbiguous icon, users hover to read tooltip
    1 · CosmeticNo impact on performanceInconsistent spacing
    📌
    Severity considers frequency (how many users), impact (how bad) and persistence (do users overcome it after learning?).

    🧾 Writing a Finding

    EXAMPLE · Usability finding
    IssueUsers could not find where to download statements.
    Evidence5 of 8 failed; median 2 min 40 s; quote: "I'd expect it under Accounts."
    Severity3 — Major
    RecommendationAdd "Statements" under Accounts and a shortcut on the account card; retest with tree test.

    🔍 Heuristic Evaluation (Expert Review)

    Experts review the interface against Nielsen's 10 heuristics, e.g. visibility of system status, match with the real world, user control and freedom, consistency, error prevention, recognition over recall, flexibility, minimalist design, error recovery, help and documentation.

    💡
    Heuristic reviews are fast and cheap, but they complement user testing — they don't replace it.
    Q. 5 of 8 users can't complete checkout. Severity?
    • A 1 · Cosmetic
    • B 2 · Minor
    • C 4 · Catastrophic
    • D Not an issue

    Module 06 · Interview Preparation

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer.

    🧪 Usability Testing Questions

    Moderated vs unmoderated testing — when do you use each?
    Moderated for complex flows, early prototypes and sensitive topics where you need to probe why. Unmoderated for simple tasks, quick validation and larger samples.
    How many users do you test with?
    About 5 per round for qualitative usability to find most major issues, then iterate. Benchmarks and quantitative studies need 20–40+.
    How do you write a good task?
    A realistic goal in the user's language with a clear end point, without revealing the path or UI labels.
    What metrics do you collect?
    Task success, time on task (median), errors, SEQ after each task and SUS at the end, plus severity-rated issues.
    What do you say when a participant asks for help?
    Turn it back neutrally: "What would you do if I weren't here?" or "What are you looking for?"

    🌳 Tree Testing & Card Sorting Questions

    Card sorting vs tree testing?
    Card sorting is generative — it shows how users group and name content. Tree testing is evaluative — it checks whether users can find items in a proposed structure.
    Open vs closed card sort?
    Open: users create and name groups (learn their vocabulary). Closed: users place cards into predefined categories (validate existing ones). Hybrid mixes both.
    What metrics matter in a tree test?
    Success rate, directness, time and especially first click — a wrong first click strongly predicts failure.
    How do you read a similarity matrix?
    It shows how often pairs of cards were grouped together; high percentages reveal clusters that become navigation categories.

    📋 Analysis & Reporting Questions

    How do you rate severity?
    By frequency, impact and persistence — from 1 cosmetic to 4 catastrophic (blocks task completion).
    What makes a good usability finding?
    The issue, evidence (how many users, metrics, quotes), severity and a specific recommendation.
    Heuristic evaluation vs usability testing?
    Heuristic evaluation is an expert review against principles like Nielsen's 10 — fast but no real users. Usability testing observes real users. They complement each other.
    How do you avoid order effects when comparing two designs?
    Counterbalance in within-subjects studies (half see A first, half see B first) or use a between-subjects design.

    🏛️ Scenario: CivicServe Government Services Portal

    CivicServe is a state portal for certificates, property tax, licences and complaints. Search logs show people search for "birth certificate" from the home page 9,000 times a month; the help desk gets 1,200 calls a month from people who can't find services. A new navigation is proposed.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Pick Methods

    Q. What should you run first to learn how citizens group services?
    • A A/B test
    • B Open card sort with ~40 services
    • C Heuristic review only
    • D Diary study

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Write Tree-Test Tasks

    ✍️ Write 3 tree-test tasks in citizens' language (don't reveal labels).
    Model answer: 1. "Your daughter was born last month. Get an official record of her birth." 2. "You received a notice saying you owe money for your house. Pay it." 3. "Street lights on your road have not worked for a week. Tell the city."

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Read Tree-Test Results

    TaskSuccessDirectnessTop wrong first click
    Birth record81%74%Health (12%)
    Pay house tax48%35%Payments (38%)
    Report street lights64%60%Utilities (21%)
    ✍️ What would you change, and why?
    Model answer: House tax is weakest: 38% first clicked "Payments", while it sits under "Property". Add "Property tax" under Payments (cross-link) or rename "Property" to "Property & house tax". Street lights: add a cross-link in Utilities. Retest the revised tree with the same tasks.

    4️⃣ Task 4 · Usability Test Plan

    ✍️ Plan a moderated usability test of the new portal: participants, tasks, metrics.
    Model answer: Participants: 8 citizens incl. 2 aged 60+, 2 who prefer Telugu/Hindi, 1 screen-reader user, mix of mobile/desktop. Tasks: get birth certificate, pay house tax, track a complaint, recover login. Metrics: success, time, SEQ per task, errors, SUS at end, severity-rated issues.

    5️⃣ Task 5 · Prioritise Findings

    Q. Which issue gets fixed first?
    • A Inconsistent icon sizes (8/8 noticed, no impact)
    • B OTP screen times out before older users finish typing (3/8 failed login)
    • C Footer colour
    • D Long page title

    Module 01 · Foundations

    What is Research Ops?

    Start from zero: what Research Operations is, why it matters, and the pillars that make research scalable.

    🧭 What is Research Ops (ReOps)?

    Research Operations is the people, processes and tools that make research faster, safer and repeatable. It handles everything around a study — recruiting, consent, incentives, data storage, tools and knowledge — so researchers can focus on the research itself.

    🔴 Without ReOps

    Every study starts from zero: hunting participants, rewriting consent forms, recordings scattered on laptops, insights lost.

    🟢 With ReOps

    Ready participant panel, approved templates, secure storage, one repository. A study is set up in days, not weeks.

    🏛️ The Pillars of ReOps

    👥
    Participants
    Recruitment, screening, scheduling, incentives, panels.
    ⚖️
    Governance
    Consent, privacy, ethics, data retention, legal.
    🧰
    Tools
    Testing, recording, analysis and repository platforms.
    📚
    Knowledge
    Repositories, templates, playbooks, training.

    📖 Key Terms

    RecruitmentFinding and booking the right participants.
    ScreenerQuestionnaire that filters participants by criteria.
    PanelA managed database of people who agreed to take part in research.
    IncentivePayment or reward for participating.
    Informed consentParticipant's voluntary agreement after understanding the study.
    PIIPersonally identifiable information: name, phone, email, face, voice.
    Data retentionHow long research data is kept before deletion.
    Research repositoryCentral, searchable home for research data and insights.
    Q. Which is a Research Ops responsibility?
    • A Writing production code
    • B Managing the participant panel and consent templates
    • C Setting product prices
    • D Designing logos

    User Recruitment

    Find, screen, schedule and reward the right participants — reliably.

    🎯 Recruitment Sources

    SourceProsCons
    Own customers (email, in-app)Real users, cheapNeeds consent; biased to active users
    Recruitment agenciesHard-to-reach audiences, fastExpensive
    Testing platforms' panels (UserTesting, Respondent, UserInterviews)Fast, globalProfessional testers risk
    Intercepts (pop-ups, in-store)In-the-momentShort sessions only
    Community / socialNiche groupsSelf-selection bias
    Customer-facing teams (sales, support)B2B accessGatekeeping, friendly-customer bias

    📝 Writing a Screener

    • Screen on behaviour: "Ordered groceries online in the last 30 days."
    • Hide the target: don't reveal the "right" answer.
    • Add exclusions: people working in UX, market research or competitors.
    • Include an articulation question (open text) to check participants can explain their thoughts.
    • Keep it under 10 questions.

    🔴 Leading screener

    "Do you use our app CartNow? Yes / No"

    🟢 Neutral screener

    "Which apps have you used to buy groceries in the last month? (select all) CartNow / BigBasket / Blinkit / Zepto / None"

    Module 03 · Panel Management

    Panel Management

    Build and maintain a healthy, diverse, compliant research panel.

    👥 What is a Research Panel?

    A panel is a database of people who have opted in to be contacted for research, with their profile attributes and participation history.

    Panel typeWhoUse
    Customer panelYour users who opted inMost studies
    Prospect panelPotential usersMarket and concept research
    Specialist panelDoctors, accountants, adminsB2B and expert studies
    Internal panelEmployees (only for limited pilots)Testing tools, not customer research

    🧱 What to Store

    FieldExampleNote
    Contact & consentEmail, opt-in date, consent versionMandatory
    ProfileCity tier, device, plan, tenureFor targeting
    Participation historyLast study date, studies countLimit frequency
    Incentive historyPaid amounts, datesFinance and tax
    PreferencesLanguage, preferred timesInclusion
    ⚠️
    Store only what you need (data minimisation). Never store sensitive data like health or finances in a panel unless essential and approved.

    🩺 Keeping the Panel Healthy

    • Frequency caps: e.g. no more than one study per participant per 3 months.
    • Refresh: recruit new members continuously; remove inactive ones.
    • Diversity checks: age, region, language, accessibility needs, device types.
    • Easy opt-out in every message.
    • Track response and show rates.
    Q. Why use frequency caps?
    • A To save money only
    • B To avoid over-researched, "professional" participants and fatigue
    • C To recruit faster
    • D Legal requirement in all cases

    Module 04 · Compliance & Ethics

    Compliance: GDPR, Privacy & Ethics

    Protect participants and the company: consent, data protection and ethical research practice.

    ⚖️ Core Ethical Principles

    🙋
    Respect
    Voluntary participation; right to stop anytime.
    🛡️
    Do no harm
    Avoid distress, embarrassment or risk.
    🔍
    Transparency
    Be clear about purpose, recording and data use.
    🔐
    Privacy
    Collect minimum data; protect it.

    📜 GDPR & Data-Protection Basics

    PrincipleWhat it means for research
    Lawful basis (often consent)Get clear, documented consent before collecting data
    Purpose limitationUse data only for the stated research purpose
    Data minimisationCollect only what you need
    Storage limitationDelete after the retention period (e.g. 12–24 months)
    Rights of individualsAccess, correction, deletion on request
    SecurityEncrypted storage, access control, no personal laptops
    International transfersCare when moving EU data outside the EU
    📌
    GDPR applies when researching people in the EU. In India, the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023 sets similar consent and data-handling duties. Always involve your legal/privacy team.

    ✍️ Informed Consent

    PurposeWhat the study is about and who runs it.
    ActivitiesWhat participants will do and for how long.
    RecordingAudio/video/screen recording and who will see it.
    Data use & retentionHow data is stored, used and when it is deleted.
    VoluntaryRight to skip questions or stop without losing the incentive.
    ContactWho to contact with questions or deletion requests.

    ⚠️ Special Situations

    SituationExtra care
    MinorsParental/guardian consent plus child's assent
    Health, finance, sensitive topicsMinimise data, extra security, trained moderators
    Vulnerable participantsAvoid distress; offer support resources
    Covert observationOnly public, anonymous behaviour; no recording identities
    Sharing clipsBlur faces/mask PII unless consent covers sharing
    Q. A participant asks to stop halfway. You should…
    • A Ask them to finish to get paid
    • B Stop immediately and still provide the incentive
    • C Keep recording
    • D Delete only half the data

    Module 06 · Interview Preparation

    Interview Preparation

    Click a question to reveal a model answer.

    👥 Recruitment & Panel Questions

    What is Research Ops?
    The people, processes and tools that make research faster, safer and repeatable: recruitment, panels, consent, data governance, tools and knowledge management.
    How do you write a screener?
    Screen on recent behaviour, hide the target answer, add exclusions (UX/market research, competitors), include an articulation question, keep it under 10 questions.
    How do you reduce no-shows?
    Over-recruit by ~20%, send reminders 24 hours and 1 hour before, offer fair incentives and make rescheduling easy.
    How do you keep a research panel healthy?
    Frequency caps, continuous refresh, diversity checks, data minimisation, easy opt-out and tracking participation history.

    ⚖️ Compliance & Ethics Questions

    What must informed consent include?
    Purpose, activities and duration, recording and who sees it, data use and retention, the right to stop without losing the incentive, and a contact for questions or deletion.
    How does GDPR affect UX research?
    For people in the EU: a lawful basis (usually consent), purpose limitation, data minimisation, retention limits, individual rights and secure storage. India has similar duties under the DPDP Act, 2023.
    A participant wants to stop halfway. What do you do?
    Stop immediately, thank them and still provide the incentive.
    How do you share session clips safely?
    Only within consent, with access control, blurring faces and masking personal data such as names, phone numbers or card details.

    🧰 Tools & Scaling Questions

    UserTesting vs Lookback vs Dovetail?
    UserTesting recruits and runs unmoderated (and live) tests; Lookback runs live moderated sessions with observers; Dovetail analyses, tags and stores research as a repository.
    How do you set up a research repository?
    A clear tag taxonomy (product area, journey stage, persona, sentiment), permissions, retention rules, atomic insights and links into roadmap docs.
    How do you democratise research safely?
    Templates, training, office hours and researcher review for simple studies; keep strategic and sensitive research with trained researchers.
    What ReOps metrics do you track?
    Time to recruit, no-show rate, incentive payout time, studies per quarter, repository reuse and compliance incidents.
    A designer posts an unblurred participant video publicly. What happens next?
    Remove it immediately, notify the privacy/legal team, follow the incident process and update training and permissions to prevent a repeat.

    💊 Scenario: MedTrack Medication Reminder App

    You need to interview 12 patients managing long-term medication (some aged 65+) and 4 caregivers in India and Germany, record sessions, and share findings with the product team. The company has no panel and no repository yet.

    1️⃣ Task 1 · Recruitment

    ✍️ Write 3 screener questions and 1 exclusion criterion.
    Model answer: Q1 "How many medicines do you take regularly?" (1 / 2–3 / 4+ ) Q2 "How do you remember to take them?" (multi-select incl. app, alarm, family reminds me, pill box) Q3 Open: "Describe a time you missed a dose." (articulation). Exclusion: works in healthcare IT, pharma marketing or UX research.

    2️⃣ Task 2 · Compliance

    Q. Which requirement applies to German participants?
    • A None
    • B GDPR — documented consent, minimisation, retention limits, care with transfers
    • C Only Indian law
    • D No consent needed for interviews
    ✍️ List 4 items your consent form must include for this study.
    Model answer: Purpose and organiser; recording (video/audio/screen) and who sees it; health data will be collected — how it's stored, protected and deleted (e.g. 12 months); right to stop or skip any question without losing the incentive; contact for questions/deletion requests.

    3️⃣ Task 3 · Tools

    ✍️ Choose tools for running, storing and sharing this research, and say why.
    Model answer: Run: Lookback for moderated remote sessions (observer room, mobile recording); phone calls for participants uncomfortable with video. Store & analyse: Dovetail with EU data residency, restricted project access, PII redaction, auto-delete after 12 months. Share: Dovetail highlight reels with faces blurred, insight pages linked in product docs.

    4️⃣ Task 4 · Panel

    ✍️ After the study, how would you start a panel from these participants?
    Model answer: Ask separately (opt-in) whether participants want to join the panel. Store only contact, language, device, condition category (not diagnosis details), participation dates and incentive history. Apply a 3-month frequency cap, easy opt-out, and annual re-consent.

    5️⃣ Task 5 · Incident

    Q. A designer uploaded an unblurred patient video to a public Slack channel. First step?
    • A Ignore it
    • B Remove it immediately, notify the privacy/legal team and follow the incident process
    • C Ask the patient for a review
    • D Add a blur later

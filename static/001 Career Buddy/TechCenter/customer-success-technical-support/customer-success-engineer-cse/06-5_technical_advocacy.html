    Module 01

    Technical Advocacy Basics

    Representing customer technical blockers directly in product roadmap discussions, with evidence, not volume.

    📣 What is Technical Advocacy?

    Technical advocacy means carrying customers' technical blockers into product and engineering conversations, backed by data. You are the bridge between what customers hit in production and what the roadmap decides.

    🗣️
    Voice of customer
    Real use cases and blockers from the field.
    📊
    Evidence
    Tickets, monitoring data, revenue at risk.
    ⚖️
    Prioritization
    Help product weigh value against cost.
    🔁
    Closing the loop
    Tell customers what happened.

    🛤️ The Advocacy Flow

    Figure 1.1 · From customer blocker to roadmap decision
    💡
    Product teams respond to evidence. One clear case with numbers beats ten urgent messages.

    ⚖️ Complaint vs Advocacy

    😤 Complaint

    • "This is broken, fix it now"
    • No data, no context
    • Creates defensiveness

    🎯 Advocacy

    • Problem, impact, evidence, ask
    • Customer context included
    • Creates collaboration

    Module 02

    Capturing Blockers

    Not every request is a blocker. Learn to recognize and record the ones that matter.

    🚧 What Counts as a Blocker

    TypeExampleSeverity
    Missing capabilityNo bulk export endpoint for a nightly syncHigh
    Platform limitRate limit too low for a legitimate workloadHigh
    Documentation gapWebhook retries undocumentedMedium
    Convenience requestWants a nicer dashboard colorLow

    📥 Where Blockers Come From

    • Support tickets and escalations
    • Customer calls and QBRs
    • Health monitoring data (repeated failures, workarounds)
    • Integration reviews
    📌
    A single ticket is a signal. The same pattern across several accounts is a case.

    Module 03

    Quantifying Impact

    Numbers let product compare your request with everything else on the list.

    📏 Impact Measures

    MeasureQuestion it answers
    Accounts affectedHow many customers hit this?
    Revenue at riskWhat is the business stake (ARR)?
    Ticket volumeHow much support does it consume?
    Engineering hoursHow much time do workarounds cost?
    Workaround costWhat does the customer spend to live with it?

    🧮 A Simple Impact Summary

    summaryBlocker: no bulk export endpoint
Accounts affected: 7 (3 enterprise)
ARR at risk: $420k
Tickets last quarter: 23
Workaround: 4 hrs/week manual paging per customer

    🤝 Stay Honest

    ⚠️
    Do not inflate numbers. If you are unsure, say "estimate". One exaggerated case costs you credibility on every future one.

    Module 04

    Writing the Case

    A good write-up is short, specific, and easy for product to act on.

    🧱 Structure of a Product Feedback Case

    1. Problem: what the customer cannot do.
    2. Context: who they are, why it matters.
    3. Evidence: tickets, metrics, quotes.
    4. Workaround: what they do today and its cost.
    5. Ask: the outcome needed, open to solutions.

    ✍️ Example Write-up

    caseTitle: Bulk export for orders
Problem: Customers cannot export more than 100 orders per call.
Context: 7 accounts run nightly syncs of 50k+ orders.
Evidence: 23 tickets, 31% of 429s in this segment.
Workaround: paging 500 calls per sync; 4 hrs/week to maintain.
Ask: a supported way to export large order sets reliably.

    ⚖️ Strong vs Weak

    ✅ Strong

    • Problem before solution
    • Numbers and examples
    • Clear, open ask

    ❌ Weak

    • "Customers are upset"
    • Only a feature name
    • Demands a deadline

    Module 05

    Roadmap Discussions

    Understand how product decides, and present so your case gets a fair hearing.

    🧭 How Product Prioritizes

    CriterionQuestion product asks
    Strategic fitDoes this support company goals?
    ReachHow many customers benefit?
    EffortHow costly is it to build?
    RiskWhat could go wrong?
    AlternativesIs there a cheaper way to solve it?

    🎤 Presenting Your Case

    1. Lead with the customer problem in one sentence.
    2. Show the data: accounts, revenue, tickets.
    3. Describe the workaround and its cost.
    4. State a clear, open ask.
    5. Ask what else product needs to decide.

    🙅 When the Answer is No

    💡
    Ask what would change the decision and whether a partial solution or workaround fits. Keep the item tracked so new evidence can reopen it.

    Module 06

    Closing the Loop

    Customers who hear back stay engaged, even when the answer is no.

    📬 Request Statuses

    StatusWhat to tell the customer
    LoggedWe recorded it and linked your evidence
    Under reviewProduct is evaluating it
    PlannedIt is on the roadmap; no date promised
    ShippedIt is available; here is how to use it
    DeclinedThe reason, and alternatives

    🛠️ Managing the Workaround

    Until a fix ships, give the customer a supported workaround, document it, and revisit it when the status changes.

    🚫 Never Over-Promise

    ⚠️
    Do not promise dates or features unless product has committed. A broken promise costs more trust than an honest "not yet".

    Module 07

    Scenarios and Interview Prep

    Two realistic advocacy cases, then the questions interviewers ask.

    📦 Scenario 1: Seven Customers Need Bulk Export

    1. Signal: repeated tickets and 429s from large syncs.
    2. Consolidate: merge into one tracked case with accounts, ARR, and hours.
    3. Present: problem, data, workaround cost, open ask.
    4. Close the loop: update all seven customers with the status.

    🙏 Scenario 2: Customer Demands a Declined Feature

    1. Understand the underlying need.
    2. Explain product's reasoning honestly.
    3. Offer an alternative or workaround.
    4. Keep tracking the request in case evidence changes.

    💬 Interview Questions

    How do you decide what to escalate to product?

    I escalate blockers that stop critical workflows or affect multiple accounts, with evidence of impact, and track lower-severity items without escalating.

    How do you handle a "no" from product?

    I learn the reasoning, look for alternatives, tell the customer honestly, and keep the item tracked for new evidence.

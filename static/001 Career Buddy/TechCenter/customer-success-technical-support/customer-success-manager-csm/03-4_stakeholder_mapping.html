    Module 01 · What Is What

    What Stakeholder Mapping Is

    Start with the basics: what a stakeholder is, why CSMs map them, and the types of stakeholder roles you'll find in every customer organisation.

    👥 What Is a Stakeholder?

    A stakeholder is anyone at the customer who affects, or is affected by, the use and renewal of your product: users, managers, executives, IT, finance, procurement.

    🗺️ What Is Stakeholder Mapping?

    Stakeholder mapping is identifying these people, understanding their role, influence, interests and attitude towards your product, and planning how to engage each of them.

    🔎
    Identify
    Who are the people that matter?
    🏷️
    Classify
    Role, power, interest, sentiment
    🎯
    Plan
    How to engage each one
    🔄
    Maintain
    Update as people and priorities change

    🗂️ Types of Stakeholder Roles

    RoleDescriptionExample title
    Economic buyerControls the budget; final yes/no on moneyCFO, VP, Department Head
    Decision-makerDecides whether to buy/renew (may be the economic buyer)VP Operations
    Executive sponsorSenior leader who supports the initiativeCOO, Director
    ChampionInternal advocate who sells for you when you're not thereOps Manager
    InfluencerShapes opinions without final authorityTeam leads, senior analysts
    End userUses the product dailyAgents, recruiters
    Admin / technical ownerConfigures and maintains itSystem admin, IT
    GatekeeperControls access to people or approvalsExecutive assistant, procurement
    DetractorNegative about your productUser who prefers a competitor
    BlockerActively prevents progressIT security lead refusing integration

    ⚖️ Champion vs Decision-maker vs Detractor

    ChampionDecision-makerDetractor
    AttitudePositive, advocatesVariesNegative
    PowerMedium (sometimes high)HighAny level
    What they need from youWins, recognition, tools to sell internallyBusiness outcomes, ROI, low riskTo be heard; concerns addressed
    RiskLeaving the companyNot seeing valueInfluencing others against you

    ❤️ Why It Matters for Retention

    • Renewals are decided by people, often ones the CSM never met.
    • Knowing the decision-maker early avoids surprises.
    • Strong champions protect accounts during budget reviews.
    • Unmanaged detractors can quietly push for a competitor.
    • Maps reveal single-threading risk before a champion leaves.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Assuming your contact is the decision-makerRenewal surprisesAsk who approves and signs
    Mapping only friendly peopleMiss detractorsMap everyone who matters
    Map created once, never updatedOut of date after reorgsReview quarterly

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Classify these people at a customer: (a) CFO who approves the renewal budget; (b) Ops manager who loves the product and trains her team; (c) IT lead who refuses to allow the integration; (d) 40 agents who use it daily.
    (a) Economic buyer/decision-maker. (b) Champion. (c) Blocker. (d) End users.
    Practice
    P2.Why is "my champion is the decision-maker" a dangerous assumption?
    Champions often recommend but don't control budget or sign. The real decision may involve a VP, CFO or procurement who you haven't engaged, causing surprises at renewal.

    Identifying Stakeholders in a Customer Organisation

    Where to find the people who matter: sales handoff notes, CRM, product data, LinkedIn, org charts and, above all, the right questions.

    🔎 Sources of Information

    SourceWhat it tells you
    Sales handoff / CRMWho signed, who was in the deal, objections raised
    Product usage dataPower users, admins, inactive teams
    Meeting attendanceWho shows up, who brings others
    Support ticketsWho reports issues; who is frustrated
    LinkedIn / company websiteTitles, reporting lines, recent hires and departures
    ConversationsInfluence, politics, priorities (the richest source)

    ❓ Questions That Reveal Stakeholders

    GoalQuestion
    Decision process"When renewal comes up, who else is involved in the decision?"
    Budget"Whose budget does this sit in?"
    Influence"Whose opinion carries weight with your VP on tools like this?"
    Risks"Is anyone sceptical about the platform? What are their concerns?"
    Expansion"Which other teams face similar challenges?"
    Change"Are there any upcoming changes in the team or leadership?"
    💡
    Ask champions to help you map: "Could you help me understand who I should be keeping informed?" Most are happy to.

    🏢 Reading Org Structures

    🔐 Ethics & Privacy

    • Record only professional information relevant to the business relationship.
    • Keep notes respectful; assume customers could read them.
    • Follow data-protection rules (GDPR, DPDP) and company CRM policies.

    It lacks decision-makers, economic buyer and executive sponsor. Ask the champion/admin for introductions, request an exec check-in or EBR, involve your AM/exec sponsor, and use LinkedIn/org info to understand the leadership structure.

    Module 04 · Champions

    Finding, Testing & Developing Champions

    A champion sells for you when you're not in the room. Learn what makes a true champion, how to test one, how to develop them, and how to protect against champion loss.

    ⭐ What Makes a Champion?

    TraitWhy it matters
    Believes in the valueAdvocates sincerely
    Has influenceOthers listen to them
    Has personal winTheir success is tied to yours (goals, promotion)
    Gives accessIntroduces you to other stakeholders
    Shares inside informationWarns you about risks and changes
    📌
    A friendly contact who likes you is a coach or supporter. A true champion has influence and acts for you.

    🗂️ Types of Champions

    TypeDescription
    Executive championSenior leader advocating at leadership level
    Operational championManager running the day-to-day rollout
    Technical championAdmin/IT owner who ensures the platform works well
    User championRespected power user who helps peers

    🧪 Testing a Champion

    TestReal champion…
    Ask for an introduction to an executiveMakes it happen
    Ask about the renewal processShares details openly
    Ask them to present results internallyAgrees and does it
    Ask about risksTells you honestly, even bad news
    ⚠️
    If your "champion" never gives access or information, they may be a friendly contact without influence. Keep searching.

    🌱 Developing Champions

    • Help them look good: share wins they can present to their boss.
    • Give them early access, advanced training, certification.
    • Provide ready-made materials: ROI one-pager, slides for internal meetings.
    • Invite them to customer advisory boards, community events, case studies.
    • Recognise them publicly (with permission).

    👋 When Your Champion Leaves

    1. Thank them; ask for a handover meeting and introductions to the successor.
    2. Flag the account as at-risk and update the map.
    3. Meet the successor quickly: restate goals and value in their terms.
    4. Re-engage the sponsor; re-onboard the new owner if needed.
    5. Stay in touch with the departing champion; they may bring you into their next company.
    ✅
    Departing champions are a common source of new customers: they buy again at their next job.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Mistaking friendliness for championshipFalse confidenceTest for influence and action
    One champion onlyHigh risk if they leaveDevelop multiple champions
    Not helping champions succeedTheir motivation fadesGive them wins and materials

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Your contact is enthusiastic but never introduces you to anyone and says "I don't know" about the renewal. Is this a champion? What do you do?
    Probably a supporter/coach, not a true champion (no influence or access). Keep a good relationship, but look for others: team leads, managers, the sponsor. Ask directly for introductions and use your AM/exec sponsor to reach decision-makers.
    Practice
    P2.List three things you can give a champion to help them sell your value internally.
    An ROI/value one-pager with their numbers; a short slide deck for their leadership meeting; customer success stories/benchmarks from similar companies (plus recognition, e.g., a case study or advisory board invite).

    Module 05 · Decision Process

    Decision-Makers & the Buying Process

    Understand who really decides: economic buyers, the decision-making unit, approval processes, procurement and budget cycles.

    🏛️ Who Decides?

    RoleTypical question they ask
    Economic buyer"Is this worth the money compared with other priorities?"
    Decision-maker"Does this achieve our goals with acceptable risk?"
    Technical approver"Is it secure, integrated, supportable?"
    Legal/procurement"Are the terms acceptable? Is the process followed?"
    User buyer"Will my team use it and does it make work easier?"

    👥 The Decision-Making Unit (DMU)

    In B2B, decisions are usually made by a group called the Decision-Making Unit (or buying committee): initiator, users, influencers, deciders, approvers, buyers and gatekeepers.

    DMU example (renewal)Initiator:     Ops Manager (champion) – raises renewal
Users:         80 agents – usage data and feedback
Influencers:   Team leads, IT lead
Decider:       VP Operations
Approver:      VP Finance (budget), Legal
Buyer:         Procurement – PO and contract
Gatekeeper:    VP's executive assistant

    🗺️ Mapping the Approval Process

    1. Ask the champion to walk you through the approval steps.
    2. List each step, owner and typical duration (finance review, legal, security, PO).
    3. Work back from the renewal date to set deadlines.
    4. Identify blockers early (security questionnaires, budget freeze).
    5. Share the timeline with the AM and champion.
    approval timeline (work back from 31 Mar)15 Jan  Value review with VP Ops (decider)
31 Jan  Business case to VP Finance (approver)
15 Feb  Security questionnaire (IT)
28 Feb  Legal review of order form
15 Mar  PO raised by procurement
31 Mar  Renewal effective

    👔 Engaging Decision-Makers

    • Speak their language: outcomes, ROI, risk (see Executive Presence).
    • Ask about their priorities, not your product.
    • Use your executive sponsor for peer-level access.
    • Bring a short, data-backed value summary.
    • Make it easy: clear recommendation and next step.

    💰 Budget Cycles & Procurement

    FactorWhy it matters
    Fiscal year (e.g., Apr–Mar in India)Budgets set months earlier
    Budget ownerThe cost may sit with a different department
    Procurement policiesVendor registration, competitive bids, approvals
    Security / compliance reviewsCan add weeks
    Signing authorityAbove certain amounts, a CXO must sign

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Engaging decision-makers only at renewalNo relationship or trustRegular executive touchpoints
    Unknown approversLast-minute delays or "no"Map the DMU early
    Ignoring the budget cycleMissed budget windowsKnow their fiscal year

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Map the DMU for a renewal where the champion is a Support Manager, the Head of CX approves, Finance owns the budget and IT must approve integrations.
    Initiator/champion: Support Manager. Users: agents. Influencers: team leads, IT. Decider: Head of CX. Approver: Finance (budget), IT (technical), Legal. Buyer: procurement. Gatekeeper: possibly the Head of CX's assistant or procurement.
    Practice
    P2.Renewal is 31 March and the customer's fiscal year starts 1 April. Why does this matter?

    Scenario: Indian enterprise customer.

    Next year's budget is likely being finalised in January–February. The renewal and any expansion must be in that budget, so present the business case to finance before budgets lock.

    Module 06 · Detractors

    Handling Detractors & Blockers

    Detractors are inevitable. Learn the types and causes of detractors, how to engage them, convert or neutralise their influence, and when to escalate.

    🗂️ Types of Detractors

    TypeDescription
    Vocal criticOpenly complains about the product
    Silent detractorDoesn't engage; quietly prefers alternatives
    Competitor loyalistUsed/likes a competitor; may push to switch
    Change resisterPrefers old process; resists any new tool
    BlockerHas power to stop progress (IT, security, procurement)

    🤔 Why People Become Detractors

    CauseExample
    Bad experienceOutage hit their team; slow support
    Product gapMissing feature they need
    Not involved in the decisionTool chosen without them
    Extra workNew tool added steps to their job
    Loyalty to another vendorChampioned a competitor before
    Personal/politicalTheir rival championed your product

    👂 Engaging a Detractor

    1. Reach out directly and respectfully; ask for 20 minutes.
    2. Listen fully: "What has your experience been? What would make it better?"
    3. Acknowledge valid concerns without being defensive.
    4. Agree specific actions and timelines; deliver them.
    5. Follow up and show progress; involve them in solutions (pilot, feedback).
    OpeningI heard you have concerns about how the platform fits your team's workflow. I'd really value 20 minutes to understand what's not working. You know the process better than anyone.

    🔁 Converting vs Neutralising

    🔄 Convert

    Fix their issue, involve them in decisions, give them a win. Many detractors become strong champions once heard and helped.

    🛡️ Neutralise

    If conversion isn't possible: reduce their influence by strengthening supporters, showing data and outcomes to decision-makers, and addressing objections in advance.

    ⚠️
    Never go around or undermine a detractor behind their back; it usually backfires politically. Be transparent and focus on facts.

    🔐 Blockers in Technical & Procurement Roles

    BlockerTypical concernApproach
    IT / SecurityData security, integrations, complianceInvolve your security team; provide certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001), answer questionnaires
    ProcurementPrice, terms, vendor policyAM leads; clear value case; flexible terms within policy
    LegalContract terms, liabilityYour legal team; standard templates
    FinanceBudget, ROIFinance-language ROI; phased options

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Avoiding detractorsTheir influence growsEngage early
    Arguing with themEntrenches oppositionListen, acknowledge, act
    Promising fixes you can't deliverTurns them hostileCommit to what you control

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.A team lead tells colleagues "this tool is useless" after an outage hit his team. How do you engage him?

    Scenario: Vocal detractor with influence over 20 users.

    Request a 1:1; listen and acknowledge the outage impact; share the RCA and fixes; ask what would rebuild confidence; offer a tailored session for his team or involve him in testing improvements; follow up regularly; if he improves, ask him for feedback publicly.
    Practice
    P2.An IT security lead blocks an integration that the customer's VP wants. What do you do?
    Understand the specific concerns; bring your security/solutions engineer; provide certifications, architecture and data-flow documents; answer the questionnaire; propose safeguards (least-privilege access, IP allowlisting); keep the VP informed of progress without bypassing IT.

    Stakeholder Engagement Plans & Multi-threading

    Turn the map into action: an engagement plan for each stakeholder, multi-threading across levels, keeping maps current, and recording them in CS platforms.

    🧭 From Map to Plan

    For each key stakeholder decide: goal (what you want from the relationship), message (what matters to them), channel & cadence, and owner on your side.

    📋 Engagement Plan Template

    engagement planStakeholder      Goal                     Message focus             Cadence           Owner

    🕸️ Multi-threading Strategy

    LevelYour counterpartCustomer counterpart
    ExecutiveVP CS / exec sponsorCOO, VP Ops, VP Finance
    ManagementCSM, AMOps Manager, department heads
    OperationalCSM, support, solutions engineerAdmins, team leads, power users
    💡
    Aim for at least 3 strong relationships across at least 2 levels in every key account.

    🔄 Keeping the Map Current

    • Update after every QBR and major interaction.
    • Watch LinkedIn for role changes and departures.
    • Review the map quarterly and before renewal planning.
    • Treat any change in key roles as a trigger for an engagement action.

    Practice

    Stakeholder: CFO. Goal: neutral → supportive before renewal. Message: ROI, cost savings, payback. Cadence: intro meeting this quarter, then twice-yearly value summary. Owner: AM + exec sponsor; CSM prepares data.
    Practice
    P2.An account has strong relationships only at the operational level. What's the risk and the plan?

    Scenario: Single-level relationships.

    Risk: decisions at executive level happen without you; budget cuts or vendor reviews surprise you. Plan: ask champion for exec intros, run an EBR with your exec sponsor, send leadership value summaries, map finance/procurement.

    Module 08 · Real-World Workflow

    Real-World Stakeholder Scenarios

    The end-to-end stakeholder workflow and worked scenarios: reorganisations, hidden decision-makers and political situations.

    🔁 End-to-End Workflow

    Identify stakeholders → Classify (role, power, interest, sentiment) → Map gaps & risks → Engagement plan → Execute touchpoints → Update map → Use before renewals & expansions

    🔀 Scenario: Reorganisation

    • The customer merges two departments; your sponsor now reports to a new VP.
    • Redraw the org map; identify the new decision-maker and their priorities.
    • Ask the sponsor for an intro; prepare a value summary for the new VP.
    • Flag risk; review the engagement plan.

    🕵️ Scenario: Hidden Decision-maker

    • At renewal, procurement says "the CIO must approve all tools over ₹50 L", whom you've never met.
    • Lesson: map the DMU and signing authority early.
    • Now: ask the champion for the CIO's priorities; prepare security/architecture and ROI material; use exec sponsor for a meeting.

    ⚔️ Scenario: Competing Champions

    • Two managers both want to own the platform and disagree on configuration.
    • Stay neutral; focus on shared business objectives.
    • Facilitate a joint session with the sponsor to agree ownership (e.g., RACI).
    • Document decisions in the success plan.

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Your champion is promoted to a role unrelated to your product. Write your next steps.
    Congratulate them; ask for a handover and introductions to the new owner; keep them as an executive ally if appropriate (they now have more influence); update the map; onboard the new owner with goals and value; review risk.
    Practice
    P2.Draw (in text) a simple stakeholder map for an account and identify the biggest gap.
    Example: VP Ops (decider, high power, neutral, weak relationship); Ops Manager (champion, +2, strong); VP Finance (economic buyer, −1, none); IT Lead (technical, 0, weak); team leads (+1, good). Biggest gap: no relationship with the sceptical VP Finance, who controls budget.

    • D Close account
    Q2.With competing champions, a CSM should…
    • A Pick a side
    • B Stay neutral and align on shared objectives
    • C Ignore both
    • D Escalate to legal

    Module 09 · Interviews

    Interview Preparation: Stakeholder Mapping

    CSM interview questions on champions, decision-makers, detractors and stakeholder strategy, with model answers, comparisons, common mistakes and scenarios.

    🎤 Concept Questions

    QuestionModel answer
    What is stakeholder mapping?Identifying people who influence adoption and renewal, classifying their role, power, interest and sentiment, and planning engagement.
    How do you identify the decision-maker?Ask about the decision and approval process, budget owner and signing authority; validate with multiple contacts.
    What makes a true champion?Believes in the value, has influence, gives access and information, and acts for you internally.
    How do you handle a detractor?Listen, acknowledge, act on valid concerns, involve them; if not convertible, strengthen supporters and show outcomes to decision-makers.
    What is multi-threading?Building relationships with several people across levels to avoid single points of failure.
    What is a DMU?The group involved in a buying decision: initiators, users, influencers, deciders, approvers, buyers, gatekeepers.
    How do you use a power–interest grid?Place stakeholders by power and interest to decide engagement: manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed, monitor.

    Module 01 · What Is What

    Customer Success & the CSM Role

    Start from the beginning: what Customer Success is, what a Customer Success Manager does, how it differs from Support, Sales and Account Management, and the types of CS engagement models.

    🌱 What Is Customer Success?

    Customer Success (CS) is the practice of making sure customers achieve the outcomes they bought a product for. In subscription (SaaS) businesses, customers can leave at every renewal, so the company only grows if customers keep getting value.

    🛠️ Customer Support

    Reactive: helps when the customer reports a problem. Measured by response time and resolution.

    🌱 Customer Success

    Proactive: reaches out to drive adoption and outcomes before problems happen. Measured by retention, expansion and health.

    💡
    A simple definition for interviews: "Customer Success makes sure customers get the value they paid for, so they renew and grow."

    👤 What Does a CSM Do?

    🤝
    Relationships
    Builds trust with users, champions and executives.
    🎯
    Outcomes
    Turns business goals into an adoption plan.
    📈
    Adoption
    Drives usage of the right features by the right people.
    ❤️
    Health
    Monitors health scores and spots risk early.
    🔁
    Renewals
    Leads or supports contract renewals.
    🚀
    Expansion
    Finds upsell/cross-sell opportunities with Sales.
    📌
    The set of accounts a CSM manages is called their book of business, often measured in number of accounts and total ARR (annual recurring revenue).

    ⚖️ CSM vs Support vs Account Manager

    Support Rep / TSECustomer Success ManagerAccount Manager / Account Executive
    Main goalFix issuesDrive outcomes, adoption, retentionSell: new deals, upsells, renewals (commercial)
    ApproachReactiveProactiveCommercial
    Talks toUsers reporting issuesUsers, champions, executivesBuyers, procurement, executives
    Measured byFRT, CSAT, resolutionHealth, GRR/NRR, adoptionQuota, bookings
    Typical meetingsTroubleshooting callsOnboarding, check-ins, QBRsNegotiations, pricing
    📌
    In some companies CSMs own renewals and expansion ("commercial CSM"); in others, Account Managers own the money and CSMs own value and adoption. Always ask which model a company uses.

    🧭 Types of Engagement Models

    ModelAccounts per CSMHow engagement happensTypical customers
    High-touch5–20Dedicated CSM, regular meetings, onsite, QBRsEnterprise, high ARR
    Mid-touch30–100Mix of meetings and automated programmesMid-market
    Low / tech-touchHundreds–thousands (pooled)Automated emails, in-app guides, webinars, communitySMB, self-serve
    Digital / scaled CSPooled teamData-driven journeys; humans on risk triggersLarge long-tail bases
    💡
    Segmentation is usually by ARR, potential, complexity and strategic value. The higher the value, the more human time each account gets.

    Module 02 · Relationships

    Building Strong Customer Relationships

    Trust is the foundation of retention. Learn what makes a strong relationship, types of customer meetings, communication cadence, multi-threading, and how to stay credible when things go wrong.

    🤝 What Is a Strong Relationship?

    A strong customer relationship means the customer sees you as a trusted advisor: someone who understands their business, tells the truth, and helps them succeed, not just a vendor contact.

    LevelHow the customer sees youExample
    1. Vendor contact"The person from the software company"Only contacted for invoices
    2. Helpful resource"Good at answering questions"Solves how-to issues
    3. Partner"Understands our goals"Joins planning; brings best practices
    4. Trusted advisor"Part of our team; we ask their opinion"Consulted on strategy beyond the product
    💡
    Trust formula often used: Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) ÷ Self-orientation. Be knowledgeable, keep promises, understand them personally, and focus on their success, not your quota.

    📅 Types of Customer Meetings

    MeetingPurposeWhoWhen
    KickoffAlign on goals, success criteria, planChampion, project team, sponsorStart of onboarding
    Check-in / syncProgress, blockers, adoptionChampion, adminsWeekly → monthly
    Training / enablementTeach features and best practicesEnd users, adminsAs needed
    QBR / EBRStrategic review of value and next goalsExecutives + championQuarterly / twice a year
    Executive check-inRelationship at leadership levelExec sponsor ↔ customer execQuarterly or at key moments
    Renewal discussionCommercial terms, future plansDecision-makers, procurement90–120 days before renewal
    Escalation / recoveryFix serious problems; rebuild trustExecs on both sidesWhen issues arise

    ⏰ Communication Cadence

    Segment / stageCadence example
    Onboarding (any segment)Weekly calls until go-live
    High-touch steady stateFortnightly or monthly check-ins + quarterly QBR
    Mid-touchMonthly email/call + twice-yearly review
    Tech-touchAutomated monthly newsletter, in-app tips, webinars
    At-risk accountWeekly, with an agreed recovery plan
    • Send a recap email after every meeting: decisions, owners, dates.
    • Prefer value-adding touches ("here's a benchmark for your team") over "just checking in".
    • Log every interaction in the CS platform/CRM so anyone can see the history.

    🕸️ Multi-threading

    Multi-threading means building relationships with several people at the customer, across levels and departments, so the account doesn't depend on one person.

    🧵 Single-threaded

    Only one champion. If they leave or change role, the CSM loses visibility and the renewal is at risk.

    🕸️ Multi-threaded

    Champion + admin + end-user leads + department head + executive sponsor. Losing one contact doesn't break the account.

    ⚠️
    Champion departure is one of the top churn risk signals. Multi-thread before it happens.

    🛡️ Trust When Things Go Wrong

    1. Acknowledge the problem quickly and honestly; don't hide or minimise it.
    2. Own the follow-up even if another team must fix it.
    3. Share a clear plan with owners and dates.
    4. Over-communicate progress until resolved.
    5. Afterwards, review what happened and what changes prevent repeats.
    ✅
    Customers often trust a CSM more after a well-handled problem than if nothing had gone wrong.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    "Just checking in" emailsNo value; get ignoredBring an insight, tip or benchmark
    No meeting recapsDecisions forgottenRecap with owners and dates
    Single-threaded accountsChampion leaves → riskMap and engage multiple stakeholders
    OverpromisingBroken trustCommit only to what you control

    Practice
    P2.Your only contact at a key account just announced she is leaving. What do you do this week?

    Scenario: Champion departure.

    Thank her and ask for introductions to her successor and other key people; get a handover meeting; review the stakeholder map and contact other users/admins/managers; schedule an intro with the new owner to restate goals and value; flag the account as at-risk in the CS platform and inform your manager/AM.

    Module 03 · Executive Presence

    Executive Presence for CSMs

    Executive presence is how credibly you show up with senior leaders. Learn its components, how to speak in business outcomes, prepare for executive conversations, and handle tough questions with composure.

    👔 What Is Executive Presence?

    Executive presence is the ability to communicate with senior leaders in a way that is confident, concise, credible and focused on what matters to them.

    ComponentWhat it looks like
    GravitasCalm confidence; you know the account and the business
    CommunicationShort, structured, outcome-focused; no rambling
    CredibilityFacts and data; honest about problems
    ComposureSteady under pressure or criticism
    Business acumenUnderstands their industry, goals, metrics
    Appearance & punctualityProfessional, prepared, on time, tech checked

    💼 Speaking the Executive Language

    Executives care about outcomes (revenue, cost, risk, speed, customer experience), not features.

    Feature language (avoid)Executive language (use)
    "We launched a new dashboard filter.""Your managers now find late shipments 40% faster, reducing penalty costs."
    "Adoption is 72% DAU/MAU.""Seven in ten of your team use the platform daily, so the process change is sticking."
    "Ticket volume went down.""Support costs per order dropped 18% this quarter."
    "We need you to use feature X.""Teams that automate approvals cut cycle time by a third; here's a plan for yours."
    💡
    Use the "So what?" test: after every statement, ask "so what does this mean for their business?" and say that instead.

    📋 Preparing for Executive Meetings

    1. Know the executive: role, priorities, recent company news, LinkedIn background.
    2. Know the account: goals, health, usage, value delivered, open issues, renewal date.
    3. Define your one objective for the meeting (e.g., agree on next-year goals).
    4. Prepare a 1-page summary or 3–5 slides maximum.
    5. Align internally: your exec sponsor, AM, support on open issues.
    6. Anticipate tough questions and prepare honest answers.

    🗣️ Communication Techniques

    TechniqueHow
    BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front)Start with the conclusion, then details if asked
    Rule of threeGroup points into three (wins, risks, asks)
    Pyramid principleMain message → supporting points → data
    Data + storyOne metric plus one real example from their team
    Clear askEnd with a specific decision or support you need
    Listen moreAsk about their priorities; let them talk
    BLUF openingIn short: you're on track to hit the 30% faster-onboarding goal by December. Two things would get you there sooner: SSO rollout and training the Pune team. I'd like your help unblocking IT for SSO.

    🔥 Handling Tough Questions

    QuestionStrong response approach
    "Why should we renew when usage is low?"Acknowledge; show where value exists; present a concrete adoption plan with dates
    "Your product had two outages this quarter."Own it; share root cause summary, fixes and SLA commitments
    "A competitor is cheaper."Focus on outcomes delivered, switching cost, roadmap fit; involve AM for commercial terms
    Question you can't answer"I don't want to guess. I'll confirm by Thursday."
    ⚠️
    Never bluff with executives. One wrong answer damages credibility more than "I'll find out".

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Feature tours for executivesWastes their timeTalk outcomes and decisions
    30-slide decksLose attention3–5 slides, BLUF
    Hiding bad newsDestroys trust when discoveredShare proactively with a plan
    No clear askMeeting ends with no progressEnd with a specific request

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Rewrite this for a CFO: "We released bulk invoice upload and your team uploaded 3,000 invoices."
    "Your finance team processed 3,000 invoices in bulk this quarter instead of one by one, saving roughly 120 hours of manual work, about ₹4.8 lakh in staff time, and month-end closing finished two days earlier."
    Practice
    P2.A VP asks in a QBR: "Why are we paying for 500 seats when only 210 are active?" How do you respond?

    Scenario: Executive challenge on value.

    Acknowledge directly ("That's a fair question"). Show where the 210 active users get value (results/metrics). Explain why the rest aren't active (teams not onboarded, SSO pending). Present a dated plan to activate key teams with owners. Offer to revisit seat count at renewal if adoption doesn't improve, which builds trust. Bring the AM in for commercial discussion.

    Module 04 · QBRs

    Quarterly Business Reviews: The Basics

    What a QBR is, why it matters, its types (QBR, EBR, MBR), who should attend, and the standard agenda structure.

    📊 What Is a QBR?

    A Quarterly Business Review (QBR) is a strategic meeting, usually every three months, where the CSM and the customer review the value delivered, progress against goals, and agree on priorities for the next period.

    📌
    A QBR is not a product demo or a support review. It's a business conversation about outcomes.

    🗂️ Types of Business Reviews

    TypeFrequencyAudienceFocus
    MBR (Monthly)MonthlyChampion, project teamOperational progress, adoption
    QBR (Quarterly)QuarterlyChampion + leadershipValue, goals, roadmap fit
    EBR (Executive)Twice a year / yearlyExecutives on both sidesStrategy, partnership, renewal direction
    Success plan reviewMilestone-basedProject ownersSpecific goal milestones
    💡
    Many companies now call quarterly reviews EBRs to emphasise executive and business focus. The name matters less than the content.

    🎯 Why QBRs Matter

    💰
    Prove value
    Show ROI before renewal conversations.
    🧭
    Align goals
    Make sure you're working on what matters to them now.
    👔
    Executive access
    Build relationships beyond the champion.
    ⚠️
    Surface risk
    Hear concerns early.
    🚀
    Find growth
    Identify new teams or use cases.

    👥 Who Attends

    Customer sideYour side
    Executive sponsor / decision-makerCSM (owner of the meeting)
    Champion / project ownerAccount Manager (if commercial topics)
    Key department leadsExec sponsor (for strategic accounts)
    Admin (sometimes)Solutions engineer / product expert (if needed)
    ⚠️
    If the decision-maker never attends your QBRs, you don't really have executive alignment, a risk at renewal time.

    🧱 The Standard QBR Structure

    Figure 4.1 · Look back → Insights → Look ahead → Agree
    Agenda itemTime (60 min)
    Objectives & agenda3 min
    Goals recap & results vs goals12 min
    Value/ROI story + adoption insights12 min
    Customer priorities for next quarter (they speak!)12 min
    Recommendations & roadmap fit10 min
    Agreed success plan, owners, next steps8 min
    Feedback3 min

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Presenting usage stats onlyNo business meaningTie metrics to their goals
    CSM talks 90% of the timeMiss customer prioritiesLeave at least a third for their input
    Same deck for every customerFeels genericCustomise to their goals and data
    No next stepsNo progressEnd with owners and dates

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Draft a 60-minute QBR agenda for a logistics customer whose goal is to reduce late deliveries by 20%.
    Objectives (3); recap goal and results: late deliveries down 12% so far (12); value story: alerts helped dispatchers act 30 min earlier, plus a customer quote (12); their priorities next quarter, e.g., expanding to two new hubs (12); recommendations: route-optimisation feature and training for night shift (10); success plan with owners/dates to reach 20% by Q4 (8); feedback (3).
    Practice
    P2.Your champion attends QBRs but the VP never does. Why is this a problem and what will you do?
    Without the VP, value isn't visible to the decision-maker and renewal/expansion depend on one champion. Ask the champion what the VP cares about, create a short executive summary, propose a 20-minute EBR with your exec sponsor, and send a one-page value summary to the VP.

    Module 05 · QBRs in Practice

    Preparing, Running & Following Up on QBRs

    How expert CSMs build a QBR: gathering data, crafting the value story, designing a deck, running the meeting, and turning it into an agreed success plan.

    📥 Data to Gather

    SourceData
    Product analyticsActive users, feature adoption, trends, licence utilisation
    CS platform (Gainsight/ChurnZero…)Health score history, success plan progress, risks
    SupportTicket volume, top issues, CSAT, open escalations
    CustomerTheir KPIs before/after (time saved, revenue, error rates), quotes
    CommercialContract, renewal date, seats, products owned
    BenchmarksHow similar customers perform
    💡
    Send a short pre-QBR survey or call to the champion: "What should we focus on? What changed in your business?" It makes the meeting about them.

    📖 Building the Value Story

    Structure the story as Goal → Action → Result → Next:

    value story exampleGoal:    Reduce invoice processing time by 30% (set at kickoff, Jan)
Action:  Automated approval workflows rolled out to 4 regional teams
Result:  Processing time 6.2 → 3.9 days (−37%); 1,100 hours saved per quarter
         "Month-end close is no longer a weekend job." – Finance Lead, Pune
Next:    Extend to vendor onboarding; target −25% onboarding time by Q2
    ROI typeExample
    Time savedHours × hourly cost
    Revenue gainedFaster sales cycle, higher conversion
    Cost avoidedFewer errors, penalties, tools replaced
    Risk reducedCompliance, security, audit readiness

    🖼️ Designing the Deck

    SlideContent
    1. Title & agendaMeeting objective in one line
    2. Executive summary3 wins, 1–2 risks, 1 ask (BLUF)
    3. Goals vs resultsTable of goals, targets, actuals, status
    4. Value & ROIThe value story with one chart
    5. Adoption insightsWho uses what; gaps; benchmarks
    6. Their priorities (discussion)Questions, not answers
    7. Recommendations & roadmapWhat to do next; relevant upcoming features
    8. Success plan & next stepsOwners, dates, next review
    📌
    Keep it to 8–10 slides and put detailed data in an appendix.

    🎤 Running the Meeting

    1. Start on time; state the objective and confirm agenda.
    2. Give the executive summary in 2 minutes.
    3. Ask open questions: "What are your top priorities next quarter?" "What would make this a great year?"
    4. Listen and take notes; adapt the agenda if a bigger topic appears.
    5. Handle concerns honestly; take detailed issues offline.
    6. Close by reading back agreed actions, owners and dates.

    📮 Follow-up

    • Recap email within 24 hours with the deck, decisions and action items.
    • Update the success plan and CRM/CS platform.
    • Create tasks for every action; share internally (AM, support, product feedback).
    • Send feature requests to product with the customer's business context.
    • Book the next review.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Building the deck the night beforeWeak data, no customer inputStart 2 weeks ahead
    Vanity metrics (logins)No business value shownOutcome metrics tied to goals
    No customer quotesStory feels abstractInclude their words
    No follow-upAgreements forgottenRecap within 24 h

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Write a value story (Goal → Action → Result → Next) for an HR platform customer whose goal was to reduce time-to-hire.

    Facts: time-to-hire 42 → 31 days; interview scheduling automated for 3 teams; 600 recruiter hours saved; next: campus hiring season.

    Goal: reduce time-to-hire by 20%. Action: automated interview scheduling for Engineering, Sales and Support hiring teams. Result: time-to-hire fell from 42 to 31 days (−26%) and saved ~600 recruiter hours this quarter. Next: apply the same workflow to campus hiring, targeting 30% faster offers by March.
    Practice
    P2.List 6 data points you would collect two weeks before a QBR.
    Licence utilisation and active-user trend; adoption of key features tied to goals; health score history; support tickets and CSAT; progress on success-plan milestones; customer KPIs/ROI and quotes; renewal date and commercial context.

    Module 06 · Executive Relationships

    Executive Check-ins & Executive Sponsorship

    Executive check-ins keep leadership on both sides aligned between QBRs. Learn executive sponsor programmes, how to run a 20-minute check-in, and how to involve executives in escalations.

    👔 What Is an Executive Check-in?

    An executive check-in is a short, focused conversation with a customer executive (VP, Director, CXO) to confirm priorities, share progress and keep the relationship strong. It's lighter than a QBR and often happens between them.

    Executive check-inQBR / EBR
    Length15–30 minutes45–90 minutes
    Prep1-page briefFull deck and data
    FocusPriorities, relationship, one askFull review and planning
    FrequencyQuarterly or at key momentsQuarterly / twice a year

    🤝 Executive Sponsor Programmes

    Many companies pair each strategic customer with an executive sponsor from their own leadership team (e.g., a VP of Engineering or CRO).

    • Peer-to-peer relationship: their exec talks to our exec.
    • Helps with escalations, roadmap influence and renewal confidence.
    • The CSM orchestrates: briefs both sides, sets agendas, follows up.

    ⏱️ Running a 20-Minute Check-in

    20-minute check-in0–3   Thank them; purpose of the call
3–8   "What are your top priorities this quarter? What has changed?"
8–13  One-slide progress: 2 wins, 1 risk, plan
13–17 Their feedback: "How are we doing as a partner?"
17–20 Agree one action / ask; confirm next touchpoint
    💡
    The most valuable output is often what you learn: new initiatives, budget changes, reorganisations, competitor evaluations.

    🚨 Executives in Escalations

    1. Escalate when: serious outage, repeated failures, renewal at risk, unresolved critical issue.
    2. Brief your executive: facts, timeline, impact, what's been done, what the customer wants.
    3. Hold an executive call: acknowledge, apologise for impact, present the recovery plan.
    4. Agree an update cadence (e.g., daily until resolved).
    5. Close with a review of fixes and prevention steps.
    ⚠️
    Don't pull in executives for every small issue; you'll lose their attention for the big ones.

    📄 Briefing Your Own Executive

    1-page executive briefAccount: Acme Logistics · ARR ₹1.2 Cr · Renewal 31 Mar · Health: Yellow
Their exec: Rohit Mehta, VP Operations (decision-maker)
Goals: −20% late deliveries (now −12%)
Wins: 2 hubs live; alerts adopted by 85% of dispatchers
Risks: Route module adoption low; competitor demo last month
Ask for this call: Commit to 2 new hubs in Q1; agree joint success plan
Avoid: Pricing (AM will handle)

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Unprepared executive sponsorEmbarrassing; loses credibility1-page brief 24 h before
    Check-ins only when renewal is nearFeels transactionalRegular, value-led touchpoints
    Executive escalations for minor issuesDiminishing attentionEscalate by clear criteria

    Practice

    "What are your top three priorities for the next quarter?" "What would success with our platform look like for you by year-end?" "Is there anything about how we work together you'd like us to change?"
    Practice

    Acknowledge to the CTO within hours; brief your exec (facts, impact, actions); get the RCA and fix timeline from engineering; hold an exec-to-exec call with apology and recovery plan (and service credits if contract allows); set daily/regular updates; follow up with a written summary; monitor health and schedule an EBR after stability returns.

    🎯 Building a Success Plan

    success plan (example)Objective 1: Reduce late deliveries by 20% by 31 Dec

    Module 08 · Real-World Workflow

    Real-World Account Management Scenarios

    Worked examples of situations CSMs face, plus the end-to-end account management workflow from handoff to renewal.

    🔁 End-to-End Workflow

    Sales handoff → Kickoff & success plan → Onboarding → Regular check-ins → QBR/EBR → Executive check-ins → Health monitoring → Renewal → Expansion

    StageCSM key output
    HandoffNotes on goals, stakeholders, promises made in sales
    KickoffAgreed success plan
    OnboardingGo-live and first value
    Steady stateRecaps, adoption progress, risks logged
    QBRValue story, updated plan
    RenewalRenewal plan 120+ days ahead

    🤝 Scenario: New Account Handoff

    • Read the sales notes; meet the AE: why did they buy? what was promised? who signed?
    • Introduce yourself to champion and sponsor within 48 hours.
    • Kickoff: confirm goals in their words; agree success metrics and timeline.
    • Flag any unrealistic promises early, and agree a plan with the AE.

    🔇 Scenario: Silent Customer

    • No reply to 3 emails, usage dropping → treat as risk.
    • Try other channels and contacts (multi-threading), and ask your AE/exec for introductions.
    • Offer something valuable: benchmark report, quick win, training.
    • Log the risk and set a recovery plan.

    👋 Scenario: New Executive Arrives

    • New execs often review all vendors in their first 90 days, a risk and an opportunity.
    • Request an intro meeting quickly; prepare a value summary in their language.
    • Ask about their priorities; re-align the success plan to them.
    • Bring your exec sponsor for strategic accounts.

    🔍 Scenario: Value Not Visible

    • Users like the product, but leadership "doesn't see the ROI".
    • Agree measurable KPIs and baselines with the champion.
    • Build an ROI summary and share it with leadership (one-pager or EBR).
    • Create regular value reports so value is visible all year, not just at renewal.

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.A new CFO joins your largest customer and announces a vendor review. Write your 2-week action plan.

    Scenario: Executive change at a strategic account.

    Day 1–2: align internally (AM, exec sponsor), gather value data and ROI. Day 3–5: ask the champion for an intro; send a short congratulatory note offering a briefing. Week 2: 30-minute meeting with the CFO focused on outcomes and cost/ROI, ask about priorities; follow up with a one-page value summary and updated success plan; flag risk in the CS platform and schedule regular exec check-ins.
    Practice
    P2.Sales promised a feature that doesn't exist. You discover it at kickoff. What do you do?
    Don't hide it. Align with the AE and product first; then tell the customer honestly what exists today, any roadmap timing (only if confirmed), and a workaround; adjust the success plan accordingly; document it and inform leadership if it risks the relationship.

    🎤 Concept Questions

    QuestionModel answer
    What does a CSM do?Drives customer outcomes and adoption, builds relationships, monitors health, and secures renewals and expansion.
    How do you build trust with a new customer?Understand their goals, deliver early wins, keep promises, communicate proactively, and be honest about problems.
    What is a QBR and how do you run one?A strategic review of value and goals: look back at results, share insights, agree next goals and a success plan with owners.
    What is executive presence?Confident, concise, credible communication focused on business outcomes, with composure under pressure.
    How do you prove value?Agree measurable goals and baselines early, track outcomes, tell a Goal→Action→Result→Next story with ROI.
    What is multi-threading and why does it matter?Relationships with several stakeholders, so the account survives champion changes.
    How do you prioritise many accounts?By risk, ARR, renewal date, strategic and expansion potential.

    🧩 Scenario Questions

    Practice
    P1."Tell me about a time you turned around an unhappy customer." (STAR)
    S: A key account had two outages and low adoption before renewal. T: Rebuild trust and secure renewal. A: Exec call with apology and recovery plan, weekly updates, training for two teams, value report showing 25% time savings. R: Health moved red→green, renewal signed with 50 extra seats.
    Practice
    P2."How would you structure your first 90 days with a new book of business?"
    30 days: review every account (health, renewal dates, goals), meet top accounts and AEs, identify risks. 60 days: build success/account plans for priority accounts, fix urgent risks, run QBRs. 90 days: regular cadences in place, renewal plans for next two quarters, expansion opportunities identified.
    Practice
    P3."A customer executive says your product isn't worth the cost. Respond."
    Acknowledge, ask what outcomes they expected, show measured value and gaps honestly, propose a dated plan to close gaps, offer an executive review, and involve the AM on commercial flexibility if appropriate.

    Module 01 · What Is What

    Retention, Churn & Renewals: The Basics

    What retention and churn mean, the types of churn, why retention drives SaaS growth, and the core metrics every CSM must know: churn rate, GRR and NRR.

    🔁 What Is Retention?

    Retention means customers keep using and paying for your product over time. Churn is the opposite: customers leave or reduce spending. A renewal is when a customer signs up for another contract period.

    TermMeaning
    RetentionCustomers (or revenue) kept over a period
    ChurnCustomers (or revenue) lost
    RenewalContract extended for a new term
    Contraction / downsellCustomer stays but pays less (fewer seats, lower plan)
    ExpansionCustomer pays more (more seats, products, upgrades)

    🗂️ Types of Churn

    TypeMeaningExample
    Logo (customer) churnA customer leaves entirelyAcme cancels
    Revenue churnRevenue lost (from cancellations + downgrades)₹10 L of ARR lost
    Voluntary churnCustomer chooses to leaveSwitches to competitor
    Involuntary churnLeaves due to failed payment, expired cardCard declined, not updated
    Contraction (partial churn)Pays less but stays500 → 300 seats
    Controllable vs uncontrollableCould we have prevented it?Poor onboarding vs company acquired/closed

    📈 Why Retention Matters

    💰
    Cheaper than acquisition
    Keeping a customer costs much less than winning a new one.
    🧮
    Compounding
    Small monthly churn compounds into big annual losses.
    🚀
    Expansion base
    Happy customers buy more and refer others.
    🏦
    Company value
    Investors value SaaS companies heavily on retention metrics.
    compounding exampleMonthly logo churn 3%  →  after 12 months: 0.97^12 ≈ 0.69
→ about 31% of customers lost in a year

    🧮 Core Metrics: Churn, GRR & NRR

    MetricFormulaMeaning
    Logo churn rateCustomers lost ÷ customers at startShare of customers who left
    Gross Revenue Retention (GRR)(Start ARR − churn − contraction) ÷ Start ARRRevenue kept, ignoring expansion (max 100%)
    Net Revenue Retention (NRR / NDR)(Start ARR + expansion − churn − contraction) ÷ Start ARRRevenue kept including growth (can exceed 100%)
    worked exampleStart ARR (1 Jan):         ₹10.0 Cr
Churned ARR:               ₹0.6 Cr
Contraction:               ₹0.4 Cr
Expansion:                 ₹1.8 Cr

GRR = (10.0 − 0.6 − 0.4) / 10.0 = 90%
NRR = (10.0 + 1.8 − 0.6 − 0.4) / 10.0 = 108%
    💡
    NRR above 100% means the existing customer base grows even without new customers, a strong sign of a healthy SaaS business.

    🤔 Why Customers Churn

    ReasonTypical signal
    No value realised / low adoptionLow logins, few features used
    Poor onboardingLong time-to-value; stuck projects
    Champion or executive changeNew decision-maker reviewing vendors
    Product gaps / bugsRepeated escalations, feature requests
    Price / budgetProcurement pushback, budget cuts
    CompetitorMentions of alternatives, demos
    Business changeAcquisition, downsizing, closure

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Starting renewal work 30 days outToo late to fix value gapsPlan 120–180 days ahead
    Ignoring contractionRevenue leaks quietlyTrack GRR, not just logo churn
    Treating all churn as uncontrollableNo learningDo churn reason analysis

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Calculate GRR and NRR: start ARR ₹5 Cr; churn ₹0.5 Cr; contraction ₹0.25 Cr; expansion ₹0.75 Cr.
    GRR = (5 − 0.5 − 0.25) / 5 = 85%. NRR = (5 + 0.75 − 0.5 − 0.25) / 5 = 100%.
    Practice
    P2.Classify: (a) customer leaves because their card expired; (b) customer reduces seats from 200 to 120; (c) customer switches to a competitor.
    (a) Involuntary churn. (b) Contraction (partial revenue churn). (c) Voluntary logo churn.

    Module 02 · Health Scores

    Customer Health Scores

    A health score summarises how likely a customer is to renew and grow. Learn what it is, the types of signals used, common scoring models and how to read a health dashboard.

    ❤️ What Is a Health Score?

    A customer health score combines several signals (usage, engagement, support, sentiment, financials) into one indicator, usually a number (0–100) and a colour (green/yellow/red), that predicts retention risk.

    Figure 2.1 · Signals combine into a weighted health score

    📡 Types of Health Signals

    CategoryExample signals
    Product usageActive users vs licences, key feature adoption, usage trend, depth of use
    EngagementMeeting attendance, email responses, QBR participation, exec access
    SupportTicket volume, severity, escalations, open critical bugs, CSAT
    SentimentNPS, CSAT, CSM pulse (gut feel), survey comments
    FinancialPayment history, overdue invoices, downgrades, budget news
    RelationshipChampion strength, multi-threading, exec sponsor engagement
    OutcomesProgress on success-plan goals, ROI achieved

    📊 Leading indicators

    Predict future risk: usage dropping, champion left, no exec engagement.

    📉 Lagging indicators

    Confirm what already happened: cancellation notice, non-renewal, downgrade.

    🧮 Scoring Models

    ModelHow it worksPros / cons
    Simple RAG (red/amber/green)CSM sets colour based on judgementQuick; subjective
    Weighted scoreEach signal scored 0–100, multiplied by weight, summedTransparent; needs tuning
    Rules-based"If usage < 40% → red"Easy to explain; rigid
    Predictive / MLModel trained on past churn dataAccurate at scale; needs lots of data
    weighted score exampleSignal          Score   Weight   Points
Usage            60      35%      21.0
Engagement       80      20%      16.0
Support          90      15%      13.5
Sentiment (NPS)  70      15%      10.5
Financial       100      15%      15.0
----------------------------------------
Health score                      76.0  → 🟢 Green

    🖥️ Reading Health Dashboards

    • Look at the trend, not just today's colour: green but falling fast is a warning.
    • Drill into the lowest component: what's pulling the score down?
    • Combine with ARR and renewal date to prioritise.
    • Use your CSM pulse to add context the data can't see (e.g., reorg rumours).

    🧰 Health Scores in CS Platforms

    PlatformHealth feature name
    GainsightScorecards (measures, weights, overall score)
    ChurnZeroChurnScore
    PlanhatHealth score with configurable metrics
    Catalyst (with Totango)Health scores & segments
    SalesforceCustom health fields/dashboards, or via a CS app
    📌
    Covered in detail in the CS Platform Proficiencies course of this track.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Too many signalsNoise; hard to explain5–8 meaningful signals
    Never validatingScore doesn't predict churnCompare with actual churn regularly
    Only usage dataMiss relationship/financial riskMix categories
    Looking only at colourMiss declining trendsWatch trend and components

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Calculate the health score: Usage 40 (35%), Engagement 70 (20%), Support 50 (15%), Sentiment 60 (15%), Financial 100 (15%). What colour is it (Green 75+, Yellow 50–74, Red <50)?
    14 + 14 + 7.5 + 9 + 15 = 59.5 → Yellow. Usage is the main drag; start there.
    Practice
    P2.An account is green (82) but its score dropped from 95 in four weeks. What do you do?

    Scenario: Weekly health review.

    Treat the trend as a warning: find which component dropped (usage? support escalations? NPS?), contact the champion to understand changes, check for org changes, and create a small action plan before it turns yellow.

    Spotting Churn Risk Early

    The earlier you spot risk, the more options you have. Learn the warning signs, risk categories, how to qualify a risk, and how to log and communicate it internally.

    🚩 Early Warning Signs

    SignalWhy it matters
    Usage decline (logins, key features)Value not being realised
    Champion leaves / role changeLoss of internal advocate
    New executive / reorgVendor reviews, priorities change
    Unanswered emails, cancelled meetingsDisengagement
    Spike in escalations / critical bugsFrustration
    Low NPS / negative commentsSentiment risk
    Requests for data export / contract terms earlyPossible switching
    Budget cuts, layoffs, M&A newsFinancial/structural risk
    Mentions of competitorsActive evaluation

    🗂️ Risk Categories

    CategoryExamplePrimary owner
    Adoption / valueOnly 30% licences activeCSM
    RelationshipChampion leftCSM + exec sponsor
    ProductMissing feature, bugsCSM + product
    ServicePoor support experience, outagesSupport + CSM
    CommercialPrice, budgetAM + CSM
    BusinessAcquisition, closureCSM/AM (limited control)

    🔎 Qualifying a Risk

    1. Confirm the signal (data + conversation).
    2. Find the root cause: ask the customer directly and respectfully.
    3. Estimate likelihood (low/medium/high) and ARR at risk.
    4. Check time to renewal: how long do you have?
    5. Decide the playbook and owners (next module).
    Respectful direct questionI've noticed fewer people logging in since August. Has anything changed in the team or your priorities? I want to make sure we're still helping with what matters most.

    📢 Logging & Communicating Risk

    risk entry (CS platform / CRM)Account: Zenflow · ARR ₹45 L · Renewal 31 Jan
Risk type: Relationship + Adoption       Likelihood: High
Signals: champion left 15 Sep; active users 62% → 38%
Root cause: new Ops head evaluating alternatives
Plan: exec intro (by 10 Oct), value review, retraining for 2 teams
Owners: CSM (plan), AM (commercial), VP CS (exec sponsor)
Next review: weekly
    💡
    Raise risks early to your manager and AM. Leadership can help only if they know; late surprises at renewal damage trust internally too.

    📉 Churn Reason Analysis

    • For every churned account, record primary and secondary reasons using a standard list.
    • Distinguish controllable (onboarding, adoption, support) vs uncontrollable (acquired, went out of business).
    • Review quarterly: top reasons → fixes in onboarding, product roadmap, pricing, playbooks.
    • Exit interviews/surveys reveal truth that dashboards miss.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Waiting for data to turn redToo lateAct on early signals and pulse
    Guessing the causeWrong fixAsk the customer
    Hiding risk from managementNo support; surprisesLog and escalate early

    Module 04 · Playbooks

    Churn Prevention Playbooks

    A playbook is a repeatable set of actions for a specific risk. Learn the common save plays, how to build a recovery plan, when to involve executives, and what (not) to offer.

    📘 What Is a Playbook?

    A playbook is a documented, repeatable response to a situation: trigger → steps → owners → timeline → success criteria. CS platforms can create tasks automatically when a trigger fires (e.g., Gainsight CTAs, ChurnZero Plays).

    🎮 Common Save Plays

    TriggerPlay
    Low adoptionUsage review, targeted training, in-app guides, success-plan reset
    Champion leftIdentify new champion, exec intro, re-onboarding for new owner
    New executiveValue briefing in their language, align to their priorities
    Product gapWorkarounds, roadmap briefing (only confirmed), product manager call
    Service failureExec apology, RCA, recovery plan, service credits (per contract)
    Budget pressureROI case for finance, right-size options via AM
    Competitor evaluationDifferentiation, switching-cost analysis, exec engagement

    🛠️ Building a Recovery Plan

    recovery plan (example)Account: Zenflow (ARR ₹45 L, renewal 31 Jan) — Health: 🔴 38
Goal: back to 🟡 60+ by 15 Dec; renewal signed by 20 Jan

Week 1   Exec call: listen to concerns; agree recovery plan  (VP CS + CSM)
Week 2   Fix top 2 support issues; daily updates             (Support lead)
Week 3–4 Retrain Ops & Finance teams (40 users)              (CSM)
Week 5   Value review: ROI to new Ops head                   (CSM)
Week 8   EBR with renewal proposal                           (AM + CSM)
Success measures: active users 38% → 65%; 0 open P1 tickets
    💡
    Share the recovery plan with the customer. A visible plan with owners rebuilds confidence.

    💸 Commercial Levers

    LeverWhen usedCaution
    Right-sizing seatsOver-bought licencesBetter a smaller renewal than a lost logo
    Plan changePaying for unused featuresKeep core value
    Payment termsCash-flow issuesApproval needed
    Service creditsContractual SLA missesFollow contract
    DiscountsLast resort, approvedCan set a bad precedent; value first
    ⚠️
    Discounts don't fix low value. If the customer isn't getting outcomes, a cheaper price only delays churn.

    👋 When You Can't Save It

    • Accept gracefully and professionally; make offboarding easy.
    • Run an exit interview: what could we have done better?
    • Record churn reasons accurately.
    • Leave the door open; some customers return when circumstances change.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Jumping to discountsDoesn't address value; margins dropFix value first
    Private recovery plansCustomer can't see progressShare plan with owners and dates
    Same play for every riskWrong fixMatch play to root cause

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.A customer's new CFO wants to cut 40% of software spend. Renewal is in 90 days. What's your play?

    Scenario: Budget pressure.

    Build an ROI case in finance terms (cost saved, revenue impact), request a CFO meeting with the AM and exec sponsor, show usage by team, propose right-sizing to retain core value instead of cancelling, confirm the decision process and timeline, log the risk and review weekly.
    Practice
    P2.Write three recovery-plan success measures for an account that is red due to low adoption and support issues.
    Active users from 35% to 65% of licences by date; zero open P1/P2 tickets for 30 days; success-plan milestone (e.g., 2 teams fully onboarded) completed by date.

    • A First option

    Q3.If churn can't be prevented, you should…

    Module 05 · Renewals

    Driving Contract Renewals

    How renewals work, the renewal timeline, roles of CSM vs Account Manager, managing the decision process, and handling common renewal objections.

    🔁 What Is a Renewal?

    A renewal is the customer agreeing to continue their subscription for another term (monthly, annual or multi-year). Renewals are where Customer Success turns into revenue.

    RoleTypical responsibility
    CSMValue, health, relationships, renewal readiness; may own renewal (commercial CSM model)
    Account Manager / Renewals repQuote, pricing, negotiation, paperwork
    Customer championInternal advocate, business case
    Procurement / legalTerms, approvals, purchase order

    🗂️ Types of Renewals

    TypeMeaningCSM focus
    Auto-renewalRenews automatically unless cancelled by a notice dateWatch notice deadlines; value all year
    Manual renewalNew order/contract must be signedPlan early; manage process
    Flat renewalSame price/seatsConfirm value
    Uplift renewalPrice increase (e.g., annual uplift %)Justify with value
    Expansion renewalMore seats/products at renewalShow growth opportunities
    Downsell renewalFewer seats/lower planProtect logo; plan re-growth
    Multi-year renewalCommitted for 2–3 yearsLong-term value plan; often discount trade-off

    🗓️ The Renewal Timeline

    Figure 5.1 · A typical renewal timeline
    ⚠️
    Many contracts require cancellation notice 30–90 days before the end date. If a customer is at risk, act before their notice window.

    🏛️ Decision Process & Procurement

    Question to answer earlyWhy
    Who decides? Who signs?Avoid surprises from unknown approvers
    What does the approval process look like?Finance/legal/security reviews take weeks
    What budget cycle are they in?Budgets may be set months ahead
    Any security/compliance reviews?Vendor questionnaires can delay signature
    Is a PO (purchase order) required?Invoices may be blocked without one
    💡
    Ask the champion: "What does your company need to approve the renewal, and how long does it usually take?" Write the steps down with dates.

    🛡️ Handling Renewal Objections

    ObjectionResponse approach
    "It's too expensive."Show ROI; right-size; involve AM on options
    "We don't use it enough."Usage plan with dates; consider seat adjustment
    "A competitor is cheaper."Value delivered, switching costs, roadmap fit, exec alignment
    "We need it to do X."Workarounds, confirmed roadmap, product call
    "New leadership is reviewing tools."Exec briefing in their language; success-plan reset

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Missing notice deadlinesCustomer auto-cancels/can't be savedTrack notice dates
    Unknown decision-makerLate surprisesMap decision process early
    Talking price before valueBecomes a discount negotiationValue review first

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Write a 120-day renewal plan for an account with ARR ₹60 L, yellow health and a new VP.
    120 days: internal review with AM (health, risks, decision process); intro meeting with the new VP. 100–90 days: value review/EBR aligned to VP's priorities; success-plan reset; fix top adoption gap. 75–60 days: proposal from AM; confirm approvers, procurement, security reviews. 45–30 days: negotiate, legal, PO. Weekly risk review throughout.
    Practice
    P2.The customer says "We don't use it enough to justify the cost" 60 days before renewal. Respond.

    Scenario: Renewal objection.

    Acknowledge; show where value exists today (teams, outcomes); identify why others aren't using it; propose a dated adoption plan; offer right-sizing via AM so they pay for what they use now, with a plan to grow; agree next steps and decision date.

    Expansion: Growing Net Revenue Retention

    Retention protects revenue; expansion grows it. Learn the types of expansion, signals that a customer is ready to grow, and how CSMs partner with Sales.

    🚀 Types of Expansion

    TypeExample
    Upsell (seats)200 → 300 users
    Upgrade (tier)Pro → Enterprise plan
    Cross-sellAdd Analytics module
    New team / regionFinance team or Singapore office adopts
    Usage-based growthMore API calls/transactions billed

    📈 Expansion Signals

    SignalWhat it suggests
    Licence utilisation > 90%Need more seats
    Hitting plan limitsUpgrade opportunity
    Requests for features in higher tierUpgrade
    Other departments asking for accessNew team expansion
    Strong health + achieved goalsReady to invest more
    Company growth, funding, hiringBudget and need increasing

    🤝 The CSM–Sales Handoff

    1. CSM spots the signal and validates need with the customer.
    2. Log an expansion opportunity (CRM) with context: who, what, why, size estimate.
    3. Warm introduction to AM/AE with a short brief.
    4. CSM stays involved: value story, references, implementation plan.
    5. After the deal, CSM onboards the new team/product.
    💡
    Expansion feels natural when it solves a problem the customer already raised, not when it's pushed for quota.

    💬 Expansion Conversations

    Value-led openerYour support team has cut response times by 35%. The sales team mentioned they struggle with the same handoffs. Would it help to see how they could use the same workflow?
    Pushy openerWe have a promotion on extra seats this month. Want to buy more?

    📣 Advocacy & Referrals

    • Healthy customers can become advocates: case studies, reviews (G2), references, speaking at events.
    • Ask at moments of success (goal achieved, great QBR).
    • Advocacy also strengthens the relationship and makes renewal more likely.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Pitching expansion to unhappy customersDamages trustFix value first
    Cold handoffs to salesCustomer repeats everythingWarm intro with brief
    Ignoring usage-limit signalsMissed growth; frustrated customerMonitor and act

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.A customer uses 98% of their 250 licences and their HR team asked for access. Describe your next steps.
    Validate with the champion (how many more users, which teams, timing); log an expansion opportunity with context; warm-intro the AM with a brief; prepare a value summary and onboarding plan for HR; stay involved through the deal and onboard the new team.
    Practice
    P2.Write a value-led expansion opener for a customer whose finance team succeeded with your invoicing module and whose procurement team has similar manual work.
    "Your finance team now closes the month two days faster with automated invoicing. Procurement mentioned they still chase approvals manually. Would it be useful to see how the same approval workflows could work for them?"

    Retention Metrics, Cohorts & Renewal Forecasting

    Expert CSMs and CS leaders measure retention precisely and forecast renewals. Learn monthly vs annual churn, cohort analysis, forecast categories and how to present a renewal forecast.

    📏 Metric Definitions in Detail

    MetricDefinition
    Logo retentionCustomers retained ÷ customers at start
    Gross Revenue Retention (GRR)(Start ARR − churn − contraction) ÷ Start ARR
    Net Revenue Retention (NRR)(Start ARR + expansion − churn − contraction) ÷ Start ARR
    Renewal rateRenewed ARR ÷ ARR up for renewal in the period
    Time to value (TTV)Days from contract start to first meaningful outcome
    Customer lifetime value (LTV)Average revenue per customer × gross margin × expected lifetime

    🧮 Worked Calculations

    examplesRenewal rate:  ARR up for renewal in Q4 = ₹4.0 Cr; renewed = ₹3.4 Cr
               3.4 / 4.0 = 85%

Logo churn:    200 customers at start; 12 left
               12 / 200 = 6%

LTV (simple):  ₹6 L ARR per customer × 80% margin × 5 years = ₹24 L

    Module 08 · Real-World Workflow

    Real-World Retention Scenarios

    The end-to-end retention workflow and worked scenarios: from health alerts to saved renewals.

    🔁 End-to-End Retention Workflow

    Monitor health → Spot signal → Qualify risk → Log & escalate → Run playbook → Recovery plan with customer → Re-measure health → Renewal plan → Renew / expand → Learn from outcomes

    📉 Scenario: Usage Collapse

    • Signal: active users 70% → 30% in six weeks.
    • Discovery: a reorg moved the process to another team who never got trained.
    • Play: map new stakeholders, re-onboard the new team, update the success plan, weekly check-ins.
    • Outcome: usage back to 65% in 6 weeks; renewal secured.

    🥊 Scenario: Competitor Evaluation

    • Signal: champion mentions a competitor demo; data export requested.
    • Play: understand their criteria; value/ROI review; switching-cost analysis; exec-to-exec call; address gaps with workarounds/roadmap.
    • Involve AM early for commercial strategy.

    💳 Scenario: Involuntary Churn

    • Signal: payment failed, card expired; account will suspend in 7 days.
    • Play: dunning emails + direct outreach to billing contact; easy payment-update link; grace period per policy.
    • Prevention: card-expiry reminders, invoices/bank transfer for larger customers.

    🏷️ Scenario: Price Increase at Renewal

    • Communicate early (90+ days) with clear reasons and value delivered.
    • Offer options: multi-year lock-in, right-sizing, phased increase (with approval).
    • Prepare champion with a business case for their leadership.

    Interview Preparation: Retention & Renewal Strategy

    CSM interview questions on health scores, churn, renewals and NRR, with model answers, comparisons, common mistakes and scenarios.

    🎤 Concept Questions

    QuestionModel answer
    What is a customer health score?A combined indicator of renewal risk built from usage, engagement, support, sentiment and financial signals.
    GRR vs NRR?GRR measures revenue kept excluding expansion (max 100%); NRR includes expansion and can exceed 100%.
    How do you spot churn risk early?Watch leading indicators (usage decline, champion change, disengagement, escalations, low NPS) and ask customers directly.
    How do you prevent churn?Qualify root cause, run the matching playbook, share a recovery plan, involve execs/AM when needed.
    When do you start renewal planning?120–180 days ahead for key accounts; before notice deadlines.
    How do you handle "too expensive"?Show ROI, align to priorities, right-size, involve AM for commercial options; discounts last.
    What is involuntary churn?Churn from failed payments/expired cards, not a decision to leave.

    Module 01 · What Is What

    What Customer Value Is

    Start with the basics: what "value" means to a customer, the types of value, value realization, time to value, and the difference between outputs and outcomes.

    💎 What Is Customer Value?

    Customer value is the measurable benefit a customer gets from your product compared with what they pay and the effort they invest. Customers don't buy software; they buy results: saving time, earning more, reducing risk.

    💡
    Simple formula: Value = Outcomes achieved − (cost + effort). A CSM increases outcomes and reduces effort.

    🗂️ Types of Value

    TypeMeaningExample
    EconomicMoney saved or earned₹40 L saved in staff time; 8% more renewals
    OperationalFaster, simpler, fewer errorsInvoice processing 6 → 3 days
    StrategicSupports company goalsEnables expansion to 3 new cities
    Risk / complianceReduces riskAudit-ready logs; fewer security incidents
    Experience / emotionalHappier employees or customersCSAT up; less weekend work

    🎯 What Is Value Realization?

    Value realization is the process of making sure customers actually achieve the outcomes they expected when they bought, and that they can see it.

    Figure 1.1 · From business objective to proven value
    StageQuestion
    Expected valueWhat did they hope to achieve when buying?
    Realised valueWhat have they actually achieved?
    Perceived valueDo decision-makers know and believe it?
    ⚠️
    A customer can realise value but still churn if leadership doesn't perceive it. Making value visible is part of the job.

    ⚖️ Outputs vs Outcomes

    OutputOutcome
    MeaningActivity or usageBusiness result
    Example500 users logged in; 2,000 reports createdMonth-end close 2 days faster
    Who caresProduct team, CSMCustomer executives
    Use in QBRsSupporting evidenceThe headline
    📌
    Outputs (usage) are leading indicators; outcomes are what customers pay for. Always connect the two.

    ⏱️ Time to Value (TTV)

    Time to value is how long it takes from purchase until the customer gets their first meaningful outcome. Shorter TTV = less risk of early churn.

    MilestoneExample
    Time to first valueFirst automated report sent (day 7)
    Time to go-liveAll teams using the system (day 45)
    Time to full valueTarget KPI reached (month 4)
    • Define a "first value" milestone at kickoff.
    • Remove blockers (SSO, integrations) early.
    • Start with a quick win before complex projects.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Reporting only usage numbersLeadership doesn't see business impactLead with outcomes
    No baseline measuredCan't prove improvementCapture baseline at kickoff
    Long onboarding before any winEarly churn riskPlan a quick first-value milestone

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice

    (a) Output. (b) Outcome. (c) Output. (d) Outcome.
    Practice
    P2.Name the type of value for: (a) passing an audit without findings; (b) saving ₹25 L per year; (c) enabling launch in two new countries.
    (a) Risk/compliance. (b) Economic. (c) Strategic.

    Module 02 · Discovery

    Discovering Customer Business Objectives

    You can't deliver value you don't understand. Learn types of business objectives, discovery questions, how to dig from surface requests to real goals, and how to capture baselines.

    🎯 Types of Business Objectives

    Objective typeExamples
    Grow revenueIncrease conversion, faster sales cycle, upsell more
    Reduce costAutomate manual work, cut tools, lower cost per ticket
    Improve efficiencyFaster processes, fewer handoffs, shorter cycle times
    Improve customer experienceHigher CSAT/NPS, faster response
    Reduce riskCompliance, security, data accuracy
    Enable strategyNew markets, scale, digital transformation

    ❓ Discovery Questions

    AreaQuestions
    Why they bought"What made you look for a solution? What was happening?"
    Success definition"In 12 months, what would make this a clear success?"
    Measurement"How do you measure that today? What's the current number?"
    Stakes"What happens if this doesn't improve?"
    Priorities"Which of these goals matters most to your leadership?"
    Constraints"What could slow this down: people, IT, budget, timelines?"
    💡
    Ask different people. A user wants "fewer clicks"; a manager wants "faster approvals"; the VP wants "lower operating cost". All are linked.

    🔍 From Request to Real Goal

    Customers often ask for features. Use the "5 Whys" to uncover the business goal behind the request.

    5 Whys exampleRequest: "We need a custom export to Excel."
Why? → "Managers build weekly reports manually."
Why? → "Leadership needs weekly SLA performance."
Why? → "They're losing enterprise customers over slow responses."
Real goal: improve SLA compliance to protect enterprise renewals.
Better solution: scheduled SLA dashboard emailed to leadership + SLA alerts.

    📏 Capturing Baselines

    • A baseline is the measurement before your product is used, the "before" picture.
    • Capture it at kickoff: numbers, data source, date, who agreed it.
    • If the customer has no data, agree a proxy or measure the first 2 weeks as baseline.
    baseline recordObjective: Faster invoice approvals
KPI: median approval time
Baseline: 6.2 days (Jan 2026, from ERP export, agreed with Finance Lead)
Target: 4.0 days by 30 June

    🤝 Aligning Multiple Stakeholders

    • List each stakeholder's goal; find the shared business objective above them.
    • Prioritise with the executive sponsor: 2–3 objectives maximum.
    • Write them in the success plan and confirm by email.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Taking feature requests at face valueSolve the wrong problemAsk why until you reach the goal
    Only asking the championMiss leadership prioritiesDiscover across levels
    No baselineCan't prove value laterRecord it at kickoff

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.A customer asks for "more dashboards". Use the 5 Whys to propose what the real goal might be.

    Scenario: Kickoff call with a retail customer.

    Why? Managers can't see store performance. Why? Weekly numbers arrive late. Why? Reports are compiled manually from 3 systems. Real goal: faster, reliable visibility of store performance to act on low-performing stores. Solution: automated daily store scorecard + alerts.
    Practice
    P2.Write three discovery questions to ask a VP at kickoff.
    "What would make this project a clear success in 12 months?" "How do you measure that today, and what's the current number?" "What happens to the business if it doesn't improve?"

    Module 03 · Metrics

    Defining Success Metrics & KPIs

    Turn objectives into measurable KPIs: baselines, targets, SMART goals, leading vs lagging metrics, and how to connect product usage metrics to business outcomes.

    📊 What Is a KPI?

    A Key Performance Indicator (KPI) is a number that shows progress towards an objective. Good KPIs are measurable, owned, and meaningful to the customer's leadership.

    ElementExample
    ObjectiveReduce support costs
    KPICost per ticket
    Baseline₹180 (Jan)
    Target₹144 by Dec
    Data sourceFinance report + help desk volume
    OwnerSupport Ops Manager

    🗂️ Types of Metrics

    TypeMeaningExample
    LeadingPredicts future outcomes% of agents using macros
    LaggingShows results after the factQuarterly support cost
    Input / activityWhat people doTrainings held, workflows built
    OutputProduct usage resultsTickets auto-routed
    OutcomeBusiness resultCost per ticket, CSAT
    Health metricGuardrail to avoid side effectsCSAT stays ≥ 90% while costs drop

    🎯 SMART Targets

    ❌ Vague

    "Improve onboarding."

    ✅ SMART

    "Reduce new-hire onboarding time from 15 to 9 days for all regional offices by 30 Sep."

    LetterCheck
    SpecificClear what changes
    MeasurableHas a number
    AchievableRealistic given resources
    RelevantTied to a business objective
    Time-boundHas a deadline

    🔗 Connecting Usage to Outcomes

    Build a metric chain: usage (leading) → process change → outcome (lagging).

    metric chain exampleUsage:    % of approvals done via mobile app     (leading)   40% → 80%
Process:  median approval time                    (output)    6.2 → 3.9 days
Outcome:  late-payment penalties per quarter      (lagging)   ₹12 L → ₹4 L
    💡
    When outcomes take months to move, report leading metrics monthly so everyone sees progress early.

    🏢 Metric Examples by Function

    FunctionOutcome KPIs
    SalesWin rate, sales cycle length, pipeline velocity
    SupportCost per ticket, FRT, CSAT, self-service rate
    HRTime-to-hire, onboarding time, attrition
    FinanceDays to close, DSO, invoice processing cost
    OperationsOn-time delivery %, error rate, throughput
    MarketingLead conversion, cost per lead

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Too many KPIsDiluted focus2–3 per objective
    Only lagging KPIsNo early signalPair with leading KPIs
    KPIs without ownersNobody tracks themAssign an owner and source

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Write one SMART KPI, one leading metric and one guardrail metric for "faster customer support".
    SMART KPI: reduce median first-response time from 9 h to 3 h by 31 March. Leading: % of tickets auto-routed (target 80%). Guardrail: CSAT stays ≥ 90%.
    Practice
    P2.Build a metric chain for an HR customer whose objective is to reduce time-to-hire.
    Usage: % of interviews scheduled through the tool (30% → 90%). Process: scheduling time per candidate (3 days → 0.5 day). Outcome: time-to-hire (42 → 30 days).
    Q1.A leading metric…
    • A Shows past results only
    • B Predicts future outcomes
    • C Is a contract term
    • D Is always revenue
    Q2.A guardrail metric ensures…
    • A Faster results
    • B Improvements don't cause harm elsewhere
    • C Higher price
    • D More users
    Q3.Which is SMART?
    • A "Be better"
    • B "Cut onboarding from 15 to 9 days by 30 Sep"
    • C "Improve a lot"
    • D "Use more features"

    Module 04 · Adoption

    Product Adoption Fundamentals

    Adoption is how much, how widely and how well customers use your product. Learn the types of adoption, key adoption metrics, and the stages users go through.

    📈 What Is Product Adoption?

    Product adoption means customers actively use the product in their daily work, especially the features that drive their business outcomes. Adoption is the bridge between buying and getting value.

    🧭 Types / Dimensions of Adoption

    DimensionQuestionExample
    BreadthHow many people/teams use it?180 of 250 licences active
    DepthHow many key features do they use?4 of 6 core workflows used
    FrequencyHow often?Daily vs monthly
    StickinessIs it part of the routine?Used in every weekly meeting
    Feature adoptionIs a specific feature used?30% use automation rules

    📊 Adoption Metrics

    MetricFormula / meaning
    Licence utilisationActive users ÷ purchased licences
    DAU / MAUDaily active ÷ monthly active users (stickiness)
    Feature adoption rateUsers of feature ÷ eligible users
    Time to first key actionDays until a user completes the core action
    Activation rate% of new users completing setup milestones
    exampleLicences: 250 · Monthly active: 190 · Daily active: 95
Utilisation = 190 / 250 = 76%
DAU/MAU    = 95 / 190 = 50%  (half of monthly users use it daily)

    🪜 Adoption Stages

    StageUser behaviourCSM action
    AwareKnows the product existsCommunication, launch emails
    OnboardedAccount set up, basic trainingGuided onboarding
    ActivatedCompleted first key actionQuick-win workflows
    HabitualUses regularlyBest practices, tips
    AdvancedUses advanced featuresAdvanced training, automation
    AdvocateChampions it internallyChampion programme, references

    🚧 Barriers to Adoption

    BarrierFix
    No training / unclear valueRole-based training showing "what's in it for me"
    Old habits / tools still usedLeadership mandate; retire the old process
    Technical blockers (SSO, integrations)Prioritise setup; involve IT early
    Complex setupTemplates, best-practice configuration
    No internal ownerIdentify and empower a champion

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Counting logins as adoptionLogins ≠ valueTrack key-feature adoption
    Training everyone the same wayLow relevanceRole-based enablement
    Ignoring the old processUsers keep the old wayRetire old tools with leadership

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Calculate: 400 licences, 260 monthly active users, 78 daily active users. What are utilisation and DAU/MAU, and what do they suggest?
    Utilisation = 260/400 = 65%; DAU/MAU = 78/260 = 30%. Many users log in monthly but few daily, so it isn't yet part of daily work. Focus on habit-forming workflows and the 140 inactive licences.
    Practice
    P2.Users still use spreadsheets alongside your tool. What do you do?

    Scenario: Low depth of adoption.

    Find out why (missing feature, habit, trust in data); show how the tool covers those needs; get the manager/executive to make the tool the official source; migrate templates; train on the specific workflow; track usage of that workflow weekly.

    Q2.Depth of adoption measures…
    • A Number of users

    • C Contract length
    • D Price
    Q3.A common barrier to adoption is…
    • A Too much training
    • B Old habits and tools still in use
    • C High NPS
    • D Fast onboarding

    Module 05 · Roadmaps

    Building Actionable Product Adoption Roadmaps

    The core of this skill: translating business objectives into a phased roadmap of use cases, features, enablement and milestones that the customer can execute.

    🗺️ What Is an Adoption Roadmap?

    An adoption roadmap is a time-phased plan showing which teams will adopt which capabilities, in what order, with what enablement, to reach the customer's business objectives.

    Success planAdoption roadmap
    FocusObjectives, KPIs, milestonesHow usage will grow to reach them
    GranularityBusiness levelTeams, features, enablement
    RelationshipThe "why"The "how"

    🔗 Objective → Use Case → Feature

    Business objectiveUse caseProduct capabilityEnablement
    Cut support cost 20%Deflect simple questionsHelp Center + AI botKB writing workshop
    Speed up agentsMacros, routing rulesAgent training
    Improve CSAT to 92%Faster first responseSLA policies, alertsLead training on views
    Consistent answersMacro libraryMacro governance session
    💡
    Every feature on the roadmap should answer: "Which objective does this move, and how will we measure it?"

    🪜 Phasing: Crawl, Walk, Run

    Figure 5.1 · A three-phase adoption roadmap
    • Crawl: foundations and one quick win for early value.
    • Walk: broad rollout of core workflows.
    • Run: advanced capabilities, automation, optimisation.

    📋 Roadmap Template

    adoption roadmap (example)Customer: Kiran Retail · Objective: −20% support cost, CSAT ≥ 92% by Dec

Phase  Team(s)          Capability           Enablement          Owner        Due     Measure
1      Pilot (2 teams)  Routing + macros     2 × 60-min training CSM          15 Jul  80% macro use
1      IT               SSO                  IT session          Customer IT  10 Jul  SSO live
2      All 6 teams      SLA policies, views  Team-lead training  CSM + leads  31 Aug  FRT ≤ 4 h
2      Content team     Help Center          KB workshop         Customer     15 Sep  40 articles
3      All              AI bot deflection    Bot tuning session  CSM          31 Oct  25% deflection
3      Leadership       Dashboards           Exec walkthrough    CSM          15 Nov  Monthly review

    ✅ Getting Buy-in

    1. Draft the roadmap with the champion.
    2. Validate capacity: do teams have time for training and changes?
    3. Present to the sponsor with the objectives and expected impact.
    4. Agree owners and dates; add to the success plan.
    5. Review progress at every check-in; adjust when priorities change.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Feature list without objectivesNo business justificationMap each item to an objective
    Everything in phase 1Overwhelms teamsPhase it; start with a quick win
    CSM-owned onlyCustomer doesn't executeCustomer owners for their tasks

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Build a 3-phase adoption roadmap outline for an HR customer whose objective is to reduce time-to-hire from 42 to 30 days.
    Crawl: integrate calendar + ATS; pilot interview scheduling with Engineering hiring (quick win). Walk: roll out scheduling and scorecards to all hiring teams; recruiter training; measure scheduling time. Run: automated candidate updates, offer workflow, hiring dashboard for leadership; target 30-day time-to-hire.
    Practice
    P2.Map this objective to two use cases, features and enablement: "Increase sales win rate from 18% to 23%."
    Use case 1: better follow-up → sequences/reminders → SDR training. Use case 2: deal visibility → pipeline dashboards and deal scoring → manager coaching session on pipeline reviews.

    Driving Adoption: Enablement & Change Management

    Roadmaps only work if people change how they work. Learn enablement methods, champion programmes, digital adoption tools and change management basics.

    🎓 Enablement Methods

    MethodBest for
    Live training (virtual/onsite)Kickoffs, complex workflows
    Recorded videosRepeatable basics
    Office hoursOngoing questions
    Knowledge base / academySelf-paced learning
    In-app guidesFeature discovery at the moment of need
    CertificationAdmins and power users

    👥 Role-based Training

    RoleTraining focus
    End users"What's in it for me": daily workflows, shortcuts
    ManagersDashboards, approvals, coaching with data
    AdminsConfiguration, permissions, integrations
    ExecutivesOutcome dashboards, value reviews
    💡
    Short, role-specific sessions (30–45 min) work better than one long generic training.

    🏅 Champion Programmes

    • Identify power users in each team who like the product.
    • Give them early access, advanced training and recognition.
    • They answer colleagues' questions and promote best practices.
    • Meet them monthly; collect feedback for product.

    📲 Digital & Scaled Adoption

    Tool / tacticExample
    In-app guides & checklistsPendo, WalkMe, Appcues, product tours
    Automated email journeysTips after day 3, 7, 14 based on usage
    WebinarsMonthly "what's new" sessions
    CommunityForums for peer help
    Usage-triggered nudges"You haven't tried automation yet" email

    🔄 Change Management (ADKAR)

    ADKAR stageMeaningCSM action
    AwarenessWhy the change is neededLeadership message on goals
    DesireWanting to participateShow personal benefits; champions
    KnowledgeKnowing howTraining, guides
    AbilityBeing able to do itPractice, support, office hours
    ReinforcementSustaining itDashboards, recognition, retire old process
    📌
    ADKAR is a widely used change model (from Prosci). Mentioning it in interviews shows you think about people, not just features.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    One big training at go-liveForgotten within weeksOngoing, role-based enablement
    No leadership messageUsers see it as optionalExec sponsor communicates why
    Not retiring old toolsParallel processes continuePlan the switch-off date

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Plan enablement for a 300-person rollout across agents, team leads and admins.
    Admins: 2-hour configuration session + certification. Team leads: 45-min dashboards/coaching session. Agents: 30-min role-based sessions by team + recorded videos + in-app checklist. Weekly office hours for 4 weeks; champion in each team; usage review after 2 weeks to target low-adoption teams.
    Practice
    P2.Users were trained but adoption is still low. Using ADKAR, which stages might be missing?

    Scenario: Post-training adoption gap.

    Likely Awareness/Desire (they don't see why or what's in it for them) or Reinforcement (old process still allowed, no follow-up). Actions: leadership message on goals, show personal benefits, retire the old process, dashboards and recognition.

    Module 07 · Goal Tracking

    Goal Tracking & Proving Value

    Track progress against goals continuously and make value visible: value dashboards, ROI calculations, value reports and success-plan reviews.

    🗓️ Goal Tracking Cadence

    CadenceWhat is reviewedWith whom
    Weekly (during rollout)Roadmap tasks, blockers, leading metricsChampion / project team
    MonthlyAdoption metrics, milestone statusChampion, managers
    QuarterlyOutcomes, ROI, next goalsExecutives (QBR/EBR)

    📊 Value Dashboards

    value dashboard layout┌ Objective: −20% support cost by Dec ───────────────┐
│ Cost per ticket: ₹180 → ₹151 (−16%)   target ₹144  │  🟡 on track
│ Self-service deflection: 22%          target 25%   │
│ Macro usage: 84% of agents            target 80%   │  🟢
│ CSAT (guardrail): 93%                 ≥ 92%        │  🟢
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
    💡
    Show objective, KPI, baseline, current, target and status on one screen, which is exactly what executives want.

    🧮 Calculating ROI

    ROI exampleAnnual benefit:
  Hours saved: 2,400 h × ₹600/h            = ₹14.4 L
  Tool replaced (licence avoided)           = ₹6.0 L
  Total benefit                             = ₹20.4 L
Annual cost (subscription)                  = ₹8.0 L

ROI = (Benefit − Cost) / Cost = (20.4 − 8.0) / 8.0 = 155%
Payback period = Cost / (Benefit / 12) = 8.0 / 1.7 ≈ 4.7 months
    ⚠️
    Agree assumptions (hourly cost, hours saved) with the customer. ROI they don't believe isn't value.

    📝 Value Reports & Stories

    • Monthly or quarterly value report: 1 page, objective-by-objective progress.
    • Include a customer quote and one concrete example.
    • Share beyond the champion: managers, sponsor, finance.
    • Collect success stories for case studies (with permission).

    🧭 When Goals Are Off Track

    1. Diagnose: adoption issue, product gap, data issue, or changed priorities?
    2. Discuss honestly with the champion and sponsor.
    3. Adjust the roadmap: extra enablement, workarounds, re-phasing.
    4. Reset targets only with agreement and clear reasons.
    5. Track recovery weekly until back on track.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead
    Value shown only at renewalToo late; feels like sellingRegular value reports
    Unagreed ROI assumptionsNot trustedAgree inputs with customer
    Hiding off-track goalsSurprise at QBRFlag early with a plan

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Calculate ROI and payback: benefit ₹30 L/year; subscription ₹12 L/year.
    ROI = (30 − 12) / 12 = 150%. Payback = 12 / (30/12) = 12 / 2.5 = 4.8 months.
    Practice
    P2.A goal (CSAT 92%) is stuck at 86% after 3 months. Outline what you do.

    Scenario: Off-track goal before QBR.

    Diagnose drivers (CSAT comments, response times, specific teams); share findings with the champion; adjust roadmap (training for low-CSAT teams, macros, SLA alerts); agree a recovery timeline; present honestly in the QBR with the plan.

    Module 08 · Real-World Workflow

    Real-World Value Realization Scenarios

    The end-to-end value workflow and worked scenarios from kickoff to proven ROI.

    🔁 End-to-End Workflow

    Discover objectives → Capture baselines → Define KPIs → Map to use cases & features → Build phased roadmap → Enable & drive adoption → Track metrics → Report value → Reset goals for next period

    ❓ Scenario: No Clear Goals

    • Customer says "we just want to use the tool".
    • Ask why they bought and what happens if nothing changes.
    • Propose 2–3 typical objectives from similar customers; let them pick and adapt.
    • Agree simple baselines and a 90-day roadmap.

    🔄 Scenario: Adoption Without Outcomes

    • Usage is high but the KPI hasn't moved.
    • Check if they use the right features for the goal, or the process around the tool is unchanged.
    • Analyse the metric chain: where does it break?
    • Adjust: workflow redesign, automation, manager coaching.

    🔀 Scenario: Priorities Changed

    • A new executive shifts focus from cost reduction to growth.
    • Re-discover objectives with the new exec.
    • Re-map roadmap to the new goals; keep wins already achieved visible.
    • Update success plan and value dashboard.

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Usage is 90% but invoice processing time hasn't improved. Diagnose using the metric chain.
    Usage (leading) is high but the process metric hasn't moved, so the chain breaks at process change. Likely approvals still happen by email outside the tool, or automation rules aren't configured. Check workflow data, interview users, configure approval automation, train managers, and track approval time weekly.
    Practice
    P2.Write a one-paragraph value summary for an executive after 6 months.
    "Six months in, your support team has reduced cost per ticket from ₹180 to ₹151 (−16%), on track for the −20% target by December, while CSAT rose to 93%. Self-service now resolves 22% of questions, and 84% of agents use standard macros. Next quarter we'll launch the AI bot and leadership dashboards to close the remaining gap."
    Q1.High usage but no KPI change suggests…
    • A Success
    • B The process or feature fit is wrong somewhere in the chain
    • C Churn
    • D Billing issue
    Q2.When a new exec changes priorities, you should…
    • A Ignore
    • B Re-discover objectives and re-map the roadmap
    • C Cancel the plan
    • D Discount

    How do you build an adoption roadmap?Map objectives → use cases → features → enablement; phase as crawl/walk/run with owners, dates and measures.
    Leading vs lagging metrics?Leading predict outcomes (usage); lagging show results (cost, revenue).
    How do you calculate ROI?(Benefit − Cost) ÷ Cost, with assumptions agreed with the customer.
    How do you drive adoption?Role-based enablement, champions, in-app guidance, leadership messaging, retiring old processes (ADKAR).
    What is time to value?Time from purchase to first meaningful outcome; shorten with quick wins.

    What an SLA Is and Why It Matters

    A Service Level Agreement is a promise about how fast support will respond and resolve. Learn what SLAs measure, how the SLA clock works, and how SLAs differ from OLAs and SLOs.

    ⏱️ What Is an SLA?

    An SLA (Service Level Agreement) is a commitment about the level of service a customer can expect, most often how quickly support will reply and fix problems.

    📄 External (contractual) SLA

    Written into the customer's contract, usually for paid or enterprise plans. Breaking it can mean service credits (refunds) or penalties.

    🏠 Internal SLA / target

    A goal the support team sets for itself, e.g., "reply to every email within 8 business hours", even when it's not in a contract.

    PriorityFirst responseResolution (example)Coverage
    Urgent30 minutes4 hours24×7
    High2 business hours1 business dayBusiness hours
    Normal8 business hours3 business daysBusiness hours
    Low24 business hours7 business daysBusiness hours
    📌
    Exact numbers differ per company and plan. On your first day, learn your team's SLA table; you'll use it on every ticket.

    📏 What SLAs Measure

    SLA metricMeasuresStarts / stops
    First Response Time (FRT)Time to the first human replyTicket created → first public agent reply
    Next Reply TimeTime to reply after each customer messageCustomer reply → next agent reply
    Resolution timeTime to solve the issueCreated → Solved (minus paused time)
    Requester / agent wait timeHow long each side waitedTime spent in New/Open vs Pending
    First Response TimeResolution Time
    Question it answers"How fast did someone respond?""How fast was it fixed?"
    Controlled mostly byQueue order and speedComplexity, escalations, customer replies
    Auto-replies count?No, usually only human replies—
    Typical targetMinutes to hoursHours to days
    ⚠️
    A quick "We're looking into it" meets FRT but doesn't solve anything. Good teams track both, so agents don't game FRT with empty replies.

    🕰️ How the SLA Clock Works

    Figure 1.1 · When the SLA clock runs and pauses
    Business hoursCalendar hours (24×7)
    Clock runsOnly during working hours (e.g., Mon–Fri 9:00–18:00), not on holidaysAll the time, including nights and weekends
    Typical forNormal / Low priority, standard plansUrgent priority, premium/enterprise plans
    ExampleTicket at 17:30 Friday, 2 business-hour FRT → due Monday 10:30Ticket at 17:30 Friday, 2-hour FRT → due 19:30 Friday
    💡
    Business hours are tied to a timezone. A customer in London and an agent in Hyderabad see the same deadline, but in different local times. Always check the timezone the SLA uses.

    🔀 SLA vs OLA vs SLO

    SLAOLASLO
    Full formService Level AgreementOperational Level AgreementService Level Objective
    BetweenCompany ↔ customerInternal teams (e.g., Support ↔ Engineering)Internal target for a measurable value
    Example"Urgent tickets answered within 30 min""Tier 2 picks up escalations within 4 hours""95% of tickets meet FRT"
    If missedCustomer impact; possible creditsInternal follow-upSignals the team must improve
    📌
    OLAs make SLAs possible: if Tier 2 doesn't pick up escalations in time, support can't meet the customer's resolution SLA.

    🎯 Why SLAs Matter

    🤝
    Trust
    Customers know what to expect and when.
    📋
    Contracts
    Enterprise deals depend on meeting agreed targets.
    🚦
    Prioritisation
    SLAs tell agents what to work on first.
    📊
    Measurement
    Managers see whether staffing and processes are working.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeWhy it's a problemDo this instead
    Using Pending while you still owe a replyPauses the clock unfairly; hides real waitUse Pending only when the customer must act
    Empty first replies to "stop the clock"Meets FRT but annoys customersFirst reply should add value (fix, question, or plan)
    Ignoring timezonesPromises the wrong deadlineCheck the SLA timezone and business hours
    Not knowing the customer's planWrong SLA appliedCheck plan/entitlement in the ticket sidebar

    Practice

    Scenario: Planning your Friday queue.

    Friday 16:00–18:00 uses 2 hours; 6 hours remain. Monday 9:00 + 6 hours = Monday 15:00 IST.
    Practice
    P2.Explain the difference between an SLA and an OLA to a new teammate, with an example of each.
    An SLA is our promise to the customer, e.g., "High priority tickets get a first reply within 2 business hours." An OLA is a promise between internal teams that helps us keep the SLA, e.g., "Engineering acknowledges escalated bugs within 4 business hours."

    Module 02 · Prioritisation

    Setting Priority Correctly

    SLA targets depend on priority, so choosing it correctly is one of the most important things an agent does. Learn the impact × urgency method, priority definitions, and how priority differs from severity.

    🚦 Impact × Urgency

    Figure 2.1 · Priority matrix

    💥 Impact

    How many users, how much of the business is affected, money or data at risk.

    ⏰ Urgency

    How time-sensitive: deadline today, getting worse, no workaround.

    📋 Priority Definitions

    PriorityDefinitionExamples
    P1 · UrgentService down or major function broken for many users; data loss; security incidentNobody can log in; payments failing for all; data breach
    P2 · HighMajor function broken for one customer/team with business impact; no workaroundCustomer can't run payroll today; integration down for one enterprise account
    P3 · NormalFunction impaired; workaround exists; limited impactExport slow; report column wrong; bug with easy workaround
    P4 · LowQuestions, cosmetic issues, feature requestsHow-to questions; typo in UI; "please add dark mode"
    💡
    When unsure between two levels, ask: "Is anything stopping their work or costing money right now?" If yes, go higher.

    ⚖️ Priority vs Severity

    SeverityPriority
    MeaningHow bad the problem is technicallyHow soon it must be handled
    Set byOften Tier 2 / Engineering (for bugs)Support (for tickets), Product/Engineering (for bug fixes)
    Based onImpact on the systemImpact + urgency + business factors
    Example of differenceRare crash in an unused admin page: high severity…but low priority if almost nobody uses it
    📌
    A typo on the login page has low severity but can be high priority just before a big launch.

    🧪 Practical Examples

    TicketPriorityReason
    "We were charged twice this month." (polite tone)HighMoney impact on the customer
    "WHY IS THE BUTTON BLUE!!!" (angry tone)LowCosmetic, despite tone
    "All 40 of our users get Error 500 on login."UrgentWhole account blocked
    "Report export takes 2 minutes instead of 10 seconds."NormalSlow but works
    "Someone logged into my account from another country."UrgentSecurity

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    • Setting priority by tone or customer seniority instead of impact.
    • Marking everything High "to be safe", which makes priority meaningless.
    • Never revisiting priority when new facts appear (it's fine to raise or lower it).
    • Ignoring contract plans: enterprise customers may have different priority rules.

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Set priority for: (a) "Can I change my invoice email?" (b) "Our checkout page is down, we're losing sales." (c) "One user can't upload files; others can; deadline tomorrow."
    (a) Low (how-to). (b) Urgent (business stopped, revenue loss). (c) High (one user blocked with a near deadline; check for a workaround).
    Practice
    P2.A customer writes in capitals and demands Urgent for a spelling mistake in a report title. How do you handle the priority and the reply?

    Scenario: Pressure from an upset customer.

    Keep priority Low/Normal based on impact, but reply promptly and with empathy, explain that it's logged with the team, offer a workaround if any (rename the title in the export), and give a timeline. Priority follows impact, not tone.

    Understanding Support Queues

    A queue is the list of work waiting for an agent. Learn how queues are built with views, the main ways of ordering them, and how tickets get routed to the right queue.

    📥 What Is a Queue?

    A queue is a list of tickets, chats or calls waiting to be handled, usually filtered by team, channel or priority. Agents "work the queue" by picking up and resolving items in a sensible order.

    ToolQueue is called
    ZendeskView
    FreshdeskTicket view / filter
    IntercomInbox / team inbox
    Salesforce Service CloudQueue / list view

    🗂️ Building Queues with Views

    example view: "Billing – needs reply"Conditions:
  Status      < Solved
  Group       = Billing
  Requester replied last = yes
Sort:  Next SLA breach (soonest first)
Columns: Subject · Priority · Requester · SLA · Updated
    • Unassigned: new work no one owns yet.
    • My open tickets: everything you own.
    • Needs reply / customer replied: tickets waiting on you.
    • SLA breaching soon: due in the next hour.
    • Pending > X days: candidates for follow-up or auto-close.

    🔢 Ordering a Queue

    FIFO (oldest first)Priority-basedSLA-based (time remaining)
    RuleFirst in, first outHighest priority firstClosest to breach first
    StrengthFair; simpleCritical issues firstBest for meeting targets
    WeaknessUrgent issues may waitLow items can starveNeeds SLAs configured correctly
    Common useSmall teams, one priority levelMixed severityMost modern help desks
    ✅
    Most teams combine them: SLA time remaining first, then priority, then oldest.

    🧭 Routing Tickets into Queues

    Routing methodHow it works
    Rules / triggersKeywords, form fields or customer plan send tickets to a group
    Skills-basedMatches language, product or tier to agent skills
    Round robinAssigns to agents in turn
    Capacity / load-balancedAssigns to the agent with the most free capacity
    Manual pullAgents pick from a shared queue
    ⚠️
    Wrongly routed tickets lose time. If a ticket lands in your queue but belongs to another team, re-route it immediately with a note; don't let it sit.

    📦 Backlog Basics

    The backlog is the number of open tickets waiting. If more tickets arrive than are solved, it grows.

    backlog checkMonday backlog: 120
Created this week: 600
Solved this week:  540
Friday backlog = 120 + 600 − 540 = 180   ← growing by 60
    💡
    A growing backlog is an early warning sign: more staffing, better macros or KB articles, or a product fix may be needed.

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Design three views you'd want as a Tier 1 agent at the start of your shift, with conditions and sort order.
    1) "Breaching in 1 hour": status < Solved, SLA due < 1h, sort by SLA. 2) "Customer replied – mine": assignee = me, requester replied last, sort by oldest update. 3) "Unassigned – my group": assignee empty, group = Tier 1, sort by SLA then priority.
    Practice
    P2.Backlog is 200 on Monday. 450 tickets are created and 500 solved during the week. What's Friday's backlog, and is that good?
    200 + 450 − 500 = 150. It shrank by 50, so the team is solving more than it receives (good).

    Module 04 · Multichannel

    Prioritising Across Chats, Emails & Phone Calls

    Your role card says "prioritising incoming chats, emails, and phone calls against response-time targets". Each channel has different speed expectations. Learn how to balance them.

    📡 Channel Expectations

    ChannelTypical response targetCustomer expectationAgent focus
    📞 PhoneAnswer within ~20–60 secondsImmediate, live conversationOne call at a time
    💬 Live chatFirst reply within ~30–60 secondsNear real-time2–3 chats at once
    📱 Messaging (WhatsApp, in-app)MinutesFast but can be asynchronousSeveral conversations
    📧 Email / web formHours (per SLA)Complete answerThorough, one at a time
    📢 Social media (public)Within ~1 hourFast public acknowledgementMove to private channel

    ⚡ Real-time vs Asynchronous

    ⚡ Real-time (phone, chat)

    • Customer is waiting right now
    • Seconds matter; abandonment if slow
    • Must be staffed continuously

    📬 Asynchronous (email, forms)

    • Customer expects a reply later
    • Hours matter; quality and completeness count more
    • Can be worked between live contacts
    💡
    General rule: live customers first (calls, chats), then emails closest to SLA breach, then the rest. But an email about to breach an Urgent SLA can outrank a simple chat; always check SLA time.

    💬 Chat Concurrency

    Concurrency is how many chats an agent handles at once. More chats = more throughput, but slower replies and more mistakes.

    ConcurrencyWorks when
    1Complex, technical or upset customers
    2Typical mix
    3+Simple, repetitive questions with good macros
    ⚠️
    If replies in any chat slow down past ~1–2 minutes, lower your concurrency (set yourself as "busy" so new chats aren't routed to you).

    🔄 Blended Agents: Juggling Channels

    A blended agent handles more than one channel in the same shift. A practical order:

    1. Incoming phone call (if you're on the phone rota).
    2. Active chats: never leave a live customer waiting.
    3. Emails about to breach SLA (especially Urgent/High).
    4. Customer replies on tickets you own.
    5. New emails by SLA time → priority → oldest.
    6. Follow-ups, internal notes, KB updates.
    📌
    Tools like Salesforce Omni-Channel, Zendesk and Freshdesk route work based on your status and capacity. Keep your status accurate (Available, Busy, Away), or the system will send you work you can't take.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    • Leaving chat customers silent while answering an email.
    • Taking too many chats and making copy-paste errors.
    • Forgetting to set status to Away on breaks, so calls and chats are routed and missed.
    • Answering easy emails first while High-priority ones approach breach.

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.You have 2 active chats, a High email breaching in 15 minutes, and 10 Normal emails. A third chat arrives. What do you do?

    Scenario: Busy Monday morning.

    Keep both active chats moving with short updates. Don't accept the third chat (set status busy, or let routing send it to an available colleague). Take 5 minutes to answer or meaningfully acknowledge the High email before breach. Then continue with the Normal emails by SLA time.
    Practice
    P2.Explain why phone and chat are usually handled before emails, and give one exception.
    Phone and chat customers are waiting live and will abandon if ignored; email customers expect a later reply. Exception: an Urgent/High email (e.g., outage or payment failure) close to SLA breach can need attention before a simple how-to chat.

    How SLAs Work in Zendesk, Freshdesk, Intercom & Salesforce

    Every major help desk has built-in SLA features. Learn where SLA policies live, how breach timers appear to agents, and which statuses pause the clock.

    ⚙️ SLA Policies

    An SLA policy is a rule set an admin creates: which tickets it applies to (conditions) and which targets apply (by priority).

    example SLA policyName:       Enterprise Support
Applies to: Organization plan = Enterprise
Targets:
  Priority   First reply   Next reply   Resolution   Hours
  Urgent     30 min        1 h          4 h          Calendar (24×7)
  High       1 h           2 h          8 h          Business
  Normal     4 h           8 h          2 days       Business
  Low        8 h           1 day        5 days       Business
Escalation: notify team lead at 75% of target; manager on breach
    📌
    Policies are checked in order; a ticket gets the first matching policy. Enterprise rules usually sit above the default policy.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    • Changing priority just to get a longer SLA (managers notice in audits).
    • Setting Pending to dodge a breach when no question was asked.
    • Not updating priority when impact changes, leaving the ticket on the wrong SLA.
    • Ignoring 75% warning alerts until the breach.

    Practice

Many teams would send C a fast acknowledgement first because of its impact, then handle B. Explain the reasoning: prevent new breaches on high-impact tickets first.
    Practice
    P2.A teammate sets tickets to Pending right after reading them "so the SLA doesn't breach". What's wrong, and what should they do?

    Scenario: Spotted during a peer review.

    Pending should mean the customer must act. Using it without asking anything hides real wait time, misleads reports and frustrates customers. Instead: reply meaningfully (answer or a specific question), escalate if stuck, and tell the lead if workload makes SLAs impossible.

    Module 06 · Real-World Workflow

    A Real-World Day: Working the Queue

    The step-by-step routine experienced agents follow every shift to protect SLAs, plus time-boxing, ownership and handover habits.

    🗓️ The Shift Workflow

    Figure 6.1 · Handover → Incidents → Breaching → Urgent/High → Replies → New → Follow-ups
    1. Read the handover notes from the previous shift.
    2. Check incidents: status page, incident channel, known-issue macros.
    3. Clear tickets breaching soon.
    4. Handle Urgent / High priority.
    5. Answer customer replies on tickets you own.
    6. Take new tickets: SLA time → priority → oldest.
    7. End of shift: follow-ups, tidy statuses, write handover.

    🎫 Working Each Ticket

    Real-world ticket workflow: Receive → Read history → Set priority → Triage → Respond → Escalate or resolve → Set correct status → Close the loop.

    StepDone well
    Read historyRead every message and internal note first
    Priority & fieldsCorrect priority, type, tags, contact reason
    RespondAnswer, ask all questions at once, or give a plan with a time
    StatusPending only if the customer must act; On-hold if waiting on another team
    Follow-upReminder set for every promise

    ⏳ Time-boxing & Ownership

    ⏱️
    Time-box
    If stuck for ~15–20 min at Tier 1, escalate with notes rather than letting the ticket stall.
    🍒
    No cherry-picking
    Don't skip hard tickets for easy ones; hard ones then breach.
    🙋
    Ownership
    Once you touch a ticket, see it through or hand it over clearly.
    📢
    Ask early
    Tell your lead when the queue is beyond your capacity, before breaches pile up.

    🔁 Shift Handover

    handover noteShift: 09:00–18:00 IST · Agent: Manasa
Incidents: Payment delays (INC-221) resolved 14:10; macro "Payments › Delay" retired
Watch:     #48122 (Urgent, Acme) waiting on Eng, update promised by 20:00 IST
           #48190 (High) customer replying today; reset link sent
Trends:    6 tickets about CSV export failing after 15:00, tagged csv_export, Tier 2 informed
Backlog:   Tier 1 open = 42 (start 55)
    ⚠️
    Promises without a handover are the most common reason for missed updates overnight.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    MistakeBetter habit
    Starting with the easiest ticketsStart with SLA risk and priority
    Not checking for incidents firstStatus page before troubleshooting
    Forgetting promised updatesSet reminders; handover notes
    Holding tickets you can't solveTime-box and escalate
    Silent when overloadedRaise it early with the lead

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice

    Practice
    P2.You've spent 25 minutes on a Tier 1 ticket and are no closer to the cause, while 6 tickets approach breach. What do you do?

    Scenario: Mid-shift, queue growing.

    Stop and escalate the stuck ticket with full notes (what you tried, evidence) and tell the customer the next step and time. Then return to the breaching tickets. Time-boxing protects the whole queue.

    Module 07 · Measuring

    Queue & SLA Metrics

    The numbers managers use to judge queue health: SLA compliance, FRT, resolution time, AHT, FCR, backlog, CSAT and occupancy, with worked calculations.

    📊 Key Metrics

    MetricDefinition
    SLA compliance (achievement)% of tickets that met their SLA target
    First Response Time (FRT)Average/median time to first human reply
    Average Resolution Time (ART)Average time from created to solved
    Average Handle Time (AHT)Average active time spent per contact (talk/typing + after-work)
    First Contact Resolution (FCR)% resolved in one interaction
    BacklogOpen tickets waiting
    CSAT% positive satisfaction ratings
    Occupancy% of available time agents spend handling contacts
    Abandonment rate (chat/phone)% of customers who leave before being answered

    🧮 Worked Calculations

    examplesSLA compliance:  460 of 500 tickets met FRT
                 460 / 500 = 92%

FCR:             240 of 400 solved with one reply
                 240 / 400 = 60%

AHT (phone):     talk 6 min + hold 1 min + after-call work 2 min = 9 min

Occupancy:       handling time 6 h of 7.5 h available
                 6 / 7.5 = 80%

Abandonment:     30 of 600 chats left before answer
                 30 / 600 = 5%
    💡
    Medians are often better than averages for time metrics: one ticket stuck for 10 days can make the average look terrible while most tickets were fast.

    ⚖️ Metric Comparisons

    PairDifference
    FRT vs Resolution timeFirst reply speed vs time to fully fix
    AHT vs Resolution timeActive agent effort vs total elapsed time (including waiting)
    FCR vs Reopen rateSolved in one contact vs solved tickets that came back
    CSAT vs NPSSatisfaction with one interaction vs overall loyalty
    Backlog vs VolumeOpen tickets waiting vs new tickets arriving

    🖥️ Reading a Dashboard

    Dashboard showsWhat it might meanAction
    SLA compliance dropping on MondaysWeekend backlog piles upWeekend cover or Monday staffing
    FRT good, CSAT fallingFast but unhelpful first repliesCoach on quality of first reply
    Backlog rising for 3 weeksVolume > capacityStaffing, deflection, product fixes
    High chat abandonment 12:00–14:00Lunch-break understaffingStagger breaks

    📈 Improving Your Numbers

    • SLA compliance: sort by SLA; acknowledge urgent items quickly; escalate early.
    • FCR: ask for all information in one message; answer every question.
    • AHT: know macros and KB; but never rush customers to cut time.
    • CSAT: empathy, clear answers, confirm the fix.
    ⚠️
    Optimising one metric can hurt another (e.g., rushing for AHT lowers FCR and CSAT). Aim for balance.

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Calculate: 1,200 tickets this month, 1,080 met SLA. 300 chats offered, 24 abandoned. What are SLA compliance and abandonment rate?
    SLA compliance = 1,080 / 1,200 = 90%. Abandonment = 24 / 300 = 8%.
    Practice
    P2.Your FRT is the best on the team, but your CSAT is the lowest. What might be happening and how would you fix it?

    Scenario: Monthly 1:1 with your team lead.

    Likely fast but shallow first replies (generic macros, missing questions, not solving). Fix: personalise replies, ask for all info at once, solve in the first reply where possible, confirm the fix, and focus on quality even if FRT rises slightly.

    Module 08 · Under Pressure

    Handling Volume Spikes, Incidents & Escalation Timelines

    When tickets flood in after an outage or launch, normal queue rules aren't enough. Learn how teams protect SLAs during spikes and keep escalations on schedule.

    🌊 What Causes Spikes

    CauseExample
    Outage / incidentLogin down for 30 minutes
    Product releaseNew UI confuses users
    Billing runMonthly renewals and failed payments
    Marketing campaignBig sale, many new customers
    SeasonalTax season, festive sales (Diwali, year-end)

    🚨 Incident Mode

    1. Confirm the incident with the incident channel/lead; check the status page.
    2. Use the approved incident macro with current information.
    3. Pause low-priority work (feature requests, How-tos can wait a few hours).
    4. Use bulk updates for related tickets.
    5. Post updates at the agreed interval (e.g., every 30–60 minutes).
    6. After resolution: notify all affected customers and return to normal order.
    ⚠️
    During incidents, never guess causes or ETAs. Use only the official statement.

    🔗 Problem & Incident Linking

    ToolFeature
    Zendesk"Problem" ticket with linked "Incident" tickets; solving the problem can solve all incidents
    FreshdeskTracker ticket with linked tickets
    IntercomTracker ticket linked to conversations
    SalesforceParent case with child cases
    ✅
    Linking turns 200 individual replies into one update, protecting SLAs for everyone.

    ⏫ Escalations & OLAs

    When a ticket is escalated, the customer's SLA keeps running (depending on configuration). OLAs make sure the next team acts in time.

    Escalation pathExample OLA
    Tier 1 → Tier 2Pick up within 4 business hours
    Tier 2 → Engineering (bug)Acknowledge within 1 business day; Urgent within 1 hour
    Agent → Team lead (exception)Decision within same day
    💡
    After escalating, you still own customer communication unless the ticket is reassigned. Keep promised updates going.

    🚫 Common Mistakes

    • Answering spike tickets one by one with different wording.
    • Forgetting to link tickets, so customers aren't told when it's fixed.
    • Escalating without telling the customer what happens next.
    • Not alerting the lead when 3–5 similar tickets arrive quickly.

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Fifty tickets arrive in 20 minutes saying the mobile app won't open. Write your plan for the next hour.

    Scenario: Spike after a mobile release.

    Alert the lead/incident channel immediately with examples. Once confirmed, create/link to a problem/tracker ticket; apply the incident macro (acknowledge, workaround if any, update time); pause low-priority tickets; bulk-update linked tickets as updates arrive; keep answering urgent unrelated tickets by SLA; after the fix, notify all linked customers.
    Practice
    P2.You escalated a High ticket to Tier 2 four hours ago (OLA: 4 business hours) and nobody has picked it up. The customer SLA is at 75%. What do you do?
    Ping the Tier 2 escalation channel/lead with the ticket link and SLA status; inform your own lead; send the customer an update with honest next steps; document in internal notes.

    Module 09 · Workforce Basics

    Capacity, Staffing & Scheduling Basics

    Why queues grow or shrink comes down to capacity. Learn the basics of workload forecasting, occupancy, schedules and how agents can help their team plan.

    ⚖️ Volume vs Capacity

    capacity checkAgents on shift:          6
Productive hours each:    6
Avg handle time/ticket:   12 minutes  → 5 tickets per hour
Capacity = 6 × 6 × 5 = 180 tickets/day

Incoming volume:          210 tickets/day  → backlog grows by ~30/day
    📌
    When volume > capacity, the backlog grows and SLAs suffer, no matter how hard individuals work. The fixes are more capacity, less volume (deflection), or faster handling (better tools).

    📈 Forecasting Workload

    • Look at past volume by hour and day of week (Mondays and mornings are often busiest).
    • Adjust for known events: releases, billing runs, campaigns, holidays.
    • Workforce management tools (and some help desks) forecast automatically.
    • Classic contact-centre staffing uses the Erlang C formula to estimate agents needed for a target answer time.

    🔋 Occupancy & Utilisation

    OccupancyUtilisation
    DefinitionTime handling contacts ÷ time available for contactsTime on productive work ÷ total paid time
    Healthy range (typical)~75–85%Varies by team
    Too high meansBurnout, rushed repliesNo time for training or KB work
    Too low meansOverstaffed for current volumeIdle time

    🗓️ Schedules & Coverage

    • Staggered shifts cover peaks (e.g., more agents 10:00–13:00).
    • Follow-the-sun: teams in different timezones hand over (e.g., India → Europe → US).
    • Stagger breaks and lunches so chat/phone queues aren't empty.
    • On-call cover for Urgent tickets outside business hours (24×7 SLAs).

    🙋 How Agents Help Planning

    🏷️
    Tag accurately
    Correct contact reasons show where volume comes from.
    📣
    Report trends
    "20 tickets about X today" can trigger a KB article or product fix.
    📚
    Write KB
    Each good article deflects future tickets.
    ⏰
    Keep status accurate
    Routing and reports depend on it.

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.5 agents, 6 productive hours each, AHT 10 minutes. Incoming volume 200/day. Does the team keep up?
    Each agent handles 6 per hour → 36/day. 5 × 36 = 180/day capacity < 200 volume → backlog grows by about 20 per day.
    Practice
    P2.Chat abandonment spikes 13:00–14:00 every day. Suggest two fixes.
    Stagger lunch breaks so fewer agents are away at once; shift one agent's schedule to cover 13:00–14:00; add a chatbot/KB suggestions to deflect simple questions during that hour.

    Module 10 · Interviews

    Interview Preparation: SLAs & Queue Management

    Questions interviewers ask about SLAs, prioritisation and queue management, with model answers, a comparison cheat sheet and scenario questions.

    🎤 Concept Questions

    QuestionModel answer
    What is an SLA?A commitment on response/resolution times, often by priority; contractual for some customers.
    What is FRT?Time from ticket creation to the first human reply.
    Business vs calendar hours?Business-hour SLAs run only during working hours; calendar-hour SLAs run 24×7.
    What pauses the SLA clock?Usually waiting-on-customer statuses and time outside business hours (configuration-dependent).
    SLA vs OLA?SLA is with the customer; OLA is between internal teams to support the SLA.

    🚦 Prioritisation Questions

    QuestionModel answer
    How do you set priority?By impact and urgency, not tone: how many affected, business/money impact, deadline, workaround.
    How do you work a full queue?SLA risk first → Urgent/High → customer replies → new tickets oldest first; live chats/calls throughout.
    Chats vs emails: which first?Live channels first since customers are waiting, unless an urgent email is about to breach.
    What do you do when you can't meet SLAs?Prioritise by impact, acknowledge customers, escalate early, and tell the lead.

    📊 Metrics Questions

    QuestionModel answer
    Name key support metrics.SLA compliance, FRT, resolution time, AHT, FCR, backlog, CSAT, abandonment.
    How is SLA compliance calculated?Tickets that met SLA ÷ tickets with an SLA × 100.
    What if FRT is great but CSAT is poor?First replies are fast but not helpful; improve quality and resolution.
    What is occupancy?Share of available time spent handling contacts; healthy ~75–85%.

    📋 Comparison Cheat Sheet

    PairKey difference
    SLA vs OLA vs SLOCustomer promise vs internal team promise vs internal target metric
    Priority vs SeverityHow soon to act vs how bad it is
    FRT vs Resolution timeFirst reply vs full fix
    Business vs Calendar hoursWorking hours only vs 24×7
    Pending vs On-holdWaiting on customer vs waiting on third party/internal team
    FIFO vs Priority vs SLA orderingOldest first vs most important first vs closest to breach first
    Phone/Chat vs EmailReal-time vs asynchronous
    AHT vs Resolution timeAgent effort vs total elapsed time

    🧩 Scenario Questions

    Practice
    P1."It's 5:45 pm. You have 3 tickets breaching at 6 pm and a customer on chat. What do you do?"
    Keep the chat moving with short updates; meaningfully reply to the 3 breaching tickets, starting with the highest impact (or acknowledge with a plan if a full answer isn't possible); if you can't, ask a colleague/lead for help; note open promises in your handover.
    Practice
    P2."A VIP customer demands Urgent priority for a minor issue. How do you respond?"
    Acknowledge their importance and respond quickly and personally, but set priority based on impact per policy. If the account has a contractual SLA or special handling, follow it; involve the account owner/CSM if needed.
    Practice
    P3."How would you reduce SLA breaches on your team?"
    Sort views by SLA, acknowledge urgent tickets quickly, use macros and KB to speed answers, time-box and escalate early, stagger breaks to cover peaks, review breach reports for patterns (time of day, ticket type), and suggest KB articles or product fixes for top contact reasons.

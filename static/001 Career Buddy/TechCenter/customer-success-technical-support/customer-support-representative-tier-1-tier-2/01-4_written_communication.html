    Module 01 · Foundations

    Why Writing Is the Core Support Skill

    Most support happens in writing: email, chat, help-centre articles and internal notes. Learn the three qualities of good support writing and a five-part reply structure that works almost every time.

    ✍️ Writing in Support

    For a support representative, your writing is the company. Customers judge the product, the brand and you from the words on their screen.

    Where you writeReaderGoal
    Email repliesCustomerSolve fully in one message
    Live chatCustomer (waiting now)Fast, short, clear
    Help-centre articlesMany customersLet people help themselves
    Macros / templatesYour teammates (then customers)Consistent, reusable answers
    Internal notes & escalationsColleagues, engineersFull context, no guessing
    💡
    Good writing directly improves the numbers you're measured on: fewer back-and-forth replies (better FCR), faster resolutions and higher CSAT.

    🎯 Clear, Fast & Jargon-Free

    Your role card asks for "clear, fast, and jargon-free phrasing using knowledge base macros/templates". Each word matters:

    🔍
    Clear
    The customer understands it on first read and knows exactly what to do next.
    ⚡
    Fast
    You reply quickly, using macros and KB links, without sacrificing quality.
    🗣️
    Jargon-free
    No internal or technical terms the customer wouldn't use themselves.
    📚
    Macros/templates
    Reusable, approved text that keeps answers consistent and quick.
    📌
    Each of these gets its own module in this course: Clear & Jargon-Free (02), Writing Fast (03), Macros & Templates (04), Knowledge Base Writing (05).

    🧱 The Five-Part Reply

    Figure 1.1 · The five-part reply structure
    1. Greeting with their name.
    2. Acknowledge the issue in one sentence, showing you read it.
    3. Answer early: the solution or the key information.
    4. Next steps: what you'll do, what they should do, and when.
    5. Friendly close, inviting them to reply.
    💡
    Many customers read on their phone and only see the preview. Put the answer in the first three lines.

    👥 Know Your Reader

    ReaderAdjust by…
    Non-technical userPlain words, step-by-step, screenshots
    Developer / IT adminPrecise technical terms are fine; include IDs, error codes
    Business owner / managerLead with impact and outcome; keep detail optional
    Non-native English speakerShort sentences, no idioms, simple vocabulary
    Upset customerEmpathy first, then a clear plan
    📌
    "Jargon-free" means free of jargon for this reader. Saying "HTTP 401" to a developer is helpful; to a shop owner it's confusing.

    ✅ Accuracy Before Speed

    • Double-check names, amounts, dates, links and account details before sending.
    • Never guess an answer; if unsure, check the KB or ask a colleague, then reply.
    • Test any steps you give (click through them yourself if possible).
    • Don't promise what you can't guarantee (fix dates, refunds outside policy).
    ⚠️
    A fast wrong answer costs more time than a slightly slower right one: the customer comes back, now more frustrated.

    Module 02 · Clarity

    Plain Language & Removing Jargon

    Practical techniques to make any reply easy to understand: short sentences, active voice, everyday words, numbered steps and analogies for technical ideas.

    📏 Plain Language Rules

    RuleBeforeAfter
    Short sentences (under ~20 words)Once you have navigated to the settings area and located the billing tab you will be able to see the option to download.Go to Settings → Billing. Click Download.
    Active voiceYour plan has been upgraded by our team.We've upgraded your plan.
    Everyday wordsUtilise / Facilitate / CommenceUse / Help / Start
    One idea per paragraph(A long block mixing cause, fix and billing)Cause. Fix. Billing note. Separate short paragraphs.
    Lead with the pointAfter investigating thoroughly, we found…Your export is fixed. Here's what happened…
    💡
    Read your reply aloud. If you run out of breath in a sentence, split it.

    🔁 Jargon → Plain English

    JargonPlain version
    "Clear your cache and cookies.""Clear your browser's saved files (here's how…)."
    "We pushed a hotfix to prod.""We've released a fix. It's live now."
    "It's a known P2 in the backlog.""Our team knows about this and plans to fix it."
    "The webhook endpoint returned 500s.""The connection to your other system failed on our side."
    "Your SSO IdP config is wrong.""Your company's login settings need a small change; your IT team can do it."
    "Escalated to L2.""I've passed this to our specialist team."
    "Ticket is pending.""I'm waiting for your reply on the question above."
    ⚠️
    Internal acronyms (L2, P1, SLA, KB, RCA) should never appear in customer replies unless the customer used them first.

    💡 Explaining Technical Things

    When you must explain a technical idea, use an analogy or describe the effect instead of the mechanism:

    ConceptAnalogy / effect-based explanation
    Cache"Your browser keeps copies of pages to load faster, like a photocopy of a notice board. Sometimes the copy is out of date."
    Cookies / session"Like a cloakroom token that proves you've already logged in."
    Two-factor authentication"A second lock: after your password, a code on your phone proves it's really you."
    Sync delay"Like a courier: changes are collected every 15 minutes and delivered in batches."
    Rate limit"Like a counter that can only serve so many people per minute; requests above that wait their turn."
    📌
    Ask: does the customer need to understand this, or just do something? Often, the steps are enough and the explanation can be one short line.

    👣 Writing Steps Customers Can Follow

    • Number the steps; one action per step.
    • Use the exact button and menu names as they appear on screen, in bold.
    • Use arrows for paths: Settings → Billing → Invoices.
    • Say what they'll see after the step ("A green banner confirms it's saved").
    • Add a screenshot or GIF for anything more than 3–4 steps.

    ❌ Hard to follow

    Go into settings and find the team bit and then you can invite people by email from there, make sure you choose the role too.

    ✅ Easy to follow

    1. Click Settings → Team.
    2. Click Invite member.
    3. Enter their email and choose a Role.
    4. Click Send invite. They'll get an email within a minute.

    ✂️ Editing for Clarity

    A quick self-edit routine before sending:

    1. Cut filler: "just", "basically", "in order to" → "to", "at this point in time" → "now".
    2. Replace any jargon.
    3. Check every question the customer asked is answered.
    4. Bold the one action the customer must take.
    5. Proofread names, numbers and links; click each link.
    ✅
    Tools like the spell-check in your help desk or Grammarly help, but they don't catch a wrong amount or a broken link; only you can.

    Module 03 · Speed

    Writing Fast Without Losing Quality

    Speed matters, especially in chat. Learn the tools and habits that let experienced agents reply quickly: macros, text expanders, shortcuts, KB links and smart chat techniques.

    ⚡ Why Speed Matters

    ChannelTypical expectation
    Live chatFirst reply within ~30–60 seconds; no long silences
    EmailWithin hours (per SLA)
    Social mediaWithin an hour or less for public posts
    Phone (follow-up email)Same day
    📌
    Speed is tracked as First Response Time and Average Handle Time. But speed without accuracy raises reopen rates, so both matter.

    🧰 Your Speed Toolkit

    📋
    Macros / canned replies
    Approved answers for common questions, inserted in one click.
    ⌨️
    Text expanders
    Type a short code like ;ty and it expands to a full sentence. Many help desks have this built in.
    🔗
    KB links
    Link a well-written article instead of retyping long steps.
    📝
    Personal snippets
    Your own saved phrases for greetings, holds and closings.
    🤖
    AI suggestions
    Help desk AI can draft replies; always review and edit before sending.
    ⚠️
    AI-drafted replies can sound confident but be wrong. Check facts, tone and links every time; you're responsible for what you send.

    💬 Chat Speed Techniques

    • Send a quick greeting immediately, then investigate: "Hi Asha! Let me check that for you."
    • Break long answers into 2–3 short messages instead of one big block.
    • Tell them when you need time: "Give me about 2 minutes to look at your account."
    • Ask for all needed info in one message.
    • Use ready snippets for common lines (hold, transfer, closing).
    • Handle 2–3 chats at once only when each is simple; drop to one for complex or upset customers.
    Chat openingHi Asha! Thanks for reaching out. I'm checking your order now. This will take about a minute.

    ⌨️ Typing & Keyboard Shortcuts

    ShortcutSaves time by…
    Ctrl/Cmd + KInserting a link (many editors)
    Ctrl/Cmd + BBold text
    Ctrl/Cmd + Shift + VPasting without formatting
    Ctrl/Cmd + ZUndo
    Win + Shift + S / Cmd + Shift + 4Screenshots
    Help desk shortcutsMost tools list them under ? (e.g., next ticket, apply macro)
    💡
    Chat roles often test typing: aim for 40+ words per minute with high accuracy. Ten minutes a day on a typing-practice site makes a big difference within a month.

    ⚖️ Speed vs Quality

    🐇 Too fast

    • Unedited macro, wrong name
    • Missed second question
    • Customer replies again → slower overall

    🐢 Too slow

    • Rewriting standard answers from scratch
    • Over-long replies
    • SLA breaches
    ✅
    The sweet spot: macro + one personal line + check every question is answered. Fast and high quality.

    🏋️ Practice & Quick Check

    Practice
    P1.Create three text-expander snippets (shortcut + text) you'd use every day.
    ;hi → "Hi {name}, thanks for reaching out!" ;chk → "Let me check that for you. This will take about 2 minutes." ;cls → "Is there anything else I can help you with today?"
    Practice
    P2.A chat customer sends a long message with three questions. How do you reply quickly but completely?
    Acknowledge immediately ("Thanks, Ravi, I'll go through all three"), then answer each in a short numbered message or one message with numbered answers, using KB links for detail, and confirm all three are resolved before closing.

    Module 04 · Reusable Text

    Using & Building Macros and Templates

    Macros (also called canned responses or templates) are the backbone of fast, consistent support. Learn how to use them well, personalise them, and write new ones your whole team can rely on.

    📋 What Macros Are

    A macro is saved, pre-approved reply text, often with automatic actions (set status, add tags). Different tools use different names:

    ToolName
    ZendeskMacro
    FreshdeskCanned response (text) / Scenario (actions)
    IntercomMacro
    SalesforceQuick Text (text) / Macro (actions)
    ⚡
    Speed
    Answer common questions in seconds.
    🎯
    Consistency
    Every customer gets the same correct information.
    🧑‍🎓
    Onboarding
    New agents give expert answers from day one.
    📊
    Clean data
    Actions in macros keep tags and statuses consistent.

    ✨ Personalising Every Macro

    An unedited macro sounds robotic. Add a personal touch in 10 seconds:

    • Use their name (check the placeholder filled correctly).
    • Add one line that refers to their situation: "I can see you've already tried resetting it twice."
    • Remove sections that don't apply.
    • Adjust tone to match the customer (formal vs casual, upset vs happy).

    🤖 Unedited macro

    Hi there, thank you for contacting support. To reset your password please click the link below…

    🙂 Personalised

    Hi Karthik, sorry you're locked out right before your meeting! Here's a fresh reset link, valid for 30 minutes…

    🧱 Anatomy of a Good Macro

    macro: Billing › Refund › ApprovedName:    Billing › Refund › Approved (within 30 days)
Use when: Refund requested within 30 days and approved

Hi {{ticket.requester.first_name}},

[PERSONAL LINE: acknowledge their reason]

I've processed a full refund of {{amount}} to your original
payment method. It usually appears within 5–7 working days,
depending on your bank.

Your access stays active until {{end_date}}. If you'd like to
come back later, your data will be kept for 90 days.

Thanks for giving us a try,
{{current_user.first_name}}

Actions: Status = Solved · Tag = billing_refund · Field: Contact reason = Refund
    • A clear name in a folder path, and a "use when" note.
    • A marked spot for the personal line.
    • Short, plain-language body with the key fact early.
    • Actions that keep data clean.

    🔣 Placeholders & Variables

    Placeholders insert ticket data automatically. Syntax differs by tool, but the idea is the same:

    Placeholder (Zendesk style)Inserts
    {{ticket.requester.first_name}}Customer's first name
    {{ticket.id}}Ticket number
    {{ticket.organization.name}}Customer's company
    {{current_user.first_name}}Your name
    {{ticket.ticket_field_ID}}A custom field value
    ⚠️
    If the data is missing, placeholders can produce "Hi ," or "Hi {{name}}". Always preview before sending; consider a fallback like "Hi there".

    🗂️ Maintaining a Macro Library

    1. Organise by folders: Billing ›, Access ›, Orders ›, Bugs ›.
    2. Name consistently: Area › Topic › Variant.
    3. Give each macro an owner; review quarterly.
    4. Update immediately when the product or policy changes.
    5. Retire unused or duplicate macros (check usage reports).
    6. Suggest new macros when you answer the same thing three times.
    💡
    Spotted an outdated macro? Don't just fix it in your reply; tell the owner or your lead so everyone stops sending the old version.

    Module 05 · Self-Service

    Writing Knowledge Base Articles

    Knowledge base (KB) articles let customers solve problems themselves and power macros and AI bots. Learn article types, structure, titles, visuals and how to keep the KB accurate.

    📚 Why the KB Matters

    🙋
    Self-service
    Many customers prefer to find answers themselves, any time of day.
    📉
    Ticket deflection
    Every customer who solves it alone is a ticket you don't receive.
    🔗
    Faster replies
    Agents link articles instead of retyping steps.
    🤖
    AI bots
    Bots like Zendesk AI agents, Freddy and Fin answer from KB content; bad articles = bad bot answers.
    📌
    In tools: Zendesk Guide, Freshdesk Solutions, Intercom Help Center, Salesforce Knowledge.

    🗂️ Article Types

    TypePurposeExample title
    How-toSteps to complete a taskHow do I invite a teammate?
    TroubleshootingFix a specific problemWhy am I not receiving password reset emails?
    FAQShort answers to common questionsCan I change my plan mid-month?
    ReferenceFacts, limits, listsFile size limits and supported formats
    PolicyRules customers need to knowRefund policy

    🧱 Structure of a Great Article

    article templateTitle: How do I export my contacts to CSV?

Summary (1 sentence):
  Export all or filtered contacts as a CSV file you can open in Excel.

Before you start:
  • You need the Admin or Manager role.

Steps:
  1. Go to Contacts.
  2. (Optional) Apply filters to export only some contacts.
  3. Click Export → CSV.
  4. The file downloads in a few seconds. Large exports (10,000+)
     are emailed to you instead.

Troubleshooting:
  • Don't see Export? Ask your admin for the Manager role.
  • Special characters look wrong in Excel? Use Data → From Text/CSV
    and choose UTF-8.

Related articles:
  • Importing contacts from CSV
  • Understanding user roles
    💡
    One article = one task or problem. If an article covers five things, split it into five.

    🔎 Titles, Search & Visuals

    DoAvoid
    Use the customer's words: "How do I reset my password?"Internal names: "Credential Recovery Procedure"
    Start with a question or action verbVague titles: "Account info"
    Include common search terms and synonymsClever or jokey titles
    Annotated screenshots (arrows/boxes)Screenshots with other customers' data
    Short GIFs for multi-click flowsVideos as the only content (not searchable)
    ⚠️
    Screenshots go out of date fastest. Note the date or product version, and update them after design changes.

    🔄 Knowledge-Centered Service (KCS)

    KCS is a widely used method where agents create and improve knowledge as part of solving tickets, not as a separate project.

    1. Search the KB first when you get a ticket.
    2. Reuse an article if it answers the question; link it.
    3. Improve it if it's unclear or outdated ("flag it or fix it").
    4. Create a new article if nothing exists and the issue will recur.
    ✅
    Mentioning KCS and articles you wrote in an interview shows you think beyond answering one ticket at a time.

    Module 06 · Formatting

    Writing for Each Channel & Formatting Well

    Email, chat, WhatsApp, social media and internal notes each need a different style. Learn channel conventions, formatting that helps readers scan, and grammar habits that keep you professional.

    📧 Email Style

    • One complete reply that answers every question.
    • Keep the original subject line; customers search their inbox for it.
    • Five-part structure; short paragraphs (2–3 lines).
    • Ask for all missing information at once.
    • Attachments: mention them in the text ("I've attached your invoice").

    💬 Chat & Messaging Style

    Chat / WhatsAppWhy
    Short messages, one idea eachEasy to read on small screens
    Reply within seconds; narrate waitsSilence feels like being ignored
    Friendly, slightly informal toneMatches the channel
    Emojis: occasional and simple (🙂 👍), never with upset customersWarmth without seeming unprofessional
    Confirm the fix before closingAvoids repeat chats
    📌
    WhatsApp Business messages outside the 24-hour customer-service window usually need pre-approved templates, so companies maintain these carefully.

    📢 Social Media Style

    • Public replies are seen by everyone; stay calm, short, empathetic.
    • Never post account details publicly; move to DM.
    • Respond quickly; social users expect faster replies.
    • Follow your brand's voice guide and escalation rules for PR-sensitive posts.
    Public replyHi Rohan, sorry your delivery is late. We know that's frustrating. We've sent you a DM so we can check your order details.

    🔒 Internal Notes & Escalations

    Internal writing has a different reader (colleagues and engineers) and a different goal: no guessing.

    internal note templateSummary:   Customer can't export reports since 4 Oct
Impact:    1 account, monthly board report due 7 Oct
Tried:     Incognito, Firefox, smaller date range (works for 7 days,
           fails for 90 days)
Evidence:  Screenshot + console error attached; request ID req_19c2
Customer told: Update by 6 Oct, 12:00 IST
Next:      Escalating to Tier 2 – Reports
    ⚠️
    Before sending, double-check you clicked Internal note, not Public reply. Notes can contain frank details that must never reach the customer.

    🔤 Formatting & Grammar

    ElementUse for
    BoldThe one key action or button name
    Numbered listsSteps in order
    BulletsUnordered options or items
    Short headingsLong emails with several topics
    Links with descriptive text"See the export guide", not "click here"

    Grammar slips that look unprofessional

    • its / it's; your / you're; there / their / they're.
    • Missing capital letters and full stops in emails.
    • Text-speak in email ("pls", "u", "thx").
    • Regional phrases that confuse global readers ("revert back", "do the needful", "prepone").
    💡
    Turn on spell-check in your browser and help desk, and read your reply once before sending. It takes ten seconds.

    Module 07 · Putting It Together

    Common Reply Types & Writing Tests

    Templates for the replies you'll write every day, plus how written communication is tested in interviews and quality reviews.

    ❓ Information Request

    templateHi {{name}},

Thanks for reaching out about [issue]. I'd like to get this fixed
quickly. Could you send me:

  1. A screenshot of the error
  2. The browser you're using (Chrome, Edge, Safari…)
  3. Whether it happens for your teammates too

In the meantime, trying an incognito/private window often fixes
this straight away.

Thanks,
{{agent}}
    💡
    Always offer something useful while waiting (a quick fix to try), so the reply isn't only questions.

    ✅ Solution Reply

    templateHi {{name}},

Good news: [the problem] is fixed.

What happened: [one plain sentence on the cause].
What I did: [the fix].
[If needed: What you need to do: numbered steps.]

[Prevention tip, if useful.]

I'll mark this as solved, but just reply if anything still
looks off and it'll come straight back to me.

Best,
{{agent}}

    📮 Escalation & Bad News Replies

    ⬆️ Escalation

    "I've passed this to our specialist team with your screenshots and the steps you tried, so you won't need to repeat anything. I'll update you by Thursday 2 pm IST. Meanwhile, exporting as CSV still works."

    📰 Bad news

    "I'm sorry. Deleted messages older than 30 days can't be restored, because our backups only cover 30 days. I know that's disappointing. What I can do is show you how to turn on automatic archiving so this can't happen again."

    👋 Closing & Follow-up Replies

    SituationTemplate line
    Confirming the fix"Could you check and let me know it's working on your side?"
    No reply after 3 days"Just checking in. Did the steps above solve it? If I don't hear back, I'll close this on Friday, and you can reply anytime to reopen."
    Closing"Glad we got this sorted! Have a great day."

    Module 08 · Interviews

    Interview Preparation: Written Communication

    Writing-related interview questions and tests, with model answers, a comparison cheat sheet and the writing mistakes reviewers notice most.

    🎤 Concept Questions

    QuestionModel answer
    What makes a good support reply?Greeting, acknowledgement, answer early, clear next steps, friendly close; plain language and every question answered.
    How do you avoid jargon?Write for the reader: everyday words, explain effects not mechanisms, use analogies, no internal acronyms.
    How do you use macros well?Pick the right macro, personalise one line, check placeholders and actions, remove irrelevant parts.
    How do you write fast without errors?Macros, text expanders, KB links; then a 10-second check of names, numbers, links and questions answered.
    What makes a good KB article?Customer-language title, one-sentence summary, numbered steps with exact UI names, troubleshooting, related links.
    Chat vs email writing?Chat: short messages, fast replies, narrate waits. Email: one complete, structured reply.
    What is KCS?Knowledge-Centered Service: search, reuse, improve or create KB content while solving tickets.

    📋 Comparison Cheat Sheet

    PairKey difference
    Chat vs EmailShort, real-time messages vs complete, structured replies
    Macro vs KB articleReusable reply for agents vs self-service page for customers
    Canned response vs Scenario (Freshdesk)Text only vs multiple actions
    Quick Text vs Macro (Salesforce)Text snippet vs multi-action automation
    Active vs Passive voice"We updated your plan" vs "Your plan has been updated"
    Public reply vs Internal noteCustomer-facing vs agents only, with full technical detail
    Jargon vs Plain language"Escalated to L2" vs "Passed to our specialist team"
    Information request vs Solution replyAsks for missing details (with something to try) vs explains cause and fix

    🚫 Common Mistakes & Fixes

    MistakeWhy it hurtsDo this instead

    Module 01

    Foundations of Strategic Technical Advisory

    What the advisory part of the Technical Account Manager role is, how it differs from support, and what you will be able to do by the end of this course.

    🧭 Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    A Technical Account Manager (TAM) does more than answer tickets. In strategic technical advisory you build deep architectural knowledge of the client's enterprise stack and use it to advise on best practices, scalability and security, before problems appear.

    🏗️
    Architecture
    Know how the client's systems fit together.
    ✅
    Best practices
    Proven ways to configure and operate.
    📈
    Scalability
    Keep working as load and data grow.
    🔒
    Security
    Protect data, access and compliance posture.
    🏥
    Analogy: support is the emergency room. The TAM is the family doctor who knows the history, spots risks early and tells you what to change before you fall ill.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Map a client's enterprise stack layer by layer and find its dependencies.
    2. Compare a client's setup with best practices and run a gap review.
    3. Spot scalability bottlenecks and size capacity with simple math.
    4. Advise on access control, data protection and compliance awareness.
    5. Write, prioritise and track clear recommendations on a roadmap.
    6. Explain the same advice to executives and engineers, and handle pushback.

    🕸️ Intermediate: Dependencies & Integrations

    Modern systems rarely fail alone. A dependency map shows what each component needs in order to work.

    ⬆️
    Upstream
    What this component depends on (database, identity provider, payment API).
    ⬇️
    Downstream
    What depends on this component (reports, mobile app, partners).
    ⚠️
    Single points of failure
    Components with no backup path.
    🌩️
    Cascading failure
    One slow service piles up requests and slows the rest.
    textORDER SERVICE depends on:
  → Payments API  (external, no fallback)   RISK: single point of failure
  → Orders DB     (one primary, no replica)  RISK: single point of failure
  → Auth service  (3 instances)              OK
DOWNSTREAM: mobile app, finance reports, partner feed

    🧪 Advanced: Reading an Architecture for Risk

    PatternStrengthTypical advisory focus
    MonolithSimple to runScaling the whole app for one hot feature; risky releases
    MicroservicesIndependent scalingNetwork failures, tracing, too many owners
    Event-driven / queuesAbsorbs spikesMessage loss, ordering, backlog monitoring
    Multi-regionSurvives region lossCost, data consistency, failover tests
    📌
    No pattern is "best". The TAM judges whether the pattern fits the client's size, skills and goals.

    Module 03

    Advising on Best Practices

    Compare how the client works today with proven patterns, rate the gaps and explain the reasons and trade-offs.

    ✅ Basic: Best Practices & Why They Matter

    A best practice is a method that experience has shown works reliably. The TAM compares actual usage with these patterns and explains gaps with reasons, not rules.

    AreaBest practiceRed flag
    EnvironmentsSeparate dev, test and productionTesting directly in production
    ConfigurationSettings in version controlManual, undocumented changes
    ReliabilityRedundancy and tested backupsBackups never restored as a test
    OperationsAlerts on key metrics, runbooksLearning about outages from customers
    ReleasesSmall staged rollouts with rollbackBig-bang upgrade on a Friday evening

    🏛️ Intermediate: The Five Pillars

    ⚙️
    Operational excellence
    Automate, monitor and keep improving.
    🛡️
    Security
    Protect data and identities in every layer.
    🔁
    Reliability
    Recover fast and keep serving users.
    ⚡
    Performance efficiency
    Right resources, low response time.
    💰
    Cost optimisation
    Pay only for what is needed.
    ⚖️
    Pillars compete. More redundancy improves reliability but raises cost. Help the client choose the balance that fits their business.

    🔍 Intermediate: Running a Gap Review

    Figure 3.1 · The four steps of a gap review
    Gap foundRiskEffortPriority
    No tested restore from backupHIGHLowDo first
    Alerts only on "server down"MEDIUMMediumNext quarter
    Inconsistent resource namingLOWLowWhen convenient

    ⚖️ Advanced: Trade-offs & Exceptions

    Sometimes a client has a good reason to deviate. Your job is to make the deviation visible and accepted, not to win an argument.

    🚫 Poor response

    "You must do it our way." Creates resistance and hides real constraints.

    ✅ Strong response

    Explain the risk, offer options, and record the accepted risk with an owner.

    textDECISION RECORD

    Module 04

    Advising on Scalability

    Help clients grow without breaking: find bottlenecks, choose how to scale and size capacity with simple math.

    📈 Basic: What Scaling Means

    Scalability is the ability to keep performing well as load grows, such as 10x users on a sale day.

    ⬆️ Vertical scaling (scale up)

    Give one machine more CPU or RAM. Simple, but has a ceiling and is still one machine.

    ↔️ Horizontal scaling (scale out)

    Add more machines behind a load balancer. Grows further and survives a failure, but the app must be designed for it.

    🔬 Intermediate: Finding Bottlenecks

    MetricWhat it tells youHealthy sign
    CPU / memoryHow close to the limitPeaks below about 70%
    Latency (p95)Speed for the slowest 5% of requestsFlat as load rises
    Error rateShare of failed requestsNear 0% at peak
    ThroughputRequests handled per secondGrows with added servers
    Database queriesMissing cacheSingle-threaded jobsAPI rate limitsNetwork bandwidthStorage growth
    📏
    Measure first. "DB CPU reaches 85% at the 6 pm peak" is stronger advice than "it might be slow".

    🧩 Intermediate: Patterns That Help

    🗃️
    Caching
    Keep frequent answers in fast memory.
    📬
    Queues
    Accept work now, process later; smooths spikes.
    🧍
    Stateless services
    Any server can serve any request.
    🔀
    Replicas / sharding
    Copy or split data to share the load.

    Analogy: a busy restaurant adds cashiers (horizontal scaling), pre-makes popular dishes (caching) and uses an order-ticket system so the kitchen is not overwhelmed (queues).

    🧮 Advanced: Capacity Planning Math

    textPEAK LOAD = 2,000 requests/s    ONE SERVER = 400 requests/s at 100%
Target utilisation = 70%  →  usable per server = 400 x 0.70 = 280
Servers needed = 2,000 / 280 = 7.14  → round up = 8
N+1 redundancy (survive one failure) = 8 + 1 = 9 servers
With 30% growth: 2,600 / 280 = 9.29 → 10, plus 1 spare = 11 servers
    ⚠️
    Run heavy load tests on a staging copy, or in an agreed window with client approval. Never surprise production.

    Advising on Security

    Defense in depth, access control, data protection and compliance awareness, plus the mistakes you will see most often.

    🛡️ Basic: Defense in Depth

    Defense in depth means several layers of protection, so that one failure does not expose everything.

    Figure 5.1 · Each layer backs up the others

    🪪 Intermediate: Access Control

    TermMeaningWhy it matters
    SSOOne company login for many toolsCentral control; easy to remove a leaver
    MFAPassword plus a second proof such as a phone codeA stolen password alone is not enough
    RBACPermissions given by role, not personEnforces least privilege at scale
    Audit logsRecord of who did what and whenInvestigations and compliance evidence

    🗝️ Intermediate: Data Protection & Secrets

    🔐
    Encryption in transit
    TLS for every connection.
    💽
    Encryption at rest
    Disks and databases encrypted.
    🗝️
    Secrets vault
    Keys and passwords never in code or chat.
    🩹
    Patching
    Regular updates with a rollback plan.

    📜 Advanced: Compliance Awareness

    FrameworkConcerned withWhat the TAM does
    SOC 2Security, availability, confidentiality controlsExplain how product features support the controls
    HIPAAProtected health information (US)Advise on safe configuration and access logging
    GDPRPersonal data of EU residentsAdvise on data location, retention and deletion options
    ⚠️
    A TAM gives guidance. Formal audits and legal decisions stay with the client's security and legal teams.

    🚫 Advanced: Common Mistakes

    MistakeBetter approach
    Everyone is an admin "to keep things simple"Role-based access, reviewed quarterly
    API keys pasted into code or chatSecrets vault with rotation
    Service accounts that never expireShort-lived credentials with named owners
    Skipping updates because "it works"Regular patch windows
    Real customer data copied to testMasked or synthetic data

    Module 06

    The Advisory Process & Roadmaps

    Turn findings into clear recommendations, prioritise them and build a roadmap the client will actually follow.

    🔁 The Advisory Cycle

    Figure 6.1 · After follow-up, the cycle starts again

    Advice works best as a repeating cycle. The stack changes, so the review must too.

    ✍️ Intermediate: Writing a Recommendation

    text# Recommendation: add read replicas to the orders database
Observation : DB CPU reaches 85% at the daily 6 pm peak
Risk        : Checkout slows down; worse on sale days
Option A     : Bigger instance (fast, higher cost, has a ceiling)
Option B     : Read replicas (more setup, scales further)
Recommend    : Option B, pilot in staging first
Owner / Date : Client DB team / before the next sale

    📊 Intermediate: Prioritising Recommendations

    textPRIORITY SCORE = (Risk x Impact) / Effort      each scored 1 to 5
Backup restore test : (5 x 5) / 1 = 25.0  → do first
Add read replicas   : (4 x 4) / 3 = 5.3   → plan next
Rename resources    : (1 x 1) / 1 = 1.0   → when convenient
    ⚡
    Quick wins first: high value and low effort items cut risk fast and build trust for the larger changes.

    🗓️ Advanced: Building a Technical Roadmap

    QuarterThemeExample items
    Q1StabiliseBackup restore test, MFA, alerting on key metrics
    Q2ScaleCaching, read replicas, load tests in staging
    Q3HardenRBAC clean-up, secrets vault, patch cadence
    Q4OptimiseCost review, architecture review for next year
    📌
    A roadmap is a shared agreement with owners and dates, not a list of ideas.

    Module 07

    Communicating Advice & Handling Pushback

    The same advice must land differently with executives and engineers. Learn to structure findings and respond to objections.

    🗣️ Know Your Audience

    👔 Executives

    Business impact, risk and cost. "Without this change, a sale-day outage could cost a full day of revenue."

    🛠️ Engineers

    Technical detail and steps. "Add read replicas and route product queries to them."

    AudienceCares aboutFormat
    Executive sponsorRisk, cost, timelinesOne page, 3 key points
    ArchitectDesign trade-offsDiagrams, options table
    OperationsRunbooks, alerts, effortStep lists, checklists

    🧱 Intermediate: Presenting Findings

    Figure 7.1 · A five-part structure for every finding
    🌟
    Start the meeting with what the client does well. It lowers defensiveness and builds credibility for the harder messages.

    🛡️ Advanced: Handling Pushback

    ObjectionStrong response
    "We have always done it this way."Ask about their goals, show data on the risk, offer a low-effort pilot.
    "We have no budget."Show the cost of the risk, split into small steps, start with free quick wins.
    "Our engineers are too busy."Reduce scope to the top three items and offer templates or workshops.
    "Is this really a problem?"Share the metric and a similar incident; let the data speak.
    🤝
    If you do not know an answer, say you will confirm with engineering and give a date. Trust grows from honesty, not instant answers.

    Module 08

    Scenarios & Troubleshooting

    Apply everything to realistic cases: slowdowns, security slips, ignored advice and over-engineered requests.

    🛒 Case Study: The Sale-Day Slowdown

    An online retailer's site slows every sale day. Support has restarted servers three times. The TAM looks at the bigger picture.

    Figure 8.1 · From repeated restarts to a root-cause fix

    Outcome: caching for product pages cut database load first (quick win), read replicas followed. Peak CPU fell to about 55% at the next sale.

    🔑 Case Study: The Shared Admin Password

    During a review, the TAM notices the database admin password posted in a team chat.

    1. Ask the client to rotate the password immediately.
    2. Move secrets to a vault and remove the message.
    3. Review who had access and check audit logs.
    4. Add MFA and role-based access to the follow-up roadmap.

    🙉 Troubleshooting: The Client Ignores Recommendations

    Six months on, none of your 15 recommendations has started.

    Likely causeWhat to do
    Too many items, no prioritiesCut to the top three with owners and dates
    No link to client goalsReframe each item in terms of their business risk
    No executive sponsorFind the sponsor and present impact and cost
    Team capacityOffer workshops, templates, or phased steps

    🏗️ Scenario: The Over-Engineered Request

    A small internal tool serves 200 users, yet the client wants an active-active multi-region deployment.

    🎯
    Challenge gently against requirements: availability target, cost, team skills. Propose a simpler design (single region, multi-zone, tested backups) and document the decision.

    Module 11

    Interview Prep

    Twelve interview questions across four levels. Say your answer out loud first, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.What does strategic technical advisory mean?
    Proactive guidance on best practices, scalability and security, based on deep knowledge of the client's stack.
    IQ2.How is a TAM different from support?
    Support fixes specific issues quickly; a TAM owns the long-term technical health and success of the account.
    IQ3.What is a single point of failure?
    A component with no backup whose failure stops the service; fix it with redundancy.

    🌿 Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you start with a new client?
    Discovery: interview engineers, collect diagrams, runbooks, dashboards and incident history, then verify the architecture.
    IQ5.How do you prioritise recommendations?
    Score by risk and impact against effort, do quick wins first and agree owners and dates.
    IQ6.Vertical versus horizontal scaling?
    Vertical makes one machine bigger (simple, capped). Horizontal adds machines (grows further, needs a suitable design).

    🌳 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.A client resists a best practice. What do you do?
    Understand their constraint, explain the risk with data, offer options, and record the accepted risk with an owner.
    IQ8.How do you advise on security without owning compliance?
    Guide configuration and usage so controls are supported, and leave formal audit and legal decisions to the client's teams.
    IQ9.How do you prove your advice worked?
    Agree measurable outcomes (peak CPU, incident count, restore time) and review them in follow-ups.

    🏔️ Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.Describe a time you changed a client's architecture direction.
    Use a story: context, data you gathered, options and trade-offs, how you built sponsor support, and the measured result.
    IQ11.How do you balance cost, risk and speed for an advisory roadmap?
    Use pillar trade-offs: protect high-risk items first, take quick wins early, and phase costly work with clear decision points.
    IQ12.How would you scale a TAM practice across 20 accounts?
    Standardise gap-review templates, health scores and roadmaps, automate data collection, and focus personal time on the highest-risk accounts.

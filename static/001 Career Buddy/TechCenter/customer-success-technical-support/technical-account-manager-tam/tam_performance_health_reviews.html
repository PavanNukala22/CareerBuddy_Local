    Module 01

    Foundations of Performance & Health Reviews

    What health and performance reviews are, how they differ from support, and what you will be able to do by the end of this course.

    🧭 Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    A Technical Account Manager (TAM) does not wait for things to break. In performance and health reviews you deliver technical health checks, SLA compliance reviews and infrastructure optimization audits so risks are found, measured and fixed before the client feels them.

    🩺
    Health checks
    Review availability, performance, security hygiene, capacity and operations.
    📜
    SLA reviews
    Prove, with data, whether service commitments were met.
    ⚡
    Performance
    Read latency, errors and load to find bottlenecks.
    🛠️
    Optimization audits
    Cut waste, remove risk and plan capacity.
    🚗
    Analogy: support is the roadside rescue. A health review is the scheduled service: you read the gauges, check the wear, and fix small problems before the car breaks down on the highway.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Plan and run a technical health check and turn findings into a health score.
    2. Calculate availability, downtime and error budget, and run an SLA compliance review.
    3. Read latency, traffic, errors and saturation, and find the real bottleneck.
    4. Audit infrastructure for waste, risk and capacity, and prioritise findings.
    5. Run reviews on a regular cadence and track trends and actions to closure.
    6. Report results clearly to executives and engineers and handle pushback.

    Module 02

    Delivering Technical Health Checks

    Review the five health areas, collect real evidence and turn findings into a score the client can follow.

    🩺 Basic: What a Health Check Is

    A technical health check is a planned, evidence-based review of a client environment against agreed expectations. It answers one question: how healthy is this system right now, and what is likely to go wrong next?

    🚑 Reactive support

    Starts when something breaks. Fixes the specific issue and closes the ticket.

    🩺 Proactive health check

    Starts on a schedule. Looks across the whole environment for risk, drift and trends.

    🟢
    Availability
    Is the service up and recoverable?
    ⚡
    Performance
    Is it fast enough under load?
    🔐
    Security hygiene
    Are patches, access and keys in order?
    📦
    Capacity
    Is there room to grow?
    ⚙️
    Operations
    Monitoring, backups, runbooks, change control.

    🔬 Intermediate: Data Sources & Evidence

    SourceWhat it gives youExample
    Monitoring and dashboardsMetrics over timeCPU, latency, error rate for 30 days
    Incident and ticket historyPatterns of failureThree database timeouts in two months
    Configuration and versionsDrift and support statusSoftware two versions behind
    Change and deployment historyCause of regressionsLatency rose after Tuesday release
    Logs and tracesDetail behind a symptomSlow query in checkout path
    InterviewsContext and unwritten risks"We have never tested a restore"
    📏
    Evidence beats opinion. "Database CPU reached 88% at the 6 pm peak on 12 of the last 14 days" is stronger than "the database feels slow".

    📋 Intermediate: The Health Check Checklist

    Figure 2.1 · The five areas of a technical health check
    AreaWhat to checkRed flag
    AvailabilityRedundancy, failover tests, restore testsSingle point of failure; backups never restored
    Performancep95 latency, error rate, slow queriesLatency rising while traffic is flat
    Security hygienePatch level, expired certificates, shared admin accountsCertificate expiring this week
    CapacityPeak utilisation and growth trendPeak above 80% with no plan
    OperationsAlert coverage, runbooks, change processAlerts exist but nobody owns them

    🧮 Advanced: Health Scoring

    A health score combines area scores using weights agreed with the client. Weights show what matters most to their business.

    textWeights: Availability 30%  Performance 25%  Security 20%  Capacity 15%  Operations 10%
Area scores (0-100): 92, 70, 80, 60, 75
Overall = (0.30 x 92) + (0.25 x 70) + (0.20 x 80) + (0.15 x 60) + (0.10 x 75)
        = 27.6 + 17.5 + 16.0 + 9.0 + 7.5 = 77.6  → Amber
RAG: Green ≥ 85   Amber 70-84   Red < 70
    ⚠️
    A score is not the finding. A critical risk, such as untested backups, can override a good overall number. Always show the evidence and the trend next to the score.

    Module 03

    Running SLA Compliance Reviews

    Understand SLI, SLO and SLA, calculate availability and downtime, and report compliance honestly.

    📜 Basic: SLI, SLO and SLA

    📏
    SLI
    The measurement: for example, the share of requests that succeed.
    🎯
    SLO
    The internal target for that measurement: for example, 99.95%.
    📝
    SLA
    The contractual promise to the client, often with service credits if missed.
    📦
    Analogy: a delivery company times each parcel (SLI), aims to deliver 99% on time (SLO) and promises a refund if it falls below 98% (SLA). Set the SLO tighter than the SLA so you have a safety margin.

    🧮 Intermediate: Availability Math

    AvailabilityDowntime per 30-day monthDowntime per year
    99%7 h 12 min3.65 days
    99.5%3 h 36 min1.83 days
    99.9%43.2 min8.76 hours
    99.95%21.6 min4.38 hours
    99.99%4.32 min52.6 min
    textAvailability = (total minutes - downtime minutes) / total minutes
30 days = 43,200 minutes. SLA = 99.9%  →  allowed downtime = 43.2 min (the error budget)
Measured downtime = 52 min  →  (43,200 - 52) / 43,200 = 99.88%
99.88% < 99.9%  →  SLA missed by 8.8 minutes

    🎫 Intermediate: Support & Service Metrics

    SLAs also cover how fast the provider responds. Always use the targets in the client's own contract; the numbers below are examples only.

    MetricMeaningExample target
    First response timeTime to the first meaningful replyP1: 15 minutes
    Time to resolutionTime until the issue is resolvedP1: 4 hours
    Backlog ageHow long open tickets have waitedNo P2 older than 5 days
    Reopen rateShare of tickets reopenedBelow 5%
    Customer satisfactionSurvey score after closureAbove 4.5 of 5

    🔎 Advanced: Running an SLA Review

    Figure 3.1 · The five steps of an SLA compliance review
    • Use the contract definition. Check what counts as downtime, the measurement window and the exclusions (planned maintenance, client-caused issues).
    • Measure from the user view where possible, not only from server uptime.
    • Report honestly when the SLA is missed and explain the root cause and the fix.
    • Use the error budget to guide risky changes: little budget left means slow down.

    Module 04

    Analysing Performance

    Read the golden signals, use percentiles and baselines, find the real bottleneck and forecast when capacity runs out.

    ⚡ Basic: The Four Golden Signals

    ⏱️
    Latency
    How long requests take.
    📈
    Traffic
    How much demand the system receives.
    ❌
    Errors
    The share of requests that fail.
    🧱
    Saturation
    How full the most constrained resource is.
    🛣️
    Analogy: on a motorway, latency is the journey time, traffic is cars per minute, errors are accidents and saturation is how full the lanes are.

    📊 Intermediate: Percentiles & Baselines

    MetricWhat it tells youHealthy sign
    Average latencyTypical speed, but hides slow requestsClose to p50
    p95 / p99 latencySpeed for the slowest 5% / 1% of requestsFlat as load rises
    Error rateShare of failed requestsNear 0% at peak
    CPU / memory peakHow close to the limitPeaks below about 70-80%
    Baseline comparisonToday against normalWithin the usual range
    📉
    Averages mislead. If 99 requests take 100 ms and one takes 10,000 ms, the average is 199 ms, which looks fine, but one user waited 10 seconds. Use p95 and p99.

    🔬 Intermediate: Finding the Bottleneck

    Slow database queriesMissing indexCache missesConnection pool exhaustedThread starvationDisk I/ONetwork bandwidthExternal API delay
    SymptomLikely causeHow to check
    High latency, low CPUWaiting on a database or external callTraces, slow query log
    High CPU on one node onlyUneven load balancing or a hot jobPer-node metrics
    Errors only at peakPool, thread or rate-limit exhaustionSaturation metrics at peak
    Latency rose after a releaseCode or configuration changeCompare change history to the graph

    🧮 Advanced: Forecasting & Load Testing

    textPeak CPU today = 55%    Threshold = 80%    Growth = +3 points per month
Linear: (80 - 55) / 3 = 8.3 months until the threshold

Compound growth of 5% per month:  55 x 1.05^n = 80
n = ln(80/55) / ln(1.05) = 0.3747 / 0.0488 = 7.7 months
    ⚠️
    Run heavy load tests on a staging copy, or in an agreed window with client approval. Never surprise production.

    Module 05

    Infrastructure Optimization Audits

    Audit infrastructure for waste, risk and resilience, and prioritise what to fix first.

    🛠️ Basic: What an Optimization Audit Looks At

    An infrastructure optimization audit checks whether the environment is efficient, resilient and well run. A common frame is five pillars:

    PillarAudit question
    Operational excellenceCan the team run, monitor and change it safely?
    SecurityAre access, patches, keys and data protected?
    ReliabilityDoes it survive failures and recover quickly?
    Performance efficiencyDoes it use resources well as demand changes?
    Cost optimizationAre we paying only for what we need?

    📉 Intermediate: Utilisation & Right-sizing

    ObservationMeaningTypical action
    CPU peak under 20% for 4-6 weeksOversizedMove down one size and re-measure
    Memory above 85% most of the timeUndersized or leakingIncrease or investigate the leak
    Idle volumes, snapshots, load balancersWasteConfirm owner, then remove
    Steady 24/7 loadPredictableConsider committed-use discounts
    Spiky loadVariableUse autoscaling
    📏
    Use peak data over a long enough window (several weeks, covering business cycles) and always keep headroom. Right-size in steps, with a rollback plan.

    🛟 Intermediate: Resilience & Recovery Checks

    🗄️ RPO (Recovery Point Objective)

    How much data the business can afford to lose, measured in time. Example: 15 minutes.

    ⏳ RTO (Recovery Time Objective)

    How long the business can wait for recovery. Example: 2 hours.

    • Is there a single point of failure (one database, one zone, one engineer)?
    • Were backups restored in a test within the last 6 to 12 months? Untested backups are unproven.
    • Is failover tested and documented in a runbook?
    • Do monitoring and alerts cover the signals that matter, and does someone own each alert?

    🧮 Advanced: Prioritising Findings

    textPriority = (Risk x Impact) / Effort      (each scored 1-5)
Untested backups   (5 x 5) / 1 = 25.0
Unpatched OS       (4 x 4) / 2 = 8.0
Oversized database (3 x 4) / 2 = 6.0
Unused volumes     (1 x 2) / 1 = 2.0
Order: backups, patching, right-sizing, clean-up
    🎯
    Take quick wins early, but never let a high-risk item wait behind low-value work.

    Module 06

    The Review Process & Cadence

    Run reviews on a regular rhythm, track trends and turn findings into owned, dated actions.

    🔄 The Review Cycle

    Figure 6.1 · Each review follows the same five steps, then the cycle starts again
    • Prepare: agree scope, period, stakeholders and success measures.
    • Collect: pull metrics, tickets and change history; interview engineers.
    • Analyse: compare with baselines and targets; score and rank findings.
    • Present: share findings and options in plain language.
    • Act: agree owners and dates, then follow up.

    🗓️ Intermediate: Review Cadence

    ReviewFrequencyAudienceOutput
    Metrics checkWeeklyTAM and client engineersAlerts, anomalies, quick fixes
    SLA reportMonthlyClient operationsAvailability and ticket metrics
    Technical health checkQuarterlyClient engineering leadsHealth score, findings, actions
    Executive reviewQuarterly or twice a yearClient leadershipSummary, risks, decisions
    Optimization auditAnnually or after major changeEngineering and financeSavings, risk and roadmap
    📝
    These frequencies are examples. Adjust them to the contract, the risk and the size of the account.

    📈 Intermediate: Trends & Health Scores

    • Compare every score with the previous review and show the direction (improving, stable, declining).
    • Use leading indicators (capacity headroom, alert coverage, patch age) as well as lagging ones (incidents, SLA misses).
    • Keep a risk register of open findings with owner, impact and due date.
    • Look for repeat findings: the same issue three reviews in a row is a process problem.

    ✅ Advanced: From Findings to Action Plan

    FindingPriorityOwnerDueStatus
    Backups never restoredHighClient DBA30 daysOpen
    Alerts have no ownerMediumClient SRE lead45 daysIn progress
    Oversized staging clusterLowClient platform team90 daysPlanned
    • Every action has one owner and one date.
    • Close the loop: verify the action was done and worked, then update the record.
    • If an action slips twice, escalate with the business impact.

    🤝 Advanced: Delivering Bad News & Handling Pushback

    🚫 Blame framing

    "Your team missed the SLA because of poor change control."

    ✅ Fact framing

    "Availability was 99.88% against 99.9%. The cause was an unplanned database failover. Here are three options to prevent a repeat."

    • No surprises: pre-brief key stakeholders before the formal review.
    • Data first, then options, then your recommendation.
    • If the client disputes a number, review the data and the contract definition together; never defend a figure you cannot show.
    • Record accepted risks with an owner and a review date.

    Module 08

    Scenarios & Case Studies

    Apply health checks, SLA reviews and optimization audits to realistic client situations.

    🟢 Case Study: The Green Dashboard, Unhappy Client

    Situation: The monitoring dashboard shows 99.95% uptime for the quarter, yet the client's customers complain that checkout fails at lunchtime.

    Figure 8.1 · From a misleading dashboard to a user-focused measure
    • Root cause: monitoring checked that servers were up, not that checkouts succeeded. A partial failure in the payment API was invisible.
    • Fix: add synthetic checkout checks and a success-rate SLI; report the SLA from the user view.
    • Lesson: a green dashboard is only as good as what it measures.

    📜 Case Study: The SLA Month

    Situation: The SLA is 99.9% per 30-day month. The client reports 58 minutes of downtime and asks whether the SLA was missed.

    textPlanned maintenance in the agreed window: 20 min (excluded by contract)
Unplanned downtime: 38 min
Allowed at 99.9%: 43.2 min
Availability = (43,200 - 38) / 43,200 = 99.912%  → SLA met, with only 5.2 min of budget left
    • Confirm the contract exclusion and that the maintenance followed the notice rules.
    • Even though the SLA is met, explain the 38-minute incident and agree prevention steps.
    • Share the remaining error budget and slow risky changes this month.

    🛠️ Troubleshooting: The Oversized Cluster

    Situation: A cluster averages 12% CPU with a 30% peak. Finance asks to cut the bill by half immediately.

    • Check at least 4 to 6 weeks of data, including month-end and promotion peaks.
    • Right-size in steps, keeping headroom and N+1 redundancy.
    • Test in staging, schedule the change, and keep a rollback plan.
    • Agree success measures: cost down, p95 latency and error rate unchanged.

    📉 Scenario: The Falling Health Score

    Situation: The health score has fallen for three quarters (86, 79, 72). The same three actions are still open.

    • Show the trend and link each open action to a business risk.
    • Ask for a decision, not just attention: who owns each action and by when?
    • Escalate to the executive sponsor with options (fix, accept risk with owner, or fund help).
    • Record the decision and review it next quarter.

    Task 2BeginnerSpot the red flags

    Red flags: monitoring covers only one signal; backups are unproven; alerts have no owner. Fixes: add latency, error and saturation monitoring; run a restore test; assign an owner and runbook to each alert.

    🌿 Intermediate Tasks

    Task 3IntermediateCalculate availability
    A 30-day month has 25 minutes of unplanned downtime and 30 minutes of planned maintenance that the contract excludes. The SLA is 99.9%. Is the SLA met?
    30 days = 43,200 minutes. Only unplanned downtime counts.
    Allowed: 43.2 min. Unplanned: 25 min. Availability = (43,200 - 25) / 43,200 = 99.942%. SLA met, with about 18 minutes of error budget left.
    Task 4IntermediateScore the health
    Weights: Availability 30%, Performance 25%, Security 20%, Capacity 15%, Operations 10%. Scores: 90, 65, 80, 70, 60. Calculate the overall score and RAG (Green ≥ 85, Amber 70-84, Red < 70).
    Multiply each score by its weight, then add.
    27 + 16.25 + 16 + 10.5 + 6 = 75.75, so Amber. The weakest areas to discuss first are Operations and Performance.

    🌳 Advanced Tasks

    Task 5AdvancedWrite a right-sizing recommendation
    A database has had a 25% peak CPU and 40% peak memory over six weeks. Write a recommendation (observation, risk, two options, advice, owner).
    Mention headroom, testing and rollback.
    Observation: peaks are 25% CPU and 40% memory over six weeks. Risk: paying for unused capacity. Options: (A) move down one instance size; (B) keep the size and use committed-use pricing. Advice: option A in staging first, keep headroom, rollback plan. Owner: client DBA with TAM review at 30 days.
    Task 6AdvancedForecast capacity
    Peak CPU is 60% and grows 4% per month (compound). The threshold is 80%. When is it crossed, and when should work start?
    n = ln(80/60) / ln(1.04).
    n = 0.2877 / 0.0392 = about 7.3 months, so the threshold is crossed during month 8. With a 3-month lead time for change, start planning by month 4.

    Module 11

    Interview Prep

    Twelve interview questions across four levels. Say your answer out loud first, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.What is a technical health check?
    A planned, evidence-based review of an environment across availability, performance, security hygiene, capacity and operations, ending in findings and actions.
    IQ2.How is a health review different from support?
    Support reacts to a specific issue; a review proactively looks across the environment for risk and trends.
    IQ3.What is the difference between SLI, SLO and SLA?
    SLI is the measurement, SLO is the internal target, SLA is the contractual commitment with consequences.

    🌿 Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you calculate availability?
    (Total time - downtime) / total time, applying the contract definition of downtime and exclusions.
    IQ5.Why do percentiles matter more than averages?
    Averages hide slow requests; p95 and p99 show what the slowest users experience.
    IQ6.How do you find a bottleneck?
    Check the golden signals, compare with a baseline, use traces and logs, and look at saturation in each layer.

    🌳 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.A client disputes your SLA numbers. What do you do?
    Review data sources and the contract definition together, reconcile timestamps, correct errors openly and agree the method for the future.
    IQ8.How do you decide what to fix first?
    Score by (Risk x Impact) / Effort, take quick wins, and never leave a high-risk item waiting.
    IQ9.How do you right-size safely?
    Use several weeks of peak data, move one step at a time, keep headroom and N+1, test in staging and keep a rollback plan.

    🏔️ Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.A health score is falling and actions are not being done. What now?
    Show the trend and business impact, ask for decisions and owners, escalate to the sponsor with options, and record accepted risks.
    IQ11.How do you prove your reviews add value?
    Track outcomes: fewer incidents, SLA met, savings achieved, actions closed on time and health score improving.
    IQ12.How would you run reviews across 20 accounts?
    Standardise templates, scoring and cadence, automate data collection, and spend personal time on the highest-risk accounts.

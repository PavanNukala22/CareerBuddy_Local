    Module 01

    Foundations of Enterprise Escalation Oversight

    What it means to be the single point of contact and primary advocate for high-priority technical issues, and how this differs from normal support.

    🚨 Course Overview: What the Role Needs

    When a serious problem hits an enterprise client, many people get involved and confusion grows. The TAM acts as the single point of contact (one voice to the client) and the primary advocate (the person who makes sure our teams treat the issue with the right urgency).

    📞
    Single point of contact
    One owner, one voice, no mixed messages.
    📣
    Advocate
    Gets the right priority and people.
    ⏱️
    Oversight
    Tracks the issue until it is truly closed.
    🔁
    Prevention
    Learns from each incident.
    🚑
    Analogy: think of a hospital charge nurse. Doctors treat the patient, but one person keeps the family informed and makes sure nothing falls through the cracks.

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    By the end of this course you will be able to:

    1. Classify issues by severity and decide when to escalate.
    2. Act as owner and advocate without overpromising.
    3. Run or support an incident bridge with clear roles.
    4. Write strong escalations to engineering and clear updates to clients.
    5. Lead a blameless root cause review and track prevention actions.
    6. Handle executive pressure and stalled escalations calmly.

    Module 02

    Severity, Triage & Escalation Triggers

    Classify issues fast, understand SLA clocks and know exactly when an issue must be escalated.

    🎚️ Basic: Severity Levels

    SeverityMeaningExampleTypical response
    Sev1Critical: production down, no workaroundCheckout fails for all usersImmediate, 24x7, bridge
    Sev2Major: serious degradation or partial outagePayments slow for 30% of usersWithin 1 hour
    Sev3Moderate: limited impact, workaround existsOne report failsBusiness hours
    Sev4Low: question or cosmetic issueWrong label in UINormal queue
    📌
    Severity is about impact. Priority is about order of work. A cosmetic bug on the CEO's demo day may get high priority but stay Sev4.

    🔎 Intermediate: The Triage Checklist

    textTRIAGE QUESTIONS (ask in the first 5 minutes)
Impact: who is affected? how many users? which function?
Scope: all regions or one? since when? still happening?
Business: revenue, legal or safety exposure? any hard deadline?
Change: any release, config change or traffic spike just before?
Workaround: is there any way to restore partial service now?

    ⏱️ Intermediate: SLA Clocks

    An SLA usually has separate clocks for first response, updates and resolution or mitigation.

    textSEV1 SLA: response 15 min | updates every 30 min | resolution target 4 hours
Ticket opened 14:10
First response due : 14:10 + 15 min = 14:25
Next update due   : 14:25 + 30 min = 14:55
Resolution target : 14:10 + 4 h = 18:10
    ⚠️
    Escalate before the SLA is breached, not after. A warning at 75% of the clock is far more useful than an apology.

    🚦 Advanced: When to Escalate

    TriggerWhy it matters
    Sev1 or major customer-visible outageNeeds immediate mobilisation
    SLA breach is likelyTime to add people or skills
    No progress after an agreed timeWork may be stuck
    Security or data exposure suspectedSpecial handling and legal care
    Repeated incident (third time)Pattern, not luck
    Executive or contractual riskBusiness-level attention needed

    Module 03

    Single Point of Contact & Advocacy

    What the TAM owns during an escalation, how to advocate effectively inside the company and how to avoid overpromising.

    🧑‍💼 Basic: What the TAM Owns

    TAM ownsTAM does not own
    Client communication and expectationsWriting every fix personally
    Making sure there is an owner and a planEngineering decisions about the code
    Tracking time against the SLACommercial credits (account team)
    Escalation to the right peopleLegal and compliance decisions

    📋 Intermediate: Ownership with RACI

    RACI clarifies roles: Responsible (does the work), Accountable (final owner), Consulted, Informed.

    ActivityTAMSupportEngineeringClient
    Diagnose root problemCRAI
    Deploy hotfixICA/RI
    Client status updatesA/RCCI
    Business impact sign-offCIIA/R

    📣 Intermediate: Advocating Effectively

    📊
    Bring facts
    Impact, timeline and evidence, not emotion.
    🎯
    State the ask
    What exactly do you need, from whom, by when?
    🤝
    Respect engineers
    Make it easy to help, avoid blame.
    🔔
    Follow up
    Check progress at the agreed time.
    💡
    Advocacy works through credibility. If every issue is "urgent", none are. Save escalation weight for real impact.

    🚫 Advanced: Advocacy vs Overpromising

    ❌ Overpromising

    "We will fix it in two hours" without engineering confirmation. Misses the date and destroys trust.

    ✅ Honest advocacy

    "Engineering is investigating; next update at 15:30; I will confirm a fix estimate then."

    Commit to actions and update times that you control. Share fix dates only after engineering confirms them.

    Module 04

    Incident Command & the War Room

    Run or support a major-incident bridge: roles, rules, update cadence and the lifecycle from detection to resolution.

    🎖️ Intermediate: Roles on the Bridge

    RoleResponsibility
    Incident commanderRuns the bridge, sets priorities, makes decisions
    Technical leadDirects diagnosis and fix work
    Communications lead (often the TAM)Owns client and internal updates
    ScribeRecords timeline, decisions and actions
    Subject expertsJoin when needed, leave when done
    📌
    In small teams one person may hold two roles, but communication and command should not be the same person during a severe incident.

    🔁 Intermediate: The Incident Lifecycle

    Figure 4.1 · Mitigation comes before root cause

    Restore first, investigate second. A rollback or workaround that ends client impact is better than a perfect diagnosis an hour later.

    📜 Intermediate: War Room Rules

    • One conversation at a time; the commander decides who speaks.
    • Decisions and actions are written down with an owner and time.
    • Side discussions go to a separate channel.
    • No blame during the incident; discuss it in the review.
    • Client-facing messages go only through the communications lead.

    📝 Advanced: The Status Update Template

    text[SEV1] Checkout failures - Update #3 - 15:30 UTC
Impact   : ~40% of checkout attempts fail (EU region)
Status   : Mitigation in progress: rolling back release 4.2
Next step: Confirm error rate drops below 1%
ETA      : Rollback complete by 15:50; next update 16:00
Owner    : Incident commander A. Rao; TAM contact: you

    Module 05

    Cross-Team Coordination & Writing Escalations

    Move issues through support tiers and engineering with clear, evidence-based escalations and resolve priority conflicts.

    🪜 Intermediate: The Escalation Path

    Figure 5.1 · Escalate up only when a trigger is met

    ✍️ Intermediate: Writing an Engineering Escalation

    textTITLE   : Checkout 5xx errors for EU customers (Sev1)
IMPACT  : ~40% failed checkouts since 14:05 UTC; estimated loss $12k/hour
TIMELINE: 13:50 release 4.2 deployed; 14:05 error spike; 14:10 ticket opened
EVIDENCE: error logs (link), dashboard (link), request IDs
TRIED   : restarted app servers: no change
ASK     : on-call engineer on bridge now; confirm rollback option by 14:40
    💡
    A good escalation lets the engineer start working without asking a single question.

    🔀 Intermediate: Handoffs

    Handoff problemFix
    Context lost between tiersKeep one running timeline in the ticket
    Client repeats the storyTAM summarises and confirms once
    Unclear ownerName one owner at every moment
    Shift changesWritten handover with open actions

    ⚖️ Advanced: Competing Priorities

    Engineering often has several urgent items. Use impact-based comparison rather than who shouts loudest.

    FactorQuestion
    Users affectedHow many people or accounts?
    Business impactRevenue, legal, safety?
    WorkaroundIs there a way to reduce harm now?
    Time sensitivityHard deadlines or growing damage?
    📌
    If you lose the priority debate, ask for the reasoning and an updated ETA so the client still gets an honest message.

    Module 07

    Root Cause Analysis & Prevention

    Blameless reviews, 5 Whys, corrective actions and the metrics that show whether escalations are improving.

    🕵️ Intermediate: The 5 Whys

    textProblem: Checkout failed for 2 hours
Why 1: The app returned 500 errors  → a database query timed out
Why 2: The query timed out        → a new report query locked a table
Why 3: The query reached production → no performance test on production-sized data
Why 4: No such test exists       → release checklist has no data-volume step
Root cause: Release process lacks a production-scale performance check
    📌
    Keep asking why until you reach a process or system cause, not a person.

    🤝 Intermediate: Blameless Post-Incident Review

    • Hold it within a few days, while memory is fresh.
    • Focus on the timeline, decisions and system weaknesses.
    • Ask "how did the system allow this?" not "who did this?"
    • Agree actions with owners and dates.
    • Share a summary with the client.

    🛠️ Intermediate: Corrective Actions

    TypeExampleStrength
    DetectAlert on checkout error rateFinds problems sooner
    PreventAdd performance step to release checklistStops the cause
    MitigateAutomatic rollbackLimits impact
    ProcessClearer escalation contactsFaster response

    📊 Advanced: Escalation Metrics

    textFour incidents: acknowledgement times 5, 15, 10, 10 min | resolution times 30, 90, 60, 120 min
MTTA (mean time to acknowledge) = (5+15+10+10) / 4 = 10 min
MTTR (mean time to resolve)     = (30+90+60+120) / 4 = 75 min
Repeat rate = repeated incidents / total incidents
Track by account, severity and root-cause category to find patterns.

    Module 08

    Scenarios & Troubleshooting

    Practise judgement in realistic high-pressure cases.

    💳 Case Study: Sev1 on Payday

    A payroll client cannot run payroll on the last working day before salaries are due. Thousands of employees will be paid late.

    Figure 8.1 · Handle the deadline first

    Outcome: a manual workaround delivered the payroll file by the bank cut-off, then a full fix and review followed.

    👔 Case Study: The Executive Demands Everything

    The client's CIO emails your CEO and demands that engineering drop everything for a Sev2 issue.

    1. Acknowledge the concern and confirm the real impact.
    2. Check severity honestly with facts; upgrade only if impact justifies it.
    3. Offer a direct update call with a clear plan and checkpoints.
    4. Keep your own leadership informed so nobody is surprised.

    🧊 Troubleshooting: Engineering Is Not Responding

    Likely causeWhat to do
    Escalation unclear or missing factsRewrite with impact, evidence and a specific ask
    Wrong person or queueFind the owning team and on-call
    Competing priorityAsk for reasoning; escalate to engineering management with impact data
    No SLA for internal teamsAgree an internal response expectation

    🔂 Scenario: The Third Repeat Incident

    The same cache failure has now hit this client three times in two months.

    🎯
    Treat repeats as a problem record: gather all three timelines, find common causes, assign one owner and set a prevention date. Share the plan with the client proactively.

    (45+60+90+25) / 4 = 55 minutes.
    Task 6AdvancedHandle the CIO email
    The CIO writes: "This is unacceptable, fix it now." Draft your reply.
    Acknowledge, facts, plan, next update time.
    Acknowledge impact; state status and current action; name the owner; give next update time; offer a direct call.

    🏔️ Expert Tasks

    Task 7Expert5 Whys
    A scheduled job crashed the database every Monday for three weeks. Do a 5 Whys.
    Go from symptom to a process cause.
    Crash from heavy job; job unlimited; no resource limits; no review of job config; process lacks capacity review. Action: limits, alerts, review step.
    Task 8ExpertDesign an escalation program
    You manage 20 enterprise accounts. What do you standardise and what do you tailor?
    Think templates versus account context.
    Standardise severity matrix, templates, review process and metrics; tailor contacts, SLAs, quiet hours and communication preferences per account.

    Module 11

    Interview Prep

    Twelve interview questions across four levels. Say your answer out loud first, then compare with the model answer.

    🌱 Beginner Interview Questions

    IQ1.What does a single point of contact do during an escalation?
    Gives the client one consistent voice, owns communication and makes sure there is an owner and plan.
    IQ2.Difference between severity and priority?
    Severity is the impact; priority is the order of work, which can be influenced by business context.
    IQ3.What is a workaround?
    A temporary method to restore service while the permanent fix is built.

    🌿 Intermediate Interview Questions

    IQ4.How do you decide when to escalate?
    Use triggers: Sev1, likely SLA breach, no progress, security exposure, repeat incidents or executive and contract risk.
    IQ5.What makes a good engineering escalation?
    Clear impact, timeline, evidence, actions tried and a specific ask with a time frame.
    IQ6.How do you run client updates during a Sev1?
    Send updates on a fixed cadence, even with no news, with impact, status, next step and next update time.

    🌳 Advanced Interview Questions

    IQ7.How do you handle an angry executive?
    Acknowledge, confirm facts, give a plan with checkpoints, offer a direct line and keep internal leadership aligned.
    IQ8.How do you avoid overpromising?
    Commit to update times and actions you control; share fix dates only once engineering confirms.
    IQ9.How do you lead a blameless review?
    Build the timeline, focus on system causes with 5 Whys, agree owned actions with dates and share a summary.

    🏔️ Expert Interview Questions

    IQ10.Describe an escalation you turned around.
    Tell a story: situation, your triage, how you mobilised people, communication, outcome and what changed afterwards.
    IQ11.How would you reduce repeat incidents across accounts?
    Track root-cause categories, run problem records for repeats, fix systemic causes and share learnings with engineering and product.
    IQ12.How would you build an escalation process for 20 accounts?
    Standardise matrix, templates and metrics; tailor contacts and SLAs; review quarterly with data.
